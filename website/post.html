What the Cheese Foundations Certificate is for

22 May 2019 · Education Programs, International Cheese Federation · 4 minute read

People ask us, most often at the point of joining, whether the Cheese Foundations Certificate is worth six weeks when they already work with cheese every day. Our answer has not changed: the certificate is not there to teach you cheese, it is there to give you the vocabulary to ask about it. Six weeks of milk composition, the cheese families, sanitation as a daily practice, and the words affineurs actually use — and at the end of it you can stand in front of a maker, a monger and an inspector and ask each of them a question they will take seriously.

That is a smaller claim than it sounds, and a more useful one. A monger who knows what a somatic cell count means will read a spec sheet differently. A new hire who can say that a rind is slipping, rather than that a cheese looks wrong, gets a straight answer from the affineur instead of a shrug. We have watched members go into the Certified Cheese Professional exam two years later and credit the foundations course for the sensory half, because the language they learned in week five is the language the exam is written in. The ladder works because each rung assumes the one below it, and this is the rung everything else sits on.

So the honest recommendation: if you have been in the trade under two years, start here. If you have been in it twenty and have never had to explain your process to anyone outside your own building, start here anyway — several of our most experienced members did, and told us afterward it was the sanitation weeks that surprised them. Cohorts are named for cheeses, they fill quickly, and Brook opens next month. Members pay member rates from the day they join.
