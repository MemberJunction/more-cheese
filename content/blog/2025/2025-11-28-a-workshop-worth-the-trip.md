Between a shutdown-delayed data backlog, a busy comment-period season, and two label webinars, it's been a heavier fall than usual for federation programming that happens entirely on a screen. So it's worth flagging that the next event on the calendar is the opposite kind: an in-person, hands-on workshop on stretched-curd technique, running December 10th.

Stretched curd — the method behind mozzarella, provolone, and a handful of less-famous stretched-curd styles — is one of those techniques that's genuinely difficult to teach remotely. The process depends on getting curd to a fairly narrow acidity window before working it in hot water; once it's in range, the curd can be kneaded and pulled into a fibrous, stretchy mass, and outside that window it simply won't behave the same way no matter how hard you work it. Reading that window is something you learn by feel, watching someone else's hands and then getting your own hands corrected in real time. A screen can explain the chemistry. It can't tell you that your curd is a shade too cool or too far past its target pH to stretch properly, which is exactly the kind of thing a workshop instructor catches in about ten seconds.

## Who this is actually for

You don't need current stretched-curd production to get something out of this one. Several past attendees have been members considering adding a mozzarella-style or provolone-style product to an existing lineup and wanting to see the failure modes up close before committing cave space and milk to the experiment — curd that tears instead of stretching, a greasy or waterlogged texture, a batch that simply won't come together. Watching those mistakes happen to someone else's curd, in a room where an instructor can explain why, is a faster education than working through the same mistakes alone at home with a full vat on the line.

It's also simply a chance to be in a room with other members again before the calendar turns over. A fair number of our events this year have skewed toward advocacy and compliance topics, for good reason given how much rulemaking activity there's been — but a workshop built entirely around craft, with no comment period attached to it, is a nice change of pace heading into the holidays. It's also a technique that travels well into a holiday case: a house-made stretched-curd cheese, sold fresh rather than aged, is a lower-overhead way to add something new to a counter without waiting months for a wheel to mature.

## The practical details

Space in hands-on workshops is capped tighter than webinar seats, so if you've been meaning to register, this week is a better time than next week. Registration and the full agenda are on the events page under the workshop's listing; members who've attended past stretched-curd sessions tell us the biggest surprise is how physical the work is — plan for a day on your feet at the vat and the hot-water bench, not a day at a desk. If the December date doesn't work for your schedule, let the Events Committee know what would; workshop scheduling for next year is still being finalized and member availability genuinely shapes it.
