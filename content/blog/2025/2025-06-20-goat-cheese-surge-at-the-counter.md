If your case has more goat cheese in it than it did a couple of years ago, you're not imagining a trend, and you're not alone. Deli specialty cheese sales are up sharply in both volume and dollars this year, while plain American cheese keeps sliding, and among the artisanal categories pulling that growth, goat cheese is out in front. At the big dairy-deli trade show earlier this month, goat cheese was the category everyone was talking about — new formats, new flavors, everything from a fresh spreadable chèvre to an aged, blue-veined wheel. Brie and blue have cooled a little from their own peak. None of this means your case needs a full reset. It does mean the conversation you're having with customers at the counter has shifted, and it's worth being ready for it.

A rep who's fielded this shift for a while put it to us plainly: customers aren't just buying goat cheese anymore, they're asking why it tastes the way it does, what "fresh" versus "aged" chèvre actually means, and whether it's the same animal's milk as the feta two shelves over. Those are exactly the questions a counter staffer trained only on "here's what's popular" can't answer well, and exactly the ones a credentialed monger can.

## Where the Certified Cheesemonger credential fits

This is the situation the Certified Cheesemonger credential was built for — not competition judging, not production chemistry, but the day-to-day work of standing behind a case and helping a customer choose well. The credential covers cheese families and styles across milk types, proper handling and cutting technique, pairing guidance, and the kind of provenance and production knowledge that turns "try this one" into a conversation a customer remembers. If your shop is leaning harder into goat and sheep milk cheeses this year, a staffer working toward this credential will be better equipped for the questions actually coming across the counter, not the ones your training manual assumed three years ago.

For makers rather than retailers, the parallel course is Goat & Sheep Milk Cheesemaking, which several cohorts have moved through this year — worth a look if you're weighing whether to add a goat's-milk line while demand is climbing, or if you're already making one and want the technical grounding to back up what your retail partners are now asking about.

## A caution against overcorrecting

We'd gently push back on any temptation to gut a case and rebuild it entirely around one trend. Trend data describes an average across a large market; it doesn't describe your specific customers, your specific region, or your specific margins. A few members who leaned hard into a single trending category in past years have told us the harder lesson was inventory risk, not demand — a case that's too heavily weighted toward one style is fragile if that style cools even slightly. Read the trend as a signal to rebalance thoughtfully, not a mandate to overhaul.
