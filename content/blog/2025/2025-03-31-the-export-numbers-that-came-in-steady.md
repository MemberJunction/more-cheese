First-quarter trade figures came in this week, and they're a useful counterweight to a lot of the anxious conversation we've been hearing on export calls lately. Total cheese exports for January through March were up from the same quarter last year, with the export market that's mattered most to our members for years remaining the largest single buyer by a wide margin. It's not a dramatic headline, and this week that's exactly the point.

We say that because a lot of what members have asked us about lately has been forward-looking and unsettled — new tariff actions taking shape, retaliatory measures being discussed by trading partners, a general sense that the ground could move under an export contract with little warning. None of that uncertainty shows up yet in a first-quarter number that mostly reflects orders placed and shipped before this spring's policy shifts took hold. Read the good number for what it is: a snapshot of where trade stood before the newest changes, not a forecast of where it's headed.

## Why the composition matters more than the total

The total is only part of the story. Growth this quarter was concentrated in a narrower set of styles and buyers than the industry saw a few years ago, and members who export a broad, generalist lineup have told us the top-line growth doesn't always show up in their own order book. If your export business leans on one style or one market doing well, a strong aggregate number can mask a weak quarter for you personally. We'd rather members check their own contracts against the actual figures than assume a healthy total means a healthy year across the board.

That unevenness is also why the federation has been cautious about calling this quarter a trend rather than a data point. A single strong quarter following a run of tariff-related noise is exactly the kind of number that gets over-read in both directions — treated as proof nothing will change, or dismissed as meaningless because change is clearly coming. Neither read is honest. The right takeaway is narrower: exporters had a solid quarter, and the policy environment they're operating in going forward looks different from the one that produced it.

## What to do with a quarter like this

If you export any volume at all, this is a reasonable moment to revisit your contract terms rather than wait for a tariff announcement to force the conversation. Members who've been through prior periods of trade uncertainty tell us the operations that came out ahead weren't the ones that guessed right about policy — they were the ones with enough contract flexibility, and diversified enough buyers, that a single policy shift in one market didn't sink the whole export line. That's not a prediction about what happens next. It's a description of what's worked before, for members who didn't wait until the number turned bad to start asking the question.

If your operation doesn't export at all, this quarter's figures are still worth a glance, if only because import competition and export demand move the same wholesale cheese market you're selling into domestically.
