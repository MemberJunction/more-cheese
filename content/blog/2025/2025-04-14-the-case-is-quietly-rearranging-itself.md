Nobody's case turns over overnight, but a few members have mentioned the same small pattern lately: goat cheese and gouda have been moving faster this spring, while a handful of longtime staples — brie, blue, havarti — have been sitting a little longer than they used to. Industry reporting on this year's dairy case backs up what those members are seeing anecdotally. It's not a dramatic reshuffle. It's the kind of shift that's easy to miss for a month or two and then hard to ignore once you notice it.

## What seems to be moving, and why it matters more this year

Deli and specialty cheese sales overall are still growing, which is the more important headline than any single variety's fortunes. But within that growth, gouda — particularly smoked styles — is picking up interest from younger buyers looking for something bolder than the standard mild-cheddar snack cube, and goat cheese continues to hold a loyal following built partly on people who want a lower-fat, higher-protein option without giving up flavor. Meanwhile a few once-reliable anchors have slowed enough that more than one member mentioned reconsidering how much shelf space they're worth.

In a normal year, this would be a shrug-and-adjust kind of trend. This year, with feed and energy costs still squeezing margins for a lot of small producers, a slow-moving wheel taking up case space is a more expensive problem than it would have been three years ago. If a cheese sits two extra weeks before it sells, that's real money tied up in inventory at a moment when a lot of members don't have slack to spare. This spring's federal milk production numbers came in a little lower than earlier forecasts too, which matters here in a roundabout way: tighter milk supply tends to support prices for makers, but it does nothing for a retailer sitting on inventory that isn't turning.

Members who sell direct at a counter have told us they're noticing something adjacent to the trend rather than the trend itself: customers asking more questions before they buy. A shopper picking between a familiar blue and an unfamiliar smoked gouda wants to know more about the second one before committing, and the members getting the most out of this shift are the ones willing to have that two-minute conversation at the counter rather than letting a shopper default back to what they already know. It's a small thing, but a case that's quietly rearranging itself rewards a monger who's paying attention to it, and mostly ignores one who isn't.

## What we'd actually suggest doing with this

We're not going to tell you to chase a trend — the case that got you loyal customers is worth more than any single data point about what's moving nationally. What we would suggest is treating this as a nudge to actually look at your own sell-through numbers rather than assume this spring looks like last spring. If you carry a gouda or a goat's-milk cheese that's been an afterthought on your shelf, it may be worth more attention than it's getting. If a brie or a blue has been your dependable seller for years and it's genuinely still moving for you, that's useful information too — not every case has to follow the national pattern, and the members whose cases don't are usually the ones who checked rather than assumed.

If you want to compare notes with other members who buy or make in your category, the Membership & Outreach Committee can point you toward others working the same corner of the case. Sometimes the fastest way to know whether a trend is real for you is to ask three people who aren't you.
