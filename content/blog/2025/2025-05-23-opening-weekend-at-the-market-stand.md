Market season is opening across most of our regions right now, and this year we're hearing from more members setting up a stand for the first time than usual. Some of that is new members finding their footing. Some of it, we suspect, is producers who lost a wholesale account this year and are looking for a channel that doesn't depend on one buyer's order sheet. Either way, a first Saturday goes better with a little preparation than without.

Recent surveys of market shoppers keep landing on the same finding: most people who attend a farmers market at all attend several times a year, and they say they're there partly for the food and partly for the chance to ask the person behind the table a question. That second part is the whole advantage a market stand has over a grocery case, and it's worth building your setup around it rather than treating the table as a smaller, worse version of a retail shelf.

## What actually sells at a table, versus a case

A cheese case sells on label and price point. A market table sells on conversation, and that changes what's worth bringing. A single distinctive wheel you can talk about for two minutes — how it's aged, what's different about this batch, why the rind looks the way it does — tends to outperform a wide, undifferentiated spread of everything you make. Bring fewer styles and be ready to talk about each one; a shopper who gets an actual answer to "what makes this one different" is a shopper who remembers your stand next week.

Samples matter more here than almost anywhere else you sell. If your state and local health rules allow it, a small, well-labeled sample plate turns a browsing shopper into a paying one more reliably than a sign does. Check your local rules before opening weekend rather than after a market manager flags something — requirements vary by state and sometimes by market, and it's the kind of detail that's much cheaper to sort out in April than to explain in June.

## The parts of the setup people forget

A cooler that holds temperature in a parking lot at noon is not the same as a cooler that holds temperature in your walk-in. If you haven't tested your transport and display setup under an actual hot afternoon, do it once before opening weekend rather than the first time it matters. Bring a backup card reader or a written log for cash sales if your primary payment method drops a connection — outdoor markets are hard on signal. And price your product for the table, not just for wholesale; a lot of first-time market sellers underprice because they're anchored to a distributor rate that assumed volume they won't move at a single stand.
