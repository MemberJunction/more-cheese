Consumer search interest in "cheese board" peaks every December, and this year's numbers are tracking the same seasonal spike we see every holiday stretch. What's different this year is the size of the category it's landing on: specialty cheese sales have kept climbing through 2025 even as buyers pull back on other grocery spending, which tells us this December's board-building customers aren't just casual — they're willing to spend for the right combination.

That's the gap a well-trained counter closes. A customer standing in front of a case with a vague plan for "a board for six people" is either going to leave with three cheeses picked at random, or leave with a board built around contrast — something firm, something soft, something with a little funk to it — because someone behind the counter asked two questions and actually listened to the answers.

## What the course actually covers

Our Counter Culture: Retailing course is built for exactly this moment, and December is when we see the biggest jump in enrollment. It's not a tasting class. It's built around the mechanics of a good sales conversation: how to read what a customer is actually asking for when they say "something not too strong," how to build a board around a budget without making the customer feel like you're upselling them, and how to talk about pairing without sounding like you're reciting a script. A counter clerk who can say "this one's going to be the loudest thing on your board, so give it its own corner" sells more cheese than one who can only say "it's good."

The course also covers the less glamorous side of a holiday rush: how to keep a case looking intentional after the fortieth customer has picked through it, how to rewrap without losing the presentation, and how to hand off a shift cleanly when you're three deep in customers and someone new is clocking in. None of that shows up in a tasting note, but it's the difference between a counter that holds up through the last two weeks of December and one that's running on fumes by the 20th.

If you're staffing up for the season with people who haven't worked a cheese counter before, this is the fastest way we know to get them from "can ring up a sale" to "can build a board a customer will brag about." Sessions run short enough to fit around holiday scheduling, and the certificate stays on a member's record for future seasons — useful if you bring back the same seasonal staff next year.

## What experienced staff still get wrong

Even veteran counter staff fall into one habit worth breaking this time of year: steering every undecided customer toward the same three crowd-pleasers. It's an understandable reflex when the line is out the door, but it also means the case's slower-moving, more interesting cheeses just sit there through the busiest weeks of the year. Part of what the course does is give staff a faster way to size up a customer's actual appetite for something less familiar, so the recommendation isn't always the safest one in the case.

New hires also tend to undersell what they know. Someone three days into the job usually has more useful information than they think — they've tasted everything on the case that week, even if they haven't sold it yet — and a little coaching on how to talk about a cheese they tried yesterday goes further than a script ever will.

Whatever your case looks like this week, the next few weeks are when most of your holiday cheese moves. A counter that can talk a customer into the right board, not just any board, is worth more than an extra case of inventory sitting in the back.
