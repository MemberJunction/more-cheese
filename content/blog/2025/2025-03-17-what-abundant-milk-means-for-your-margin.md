The national milk supply has been running ahead of last year for weeks now, and that's the story behind a number a few members have asked us about: why Class III futures are sitting flat even though cheese and powder exports are strong. More cows, more milk per cow, and a domestic market that's absorbing it just fine — which is good news if you're buying, and a flatter curve than you'd like if you're the one filling the vat.

For a mid-sized creamery, "flat" doesn't feel neutral. It feels like a squeeze, because feed costs have eased from where they were a year ago but other inputs — propane for the cave, packaging, labor — haven't followed. A few members on last month's advocacy call described it the same way: input costs go up on their own schedule and come down on their own schedule too, and this spring those two schedules aren't lining up.

"We keep waiting for the month where everything eases at once," one farmstead member told us on that call. "It's never everything. It's feed one month, propane the next, and you never get to bank the relief before the next thing shows up." That's the pattern behind the flat headline number: the market looks calm from a distance and busy up close.

## Where the pressure actually shows up

It rarely shows up as one bad number. It shows up as a slower resupply of cultures, a delayed equipment repair, a shift that runs one person short because the second hire didn't get made. If your income-over-feed-cost math has been tighter than usual this winter, you are reading the same market everyone else is — you're just standing closer to it.

A few things worth doing this month if margin has been the recurring topic on your end:

- **Re-run your cost-per-wheel, not just your cost-per-hundredweight.** Milk pricing swings get reported in bulk terms; your actual exposure is in finished units, and the gap between the two can hide a problem for months.
- **Check whether your wholesale contracts have a pricing-adjustment clause and when it triggers.** If you locked in a price last fall, this is the week to know exactly when you can revisit it.
- **Talk to your distributor about order timing.** A couple of members told us shifting a delivery by even a week changed which price they were filling against.

## What we're watching

The federal milk production report due out this week is the number that will move the rest of the spring's conversation — if the growth in supply keeps outpacing demand, expect the flat pattern to hold into summer. We'll keep the market notes on the members' page updated as that comes in, and the Membership & Outreach Committee is compiling a short list of members willing to talk through margin strategy one-on-one for anyone who'd rather not do this math alone.

If you're a smaller producer feeling this more than the headline numbers suggest you should, you're not imagining it — smaller operations carry fixed costs across fewer units, and that arithmetic gets less forgiving exactly when prices go flat. Reach out to the committee if you want a second set of eyes on your numbers before you make a call on staffing or equipment this spring.

None of this is a prediction about where your specific milk check lands next month — regional basis, your contract terms, and your own herd or supply arrangement all move that number in ways a national report can't capture. What the report does tell you is the direction the whole industry is leaning, and this spring it's leaning toward volume over price. Plan your cash flow with that lean in mind rather than waiting for a rebound that the supply numbers aren't currently pointing toward.

A production surge like this one usually resolves one of two ways: either demand keeps climbing to meet it, which is what the export numbers suggest could happen, or supply growth slows on its own once thinner margins discourage expansion. Either path takes a few months to show up in the price you're actually paid. In the meantime, the most useful thing most members can do is exactly what our margin worksheet walks through — separate the costs you can move this quarter from the ones you're locked into, and spend your attention on the first group. If you haven't pulled that worksheet from the members' page yet, it's worth ten minutes before your next order goes out.
