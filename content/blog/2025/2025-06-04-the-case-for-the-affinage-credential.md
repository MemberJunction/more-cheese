We've had more questions about the Advanced Affinage Certificate in the last few weeks than in most full quarters. Buyers keep telling us that continental, washed-curd styles are gaining shelf space, and a fair number of members are looking at a cave that's been doing one job for years and wondering if it should start doing a second. This year's round-up of award-winning cheeses pointed the same direction in its own way — judges kept rewarding bold, boundary-pushing wheels over familiar standbys, and a good few of them were aged, rind-washed styles that ask more of the cave than a fresh curd does.

That's the right instinct, and also the right moment to be precise about what the credential does and doesn't verify.

## What the certificate actually tests

The Advanced Affinage Certificate is built around judgment under changing conditions, not a single recipe for a single style. Candidates are assessed on reading a wheel's surface and interior at different stages, adjusting humidity and turning schedules in response to what they find rather than a fixed calendar, and diagnosing early problems — a rind going slick, a paste developing unevenly — before they become a lost batch. It assumes you already have foundational make knowledge; it isn't a substitute for the Cheese Foundations Certificate, and the exam will assume that baseline rather than reteach it.

It does not certify a specific style. Passing it doesn't mean you're qualified to make a particular Alpine or washed-rind cheese you've never worked with — it means you can apply sound affinage judgment to a cave and a wheel you understand. Members preparing for the exam often work through Maturation Planning for Small Rooms first, since it gives structured practice with the same judgment calls the certification tests, at a lower cost of a mistake than a live wheel. If your interest is really in a specific style transition rather than affinage generally, start with the course, see how it goes on a small batch, and decide from there whether the certificate is worth pursuing this cycle.

## What it doesn't promise

None of this is a promise about what will sell. Consumer taste toward aged and washed-curd styles is a real trend, but shelf demand moves faster than most caves can retool, and a credential is a floor under your judgment, not a forecast of demand. If you're weighing whether to commit cave space and cash flow to a new style this year, that's a conversation for your own numbers first — the Advanced Affinage Certificate can tell you that you're ready to execute it well. It can't tell you whether your market wants it yet.

Recertification runs on the same three-year cycle as our other credentials. If you earned yours in an earlier cohort and aren't sure where you land on that clock, your member portal has the date — worth checking now rather than discovering a lapse when a buyer or competition asks for proof.

If you're on the fence, the Education Committee holds office hours most months specifically for members thinking through a credential decision like this one. Reach out before you register — it's a shorter conversation than reworking a study plan halfway through.
