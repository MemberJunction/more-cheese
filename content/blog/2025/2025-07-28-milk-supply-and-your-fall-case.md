The federal milk price reports that came out this month tell a story a lot of our members are already living: the national herd is near a three-decade high, milk per cow keeps climbing, and there is simply more milk moving through the system than there was a year ago. Class I base pricing ticked up for July, but the broader signal, abundant supply and elevated stocks of cheese and butter sitting in cold storage, is the one that matters more for how your wholesale conversations go this fall.

For members selling into a saturated commodity market, that abundance is a headwind. For members selling specialty and artisan wheels on their own terms, it is closer to neutral, sometimes even a small tailwind, because your buyer's alternative got cheaper and your differentiated product still has to be differentiated on taste and story, not on the block market's mood that week.

That split shows up in the trade press too: recent reporting on specialty cheese trends points to continued growth in specialty and artisan varieties even as plainer, commodity-style American cheese slumps at the case. If your wheels sit on the specialty side of that line, this is a summer where the aggregate numbers are quietly working in your favor, even if it doesn't feel that way from inside a tight margin.

## Reading the export numbers correctly

The other half of this month's data is trade, not domestic supply. Milk-fat-equivalent exports hit a new monthly record in the most recent report, driven by strong movement in cheese, butter, dry whey, and lactose. Skim milk products moved the other direction. If your creamery sells any volume through a distributor with export relationships, ask them plainly which side of that split you're on. A distributor whose book is heavy in the categories that are growing is a different conversation partner right now than one whose book is heavy in the categories that are shrinking.

None of this is a reason to change your pricing overnight. It is a reason to look at your fall order sheet with the actual numbers in front of you instead of a general sense that "milk is up" or "milk is down." The federation doesn't set or publish dues or program pricing recommendations, but our Membership & Outreach Committee can point you toward the public trade and export reports members use most, and our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course walks through building your own cost model against a moving milk price rather than reacting to headlines.

## What we're hearing from the smaller creameries

Several farmstead members have told us this summer that the abundant-milk story reads very differently at their scale than it does in a national aggregate. A herd of forty cows doesn't benefit from a record national milk-fat export number the way a large processor does; what it feels is a feed bill that hasn't come down even as the milk check has moved around. That gap, strong aggregate numbers next to real cash-flow pressure on the ground, is exactly why the federation has kept its farmstead and creamery-closures resources active this year. If you're a small producer feeling squeezed between input costs and a soft commodity floor, reach out to the Membership & Outreach Committee; we can connect you to a member who has been through the same stretch.

The short version for your Monday planning meeting: supply is up, exports are strong in some categories and weak in others, specialty cheese is still outgrowing plain commodity product at the case, and none of that should be the whole basis for your fall pricing on its own. Pull the actual report, not the headline, before you write anything down.
