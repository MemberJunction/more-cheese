If you've been bracing for another rough line in the ledger, this month's numbers deserve a second look. National milk production came in more than three percent above last year, and that extra supply has been pushing farm-gate prices down through the summer. On its own, that's the kind of headline that's been landing hard on small producers all year. But it isn't the whole story.

Feed costs have been falling at the same time, and by more than a little. Corn, soybean meal, and premium alfalfa have all eased back from where they sat earlier in the year, and the result is a margin between the milk check and the feed bill that's meaningfully wider than it's been in months. For members who've spent 2025 watching every input line item, that's not a headline — it's a number worth sitting with for a minute.

## What this does and doesn't fix

None of this undoes the year our smaller and mid-sized farmstead members have had. A wider margin in August doesn't refill a barn that had to cut its herd in the spring, and it doesn't reopen a cave that went dark in March. If your creamery has been running on thinner staffing or a shorter cheese lineup, that adjustment doesn't reverse itself just because feed got cheaper.

What it does mean is that the calculation for the next few months looks a little different than it did in June. If you've been putting off a decision — whether to hold a price, whether to bring a seasonal hire back, whether this is the month to finally get the humidity sensor replaced — this is a reasonable window to redo that math with fresher numbers. The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course walks through exactly this kind of recalculation, and it runs a new cohort most quarters if you want a structured way to work through it rather than doing it alone at the kitchen table.

## Watching the other side of the case

Retail demand for specialty and artisan cheese has stayed strong this year even as some of the mass-market cheese categories have softened, and that's been especially true for goat cheese and for the Hispanic and Italian cheese categories at the deli counter. If your creamery sells direct or through a specialty retailer, that's worth knowing going into fall ordering conversations — the case is shifting, not shrinking.

The wholesale side gave a similarly mixed signal early this month. The most recent global commodity auction showed prices ticking up again after a rough stretch earlier in the year, even as cheddar and mozzarella eased slightly within that same auction. That's the kind of split reading that makes a single headline number hard to trust — cheese, butter, and whey aren't moving together right now, and a producer whose mix leans heavily toward one of those categories is going to feel this year differently than a neighbor whose mix leans toward another.

It's also worth noting, without treating it as either good or bad news for our members, that we're continuing to see consolidation among larger processors this year — another regional cheesemaker changing hands this month, the way several have over the past year. For a small or mid-sized creamery, that trend mostly shows up indirectly: a processor you sell milk to changes ownership, or a distributor you rely on gets absorbed into a larger one with different minimums. If a deal like that touches your supply chain, it's worth a call to your regional rep sooner rather than later, so we can flag it if other members are navigating the same transition.

We'll keep tracking the margin numbers through the fall and flag it here if the trend reverses. In the meantime, if your books tell a different story than the national averages — better or worse — the Membership & Outreach Committee wants to hear about it. Regional conditions vary more than a single number can show, and what we hear from members is what shapes the advocacy priorities we bring to the next comment period.
