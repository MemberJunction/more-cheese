This week's spotlight member manages the cheese counter at a specialty shop she asked us not to name directly, though longtime readers will recognize the type: a single storefront, a case that turns over fast, and a weekend coming up that she says outsells every other week on the calendar combined. Grilling staples and deli prepared foods are consistently among the categories that see the biggest sales lift in the week leading into this holiday, and she says the cheese case rides that same wave whether or not shoppers think of cheese as a grilling item. We won't use her real name either, at her request, but her case-planning routine for this holiday is worth sharing, because it's a version of a problem a lot of our retail members are solving right now.

Her planning starts almost three weeks out, not the Monday before. She orders extra volume on the styles that move fastest in warm weather — fresh mozzarella, a soft-ripened brie-style, a couple of accessible cheddars that don't need explaining — and she deliberately under-orders the harder sells, the washed-rinds and the aged alpine styles that need a conversation to move. "Nobody wants a conversation on the third of July," she told us. "They want to grab, they want it to look good on a board, and they want to be out the door in five minutes." That's not a complaint about her regular customers, she was quick to add — it's just a different customer, in a hurry, for one specific weekend.

## Staffing the case, not just stocking it

The harder problem, she says, isn't the cheese order — it's staffing the counter itself. A quiet Tuesday can run with one experienced person. This weekend needs at least two people who both know the case well enough to answer a rushed question without slowing the line, plus someone dedicated just to restocking and wiping down the tasting station between waves of customers. She's learned the hard way that an inexperienced hire at the counter on a weekend like this does more harm than good — a slow, uncertain answer to "what pairs with this" sends a shopper to the next case, not back to yours.

Sampling is the other piece she's rebuilt over a few holiday seasons. Early on she offered tastes of everything in the case; now she picks three cheeses to sample, always including one easy crowd-pleaser and one slightly more interesting cheese that she's hoping to convert a curious customer into a repeat buyer on. "The sample is doing two jobs," she said. "One is making the sale today. The other is making the sale in September, when they come back asking for the thing they tried on the Fourth."

## What she'd tell a member doing this for the first time

Her advice for members running a counter through their first big holiday weekend is to plan the labor before the cheese order, not after. "I used to build my order first and figure out staffing around it. Now I know how many hands I actually have, and I order the case to match what those hands can move well, not what would look impressive sitting there." She also keeps a short written debrief after each holiday weekend — what sold out too early, what sat, what a customer asked for that she didn't have — so next year's plan starts from data instead of memory. It's a small habit, and by her account it's the single thing that's made each year's weekend run smoother than the one before.
