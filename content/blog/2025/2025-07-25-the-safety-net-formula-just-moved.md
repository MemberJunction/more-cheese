Members who've been through the margin coverage sign-up process before know it's usually a January-into-March conversation, not a July one. This year is a little different. A federal budget measure enacted earlier this month adjusts the underlying formula for the national dairy safety-net program, and while the changes won't hit most operations' paperwork until the next enrollment window, the fine print is worth understanding now rather than scrambling for it later.

The short version: the program that pays producers when the gap between the national milk price and average feed costs falls below a chosen threshold is getting more generous coverage tiers and, for many smaller operations, a lower cost to enroll at the levels that matter most. That's a meaningful shift for exactly the kind of small farmstead creamery that's been squeezed hardest this year by feed and energy costs running ahead of milk prices.

## Why the timing matters this particular year

We wouldn't normally spend a July post on a program most members interact with in the spring, but this is not a normal year for margins. Milk prices have been easing after a run of stronger months, feed costs haven't given much ground, and energy costs are up for anyone running mechanical cooling through a hot summer. A safety-net program with a better formula is only useful if members actually understand it well enough to enroll at the right coverage level when the window opens — and historically, the members who benefit least from these programs are the ones who signed up on autopilot at whatever tier they picked the first year, without revisiting it as their operation or the market changed.

The Membership & Outreach Committee has flagged this as a topic they want in front of members before the next enrollment cycle, not during it. Their concern, which we share, is that a genuinely improved program does no good if smaller operations don't have the bandwidth to reevaluate their coverage choice against the new numbers.

We've submitted comments in the past on dairy pricing reform more broadly — the topic covers everything from the margin program's formula to how regional price differentials get calculated — and we'll continue to push for two things specifically: clearer plain-language guidance issued well ahead of enrollment, not buried in a technical bulletin, and a coverage calculator that lets a two-person operation model their own numbers in ten minutes instead of an afternoon.

If you've ever sat down with your accountant to work out what coverage level actually made sense for your farm and found the process harder than it needed to be, that experience is exactly the kind of specific, concrete detail that makes a difference when we bring member input into a comment period. Written accounts from members who've actually used the program carry more weight than anything we can write on their behalf.

## What to do now

You don't need to act on this today — the next formal enrollment window isn't open yet, and we'll flag it clearly when it is. But if you want a head start on understanding what changed, the advocacy page on the member portal has a plain-language summary of the formula adjustment, and the Membership & Outreach Committee is collecting member questions now so they can build the guidance members actually need before the window opens. If dairy pricing reform is a topic you've weighed in on before, or want to for the first time, that's the place to start.
