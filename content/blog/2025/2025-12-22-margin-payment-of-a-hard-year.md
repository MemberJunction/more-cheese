The federal margin coverage program for dairy producers finally paid out this month. For farmstead members enrolled at the top coverage tier, that meant a modest check tied to a margin that slipped to its lowest point of the year — the first, and so far only, trigger the program has produced in 2025. It arrived in the same month several members told us they were doing the closing math on a year they'd rather not repeat.

That's worth sitting with, because a single payment at year's end isn't the same story as a program working the way members hoped when they signed up back in January. Feed costs held stubborn through most of the year while milk prices drifted lower, and the margin between the two only crossed into payment territory once, right at the finish line. If you enrolled at a lower coverage tier, you likely saw nothing at all — the program is built so that top-tier coverage pays first and most often, which is exactly why so many members treat that decision as the whole ballgame each January.

For most of 2025, the margin sat comfortably above the payment threshold, which is normally good news — it means milk prices and feed costs were roughly in balance. But "roughly in balance" isn't the same as "profitable," especially for a mid-sized farmstead operation carrying higher per-hundredweight costs than a large commercial dairy. A member who called our Membership & Outreach Committee back in October put it plainly: the margin program only tells you when things get bad enough to trigger a check. It doesn't tell you when things are merely tight enough to hurt.

## What changes for 2026

The bigger news for producers isn't December's small check — it's what changed in the program for the years ahead. Coverage has been extended through 2031, and two changes matter for members reading this over the holiday. The production limit at the highest coverage tier moved up by a million pounds, to six million pounds of milk, which is a meaningful bump for a mid-sized operation that was bumping against the old ceiling. And multi-year, "lock-in" contracts are now available through the end of 2031, meaning a member who wants to set coverage once and not revisit the paperwork every winter finally has that option.

Locking in for multiple years isn't free of risk, though. A contract that looks smart at today's feed and milk prices can look expensive if either one moves sharply in your favor next year, and breaking a multi-year commitment isn't as simple as skipping a renewal. Neither change fixes the underlying problem: forecasts for 2026 point to milk prices falling further, not recovering, even as production keeps climbing on larger herds and stronger per-cow output nationally. A margin program is insurance, not a business plan. But insurance that finally paid out when a hard year called for it, with fewer enrollment headaches ahead, is still worth having.

## What to do before the county office reopens

Sign-up windows for the coming coverage year typically open through your local Farm Service Agency office, and the paperwork goes faster when you've already pulled last year's production numbers. If your creamery's feed and milk figures moved a lot in 2025 — more cows, a ration change, a supplier switch — don't just re-enter last year's figures and call it done. Run the six-million-pound ceiling against your actual production before you pick a tier, and weigh the multi-year lock-in against how much your operation is likely to change between now and 2031.

Our Membership & Outreach Committee keeps a running list of the federal support programs that touch specialty and farmstead producers, alongside plain-language notes on what changed each year. If you're weighing whether to lock in a multi-year contract or keep re-enrolling annually, that's a reasonable place to start before you call your accountant.
