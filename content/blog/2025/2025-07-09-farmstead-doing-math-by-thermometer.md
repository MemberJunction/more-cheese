We don't usually lead a spotlight with a spreadsheet, but the member we spoke with this week did, so we're following her lead. She runs a fifty-cow farmstead operation in the upper Midwest, ages her own wheels, and has been a federation member for six years. She asked us not to use her name — not because there's anything unusual about her situation, she said, but because "half the members reading this are living the same week I am, and I don't need to be the face of it." We're honoring that, and we suspect she's right about the other half.

Her June, in her words: three days over ninety-five degrees, back to back, in the middle of the month. Her herd is small enough that she knows individual cows by udder shape, not ear tag, and she watched three of her steadiest producers go quiet at the water trough instead of the feed bunk for most of an afternoon. "You can see it before you measure it," she told us. "They just stand differently." By the time she pulled her monthly totals, she was down enough pounds shipped that she started doing math she hadn't done since a bad winter three years ago — not "can I make the mortgage," but "which cheese do I not make this month."

## The choice that isn't really a choice

She didn't stop making cheese. She made less of the thing that takes the most milk per wheel and more of something faster to turn over, which meant less of her flagship aged wheel going into the cave this quarter and more of a younger, quicker-selling cheese going out the door instead. It's a trade she's made before, in other hard months, and she's candid that it isn't free — the aged wheel is where most of her margin lives, and skipping a batch now means a gap in her cave inventory eight months from now, when she'd normally be selling last winter's work. "You're always trading a problem today for a smaller one later," she said. "The trick is making sure it actually is smaller."

What she wants other members to know isn't a fix, because she doesn't have one. It's more of a reframe. She spent the first few days of the heat wave treating the yield drop as something she'd done wrong — a management failure she should have prevented. A conversation with another member at a regional workshop last year is what talked her out of that. "She told me the cows don't care how good your barn is when it's that hot for that long. That helped more than any fan I've bought." She's since added a second water trough and moved her afternoon feeding half an hour later, both small, both helpful, neither one a complete answer.

## What she's asking of the rest of us

Her one concrete ask, which we're passing along: she'd like to see more members talking openly about a rough month instead of only posting the good ones. "Everyone's case photos look great. Nobody's posting the week they skipped a batch." If that's you this month, the Membership & Outreach Committee has been hearing similar stories all summer and is a reasonable place to start that conversation — not for a solution, necessarily, but for the fact that you're not doing anything unusual by having a hard July.
