The ICF Annual Conference 2025 opens in Sacramento on July 15, which puts us at roughly the two-month mark this week. That's early enough that nothing about your trip feels urgent yet, and that's exactly the problem — the members who scramble in late June are usually the ones who told themselves in May that there was plenty of time. Registration and travel planning tend to follow a predictable pattern across trade associations: the deadlines set too far out get ignored, and the ones set too close get missed, which is roughly why we're sending this reminder now rather than in April or in late June.

A few things worth doing now rather than later. If your creamery is sending more than one person, block the dates on the shop calendar before you settle who's covering the cave and the counter while you're gone; that's a harder conversation in week six than it is in week one. If you're presenting, entering the competition, or sitting for any of the on-site credential exams, those all have their own registration windows that close well before the conference itself, so check them separately rather than assuming the general pass covers it.

## What's on the agenda, and what the heat means for your van

This year's program leans into a theme that's been running through the blog since January: doing more with a tighter operation. Sessions on the current docket include a maturation-planning track for small aging rooms, a pricing workshop built around this year's input costs rather than last year's, and a full day on export documentation for members who are newly shipping or thinking about it. The Awards & Competition Committee has also confirmed judging slots will run in parallel with sessions this year, so if you're a Competition Judge Accreditation holder hoping to sit on a panel, let the committee know your availability before the July cutoff rather than after. For members who can't travel, we're told a handful of sessions will stream, though the full schedule for that hasn't posted yet; we'll flag it here as soon as it does.

It's also worth saying plainly: Sacramento runs hot in mid-July, and if you're driving a van full of wheels for a competition entry or a vendor table, that's a logistics question, not just a comfort one. Perishable cargo held above 40 degrees for more than a couple of hours starts moving into unsafe territory fast in that kind of heat, so a cooler with real ice capacity for the drive and the parking lot matters more than one built just for the exhibit floor. "I used to pack for the exhibit hall and forget about the two hours getting there," one member who's shown wheels at three ICF conferences told us. "Now I pack for the drive first and the booth second."

## What to do this week

If you haven't registered, do it now while the standard rate still applies; the late-registration bump tends to land in June. If you're bringing a competition entry, check the Awards & Competition Committee's category list against what you're actually planning to submit — categories fill, and a late switch is harder than an early one. And if this is your first ICF conference, the Membership & Outreach Committee runs an informal first-timer meetup on the opening evening; ask at check-in and someone will point you to it.
