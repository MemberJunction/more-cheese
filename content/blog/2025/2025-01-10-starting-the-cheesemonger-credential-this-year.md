Every January, Certified Cheesemonger enrollment ticks up, and it's not hard to see why. Counter staff who spent November and December running a holiday case finally have a slower week to think about where their career goes next, and this is the credential built specifically for that side of the business — not the make room, not the lab, the case.

The program covers cutting and wrapping technique, proper storage and rotation so nothing on your shelf turns before its time, and the harder-to-teach skill of building a balanced case across styles, milk types, and price points. It also spends real time on customer guidance: how to ask a few questions and land on a recommendation someone will actually enjoy, rather than defaulting to whatever's easiest to slice. It's valid for four years, longer than the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate's two-year cycle and shorter than the Certified Cheese Professional's flagship track, which makes sense given what it's asking you to hold onto.

## Who it's actually for

We get asked whether Certified Cheesemonger is a stepping stone to the CCP. It isn't, and we don't want to oversell it as one. The CCP is a make-through-business credential aimed at people running production; Certified Cheesemonger is a retail credential aimed at people running a case. Plenty of members hold both because their job touches both sides, but you don't need one to start the other, and a counter clerk who has no interest in ever running a vat loses nothing by skipping the CCP entirely.

Current retail trend reporting backs up why the case-building piece matters more than it used to. Heading into this year, several forecasts pointed to continued growth for specialty and artisanal styles against mass-market American cheese, alongside rising interest in heritage-style and internationally inspired varieties. That's exactly the kind of shift a good monger needs to read in their own case rather than guess at — knowing when to make room for something newer on the shelf, and when to hold your ground on what already sells. A separate federal outlook released this week also nudged expected cheese and dairy product prices upward for the year, which matters to a counter's margins even when the case itself looks unchanged.

None of that changes what the credential actually tests, and we'd rather be clear about that than let the exam feel bigger than it is. It's a practical, hands-on evaluation of cutting technique, storage judgment, and the conversation you have with a customer standing at the case — not a written test of market trends. But understanding why your case looks different than it did five years ago is part of doing the job well, and the program leaves room for that context alongside the fundamentals.

If you're deciding whether to enroll this year, don't wait for a slower month that isn't coming. The exam windows run on a fixed federation calendar, not on your shop's schedule, and the next one is closer than January makes it feel. Ask your Membership & Outreach contact for the current syllabus and exam dates, and if you've got a counter clerk who's ready for more responsibility, this is the credential that tells you — and them — whether they are.
