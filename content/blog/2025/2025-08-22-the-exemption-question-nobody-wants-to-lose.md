Earlier this month, the federal food-safety regulator formally proposed pushing back the compliance date on its expanded food-traceability recordkeeping rule by thirty months, opening a comment period that closes in early September. Cheese is one of the foods explicitly covered once the rule takes effect, so this is worth a few minutes of attention even though "compliance date extension" sounds like the kind of notice most members would scroll past.

The rule itself asks covered facilities to keep detailed lot-level records — harvest, cooling, first receiving, shipping, and a handful of other tracking events — for foods on a federal high-risk list, cheese among them, so that a contamination trace-back can move in hours instead of days. Most of our largest members were already gearing up to comply before the extension. What matters more to the rest of our membership is the exemption built into the rule for small producers whose direct-to-consumer sales fall under a set dollar threshold, and for facilities already covered by other traceability requirements that overlap with this one. That exemption is the part we're watching closely, because a rule this detailed only works for small creameries if the exemption language keeps pace with how a real farmstead operation actually sells its product.

## Why the extension matters for the exemption, not just the deadline

An extended timeline is good news on its own, but it also opens a second comment window on a rule that a lot of small producers assumed was settled. That's useful, because the exemption thresholds were written with a particular sales pattern in mind — largely direct farm-to-consumer sales — and don't always describe cleanly how a member selling through a mix of a farm stand, a regional distributor, and a handful of wholesale accounts actually operates. A creamery that qualifies for the exemption through direct sales alone can lose that exemption the moment a meaningful share of the same product moves through a covered distributor, even if the total volume hasn't changed much year over year.

We don't think that's an outcome anyone intended, but intent doesn't matter much once a facility is out of compliance because a sales channel it added for reasonable business reasons happened to cross an exemption boundary it didn't know was there. The additional runway from this extension gives us a real chance to make that case with specifics before the rule locks in for good, rather than after a member discovers the gap during an inspection.

## What we're asking members for

If your creamery sells through more than one channel — direct, wholesale, distributor, farmers' market, any combination — and you're not certain how that mix reads against a federal exemption threshold, that uncertainty itself is useful information for the comment we're preparing. A short account of your current channel mix, how it's shifted over the past couple of years, and whether you've had to change any recordkeeping as a result gives our comment real substance instead of a general objection. We compile these anonymized and pattern-matched, not attributed to any one creamery, unless a member specifically wants their story told by name.

The comment window on this extension closes in early September, which doesn't leave much runway. If you want your situation counted in what the federation submits, reach out to the Membership & Outreach Committee through the member portal this week rather than next.
