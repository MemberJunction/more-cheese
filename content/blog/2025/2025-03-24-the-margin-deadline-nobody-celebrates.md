Enrollment for the federal government's annual dairy margin-protection program closes at the end of this month, and every year we watch a version of the same pattern: the members who signed up early are checking a box and moving on with their week, and the members who haven't decided yet are asking us the same question. Is it worth it this year?

We don't sell insurance and we're not going to pretend we know your operation better than you do. But we can tell you what's different about 2025, because it's the thing most of the calls to our member line have been about since January.

## What's actually moving

The program pays out when the gap between the national milk price and the average cost of feed falls below a threshold you choose when you enroll. That gap is called the margin, and it's the number that decides whether a payment lands in your account at all. This year, federal forecasters have been revising their milk price estimates downward month over month, while feed costs — grain, hay, the input side of the ledger — have stayed stubbornly high in most regions. That's a narrower margin than a lot of producers budgeted for in January, and it's exactly the scenario the coverage exists for.

None of that is news if you've been in the trade through the last two years of feed and energy inflation. What's worth saying plainly is that the tighter margin outlook this spring is showing up in real decisions at the farmstead level — a member told one of our regional volunteers last week that she was deciding between paying for coverage and paying for a cave dehumidifier repair, and that those two line items were genuinely competing for the same few hundred dollars. That's not a complaint, just an accurate description of where a lot of small operations are right now.

## What we can and can't tell you

We are not in a position to tell you what coverage level to pick, and we'd be doing you a disservice if we tried — that decision depends on your herd size, your existing risk exposure, and probably a conversation with whoever does your books. What we can tell you is where the deadline sits on the calendar and where to go to actually enroll: your regional farm service office handles the paperwork, not the federation, so if you've been meaning to call and haven't, this is the week to do it before the window closes.

We can also tell you what we're hearing from the Membership & Outreach Committee, which fields more of these calls than any other part of the federation. Producers who've been burned by a margin year before tend to enroll almost automatically now, treating it the way you'd treat any other fixed cost of doing business. Producers who've never triggered a payment sometimes talk themselves out of it, reasoning that the premium is money they'll never see again. Both instincts make sense. Neither one is really about the insurance — they're about how much uncertainty a given operation can absorb before it changes what gets fixed and what gets deferred.

## The unglamorous parts pay off

If there's a theme running through this era, it's that the boring administrative tasks — the margin coverage enrollment, the renewal reminder, the recordkeeping you do because a regulator might ask for it someday — tend to matter most in exactly the years when nobody has the bandwidth to think about them. We're not going to dress that up as good news. It's just the shape of the year we're in.

If you want a plainer walk-through of how the coverage levels and the payment formula fit together before you make a call, our Education Committee keeps a short explainer on the member portal that isn't trying to sell you anything either way. And if you've already enrolled and want to compare notes with other members who make what you make, the regional chapter calls are open to anyone — details are on the events calendar.
