After six weeks of telling members to expect delays, we're finally able to tell some of you the opposite. The federal funding lapse that started this stretch stalled everything from comment-period clocks to export paperwork, and new appropriations legislation now funds the affected agencies through next September. That doesn't undo the backlog. It does mean the backlog has a direction again, which is more than we could say for most of October.

Members asked us two things all fall: what's actually stuck, and what happens when it isn't. Here's where things stand as of this week, and what we're asking members to actually do about it.

## The comment periods are reopening on staggered clocks

We told you in late October that at least one open rulemaking docket — the interstate cheese shipment proposal our advocacy committee has been tracking for members like the one who reads every bill text before anyone else does — had its comment window paused mid-lapse. Paused isn't cancelled. Agencies are now publishing revised deadlines individually, and they aren't all reopening on the same schedule. If you had a comment drafted and sitting in a folder, now is the moment to check the docket number against the agency's posted notice rather than assume your original date still applies. We'll flag any interstate shipment or raw-milk aging docket update in the members' advocacy digest as soon as we see it, but we'd rather you double-check than miss a window because we were slow to post it.

If you sat out the fall comment period entirely because the deadline felt like a moving target, this is the moment to reengage. A rule doesn't stop moving toward a decision just because the public comment window went quiet for six weeks — it just means the record has a gap where your input should have been.

## What's still trickling, not flooding

If your creamery ships internationally, you've probably felt this one directly. Weekly export sales data and some certification processing fell behind during the lapse, and agencies have said the catch-up will be gradual rather than a single dump — expect trickled releases over roughly the next several weeks rather than everything landing at once. That matters for planning: if you're waiting on a certificate tied to a shipment window, build in slack rather than assuming a single catch-up day clears the queue.

Our Education Committee has heard from a handful of members that their usual documentation turnaround has roughly doubled; we don't have a federation-wide number yet, but if you're seeing something similar, tell your regional rep so we can track the pattern. The same goes for anyone whose state agriculture office also runs on federal grant timing — a few members have told us their state-level inspections are running behind for reasons that trace back to the same lapse, one step removed.

Nothing dramatic is required of you here. Check your open dockets against posted reopening dates before you assume a deadline held. If you have export shipments pending certification, call your usual contact rather than waiting on an automated update — humans are working through a stack, and a polite check-in doesn't hurt. And if a comment period reopens on a rule you care about, don't wait until the new deadline's final week. The clock has already cost you once this fall.

We'll keep this thread updated as individual agencies post their revised schedules. If you're not already getting the advocacy digest and want it, your membership dashboard has the sign-up under communications preferences.
