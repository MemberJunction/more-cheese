This week's Cheese Photography webinar is a smaller, quieter event than most of what we preview here — no travel, no badge, just an hour on your calendar — but it's one of the more practically useful sessions on our calendar this year, and it's free. If you've ever tried to photograph your own cheese for a listing, a market sign, or a social post and ended up with something that made a beautiful wheel look flat and gray, this session was built for exactly that problem.

The session's core argument, previewed for us ahead of time, is that most member photos fail for the same handful of fixable reasons: light coming from the wrong direction, a background that competes with the cheese instead of framing it, and a cut surface photographed before it's had a minute to breathe and show its actual paste color. Side light or light from behind tends to reveal texture and rind detail far better than light straight on from the front, which flattens everything into one plane. None of that requires equipment beyond a phone and a window. The session spends most of its hour on light and staging rather than gear, which is the right emphasis for a member base that mostly isn't going to buy a studio setup this season.

## Why this matters more than it sounds like

We know a webinar about photography can read as the least urgent item on this list next to pricing formulas and food-safety rules, so we'll say the case for it plainly: for a direct-to-consumer seller, the photo is often the entire pitch. A shopper scrolling a farmers' market page or an online listing decides whether to read further based on the image alone, well before they see your price or your story. Members who sell primarily through a counter or a wholesale account can treat this as optional. Members building a direct-to-consumer channel — and industry trend coverage this cycle keeps pointing to direct and online sales as one of the categories growing fastest for smaller dairy producers — largely can't. A specialty-cheese trade report this spring made a related point about in-person sampling: the tactics that move a wedge are cheap and human, not expensive. A good photo is the online equivalent of that same idea — it's not a production budget that sells the cheese, it's someone paying attention to the details a shopper actually notices.

There's also a cost angle worth naming. A member weighing where to put a thin marketing budget this year doesn't need to hire a photographer to fix this problem. A window, a plain board, and fifteen minutes of patience with where the light falls will get most members most of the way there. That's a genuinely low-cost fix in a year where several of you have told us every dollar is being watched twice before it's spent.

If you're already enrolled in Storytelling & Provenance in Marketing, this week's session pairs well with it — the course covers what to say about your cheese, and the webinar covers how to make someone stop scrolling long enough to read it. You don't need to have taken the course to get value from the webinar on its own, though; it's a standalone hour, not a prerequisite chain, and either can be taken without the other.

## The practical details

The session is virtual, free to all members, and runs about an hour with time for questions at the end. Bring a wedge or a wheel you're willing to photograph on camera if you want live feedback; the instructor has said they'll critique two or three member photos submitted in the chat if time allows. Registration is through your member portal under this week's events listing. If you can't make the live session, a recording goes up afterward, though the live critique portion is the part members tend to say was worth attending for in previous runs of this session.
