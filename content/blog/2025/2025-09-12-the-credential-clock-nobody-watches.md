Every credential in the federation's program has a different clock, and the one members forget about most is also the shortest. The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate is good for two years, less than half the validity window of the Certified Cheese Professional credential and shorter than the Certified Cheesemonger cycle too. It's the credential most likely to have quietly lapsed by the time someone checks their member profile in January.

That two-year window isn't an accident. Food-safety practice, testing methods and recall procedure change faster than affinage technique or counter skills, and federal guidance in this space has kept moving through 2025 as well. Recertifying more often keeps the credential meaning something real, rather than a badge earned once and never revisited. The trade-off is that it's the easiest one to lose track of, especially for a solo cheesemaker who earned it during a slower season and hasn't thought about it since.

## Why fall is the window, not winter

If your Food Safety & HACCP Certificate is due for renewal, now is the practical moment to schedule it. Recertification coursework runs on the same calendar as everything else the federation offers, and the fall slate is typically the last one before production ramps up for the holiday case and cave space gets tight. Members who wait until December tend to be scheduling recert study time around their busiest six weeks of the year, which is exactly backwards.

The same logic applies, just on a longer clock, to the Certified Cheese Professional credential and Certified Cheesemonger. Neither expires as often, but both quietly time out for members who assumed a credential earned once was earned for good. Check the expiration date on your member profile rather than trusting memory; several members have told us they were surprised to find a credential a full cycle past due, sometimes discovered only when a buyer or an inspector asked for current documentation.

## What recertifying actually involves

For the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate, recertification isn't a full retake of the original coursework. It's a shorter review focused on what's changed since your last cycle — updated hazard analysis expectations, current recall procedure, and any labeling requirements that shifted in the meantime, which after a year like this one is not nothing. For Certified Cheesemonger and Certified Cheese Professional, the Education Committee's guidance is similar: recertification checks currency, it doesn't re-test fundamentals you've already demonstrated.

Members sometimes ask whether a lapsed credential can simply be picked back up later at no cost beyond a late fee. It can't, in most cases; letting the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate lapse past its window typically means retaking the full course rather than the shorter review, since the whole point of the short cycle is to confirm current knowledge, not old knowledge with a new stamp on it. That's the detail that turns a missed renewal date into a much bigger time commitment than it needed to be.

If you're not sure where you stand, the fastest path is your member profile's credential tab, which shows the exact expiration date rather than a rough estimate. From there, the Education Committee can point you to the next available recertification session before the fall calendar fills in around the holiday rush.
