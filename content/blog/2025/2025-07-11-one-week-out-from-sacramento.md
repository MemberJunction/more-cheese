A week from today, badges start printing at the front table in Sacramento. If you registered back when we first opened it up in May, or even three weeks ago when we last wrote about how close this was getting, this is the week the to-do list stops being abstract. A few things worth checking before you're standing in a hotel lobby wishing you'd checked them earlier.

## Getting your entries in

If you're planning to compete, entry intake closes before the opening session, not at it — bringing a wheel to the front desk on the first morning is too late, no matter how good the wheel is. Judges need time to catalog and stage entries the day before tasting begins, and that day is coming up fast. If your entry needs to travel by refrigerated transit rather than in your own car, check with your carrier about arrival timing now rather than assuming a next-day delivery will land where it needs to. We've had entries miss a category by hours before, and it's a hard call to make at the table when it happens.

If you're judging rather than entering, your accreditation status should already be current — this isn't the week to discover a Competition Judge Accreditation lapsed quietly sometime over the winter. Check your member portal if you're not sure.

## The sessions worth building your day around

The full schedule has been posted for a while, but a few sessions are worth flagging if you haven't planned your days yet. Given the summer a lot of members are having, the cave and barn heat-management sessions are likely to run over their allotted time in the hallway conversation afterward, so budget for that rather than sprinting to the next room. If you came up through one of this year's cohorts in Cave Management or a related course, the instructor leading that track will be there in person and has said she's happy to talk specifics with anyone who corners her at a break.

For anyone doing due diligence on the state of the retail category before conversations with buyers on the trade show floor, an industry report released last month put fresh numbers behind something most of you already sense from your own case: specialty and artisan cheese keeps growing faster than the broader category, driven in part by exactly the kind of small-batch, aged, and regional work this federation's membership makes. That's useful context walking into a booth conversation, even if it isn't the kind of number this federation tracks or vouches for independently.

Sacramento in mid-July runs hot, often into the high nineties by afternoon, so if you're used to a cooler home climate, plan your walk between venues accordingly and don't leave anything temperature-sensitive in a parked car longer than you have to — that includes competition wheels waiting for drop-off and any samples you're carrying for a buyer meeting. The venue's loading dock has refrigerated holding available for entries and samples; ask at check-in rather than guessing where to put something.

If this is your first time at an in-person conference, the volunteer desk near registration is staffed by members, not contractors, and they are genuinely the fastest way to get an odd question answered — faster than the app, in our experience. Introduce yourself. Most of the people behind that table were first-timers themselves within the last few years, and they remember what it's like to not know where anything is.

We'll see many of you there next week. For members not attending, we'll have a recap the following week, and the Education Committee has said session recordings for the credential-track talks should be posted within a few weeks after.
