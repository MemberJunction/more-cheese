Most years, we spend the last week of December writing about holiday cases and closing inventory. This year we're spending it on paperwork, because the federal margin support program that a lot of our smaller members lean on has been rewritten for 2026, and the enrollment window opens in the second week of January. If you've been meaning to look at it, this is the week to actually read the fine print rather than skim it between deliveries.

The basics haven't changed: the program pays out when the gap between the national all-milk price and feed costs falls below a level you choose when you enroll. What has changed is the shape of the choice. Producers can now lock in a coverage tier for a full six-year stretch instead of re-electing every year, and that multi-year commitment comes with a meaningful discount on the premium. For an operation that's been treating the annual re-enrollment form as a January chore to put off, that trade is worth thinking through now, not in the second week of February when the deadline is bearing down.

## Why the timing matters this year

The forecasts we've been tracking point toward a softer 2026 than 2025 was. The federal milk order's Class I base price is set to open the new year noticeably lower than it closed 2025 at, and outlook work from federal agricultural economists suggests the all-milk price could fall by roughly a tenth from where it sat this year, even as national herd sizes and per-cow output keep climbing. None of that is unique to our members — it's the same math every dairy operation in the country is reading — but it does mean the margin between milk revenue and feed cost is the number worth protecting before the year gets going, not after a bad quarter forces the question.

A six-year lock-in isn't the right call for everyone. If your operation is mid-transition — a new owner, a herd size that's about to change, a move from wholesale to more direct sales — a shorter commitment or the no-cost catastrophic tier might suit you better this cycle. The point isn't that everyone should take the longer lock-in. It's that the choice is bigger than it used to be, and a bigger choice deserves more than the ten minutes most of us give the form.

## What we're asking members to check

We've heard from a handful of members that the updated tool for estimating which coverage level fits their operation is more useful than the version from a few years ago, but it still assumes you've got clean numbers on your feed costs and milk sold going in. If your bookkeeping runs behind, close out what you can from 2025 before the window opens rather than trying to estimate on the fly in January. We've also flagged, in comments to the program's regional offices, that the enrollment period still runs through the exact stretch when small creameries are catching up on holiday-season paperwork, and asked that future cycles consider opening earlier. We don't expect that to move for 2026, so plan around the calendar we've actually got.

If you enroll and something about your operation doesn't fit neatly into the standard tiers — split ownership, a herd that changed hands mid-year, milk sold under more than one label — reach out through the member portal before you file. We can't fill out the form for you, but we can tell you what questions the regional office is likely to ask, and that alone has saved members a second trip to the office in past years.
