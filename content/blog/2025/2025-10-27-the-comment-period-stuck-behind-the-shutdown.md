The federal government entered its fourth week of a funding lapse this week, and members keep asking us the same question in different words: what happens to the rules we're waiting on. The honest answer is that we don't fully know, and neither does the regulator whose comment period on interstate cheese shipment closed back in September without a follow-up notice.

That rule matters more than its dry title suggests. A small farmstead producer who wants to ship an aged wheel across a state line runs into a patchwork of thresholds that differ by product type and by which side of a border the buyer sits on. Several of our members have spent the better part of two years documenting exactly where that patchwork breaks down for them — a distributor who can carry a load three states over but not four, a retailer who has to special-order paperwork nobody else asks for. The comment period was supposed to be the moment that record got read.

## What the shutdown actually changes

A funding lapse doesn't cancel a rulemaking. It does slow the staff work behind it — the legal review, the economic analysis, the interagency sign-off that has to happen before a comment period's record becomes a proposed rule. We've heard from members who assumed a closed comment period meant a decision was imminent. It usually means the opposite: the clock on the agency's internal work hasn't really started, and a funding gap pushes that start date further out.

This is also the kind of stretch where the federal margin-coverage backstop that many members rely on gets tested in ways it wasn't built for. The production data that feeds those risk calculations has itself gone quiet during the lapse, which means members waiting on both a rule and a payment estimate are waiting on two different kinds of federal work that happen to have stalled at the same time. Members managing both at once — a thin margin and a stalled rule they were counting on — have told us the shutdown is the part they didn't plan for, even though the price swings were.

## Where advocacy actually helps right now

We can't move a federal calendar. What we can do, and are doing, is keep the record current so it's ready the moment agency work resumes. That means:

- Collecting specific interstate shipment cases from members — the exact product, the exact states, the exact document that held things up — so our comment isn't abstract when the window reopens.
- Coordinating with the handful of state agriculture committees that have some authority here independently of the federal calendar, so a shutdown on one side doesn't freeze every channel a member has.
- Keeping members briefed honestly, including telling you plainly when the answer is "we don't know yet," which has been more often than we'd like this month.

If interstate shipment has cost you a sale or forced you into a workaround, that story is exactly what strengthens the record. Send it to the Membership & Outreach Committee through the member portal, with as much specific detail as you can — dates, states, product type. We keep a running file and it's the first thing we hand over once the comment period actually moves again.

None of this is legal advice about what you may currently ship where; the rules in effect today are the ones in effect today; regardless of the shutdown, changing your own shipping practice without asking your existing counsel or your state regulator first is not something we can substitute for.
