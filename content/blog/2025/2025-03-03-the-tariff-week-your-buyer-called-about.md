Members who ship across a border, and members who buy from someone who does, spent last week on the phone instead of in the cave. New tariff lines took effect this week on goods moving across two of the country's busiest trade lanes, dairy products among them, and the calls we fielded were less "what does this mean politically" and more "what does this do to my March order."

Here is the plain version. A new round of duties on goods crossing two of our members' busiest lanes went into effect this week, and the countries on the other side of both borders had already signaled duties of their own in response, cheese and other dairy products among the categories named. None of it is retroactive to product already on the water, and none of it changes anything about food-safety paperwork or labeling — this is a customs and pricing story, not a compliance one. But it does change landed cost, and landed cost changes what a distributor is willing to pay a small producer this quarter.

The trigger was announced with an exact effective date, which is unusual — most trade actions arrive with a window, not a single Tuesday. That precision cuts both ways. It gave members with an export or import desk a hard date to plan around instead of a moving target, but it also meant nobody had much runway between "this is confirmed" and "this is in effect."

## What we're hearing from members

A distributor member who moves specialty cheese across one of the affected lanes told us their broker had three different duty schedules drafted before the ink was dry on the final one — worth remembering the next time a number looks final and isn't. A retailer member who imports a handful of aged cheeses for a tasting counter said their supplier simply held a shipment rather than guess at the new rate. Neither is unusual in a week like this one.

If you export, the practical question isn't "will there be a tariff" — there already is one — it's "does my invoice date fall before or after the line opened, and does my customs broker know which schedule applies to my HS code." If you don't export, the practical question is whether your imported inputs — cultures, rennet, packaging — got more expensive, because several of ours did.

## What we're doing about it

Our advocacy team has already flagged the escalation to the working group that tracks agricultural trade rules on members' behalf, and we'll keep the membership posted as the schedules settle rather than react to every headline in between. If your business ships internationally and you haven't already, this is a good week to walk your invoice dates and duty codes with your customs broker — not your cheesemaker.

We know this lands on top of a year that's already asking small producers to watch every line of the budget more closely than usual. We don't have a fix for a tariff schedule, and we're not going to pretend one exists. What we can do is keep the membership's read on it current and specific, instead of vague and alarming, and make sure the people who set trade policy hear from actual small producers, not just the largest exporters.

If your business is affected and you want it counted in what we tell regulators, send a two-line note to advocacy@ — actual member impact, in actual numbers, is what makes our comment letters land. Tell us the lane you ship or buy on, roughly what the new duty adds to a case, and whether you've already had to hold or reprice a shipment because of it.

A few members asked whether this changes anything about the Membership & Outreach Committee's export-readiness workshop series. It doesn't — if anything, a week like this one is the argument for taking that workshop rather than skipping it, since half of what it covers is exactly this kind of duty-schedule literacy. If you ship internationally and haven't sat through it yet, watch for the next date on the events calendar.

In the meantime, treat any number you see in a headline this week as provisional until your own broker confirms the code and rate that apply to your specific product and lane. We'll do the same before we put a number in front of the membership.
