Grocery inflation has been the story of this spring, and it isn't a subtle one — shoppers are seeing higher numbers on nearly every aisle, and food-at-home prices have been climbing faster than overall inflation since February. You'd expect a category like specialty cheese, which is rarely the cheapest option in the case, to be one of the first things a squeezed household trims. Several of our retail members are telling us the opposite is happening.

A member who runs the cheese section at a mid-sized independent grocer put it this way on a committee call this month: her overall grocery basket data shows customers trading down in plenty of categories, but her cheese case has held its unit volume and even picked up a little in her more expensive wedges. She doesn't think her customers have more money than they did in January. She thinks they're spending it differently.

## Trading down everywhere except here

That pattern lines up with something we've heard from several members this spring: when a household is watching its total grocery bill, a good cheese purchase becomes one of the few affordable indulgences left standing. A well-chosen wedge is still a fraction of the cost of a restaurant meal, and members in direct retail say customers increasingly describe it that way — as a small, deliberate splurge rather than a routine staple.

That has real implications for how you talk to customers at the counter right now. If someone is choosing a cheese as their treat for the week rather than grabbing whatever's on sale, price isn't the only lever that matters, and in some cases it may not be the primary one. What matters more is a confident recommendation: something seasonal, something they haven't tried, something with a story you can tell in two sentences while they're standing at the case.

## What to try this spring

A few things members have found useful as this shift plays out:

- Lean into seasonal framing. Members report more interest in lighter, fresher styles as the weather turns, and pairing suggestions — a spring vegetable board, a lighter white wine — are landing better than a straight price call-out.
- Don't undersell your story. If a wheel comes from a small producer with a specific make or aging story, say so. Members tell us customers who are already treating the purchase as a splurge want to feel like they picked something worth it.
- Watch your slower movers rather than your best sellers. A few members have found their mid-tier cheeses are the ones losing ground to trading down, while both the everyday basics and the true showpieces are holding steady.

None of this means cost pressure has disappeared — your own input costs are still very real, and nothing here should talk you into pricing below what your margin needs. But if you've been bracing for cheese sales to soften along with the rest of the grocery basket, several of our members are seeing a different story so far this spring, and it's worth factoring into how you staff, stock, and talk to customers over the next few months.
