Federal food-safety rules have always drawn a line between operations large enough to bear the full compliance load and smaller ones that get a lighter version, and this month one of those lines moved again. A compliance threshold tied to average sales over a rolling three-year period is now catching a wider band of producers than it did a year ago, which means some members who've spent years comfortably under an exemption are waking up to find themselves inside it.

We hear from members every time one of these thresholds shifts, and the question is almost always the same: nobody told me this was coming. That's fair, and it's also exactly the gap the federation exists to close. The rules are written in federal registers and phase-in schedules, not in language built for a two-person creamery watching its sales grow the way it's supposed to.

## Why a small-producer exemption is worth fighting for, not just tracking

Our position, and the reason the Food Safety Committee keeps a standing watch on this topic, is that exemption thresholds should scale with the actual risk a small operation presents, not just its revenue line. A creamery that grew from $400,000 to $550,000 in gross sales over three years didn't necessarily get riskier in that time — it got more successful, and it's now facing a documentation burden that was designed with a very different scale of operation in mind. We think that distinction matters, and we've said so in the comment opportunities where we've had standing to say it.

That said, we're not going to pretend the rules don't apply once you cross the line. If your growth has put you inside a compliance threshold this year, the honest advice is to start building your written food-safety plan now rather than waiting for an inspector to ask for one. A plan built calmly over a few weeks is a much better document than one assembled the week before a visit.

## What we're asking for, and what we can do for you now

We're watching for the next comment period on how these thresholds are administered, and when one opens, we want testimony from members who've actually crossed a threshold recently — what changed for your business, what it cost you in time and paperwork, and whether the compliance requirement actually matched the risk. A specific account from someone who lived through it is worth more to a regulator than a general objection from an association.

In the meantime, if you're not sure whether this year's numbers put you on the wrong side of an exemption, that's worth figuring out now rather than in an audit. The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate walks through what a compliant written plan actually needs to contain, and it's built for creameries doing this for the first time, not for a food-safety department that doesn't exist yet at your scale. If you want to tell us how a threshold shift has hit your business, or you'd like help thinking through where your numbers land, the Food Safety Committee is the place to start.
