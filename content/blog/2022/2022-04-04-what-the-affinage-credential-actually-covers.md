We've had more questions about the Advanced Affinage Certificate this spring than we've had in the last two cycles combined. Some of that is timing — inventory is finally back to the point where several members are running full cave programs again instead of managing whatever fit around a smaller crew. Some of it is that a credential which used to feel optional now shows up in job postings we didn't write.

Part of the interest is also competitive. Alpine-style wheels swept the top spots again at this year's major cheese contest, and more than one member has told us that judging notes on rind development and paste texture are pushing their own caving programs to get more rigorous, not less. If your competition entries are going to hold up against that level of finish, the cave work behind them has to be deliberate.

Here's what the credential actually is, and isn't.

## What the exam tests

The Advanced Affinage Certificate is not a wine-and-cheese pairing badge. It's built around the judgment calls a caving program makes weekly: reading a rind that's drying faster than the humidity log says it should, deciding whether a bloom on a washed-rind wheel is on-program or off, and knowing when a batch's moisture loss curve means you pull it early rather than hold for the planned window.

The written portion covers cave environment management — humidity and airflow targets by cheese family, brining and washing schedules, and how to document deviations so the next person on shift isn't guessing. The practical portion is evaluated rind assessment: candidates work a flight of wheels at different stages and explain, out loud, what they'd do next and why. There's no partial credit for a right answer with no reasoning behind it.

It sits above the Cheese Foundations Certificate and the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate in our credential ladder, and most members who take it already hold one or both. That's by design — affinage judgment doesn't mean much if the sanitation and food-safety fundamentals underneath it aren't solid. Milk supply has also been unusually tight this quarter, which means fewer members can afford to lose wheels to an inconsistent cave; a credential that formalizes good judgment has more value when every vat counts.

## Who it's actually for

This one isn't aimed at a member running a single cave alone. It tends to pay off fastest for programs bringing on a second or third person in the cave — a lead affineur who needs a shared vocabulary with a newer hire, or a creamery that's finally scaling caving beyond one person's memory. If that's not your situation yet, the Cheese Foundations Certificate is still the right starting point.

We know a handful of members have had this on the list since caves were running short-staffed, and we'd rather you take it when your team can actually use it than rush it now because the case is full again. "I didn't want to certify until I had a second person in the cave to actually use it with," one member told us at a recent workshop. "Before that it was just a piece of paper about a job I was already doing alone."

Registration for the next sitting is open through the member portal, and current holders of the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate get the prerequisite waived automatically. If you're not sure which credential is the right next step for your program, our Education Committee is a good first call.
