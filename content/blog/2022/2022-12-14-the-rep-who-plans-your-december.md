Most of our member spotlights feature the person behind the counter or the vat. This one is about someone most shoppers never meet at all: a regional sales rep for a member dairy-supply distributor, whose entire December is built around making sure other members' busiest week goes smoothly. She asked that we not use her name or her employer's — "I'd rather this be about the job than about me" — but she agreed to talk us through what the role actually looks like this time of year.

"Nobody notices a good distributor," she told us between stops on her route. "You only hear from a shop owner when something didn't show up. My job in December is to make sure I stay invisible in the best way."

## What years on a route teach you

She's called on some of the same shops for close to a decade, and in that time she's watched a few of them close, watched others double their footprint, and picked up newer accounts that didn't exist five years ago. What hasn't changed, she says, is the value of actually knowing the person on the other end of the order. "Anybody can email a PO. What I can do that a website can't is tell you your usual case-liner supplier is running two weeks behind, before it becomes your emergency instead of mine."

That relationship-first approach has mattered more this year than usual. Holiday spending is up compared to last year, but a lot of that increase is inflation rather than shoppers buying more — actual purchase volume held roughly flat or dipped slightly across most of the season, and people bought for fewer people than they did the year before. Plenty of her accounts have been watching margins more carefully as a result, and some have trimmed back on the extras — decorative ribbon, premium boxes — in favor of keeping the essentials well stocked. "Nobody's cutting back on wrap film in the middle of their busiest week," she said. "But I've had more conversations this year about which SKUs are actually worth carrying than I can remember having before."

## The part of the job that doesn't show up on an invoice

Ask her what's kept her doing this work for years and she doesn't talk about the product at all. "I've watched some of these shops go from a folding table at a farmers market to a real storefront. That's the part that keeps you coming back — you're not just moving boxes, you're watching somebody build something." She's candid that the work has gotten harder as some of the smaller regional distributors she used to work alongside have consolidated or closed, but she says the relationships are still what make the long December drives worth it.

For members on the receiving end of a route like hers, her advice is simple: talk to your rep before the rush, not during it. "If you tell me in October what you think you'll need in December, I can actually plan for it. If you tell me the week of, I'm doing the best I can, but I'm not a magician."
