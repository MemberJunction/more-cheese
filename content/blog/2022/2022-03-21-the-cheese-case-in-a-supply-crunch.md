If your packaging order came back partial this month, you are not alone. Glass, cardboard, and aluminum have all been running tight through the first quarter, and the ripple is reaching cheese rooms that had never given a second thought to where their wax paper or shrink film comes from. A member on last week's call put it simply: "I can make the cheese. I can't always wrap it."

The shortage isn't really about any one material. It's driver shortages stacked on top of container backlogs stacked on top of plants running below capacity, and cheesemakers are competing for the same cardboard as beverage brands and produce packers. Several members have told us their usual case-and-wrap suppliers have started rationing order sizes or pushing lead times out by weeks rather than days. For a small creamery working close to the edge of its cash cycle, a six-week wait on shrink bags is not a minor inconvenience — it's a production plan.

It doesn't help that this is layering on top of an already strained input picture. Trade reporting this month has flagged falling cow numbers across the top milk-producing regions, fertilizer shortages limiting pasture and stored feed, and cost inflation running ahead of the milk price gains that would normally cushion it. None of that is packaging-specific, but it's the backdrop your suppliers are also operating against, which is part of why patience on the phone with a case vendor buys you more this spring than it used to.

## What members are doing about it

The most common fix we're hearing about is the least glamorous one: a second supplier, even a smaller or pricier one, kept warm on standby before you need it. A few members have gone further and started pooling packaging orders with a neighboring creamery to hit better minimums, splitting a truckload of cardboard the way they might split a milk hauler's route. It won't fix the underlying shortage, but it smooths out the worst of the whiplash, and it means one supplier's bad week doesn't become your bad month.

There's also a quieter shift happening on the retail side. Buyers tell us they're more forgiving right now about label stock or wrap substitutions than they were two years ago — a plain kraft wrap in place of a printed one isn't costing anyone a sale this spring, as long as the cheese inside is doing its job. If you've been holding off on a packaging change because you assumed customers would notice or mind, this may be the season to test that assumption. A few members have told us the substitution went entirely unremarked, which says something about where a shopper's attention actually lands at the case.

## The upside underneath the friction

None of this is happening against a backdrop of weak demand — quite the opposite. Retail cheese cases have been busy, and members across categories are reporting steady or growing interest in artisan and farmstead product even as input costs climb. That's the strange shape of this stretch: strong sell-through, tight materials, and a member base that is, by and large, finding workarounds rather than pulling back.

If packaging has become a bottleneck in your production planning, our Membership & Outreach Committee has been collecting notes on which distributors members are having better luck with this quarter — reach out and we'll pass along what we're hearing. And if you haven't looked at your own margin math since materials costs moved, now's a reasonable week to do it. A ten-cent jump in wrap cost per unit is easy to lose in a busy season, but it adds up the same way an extra cent on the milk check does.
