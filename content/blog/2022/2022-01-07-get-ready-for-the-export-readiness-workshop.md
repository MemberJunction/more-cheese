Registration is open for Workshop: Valley Export Readiness, running February 1 in Seattle. It is an in-person day, capped at a small group, and it is aimed squarely at members who have never shipped a pallet across a border and don't want their first attempt to be a live shipment with a real customer waiting on the other end.

We scheduled this one for early February on purpose. A lot of members spent December fielding questions from an overseas buyer who found them at a fall trade show, and January is when those conversations either turn into a signed order or stall out over paperwork nobody on staff has filled out before. If that sounds like your situation, this workshop exists for exactly that gap.

## What the day actually covers

The morning is customs documentation: certificates of origin, health certificates, and the specific forms that differ by destination country. This part of the curriculum got a rewrite this year, because health-certificate requirements for dairy shipments into the European Union changed in mid-January, adding animal-health monitoring and veterinarian sign-off steps that were not required before. If your buyer is anywhere in the EU, or your freight route transits through it, this is not a paperwork detail you can skip — it is the difference between a shipment clearing and one sitting at a port. We will walk through a real (anonymized) set of paperwork from a member shipment start to finish, including the two or three places where first-time exporters most commonly get held up at the border under the current rules.

The afternoon shifts to cold-chain logistics — how to choose a freight partner who has actually moved dairy before, what transit-time buffer to build in for a wheel that needs to arrive in good condition, and how to price the shipment so the freight cost doesn't quietly eat your margin. We will close with time for members to bring their own paperwork and get eyes on it before they submit anything, including a checklist for the new EU health-certificate fields if that's your destination market.

This is also a good moment to be honest about capacity. Winter illness has been running through a lot of workplaces this year, freight and distribution included, and that has meant more schedule slippage than usual at every step of a shipment. Build extra lead time into your first export order if you can, and treat a quoted transit time as a floor, not a guarantee, until you have a few shipments of history with a given carrier.

## Who should come

This session is built for members who are past the "should we even try exporting" conversation and into "we have an order, now what." If you are still deciding whether export makes sense for your creamery, the Events Committee can point you to recorded material from past sessions instead — no need to travel to Seattle just to explore the idea. If you already have a country and a buyer in mind, though, this is the workshop to bring your actual paperwork to.

Seats are limited and have filled in past years, so register through the member portal sooner rather than later. Questions about content or logistics go to the Events Committee; questions about the certificates themselves are exactly what the workshop is for.
