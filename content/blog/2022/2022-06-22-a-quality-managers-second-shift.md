We don't always name names in a spotlight, and this month is one of those times, at a member's request. She's a quality manager at a mid-sized creamery in the upper Midwest, a federation member for a little over four years, and the kind of person whose job title undersells what she actually does day to day.

Her mornings are environmental monitoring: swabbing drains and floor mats, tracking results against a zone map she built herself, watching for the slow drift that tells you a corner of the plant needs more attention before it needs a recall. That part of the job hasn't changed much in years, and she says it shouldn't — a monitoring program that gets exciting is usually a monitoring program that's about to have a bad month.

She's not alone in feeling stretched. Industry reporting this year has described food-safety staffing as running at something close to a crisis level across processors of every size, with plenty of promised support slower to arrive than the plants that need it. A single quality manager covering a program built for two people isn't a story unique to her creamery, and she says that context is part of why she finally pushed for help rather than just absorbing the extra hours herself.

## What's new is who else is doing it

What has changed, over the past year, is that she's no longer the only person who can read that zone map. Hiring has been tight across the industry, and she made the case to ownership that training two junior staff to run swabs and interpret results wasn't a nice-to-have, it was the only way her own vacation days would ever mean anything. "I used to be the only one who knew where the risk actually lived in this building. That's not a strength, that's a single point of failure with a name."

She built the training around the federation's Food Safety & HACCP coursework rather than writing her own curriculum from scratch, partly to save time and partly because she wanted her new hires learning from a source broader than just her own plant's history. "I know our building. I don't know every way a plant like ours can go wrong. The course does."

## Why this is a membership story, not just a training story

She's blunt that none of this would have gone as smoothly without the wider membership to check her plan against. Before she rolled out the new training schedule, she ran it past two other quality managers she'd met at a past conference, both of whom had already been through a similar hiring stretch. One caught a gap in her swab rotation she hadn't noticed in three years of running it solo. "That's the whole reason I keep renewing. Not the newsletter. The fact that I'm not the only person who's ever had this exact problem."

If you're building out a food-safety team of your own this year, our Food Safety Committee can connect you with members who've done the same, and the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate remains the fastest path to a shared vocabulary between a veteran quality manager and a first-week hire.
