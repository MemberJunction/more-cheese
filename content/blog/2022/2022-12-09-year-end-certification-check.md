Every December we get a small wave of members asking the same question: how close am I to finishing a credential I started earlier this year? It's a fair question for the calendar to raise. The Certified Cheese Professional (CCP) pathway and the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate are both built from a handful of courses and exams that people tend to chip away at over a year, and December is when the gaps become obvious.

## What each credential actually requires

The CCP is the broadest of the two — it draws on coursework across sensory evaluation, affinage, and business fundamentals, and most members take twelve to eighteen months to complete it around their regular work. It's not a single test; it's a portfolio of coursework plus a final assessment, which means partial progress carries forward and nothing you've already finished expires while you're working toward the rest.

The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate is narrower and more exam-focused. It covers hazard analysis, critical control points specific to small-batch cheesemaking, and the documentation practices that hold up under a health inspection. Members who complete it tend to say the same thing afterward: the certificate isn't what changed their operation, the process of building out a real HACCP plan while studying for it was. If you've been meaning to formalize a food safety plan that currently lives mostly in your head, working toward this certificate is a reasonable way to force the issue.

## The five-minute version

Log into your member portal and pull up your course history under the Education tab. For CCP, check which of the required tracks show as complete versus in-progress — if you're one course away from a track, that's worth prioritizing before winter registration opens, since some sections fill early. For the HACCP certificate, check whether your practical assessment is scheduled; it's the piece people put off longest, and it's also the piece that actually needs to happen before the credential posts.

Neither credential requires you to start over if you let a track sit for a while — this isn't a use-it-or-lose-it situation. But knowing exactly where you stand now, while winter course seats are still open, gives you more choice about pacing than finding out in March that a track you needed only runs in the fall.

If you're carrying an active certification and it's due for renewal, renewal requirements are listed on your credential page in the portal, and the Education Committee can answer specifics if your situation doesn't match the standard renewal path.

Recertification, for members who already hold a credential, tends to get put off for a different reason than the initial track does — it's not that the requirements are unclear, it's that renewal windows are easy to lose track of when they're measured in years rather than months. A credential earned two winters ago can quietly slide past its renewal date while you're focused on this season's cheese, and catching that in December, while you still have time to schedule whatever continuing education the renewal requires, is a lot less stressful than catching it the week the credential is due to lapse.

Winter course registration is the other reason this week specifically matters. Sections for the credentials that feed into both CCP and the HACCP certificate tend to be smaller and fill faster in winter than in the busier spring and fall terms, simply because more members have calendar room to study between the holiday rush and spring cheese-case planning. If you wait until January to check your standing, you may find that the one course you actually need for your track is already full, and the next section doesn't run again until summer.

That's the practical case for spending five minutes on this now rather than treating it as a New Year's resolution. Either way, five minutes with your course history this week is worth more than another few weeks of not knowing where you stand.
