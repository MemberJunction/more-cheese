We heard from a member this week who spent her Fourth of July behind a folding table instead of in front of one, and she wanted to talk about something we don't get asked about often: what it actually takes to keep a cheese case honest when the thermometer won't cooperate.

She runs a small farmstead operation with her husband, the kind of two-person outfit where "market day" means loading the truck at five, holding a tent line against the wind, and still making it home in time to milk. This year's holiday weekend landed in the middle of a heat wave that pushed demand records across parts of the country and put real strain on the power grid in several regions — not exactly the backdrop you want for a table full of soft-ripened wheels and a cooler that's already working overtime.

## What she changed for one weekend

Nothing in her routine was new, she told us — it was just more of it, sooner. She doubled her ice rotation, moved her most temperature-sensitive items (a bloomy-rind favorite that softens fast) to the coolest corner of the tent, and cut her table display down by nearly a third so less product sat exposed at once. She also started checking case temperature with a probe thermometer every half hour instead of at the start and end of the day, a habit she picked up from one of our food safety workshops a couple of years ago and had, by her own admission, gotten a little loose about since.

"You get complacent in a normal June," she said. "This wasn't a normal June, and it definitely wasn't a normal Fourth."

None of this is dramatic. It's the unglamorous, slightly boring discipline that keeps a market table compliant and a customer's lunch safe, and it's exactly the kind of thing that's easy to let slide when a line is forming and you're the only person working the stand. She sold out two hours early, which she credits partly to the holiday crowd and partly to the fact that nothing in her case looked tired by two in the afternoon.

## The part worth repeating to your own crew

If you run a market stand, a farm store, or even a retail counter with a case that sees direct sun for part of the day, this is a good week to walk your own team through the basics again: probe thermometers checked and calibrated, product rotation planned before the doors open rather than improvised at noon, and a clear call on what comes off display first if temperatures start climbing faster than expected. Our Food Safety Committee has fielded a few member questions this month about hot-weather handling at outdoor events, and we're planning to fold some of that into an upcoming session — watch for details in a future newsletter.

For now, our thanks to a member who spent her holiday weekend keeping a good case good, heat wave and all, and who was generous enough to walk us through exactly how she did it.
