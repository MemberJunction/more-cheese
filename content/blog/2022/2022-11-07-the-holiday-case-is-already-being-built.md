Two things are true about the cheese case this week, and they pull in opposite directions. Federal export data released this fall shows another strong month for American cheese shipped abroad, extending a growth streak that's now run more than a year. At the same time, grocery buyers are telling us the holiday promotional calendar arrived earlier than usual, with advent-style cheese assortments and multi-buy deals already live in some regions before Thanksgiving. Strong exports and aggressive domestic discounting are usually a sign of a market with plenty of product moving. This year it's also a sign of a market where two very different customers — an overseas buyer and a price-conscious holiday shopper — are pulling on the same finite wheel inventory at the same time.

## What the export numbers actually mean for your case

A record export month sounds like unambiguously good news, and mostly it is — strong overseas demand supports the milk price, which supports what a plant can pay for the milk that becomes your cheese. But members selling direct to retail have flagged a real friction point this fall: when export demand pulls hard on a particular style, the wholesale allocation available for domestic specialty accounts can tighten right when retailers want more inventory for holiday programs, not less. If you supply a retail account and haven't confirmed your November and December volumes are locked, this is the week to make that call rather than assume last year's allocation carries forward.

For members buying rather than selling, the practical read is simpler: order early where you can, and don't be surprised if a supplier quotes a shorter lead time than usual on a popular holiday style. A strong export market is a good problem to have, but it's still a supply constraint from where a shop counter or wholesale buyer is standing.

## The promotional squeeze, and what to watch through the season

On the retail side, the story is about margin, not supply. Several members running retail counters or wholesale accounts into grocery report holiday cheese promotions launching with steeper discounts than last year, aimed at moving volume during a season when household grocery budgets are stretched thinner by broader price inflation. A deep discount on a marquee holiday cheese can be a genuinely good traffic driver — it gets a customer to the case who then buys three other things at full price — but it only works if the economics were built into your fall pricing, not bolted on in November because a competitor moved first.

If you haven't already, this is a good week to sit down with your fall price sheet and ask which items can absorb a promotional discount without going underwater, and which ones need to stay full-price and let the discount live somewhere else in the case. A promotion built around a loss leader you can actually afford is a marketing tool. A promotion built around panic-matching a competitor's price is a margin problem wearing a marketing costume.

Two things worth tracking as the season runs: whether the export growth streak holds through the holiday shipping window, since a slowdown there could ease some of the domestic allocation pressure, and whether the early promotional push holds through December or fades once shoppers are already committed to their holiday menus. Members who track both — what's leaving the country and what's landing in the discount bin — tend to make sharper calls on their own pricing than members watching either number alone.

If your account has questions about export allocation language in a wholesale contract, or wants a second read on a promotional pricing plan before it launches, the federation's member advisory line is a good first stop, and several members on the Membership & Outreach Committee have been through exactly this kind of holiday squeeze before and are generally glad to compare notes.
