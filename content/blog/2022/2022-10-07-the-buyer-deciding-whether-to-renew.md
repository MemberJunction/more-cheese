Most of our renewal reminders go out to members who've long since stopped thinking about them — the notice arrives, the card on file gets charged, nothing changes. This week's spotlight member is not that member. He buys specialty cheese for a mid-sized retail account, joined the federation a little over a year ago, and has deliberately kept his renewal off auto-pay every year since, which means every fall he actually has to decide, on purpose, whether membership earned its keep.

"I know that's more work than it needs to be," he told us. "But it means I actually look at what I used the membership for before I pay for another year of it. If I put it on autopilot I'd stop asking that question, and I think the answer would eventually be no without me noticing."

## What he's actually weighing

His job is buying, not making, which means his relationship to membership looks different from a cheesemaker's. He's not taking courses toward a credential or aging anything himself. What he uses, he said, is the member directory and the people he's met through it: producers he's discovered at competition tables, other buyers he can call when a supplier falls through, and a rough sense of which regions are having a good or bad year that he wouldn't get from a distributor's catalog alone.

This fall, that network mattered more than usual. Suppliers have been adjusting wholesale prices more often than they used to as feed and packaging costs move, and he said having other buyer-members to compare notes with — is this increase specific to my supplier, or is everyone seeing it — has been genuinely useful rather than nice-to-have. "I called two other buyers I only know through the federation to ask if their cost increases looked like mine," he said. "That's not something I could do with people I compete against directly, but I can do it with people two states away who buy from different producers." It's the same instinct behind a lot of the fall-planning conversation we've been hearing from retail members generally, as holiday case decisions get made earlier every year and a bad guess costs more to fix once the season's underway.

Asked why he doesn't just simplify his life and set membership to renew automatically, he was matter-of-fact about it. "Because the year I stop finding it useful, I want to actually notice that, not just keep paying because it's easier than canceling," he said. "So far every year the answer's been an easy yes. But I'd rather the yes be a real decision than a default."

He's currently in the window where his membership would lapse if he let it, and he's already told us he's renewing — this isn't a cliffhanger. But his habit of forcing the annual re-evaluation is one we think is worth normalizing rather than treating as unusual. If you're a member who's never once looked at your own renewal notice before the card gets charged, his question is a reasonable one to ask yourself this year: what did you actually use it for, and would you buy it again on purpose?

If you're a retail buyer looking for the kind of cross-region comparison he described, the Membership & Outreach Committee keeps informal connections between buyer-members in non-competing markets — reach out if you want an introduction before your own renewal notice lands.
