Talk of reopening the federal milk pricing formulas has been building since the start of the year, and it reached our committee's agenda again this month. Nothing is being decided in April. What is happening in April is the part that actually matters for a smaller processor or farmstead operation: the shape of the eventual proposal is still being drafted, and the people writing it are working mostly from cooperative and large-processor input.

The formulas in question set how Class III and Class IV milk get priced across the federal marketing order system, and they have not had a comprehensive overhaul in more than two decades. A change of that scale ripples outward. It affects what a co-op pays a member for raw milk, which affects what a small creamery pays for its milk supply, which affects a wheel's cost before a single culture goes into the vat. Most of that ripple lands on decisions our members did not sit in the room for.

## Why this matters for cheesemakers specifically

The federal order conversation is being driven largely by fluid milk and bulk commodity concerns — Class I differentials, component pricing, how butterfat gets factored in. Specialty and artisan cheese producers are a smaller, quieter constituency in that room, and the formulas that get set will apply to them regardless of whether their situation got mentioned. A pricing change built around bulk commodity assumptions can land oddly on a producer buying small lots of milk for a hand-ladled wheel.

Feed costs are already running high this spring, and several members have told us their operating margin is thinner than the record milk prices in the headlines would suggest. That is exactly why the formula conversation matters now rather than later: a member already absorbing higher feed bills has less room to absorb a pricing structure that was not built with their operation in mind. Our Membership & Outreach Committee has started collecting short, specific accounts from members: what a milk price change of even a few cents per hundredweight does to a monthly cost sheet, and where the current formulas already create friction for a small or mid-sized operation. We are not asking for position papers. A paragraph, with real numbers from your own books, is more useful to us than a general statement of concern.

## What we are asking members to do

If you buy milk under a federal order and have an opinion about how the current pricing formulas serve — or fail to serve — a specialty cheese operation, send it to the committee this month rather than waiting for a formal comment period to open. Early input shapes what gets proposed; input that arrives after a proposal is public mostly just reacts to it. We would rather members were in the first group.

We will keep this update short and factual as it develops, because the formula details get technical fast and we do not want to overstate where the process actually stands. Nothing has been decided, no hearing has been scheduled, and any final rule is a long way off. What is true today is that the conversation is starting now, among people who mostly do not haul milk for a living, and we think our members' numbers belong in it early rather than late.

One detail members should understand going in: the current order system already prices milk differently by region, in ways that were built around fluid milk logistics rather than manufacturing use. A cheese operation drawing milk in a region set up mostly to serve fluid bottling can end up on the wrong side of that math compared to an operation in a region built around manufactured dairy products. Any reform conversation that starts from "modernize the whole system" risks smoothing over that regional unevenness rather than fixing it, unless people who actually make cheese from that milk are in the conversation saying so.

That is the specific gap our committee wants to close. Trade press covering this so far has quoted co-ops, bottlers, and national dairy groups almost exclusively. A federation representing cheesemakers and creameries is a different constituency with a different cost structure, and if we do not put that difference on the record now, it is unlikely to show up later on its own.

If you want to talk through what this could mean for your own milk supply before writing anything down, the Membership & Outreach Committee is the right first call. We would rather have a longer conversation with a member in April than receive a rushed comment after a proposal has already taken its final shape.
