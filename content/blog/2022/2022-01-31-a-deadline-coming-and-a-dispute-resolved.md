Two pieces of news are worth a place in your files this week, even if neither one is dramatic enough to make the front of a trade newsletter. One is a deadline you should not let sneak up on you. The other settles, at least for now, a fight that has been dragging on for years over how much American cheese can move across one particular border.

## A margin-coverage deadline three weeks out

The federal dairy margin-protection program — the one that pays out when the gap between milk prices and feed costs gets too thin — closes its 2022 enrollment window on February 18. If you make fluid milk or run your own herd and you have been meaning to look at whether the coverage tiers are worth the premium this year, three weeks sounds like plenty of time. It also sounded like plenty of time in December, and here we are.

We are not going to tell you what tier to pick. That is a conversation for your own books, your own feed contracts, and — for anyone carrying real exposure — an accountant who knows dairy margins, not a federation newsletter. What we will say is that a signup window is only useful if you use it: put a date on the calendar now, pull your last twelve months of feed costs, and make the call before mid-February arrives on its own schedule. This year's milk prices have been unusually strong by recent standards, which changes the math on how much a given coverage tier is actually worth relative to its premium — one more reason not to guess at the number from memory.

## A shipping dispute that went the industry's way

Separately, a long-running dispute over how a major trading partner administers its dairy quotas was resolved this month in favor of U.S. exporters, after producers and processors had spent more than a year arguing that the quota system was structured to keep American cheese and butterfat out regardless of what the underlying trade agreement said on paper. For members who ship north, or who have been sitting on a filled-but-frustrated export license, this is the kind of ruling that changes what's actually possible on your next shipment — not the price you'll get, but whether the shipment clears at all.

If you are shipping into that market, or thinking about starting, this is a good week to check with your customs broker on whether the quota administration has actually changed on the ground yet. Rulings take time to become paperwork, and paperwork takes time to become a shipment that clears without a hold. Members prepping for cross-border shipments can also get a walkthrough of the current documentation requirements from our Membership & Outreach Committee, who fielded more than a few calls on exactly this last week.

Neither of these stories moves your milk check this month. But a closing signup window and a resolved dispute are the kind of quiet, practical items that add up — worth ten minutes with your notes before you file them away, and worth mentioning to anyone at your creamery or shop who tends to let the paperwork slide until the deadline is already gone.
