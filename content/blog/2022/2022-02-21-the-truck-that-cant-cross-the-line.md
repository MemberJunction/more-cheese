A member wrote to us two weeks ago with a plain problem: a pallet of a 65-day aged raw-milk
cheese, correctly made, correctly labeled, sitting at a distribution dock because the
receiving state's inspector wanted paperwork the shipping state doesn't require. Nobody was
wrong, exactly. The federal aging-and-labeling standard for raw-milk cheese in interstate
commerce hasn't moved in decades, and states have filled the gaps with their own add-ons —
which is exactly the situation our advocacy committee has been logging for two years now.

We're not writing this to relitigate whether raw-milk cheese belongs on a truck. That question
was settled long ago, with a 60-day aging floor and a label requirement, and it stays settled.
What isn't settled is the patchwork of state-level documentation sitting on top of it, and
that's where members keep losing time and product.

### What the federation is asking for

This month we filed a short comment with the record on interstate cheese shipment, alongside a
handful of other trade groups doing the same thing on their own timelines. Our ask is narrow:
a single, federally recognized documentation packet — batch date, aging location, moisture
testing records — that any receiving state has to accept without a second, state-specific form.
Not a change to the aging rule. Not a fight about raw milk itself. Just one form instead of
however many a shipping lane happens to touch.

We heard from eleven members while drafting the comment, most of them small producers who ship
across two or three state lines regularly. One line from that feedback stuck with our policy
staff: "I don't mind proving my cheese is safe. I mind proving it four different ways to four
different people who are asking about the same 65 days." That's the sentence that opens our
filing, with the member's permission and without their name attached.

This request isn't happening in a vacuum. Milk prices have been climbing into 2022 — federal
market reporting has had Class III running well above where it sat a year ago — and feed and
transport costs are following the same line upward as energy markets have gotten more volatile
this month. A stalled truck costs more to re-route or hold now than it did even six months ago.
That's a second, quieter reason the paperwork problem matters: every extra day a load sits is a
real number on somebody's ledger, not just an inconvenience.

## If you've hit this wall

Tell us. Our advocacy log only has weight in a rulemaking record if it has volume behind it, and
right now we're working from the handful of stories members have already shared. If a shipment
of yours has been held, delayed, or kicked back over documentation a receiving state added on
top of the federal standard, send the committee the lane, the state, and what they asked for —
no need to attach financials or anything you'd rather keep private. We keep a running list, and
it's that list, not a general sense that something's wrong, that gets read into a comment
period.

If you want to get more involved than a one-time report, the Membership & Outreach Committee
can point you toward the advocacy volunteer track, and it's a lighter lift than people expect —
most of it is exactly what you just did reading this post: telling us what actually happened at
your dock.
