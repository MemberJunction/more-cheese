A specialty cheese buyer at one of our member retail shops told us the fall case is the one she plans hardest for and enjoys the least while it's happening. "Everyone wants the same six things in October," she said. "Firm alpine styles, a good aged cheddar, something with a story about a cave. The trick isn't finding those — it's finding them from a producer who can actually hold the volume through the holidays."

She's been a federation member for several years, and she's built a reputation among the other buyers she talks to for doing her fall sourcing early, often weeks before she actually sits down to taste. "By the time I'm tasting a wheel for the case, I've already ruled out half the producers on my list because they couldn't tell me a firm number for December," she said. "Taste matters, but it's not first. First is: can you actually get me the volume, on time, at a price that still lets me make money in January when the traffic slows down."

## Reading a fall case like a spreadsheet

That's a more mechanical way of thinking about buying than most people expect from someone whose job description sounds like tasting cheese for a living, and she's candid that it took her a while to get there. Early on, she says, she built cases around whatever tasted best in the room. "You end up with a beautiful case and a supply problem three weeks later," she said. "Now I build the spreadsheet first — production capacity, price stability, how the producer handled last year's holiday rush — and the tasting tells me which of the qualified options actually deserves the space."

This fall's case has been a harder build than most, she says, and not because the cheese itself has changed. Alfalfa prices climbed through the summer, and several of her regular producers have quietly adjusted wholesale terms to reflect it. "I don't begrudge anyone for repricing when their feed bill jumps like that," she said. "But it means I can't just reorder what worked last October. I have to ask each producer what actually changed on their end, and decide whether the case can absorb it."

## What she tells newer buyers

Her advice for members newer to cheese buying is blunt: talk to your producers about their cost pressure before you talk to them about your margin. "If you wait until you're negotiating price to ask about their costs, you're already behind," she said. "The buyers I respect most ask early, and they ask like they actually want to know the answer, not like they're building a case to negotiate down."

She also credits the federation's producer directory with making that kind of early conversation easier than it used to be — a place to check who's shipped reliably before, rather than relying entirely on a rep's pitch. If you're building a fall case of your own and want to compare notes with other retail and distributor members, the Membership & Outreach Committee keeps a running list of members willing to talk through their sourcing approach; reach out and they'll make an introduction.
