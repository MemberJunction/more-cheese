Milk checks are strong this spring, and that has been the headline members keep sending us. It is not the whole story. A member who runs a small farmstead operation forwarded us a hauling invoice last week with a new line on it: a fuel surcharge, itemized separately from the base rate for the first time in the cooperative's memory. It was not large. It was also not going away.

Diesel has climbed steeply since late February, and the increase has moved through the supply chain faster than most producers can reprice around. A milk truck runs on diesel whether the price at the pump is $3.50 or $5.00, and haulers who absorbed the difference for a few weeks are now passing it through as a surcharge tied to a fuel index. For a member shipping raw milk to a regional processor, that line item eats directly into the spread between what the milk check pays and what it costs to make and sell the finished wheel.

## Where the pinch actually lands

The people who feel this first are not the largest operations, which often have volume enough to negotiate a flat hauling rate regardless of index swings. It is the small and mid-sized creameries, especially the ones far enough from a processing hub that hauling was already a meaningful cost before this spring. Add rising feed costs on top — hay and grain prices have not pulled back the way some producers hoped — and a milk check that looks record-setting on paper is doing less work than it used to.

We are not going to tell members what to charge for a wheel of cheese; that is a business decision only they can make with their own numbers in front of them. What we can say is that a fuel surcharge buried in a hauling invoice is easy to miss when you are pricing off last quarter's cost sheet. If you have not pulled your hauling and freight line items into your cost-per-wheel math recently, this is the season to do it — before a summer of high diesel prices makes the gap even harder to see coming.

A few members on last week's committee call also flagged something worth passing along: retail buyers are starting to ask, unprompted, why prices moved. A plain answer — fuel, feed, both — tends to land better with a regular customer than silence does.

## What we are watching

We do not expect diesel prices to settle back down quickly, and feed costs remain tied to a global grain market that is under real strain this year. Members who ship whey, buy in cultures from outside suppliers, or rely on refrigerated distribution for wholesale accounts should expect the same surcharge conversation to show up in those invoices too, if it has not already.

We will keep tracking hauling and input costs through the summer and report back if the picture shifts.
