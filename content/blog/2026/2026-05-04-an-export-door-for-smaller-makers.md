For most of the last few years, exporting has been something a handful of the federation's larger producer members did and almost nobody else. The paperwork alone — health certificates, country-specific labeling requirements, the documentation trail a customs broker wants before a pallet ever leaves the dock — has kept it out of reach for a creamery running a two-person office. Lately, that's starting to change, and not just among the members you'd expect.

## Why this is landing now

Cheese exports have set a new monthly record for three consecutive months, and federal forecasters have been revising both milk production and dairy product price projections upward through the spring as global demand for U.S. cheese, whey, and dairy proteins has held firmer than expected. Domestic cheese prices have room to hold steady even as export volumes climb, which is part of why the math is starting to work for producers who never seriously considered shipping outside the country before.

We've noticed the shift mostly in our inbox. A handful of smaller producer members reached out this spring asking what a first export shipment actually involves — not the theoretical version, but the real one: which certificate, which broker, which market is forgiving enough for a first mistake. That's a different question than the one bigger members have been asking us for years, which tends to be about scaling an existing export relationship rather than starting one.

Part of what's driving the question is simpler than trade policy: the domestic case is crowded. A member who's spent the last few years fighting for shelf space against a growing field of artisan competitors is naturally going to wonder whether a market she hasn't tried yet might be easier to break into than the one she already knows too well. Whether that's actually true depends entirely on the market and the producer, but it's a reasonable question to start asking out loud.

## What we'd tell a member weighing this

If you've never shipped a pallet outside the country, don't start with the biggest market you can think of. Start with a single buyer relationship in a market where the paperwork is more forgiving, and build the muscle before you scale it. Our Export Documentation Essentials course walks through the health certificate and customs process step by step, using the kind of small-batch shipment a first-time exporter is actually likely to send, not a theoretical container load.

It's also worth saying plainly: strong export figures at the national level are not a guarantee that exporting will pencil out for your creamery specifically. Freight costs eat margin fast on small volumes, and a buyer relationship that falls through after your first shipment can leave you holding inventory you priced for a market that didn't come through. Talk to your accountant about what an export line actually does to your margin before you commit production capacity to it.

If you're weighing a first shipment, the Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding more of these questions than usual this spring and is a good first call before you spend time on the paperwork.
