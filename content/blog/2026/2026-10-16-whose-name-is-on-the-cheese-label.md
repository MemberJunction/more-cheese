"Cheese" is a legal word as well as a delicious one. Federal rules set out what a product has to contain before it can carry certain dairy names, and those rules are called standards of identity. This summer, the question of who enforces them came back into view.

We want to explain what is happening, where we stand and where you can help, because a label is one of the few things every member prints.

## What happened

On July 20, a bipartisan group of senators and representatives sent a letter to the acting head of the federal food regulator. They are the original sponsors of a bill introduced in the spring, which would direct the regulator to enforce the existing standards for milk, cheese and yogurt and to act against imitation products that use traditional dairy names.

The letter asks the regulator to do two things: enforce the standards it already has, and withdraw a February 2023 guidance document that the lawmakers say takes a weak approach to plant-based products using dairy names. The group states that it does not want to block the sale of dairy substitutes. Its argument is about names, not about shelves.

The bill itself is still only a bill. A letter is a request, not a rule, and nothing on your label has to change because of it.

## Why cheesemakers should care

There are two ways to read this, and members hold both views.

**The protective reading.** If the standards are enforced, a product that does not meet the definition of cheese cannot use the word. Makers who spend months aging a wheel say this is a matter of fairness. Shoppers should be able to trust what the word means.

**The cautious reading.** Naming rules cut in more than one direction. A rule written to keep a plant-based product out of the cheese case can also affect processed, cultured or novel dairy products that already sit in it. Some members who make fresh and cultured products told us they want to see the exact text before they take a position.

One caveat from our own members is worth passing on. A cheesemaker in the Northeast, who makes sheep's milk cheese in a very small shop, said: "I don't have a lawyer on retainer. I want to know what I have to change and when, in plain English."

That is the standard we are holding ourselves to.

**Where we stand.** The federation's position is narrow and practical. We support clear enforcement of the standards that already exist. We want any change to be written with a transition period that small makers can plan around, and we want the meaning of the word "cheese" to stay stable for the products members already make. We have not taken a position on any specific bill text, because there is not yet a final one to read.

**What we are asking of you.**

- **Send us your label.** The Standards Committee is collecting examples of labels that use a style name, a regional name or a descriptor such as "cultured" or "spread", so we can see where a naming rule could bite.
- **Tell us about your sales channel.** Shipping across state lines changes which rules apply to you, and we want to know how many members are in that position.
- **Do not reprint your labels yet.** There is no deadline and no rule. If you are planning a new label run for the holiday case, ask your printer for a short run you can adjust.

Our labeling-law course goes through what a standard of identity is, how to read one and how to check a label against it. It is a good use of an evening this fall.

If a rule does emerge, we will write about it here and open a member comment period before we submit anything. This post is general information, not legal advice. Your own counsel is the right person to ask about your labels.
