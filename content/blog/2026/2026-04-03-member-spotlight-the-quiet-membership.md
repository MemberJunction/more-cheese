Most of what we write about here is a member doing something. This week we're writing about a membership that, for three straight renewal cycles, mostly didn't. It's a composite drawn from a pattern our Membership & Outreach Committee sees often enough to have a name for it internally: a seat paid for by a distributor or a larger food company on behalf of an employee, quietly auto-renewed every year, rarely logging into a course, almost never answering an outreach email. Not a lapsed member. Not an engaged one either. Just present, on the books, and silent.

This spring, one of those seats finally wrote back.

## What the silence usually means

A membership like this isn't a mystery to us so much as a familiar shape. The company pays the dues as a line item, often folded into a broader professional-development budget, and the person whose name is on the account didn't necessarily ask for it or choose it. They may have joined a webinar once, two years ago, and never come back. Our outreach emails to accounts like this get the lowest response rate of any segment we track, and we've generally assumed that meant disinterest rather than anything more complicated.

What we hadn't accounted for, until this member's reply, was how often the silence is closer to overload than disinterest. The reply we got this spring came from someone managing quality assurance across several plants for their employer, added to the federation roster two renewals ago by a manager who has since moved on, with no real onboarding to what membership actually offered. They didn't know the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate existed. They didn't know there was a committee that fields exactly the kind of interstate shipment question they'd been quietly stuck on for months. Nobody had ever told them, and the auto-renewal meant nobody at their company had ever had a reason to ask.

## What changed, and what we're doing differently

What changed wasn't a campaign. It was a single direct phone call from a Membership & Outreach volunteer who noticed the account's tenure and reached out person-to-person instead of through the standard renewal email, asking a simple question: what would actually be useful to you this year? The answer took less than ten minutes to walk through, and the member has since enrolled in a course they didn't know applied to their job.

We don't think every quiet, corporate-funded seat wants a phone call, and we're not proposing to cold-call the whole segment. But the committee is piloting a small check-in for accounts that have renewed three or more years with no logged activity, on the theory that at least some of that silence isn't a "no," it's a "nobody asked me the right question yet." If you know a colleague whose employer covers their dues and who's never quite figured out what to do with the membership, point them to the Membership & Outreach Committee. It costs them ten minutes, and it cost us one phone call to find out it mattered.
