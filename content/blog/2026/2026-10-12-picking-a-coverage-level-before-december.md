If your creamery milks its own herd, a deadline just started ticking. Enrollment for the 2027 federal dairy margin safety net opened on October 5 and closes on December 18, according to the trade press we read this week. It runs through the local federal farm service office, and the window is much longer than the one-month window producers had last year.

This is not a post about whether to enroll. It is a post about how to sit down and make the decision with your own numbers, because that is the part members tell us they put off.

## What changed for 2027

Three details are worth knowing before you open the paperwork.

- **Coverage levels run from $4.00 to $9.50 per hundredweight** of milk. Higher levels protect more of your margin and cost more to carry.
- **The first tier now covers the first 6 million pounds** of annual production, up from 5 million. For a small herd, that usually means every pound you ship or make into cheese sits in the first tier.
- **A one-time, multi-year election earns a premium discount** of 25 percent, per the coverage we read. It also locks in your choice, so read the terms before you commit.

A modest administrative fee applies each year, and a no-premium catastrophic option exists. We do not give figures for anything federal beyond what is published, so please check the farm service office for current numbers.

## A kitchen-table method

A farmstead member from the upper Midwest told us last week: "I used to pick whatever level my neighbor picked. Then I wrote down what a bad month actually costs me, and the answer was different."

That is the right instinct. Here is a simple order of operations.

1. **Start with your make sheet, not the program.** Work out what it costs you to produce a hundredweight of milk, counting feed, labor, fuel and the interest on any loan you carried this year.
2. **Find your floor.** Ask what margin per hundredweight you need to keep the creamery running without borrowing against next season's wheels.
3. **Look at the gap.** If your floor sits well above the lowest coverage level, the cheaper options may protect very little of what you need.
4. **Decide what you would give up to pay for it.** A premium is a cost line. Put it next to the other lines, including the ones that feel optional.
5. **Write the decision down with the date.** In two years you will want to know why you chose what you chose.

One reminder about this year's mood. Costs have eased since the 2025 squeeze, and several members have said they feel relieved. Feed and borrowing costs are still creeping up, though, as we wrote at the end of September, so a lower-stress year is a good time to price protection, not a reason to skip it.

**Cheesemakers who buy milk.** If you buy your milk from a neighboring farm instead of milking your own herd, this program is not yours to enroll in. It still matters to you, because a supplier who feels protected is a supplier who keeps the herd. Ask your milk supplier what they are planning. It is a good conversation to have before you talk about next year's price.

**What to do this week.** Pull your last twelve months of feed, fuel and loan statements and put them in one folder. Then take the **Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese** course materials, which walk through cost per hundredweight and cost per pound of finished cheese, and use the worksheet for your own herd. The Education Committee holds open office hours for members who want a second set of eyes, and we are glad to put you in touch with a peer who has done the arithmetic.

This is general information, not financial, tax or legal advice. The program rules and your own accountant decide what is right for your operation.
