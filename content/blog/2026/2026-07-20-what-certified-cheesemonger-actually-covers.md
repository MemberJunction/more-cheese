Members heading to Madison this week aren't only packing competition entries. A number of you have a credential exam on the calendar too, and the question the Membership & Outreach Committee has fielded most this summer is which one to sit for first. For anyone working a retail counter rather than a make room, the answer is usually Certified Cheesemonger, and it's worth being specific about what that credential actually asks of you, because the name alone undersells it.

Certified Cheesemonger is not a tasting quiz. It covers four areas members tell us they didn't expect all at once: production knowledge across the major style families, proper storage and handling for a working case, pairing logic that holds up under a customer's actual questions, and the food-safety basics that keep a counter compliant. The exam mixes written questions with a hands-on component — cutting a wheel cleanly, wrapping for sale, describing a cheese to a customer who's never heard of it. Members who've taken it say the hands-on portion is where people who "know cheese" but haven't worked a counter get surprised.

## Why the demand has picked up

Part of this is simply volume. Wisconsin's specialty cheese output rose again last year, and national production data through spring showed the growth concentrated in Italian-style and specialty categories rather than commodity cheddar, which was actually down slightly year over year. More cheese moving through more retail cases means more counter staff, and a credential that a shop can point to when it hires or promotes someone has real value beyond the certificate itself. Several members who run retail counters have told us they now list Certified Cheesemonger as a preferred qualification in job postings, not just a nice-to-have.

The other part is simpler: a credential gives a new hire a study path. "Learn the case" is vague. A syllabus with four sections and a practical exam at the end is not, and members training seasonal or first-year staff have told us the structure alone cuts weeks off the usual ramp-up.

## Where it fits with the rest of the ladder

Certified Cheesemonger sits earlier in the credential path than Certified Cheese Professional, and it doesn't require the Cheese Foundations Certificate first, which surprises some members who assume everything in our credential stack is sequential. If you're brand new to the counter, Certified Cheesemonger is a reasonable place to start on its own. If you're aiming eventually at CCP or a specialization like Advanced Affinage or Sensory Evaluation, it's still a sound foundation — the production and handling material overlaps enough that you won't be relearning it later.

One practical note for anyone testing this month: the written and hands-on portions are scheduled separately at most sittings, and members who've done both recommend leaving a full day between them if your schedule allows it rather than trying to do both back to back. A few members sitting for exams at this week's conference told us they're using the travel days on either side to review rather than cramming the morning of.

If you're deciding what to study for next, the Education Committee keeps a short guide to each credential's actual content, not just its name, and it's worth five minutes before you commit study hours to the wrong one.
