Members plan their Madison trip around the competition floor, the sessions, and the people they only see once a year — and then find out too late that the federation also runs several credential exams on-site that same week. This year's Annual Conference offers in-person sittings for the Certified Cheese Professional exam, the Cheese Foundations Certificate, and the Competition Judge Accreditation assessment, all scheduled around the main conference days rather than tacked onto either end of it. If a credential has been sitting on your list for a while, three weeks out is exactly the right time to check whether this is the year you finally sit for it without a separate trip.

The scheduling is deliberate rather than an afterthought. The Education Committee sets exam blocks in the early morning and the last afternoon slot, on the theory that a member who is already traveling to Madison for the conference sessions and the competition floor would rather sit an exam once, in a single trip, than plan a second one later in the year. It works well enough that on-site sittings for the CCP and Foundations exams have filled every year we have offered them, usually a week or two before the conference itself.

## Why the seats go first

Exam capacity at the conference venue is smaller than at a standalone testing sitting, because the same rooms are shared with breakout sessions for most of the week. That means member demand outpaces supply well before the conference dates arrive, and registration for an on-site exam closes earlier than registration for the conference itself. If you are planning to attend Madison anyway and have been meaning to sit for a credential, do not assume you can add it once you have already registered for the conference — check the exam registration page directly, since it runs on its own deadline.

The Judge Accreditation assessment in particular tends to surprise members who assumed it only ran as a separate track earlier in the year. Because this year's conference falls close to the competition itself, the committee added an on-site sitting so members who want to judge future competitions can complete the written and tasting components while the categories and defect language are still fresh from watching this year's judging.

## What to bring, and what not to assume

An on-site exam runs on the same rules as a standalone sitting — you still need to bring whatever eligibility documentation your credential requires, and a conference badge does not substitute for it. Members who assumed showing up with a badge was enough have been turned away from a testing block before, which is an unpleasant way to spend a morning you traveled a long way for. If you are unsure whether you meet a credential's prerequisites, the certifications page lists them plainly, and the Education Committee would rather answer that question by email this week than at a check-in table in Madison.

One more thing worth knowing before you register: results from an on-site sitting take slightly longer to post than a standalone exam date, because the same staff running your exam block are also working the conference floor that week. That is a fair trade for not having to plan a second trip, but it means you should not expect same-week results the way you might from an exam sat on its own. If a credential has been on your list, this is the week to register for it — not the week after the conference, when the seats for this year's sittings will already be gone.
