October is when a cheese counter either plans its holidays or gets run over by them. The wheels you will sell in December need to be chosen, cut and priced before the first big weekend, and the small decisions you make now decide how much of the season you keep.

This post is a planning checklist for retailers and for makers who sell at their own counter or stand.

A published retail analysis of last Christmas found that cow's-milk cheese volumes rose about 4 percent in the two weeks before the holiday, and spend rose about 6 percent. Shoppers made hundreds of thousands of extra cheese purchases. Snacking formats grew fastest, and blue and cheddar styles both did well.

The analysts tied much of that to the cheeseboard, which they expected to feature in celebrations for about a third of shoppers. Premium and specialty cheeses gained from those extra purchases, and the report's advice was to keep premium ranges strong, with clear quality cues. It also noted that the uplift leaned on well-timed deals, and warned that promotions should add value without eroding margin.

That is a UK data set, so treat it as an indicator and not as a forecast for your town. A separate tracker of online artisan-cheese sales showed a dip in 2025 and forecast a rebound this year, so a modest uptick in gift and board orders is plausible. Both fit what members have told us: a board needs three or four cheeses, and customers want help choosing them.

**Cut sizes are a pricing decision.** A whole wheel at a holiday price is a hard sell. A smaller wedge is easier. Cutting down is also where margin leaks, so decide on paper first.

- **Pick two or three target weights** for the season and stick to them. Fewer weights mean faster wrapping and less trim.
- **Know your trim loss.** Every cut leaves rind ends and crumbs. Track what you sell as snack cuts or sample pieces, and fold that into the per-pound price.
- **Cut ahead, not on demand,** for the two busiest days. Pre-cut, wrapped and labeled pieces sell faster and move the line.
- **Keep one whole piece on display.** Shoppers trust a cheese they can see cut.

**A board, not a pile.** Cross-merchandising is the other lever that research kept returning to. Put jam, crackers and a knife next to the cheese, and the basket gets larger without a discount.

A few things we have seen work:

- A fresh or lactic cheese on every board. Fresh styles were the fastest-growing cheese category in one large import market this year, and they cut and portion into small cups easily.
- A "board in a box" of three cheeses at different textures: one fresh or soft-ripened, one firm, one blue or washed-rind.
- A tasting plate that changes each weekend. Keep samples small and keep the allergen card in view.
- A handwritten note about who made the cheese. A short story beats a long adjective.

For the **Counter Culture: Retailing** course, we often hear from members that the sample plan was the most useful part. Bring a plan, bring a calendar and block out which three days need extra hands.

## One thing for this week

Take a sheet of paper and write down the ten cheeses you expect to sell most between Thanksgiving and New Year's Day. Next to each, write the cut size, the pre-cut quantity for your two busiest days and the day you will place the order. If three of them are aged more than a few months, your order date was probably last month.
