Every distribution rep we talk to has a map in their head, and this member's map got two holes
punched in it last year when a pair of longtime farmstead accounts closed within a few months
of each other. She works the upper Midwest for a mid-sized specialty distributor, the kind of
job where a route is really a set of relationships built one delivery at a time, and losing two
stops that fast meant more than a smaller invoice. It meant rethinking who the route was for.

"I spent about six weeks deciding whether to just run a shorter route and call it a smaller
year," she told us. "Then I decided that wasn't actually a decision, that was just letting the
closures decide for me." Instead she went looking for replacement accounts, not to fill the
exact same slots, but to rebuild the route around who was actually still standing after a hard
stretch for small creameries.

## What rebuilding meant in practice

The two creameries she lost were both small farmstead operations, the kind that had been
squeezed hardest by the feed and energy costs the federation has been writing about for the
better part of a year. Rebuilding around that reality meant she couldn't just look for two more
farmsteads exactly like them. She widened her search to include a couple of mid-sized producers
who'd held steady through the same period, on the theory that steadier accounts now made a more
resilient route than chasing volume back to where it used to be.

That is a quieter kind of resilience planning than the federation usually writes about, and it
is worth naming directly: a route rebuilt around who survived a hard year, rather than around
who used to be on the list, is a different route on purpose. She also started asking new
accounts a question she hadn't bothered with before losing the old ones — not just their
current volume, but what their own cost pressure looked like, so she wasn't signing up for
another set of accounts one bad quarter away from the same outcome.

With this month's firmer block and barrel cheese prices, some of her retail accounts are
finally seeing a little room in their own margins, which she says has made the conversation
about adding volume easier than it would have been in the fall. That is not a guarantee the
market stays this way. It is one rep, in one region, describing what the current numbers feel
like from the account side rather than the forecast side.

## What she'd tell another rep

Her advice for anyone facing the same choice: don't wait until the route is already visibly
short-handed to start rebuilding it. "The two accounts I lost, I knew about one of them three
months out. I should have started looking that same week instead of waiting to see if the
other one held on too." She also credits a regional distributor roundtable, arranged informally
through other members, for giving her a faster read on which producers in her territory were
actually stable enough to court.

If you're rebuilding a route of your own this spring, the Membership & Outreach Committee keeps
an informal list of members willing to compare notes by region — ask for an introduction rather
than starting from scratch.
