If your milk check landed on your desk this week and looked almost boring, that's not a mistake. Late-February forecasting already had analysts calling for a bullish March, pointing to competitive international prices and strong U.S. dairy exports pulling futures upward. By the time the check actually arrived, the improvement was no longer a forecast — it was a number on the page. After two years of members telling us they'd settle for "predictable," predictable finally showed up.

None of this means the squeeze is over. Income-over-feed-cost margins were still expected to run thinner in 2026 than they did in 2025, even with the improvement, and members who locked in feed contracts during the worst of last year's run-up are only now working through them. But the direction has changed, and direction matters as much as level when you're the one writing next quarter's budget. A cost structure that's easing, even slowly, is a different planning problem than one that's still climbing.

## What actually moved

The turnaround analysts flagged in late February came from two places at once: exports held up better than expected, and government dairy product purchases added a floor under prices that hadn't been there earlier in the winter. Neither of those is something a single creamery can influence, which is exactly why we track it centrally and put it in front of members rather than leaving everyone to piece it together from a dozen trade newsletters. One analyst's note put it plainly — this was a "dramatic turnaround" from projections issued barely a month before, when the same forecasters were still bracing for margins below $9 per hundredweight for most of the year.

For members setting spring pricing, the read we'd offer is: don't chase last year's caution into this year's recovery. A market that's stabilizing after a hard stretch is exactly the moment producers under-price out of habit, because the instinct built up over two lean years doesn't switch off the week the numbers turn. If your costs have genuinely come down, say so to your buyers. But don't assume the whole case needs a discount to move — a market that's finding its footing rewards sellers who hold a line as much as it punishes ones who don't notice conditions changed at all.

## What we're watching next

The federal risk-management calendar for March flags the usual checkpoints — Dairy Margin Coverage enrollment windows, the next federal outlook release, and the futures dates that tend to move the most on new export data. We'll keep tracking those in the market notes members get through the portal, and we'd rather flag a forecast that doesn't hold than stay quiet and let you get caught flat.

If you make pricing decisions off these numbers, the federation's cost accounting materials walk through how to translate a milk-price move into a per-wheel cost adjustment without overcorrecting. Reach out to the Membership & Outreach Committee if you'd like a copy, or if you just want someone to sanity-check your spring price sheet before you send it out.
