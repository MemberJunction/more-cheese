The Annual Conference is behind us, the market season is winding down, and members are starting to ask what the fall calendar holds. Two in-person workshops close out the year: **Workshop: Curd Food Safety** in Boise on Tuesday, October 27, and **Workshop: Hollow Export Readiness** in Bra on Thursday, November 19. We are mentioning them now because workshops are small rooms, and small rooms fill.

If you were on a waitlist for something earlier this year, you already know how that goes.

## Who tends to get the most out of a workshop

A workshop is not a conference with fewer people. Nobody is walking the floor with a lanyard, and there is no keynote to sit back and enjoy. You are expected to bring a problem, and the room works on it with you.

The members who tell us they got the most out of a workshop were usually in one of three situations:

- **Someone new to a cave or a vat.** A member who took over a family creamery this summer told us she wanted "a room full of people who will say *that happened to me too*." That is what a small room is good at.
- **Someone with a specific decision on the table.** A style to add, a style to drop, a longer aging window to try.
- **Someone mentoring.** Several of you have an apprentice who has outgrown the shop and needs a wider set of voices.

Small makers are organizing this way elsewhere too. A cross-border training project launched in June 2026 is built around networking and knowledge exchange among small cheese makers, which tells us the instinct to learn side by side is widespread.

If none of those fits, a course cohort may suit you better. The Education Committee can point you to one.

Aging is on a lot of minds this year. Members have told us they are weighing longer maturation for some styles, and the federation's attention has turned toward modernizing raw-milk aging rules. For background, the long-standing U.S. rule requires that raw-milk cheese age at least 60 days before sale. Researchers have questioned for years whether aging time is the right control by itself, and some countries rely on animal health, milk handling and microbiological limits instead. Any change to the rule would affect cash flow, inventory and shelf life, which is why makers have such strong views.

We are not going to speculate about what any regulator will do, and a workshop is not a place for legal conclusions. It is a place to compare how you plan your room if a longer window ever arrives. Our course on **Maturation Planning for Small Rooms** is a good warm-up. Our **Listeria Control in Small Creameries** course is the place for the testing side of the same conversation, and we describe what the rules require there, not what is safe for your plant.

Fall is when many of you sit down with the year's books. The national margin program for dairy farms, for instance, was forecast to show a lower average margin in 2026 than in 2025, with its payments concentrated early in the year. Whatever your own situation, a workshop seat is easier to justify when you can say what question the day will answer. Bring a cave cost, a make sheet, a price list or a draft label, and ask the room to poke at it.

Also bring something to taste. Tasting a cheese beside makers who didn't make it is one of the quickest ways to learn what a customer actually notices.

## Practical notes

Details of each program and how to register will be posted on the member events page, and the Events Committee will send a reminder to members when registration opens. We don't list fees in blog posts, so please check the membership and events pages for the current information.

If you can't attend, tell us what you would have wanted to ask. We keep a running list of questions, and they shape what the next small gathering covers.

For now, put October 27 or November 19 in your calendar, and decide which of your questions deserves a room full of makers.
