The ICF Annual Conference opens in Madison, Wisconsin next Tuesday, and by now most members have their registration, their hotel, and their competition entries squared away. What has come up more than usual this year, in the calls the Events Committee has been fielding, is a logistics question we don't usually get: how do you keep a wheel of cheese in good shape on a multi-hour drive when the outside temperature is pushing past what most refrigerated transport is built to fight against comfortably.

We wrote earlier this week about what this summer's heat is doing to milk supply. The same weather is now the thing standing between a lot of members and a clean drop-off at the competition check-in table.

## What actually goes wrong in a hot van

A soft-ripened wheel that's been aging at 50–55°F does not need to reach 90°F to suffer — it needs only enough temperature swing to sweat, soften unevenly, or start a rind reaction it wasn't due for yet. A few things members who've made this drive before have told us matter more than they expected:

- **Pre-cool the vehicle, not just the cooler.** A cargo area that's been sitting in a parking lot at 95°F holds that heat for a surprisingly long time even with a good cooler inside it. Run the air conditioning for fifteen minutes before you load, not after.
- **Pack for the stop, not just the drive.** Every fuel stop and rest break is a window where a cooler lid is open and hot air gets in. Members hauling multiple entries have had good luck packing categories that travel worst — soft-ripened, fresh, anything cream-washed — closest to the lid, so they're the last thing opened to air and the first repacked.
- **Bring a second thermometer.** A cooler's built-in gauge tells you the air temperature, not the paste temperature. A quick probe check at your last stop before Madison is worth the two minutes it takes.

## What the check-in table is actually checking

Competition intake volunteers will tell you the same thing every year: a wheel that arrives visibly stressed doesn't get disqualified, but it also doesn't get judged at its best, and in a category as tight as this year's is expected to be, a soft or sweaty rind at drop-off is a disadvantage you gave yourself for free. If you're unsure whether an entry survived the trip, ask the intake volunteer for a quick look before you leave the table — they've seen enough of these to tell you honestly whether it needs a few hours to recover before Wednesday's judging.

For members who haven't made the trip before, the Competition Judge Accreditation program covers exactly this kind of handling knowledge from the other side of the table — worth a look if you're curious what the people evaluating your wheel are actually trained to notice.

Not every member is making the drive themselves. If you're routing entries through a distributor's cold chain rather than hauling them yourself, this is the week to confirm — not assume — that your shipment is actually going in refrigerated capacity and not general freight with an ice pack taped to the box. A short phone call to your logistics contact now is cheaper than a disqualified entry on Wednesday morning. Ask specifically what temperature the trailer is set to hold, and ask whether your box will be loaded near the door, where temperature swings are largest every time it opens along the route. It's also worth building in a buffer day if you can — an entry that arrives Monday afternoon has time to rest and stabilize before Tuesday's intake, while one that arrives an hour before the table closes does not, and any stress from the trip travels straight into the judging.

None of this is only about the cheese. A hot cab is hard on the person driving it too, and several members hauling a full van of entries have told us the temptation is to push straight through rather than stop, precisely because every stop is a chance for the cargo area to heat back up. That's the wrong trade to make. Plan your stops in advance, use them to check your cooler, and treat the extra twenty minutes as part of the trip rather than a delay from it.

We'll see many of you Tuesday. Drive safe, pack cold, and if the forecast holds, budget an extra stop — for the cheese and for yourself.
