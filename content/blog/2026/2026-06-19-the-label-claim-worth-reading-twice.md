A few members have asked our Membership & Outreach Committee this month whether they're required to change their labels because of the new voluntary origin-claim rule that took effect on January 1 of this year. The short answer is no — it's voluntary, exactly like the name says. The longer answer is that "voluntary" is doing a lot of work in that sentence, and it's worth ten minutes of your time to understand what the rule actually changed before you decide whether to use it.

The rule sets a stricter bar for anyone who wants to put a domestic-origin claim on a product: to use it, the item generally needs to be derived from milk sourced and processed in the country, from birth or milking through the finished product. That's a meaningful tightening from the looser standard that used to apply, and it matters most to members whose supply chain includes any imported component — a cultured starter sourced overseas, milk brought in during a seasonal shortfall, or a co-packing arrangement that touches a facility outside the country. If any part of that chain runs through an import, the claim isn't available to you, even if the cheese itself is made and aged entirely at your own facility.

## Why this is worth reading even if you don't use the claim

Here's the part that catches members off guard: even if you never intend to put the claim on your own label, your buyers might start asking about it anyway. A stricter standard tends to raise expectations across the whole shelf, and a retail buyer who's used to seeing the claim on competitors' packaging may start asking every supplier — including ones with no claim at all — to document where their milk comes from. One member who sells through several regional grocers told us it's already happened to her: "Nobody asked me this two years ago. Now it's on the vendor questionnaire every single renewal." Knowing the rule, even if you opt out of using it, means you can answer that question with confidence instead of scrambling.

Layered on top of that is a separate front-of-pack nutrition labeling proposal still working through the federal rulemaking process, with an effective date and compliance runway that keep shifting as the agency finalizes design specifics. It hasn't reached final form yet, but it's the kind of thing worth watching now rather than reacting to later, since packaging redesigns and print runs take lead time most small producers don't have to spare on short notice.

Producers already using the claim should also note that the standard now expects documentation on hand and available within a day of a regulator's request, not filed away somewhere for an eventual audit. That's a shorter turnaround than a lot of small operations are set up for, so if you're making the claim, it's worth confirming now — before anyone asks — that you could actually produce the paperwork on short notice.

We'll keep tracking both of these through the Membership & Outreach Committee and flag anything that moves from proposal to final rule. If your supply chain touches an import at any point and you're unsure whether that disqualifies you from the origin claim, that's exactly the kind of question worth bringing to the committee before you print new labels rather than after.
