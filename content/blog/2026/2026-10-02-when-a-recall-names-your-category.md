This week our inboxes filled with the same question, phrased a dozen ways: "Is this about us?" A federal food-safety regulator and the national public-health agency are investigating an *E. coli* O26 outbreak linked to raw-milk cheese from a single producer, and the cheese has been recalled nationwide. As of the latest federal update, 13 people in nine states are confirmed ill. Eight were hospitalized and three developed a serious kidney complication. More than half of the patients are small children.

We are not going to name the producer here, and we are not going to speculate about what went wrong inside its plant. The investigation is ongoing and the details belong to the regulators. What we can do is talk about what a recall like this means for the rest of you.

## What the outbreak does and does not tell us

Here is what the public notices say. Genetic testing shows the bacteria from sick people are closely related, which points to a common source. Illnesses began in July and ran through late August, and public-health officials caution that the true count is likely higher, because mild cases are often never tested and recent cases take weeks to appear in reports. The recalled cheeses are shelf-stable-style products with a long life, so officials are asking people to check their refrigerators and freezers.

Here is what the notices do not say: anything about whether the cheese was aged, or how. That matters, because the instinct in the cheese trade is to read an outbreak through the lens of the aging rule. Without those facts, reading anything into it is guesswork. We would rather you wait for the investigation than rebuild your make process around a rumor.

What we do know is that the public does not distinguish between producers. A headline that says "raw milk cheese" lands on every counter and every farmers' market table.

"A customer asked me on Saturday whether our cheese was 'one of those,'" a farmstead maker in the Mountain West told us. "I said we make it differently, and she said, 'How would I know?' I didn't have a good answer. I do now." She has since printed a one-page summary of her testing program for the stand.

## Where the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate fits

Members often think of this credential as a hiring checkbox. It is more useful as a way of building a document you can show people. The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate walks you through the structure of a hazard analysis: identifying where a hazard could enter your make, choosing the points that control it, setting limits, monitoring them, and writing down what you do when a limit is missed.

That is the same exercise a customer, an auditor or a worried parent is asking you to have done. For raw-milk makers in particular, it forces precise answers to the questions that matter:

- Which pathogens does your plan name, and which test do you run for each?
- What is your milk-supply control, and how do you verify it?
- Who reviews your records, and how often?
- If a test result is positive, what do you do with the wheels in the cave, and in what order?

The certificate is a way of practicing those answers before you need them. It does not make a cheese safe, and nothing we publish should be read as telling you your product is. It gives you a structure and a vocabulary. Your own testing, your supplier controls and your regulator's requirements remain the standard.

**What to do this week.**

1. **Check your own stock and your distributors' lists** against the recall notice, especially if you repackage or cut.
2. **Pull your hazard analysis and read it cold.** If you do not have one, treat that as the finding.
3. **Talk to your team.** Everyone on the floor should be able to say what a deviation looks like and who they tell.
4. **Look at the Listeria Control in Small Creameries course** if your environmental swab program is more than a year old. The organism is different, but the habits of a good monitoring plan are the same.

The Food Safety Committee is collecting member questions on the outbreak and will share what it can in a member call once more is public. Send yours to the committee through your member portal.

We do not take any pleasure in writing posts like this one. If your plan is solid, a week of reviewing it will cost you an afternoon. If it is not, this is a far better week to learn that than the next.
