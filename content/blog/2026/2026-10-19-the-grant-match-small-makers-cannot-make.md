Some policy fights are about a headline number. This one is about a footnote in a funding notice, and it could matter more to small makers than almost anything else we have followed this year.

Here is the short version. A federal grant program created in the 2018 farm bill helps small dairy farms and processors upgrade facilities, diversify products and reach new markets. It is delivered through four regional initiatives, it has never required a match, and it has reached more than 900 businesses in over 40 states. This year's round carries about $12.65 million. In September the federal agriculture department's new funding notice added something it had never contained: a 25 percent cost share.

## Why a match is a wall, not a hurdle

The match is not charged to you. It falls on the four initiatives that administer the grants, and those initiatives are housed at public universities, a state agriculture department and a university and industry partnership. By the coalition's own account, they have no pool of money to match with, and they do not keep the dollars they award.

Industry groups say that without a fix, the requirement will end the program as it stands. That is a strong claim, so we checked it against how the program has been used. Past awards have paid for wastewater treatment, labeling work, equipment and expansion. These are the unglamorous line items that decide whether a ten-person creamery can stay open when a bigger neighbor consolidates.

A farmstead maker in the upper Midwest put it to us plainly last week. "We used our award for the drain and the cooler," she said. "Nobody puts a drain on a brochure. We also could not have paid for it any other way."

The program also has some history. It was frozen in 2025 and then restored after the dairy community pushed back. Members who remember that stretch will recognize the pattern: the money survives, then the conditions attached to it quietly change.

## What is being asked

On October 12, a coalition of 28 national, regional and state organizations wrote to the head of the agriculture department. They are asking for three things:

- Drop the new 25 percent cost share.
- Reissue the current funding notice without a required match.
- Extend the application deadline by 60 days. The deadline in the letter is December 1, so please check the notice itself before you plan around any date.

A senator from a major dairy state has also written to demand a reversal. We have not seen a response from the department, and we will not guess at one.

We want to be careful about our own role. The federation is not one of the 28 signers, and we are not claiming credit for anyone else's letter. What we can do is tell you accurately what is at stake and gather the member evidence that makes letters like this one persuasive.

If you have ever applied for one of these grants, whether you won or not, we would like to hear three things:

1. What the money paid for, in plain terms: a vat, a drain, a label redesign, a cave build-out.
2. Whether you would have done the project without it, and on what timeline.
3. Whether you were planning an application this round, and what the match requirement does to that plan.

Send your answers to the Membership & Outreach Committee. We will strip anything identifying before we share it, and we will tell you before any of it goes anywhere. The Standards Committee will fold the pattern into the federation's own comments on funding rules.

If you are mid-application, do not wait on this fight to resolve. Gather your quotes, your project timeline and your records now, and treat the December 1 date as real until the notice changes. If the requirement is dropped, you will already be done. If it is not, you will know where you stand.
