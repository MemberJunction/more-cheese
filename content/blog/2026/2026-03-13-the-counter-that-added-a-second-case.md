Not every member story this stretch is about a creamery holding on through a hard year. Some are about the opposite problem, which is its own kind of hard: a shop that grew faster than its floor plan. A member who runs a specialty cheese counter in a small Midwestern grocery told us this week that she spent January and February doing something she never expected to be doing this early in her tenure — arguing with her store manager for a second case.

She got it. The counter now runs two full-size cases instead of one, split roughly between an everyday lineup and a rotating "discovery" section that changes every few weeks. That second case is a bet, and she's the first to say so. Specialty cheese sales have been climbing nationally, and buyers have told us the growth is concentrated in cheeses that give a shopper a reason to stop and read the label — a flavor combination, a story, something more than a familiar block. A rotating case is built for exactly that kind of browsing, but it also means more product that can sit unsold if nobody bites.

## What made the bet less risky

What she credits with making the decision easier wasn't a gut feeling about the market — it was two years of coursework she'd mostly finished before she needed it. She'd worked through her Certified Cheesemonger credential slowly, one module at a time between shifts, and admits she nearly let it lapse twice when the store got busy. What kept her going, she said, was less about the certificate itself and more about the habit it built: tasting deliberately, writing down what worked and what didn't, treating a case reset as data instead of guesswork.

That habit is what let her propose the second case with actual numbers behind it instead of just enthusiasm. She'd been tracking which SKUs turned over fastest in her existing case for months, which meant she could tell her store manager which categories were underserved rather than just asking for more shelf space on faith. "I wouldn't have known what to measure before the credential," she told us. "I would've just asked for the case and hoped."

## The part that isn't finished yet

She's candid that the second case isn't a guaranteed win. Sell-through on the rotating section has been uneven — strong some weeks, flat others — and she's still learning how far out to plan a discovery rotation versus how much to leave open for what's moving. That's the honest version of a growth story: not a straight line, but a manager with better tools for reading the line she's actually on.

For members thinking about a similar expansion, her advice is to build the tracking habit before you build the case, not after. If you're working toward Certified Cheesemonger yourself and finding the pace hard to sustain, the Education Committee can connect you with a study cohort — sometimes the thing that gets you through the slow modules is knowing someone else is stuck on the same one.
