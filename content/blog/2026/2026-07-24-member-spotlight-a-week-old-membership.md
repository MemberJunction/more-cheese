Most of our new members have a few months of newsletters and course emails behind them before they show up to their first conference. Nia Thompson had eleven days. She joined the federation on July 17 and was on the Madison trade floor the following Tuesday, which made her one of the newest members most attendees talked to all week — and gave us an easy excuse to ask what that felt like from the inside.

Thompson is a junior cheesemonger at a small retail shop in Brooklyn, and she told us the decision to join wasn't really planned around the conference at all. "My manager mentioned the federation had a member rate on conference registration, and the deadline for that was closing, so I joined mostly to get the discount," she said. "I didn't think much about what membership actually meant until I was already on the plane."

## What eleven days looks like from the inside

Her first exposure to us, in order, was a welcome email, a portal login she didn't have time to explore, and then a trade floor with several hundred people who all seemed to already know each other. She told us the judging observation sessions we previewed and recapped this month were what made the week click for her. "I sat in on one where a judge explained why a wheel that looked perfect to me got marked down for something in the paste I couldn't have named. That was the first time membership felt like it was actually going to make me better at my job, not just a line on an expense report."

She's candid that she skipped most of the governance and advocacy conversations happening around her that week — Standards Committee sessions, comment-letter discussions, the kind of institutional memory that longtime members take for granted. "I have no idea what most of the acronyms people were using meant," she said. "But nobody made me feel behind for not knowing. A few people just explained things without making it weird."

## What she's taking home to her case

Thompson's counter has been leaning into the kind of format that travels well on a lunch order or a shared plate rather than a solitary wedge — she mentioned stretch-and-melt items and Mediterranean-adjacent pairings as two things her regulars keep asking for, which tracks with what industry trend-watchers have been flagging for this year's specialty case more broadly. It's also useful context for a brand-new member: the counter conversations she's having in Brooklyn are the same ones the federation's own market data points toward, even if she didn't know that connection existed two weeks ago.

She's planning to start with the Certified Cheesemonger credential once she's caught up on sleep, having heard several members mention it on the floor this week. She's also holding onto something less concrete: a sense that the counter work she does every day connects to a much larger set of people thinking through the same problems, on the same timeline, from very different sides of the case.

"I assumed this would be mostly a discount card," she told us on her way to the airport. "It's turning out to be more like finding out there's a whole trade behind the thing I already do every shift."

For any member who joined recently and is wondering whether a first conference is worth the trip before you know anyone there, Thompson's answer, unprompted, was that she'd do it again even having gone in cold. If that's you, the Membership & Outreach table near the entrance exists partly for exactly this — a two-minute orientation before you're thrown into the deep end of your own first trade floor.
