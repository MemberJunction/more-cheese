Two days after this week's new tariffs on cross-border dairy trade took effect, we started hearing from members that the paperwork behind a shipment had suddenly become the whole conversation. Members who've shipped the same wheels across the same border for years are now getting questions from customs brokers they've never heard before. A commercial invoice that used to be a rubber stamp is now the document someone is reading line by line.

This is the moment to treat your export paperwork like the technical document it actually is, not the administrative afterthought it's been able to get away with being. Export Documentation Essentials, one of our standing courses, exists for exactly this kind of week — not because the rules changed overnight, but because the rules that were always there just started getting enforced with a lot more attention.

## The documents that matter more than usual right now

A commercial invoice needs to describe your product the way the harmonized system actually classifies it, not the way your label describes it to a customer. "Aged raw-milk cheddar, 6 months" is a lovely thing to put on a wheel; it is not, on its own, enough for a customs officer trying to match your shipment to a tariff code. Pair your invoice language with the correct harmonized system code, and make sure that code is the same one on your certificate of origin and your packing list — a mismatch across those three documents is one of the fastest ways to get a shipment held at the border for review, and a held shipment on perishable cheese is a cost you can't get back.

Your certificate of origin deserves particular attention this month if you're relying on any preferential duty treatment under an existing trade agreement. Preferential rates typically require the certificate to state clearly how the product qualifies — where the milk came from, where it was processed, and whether it meets the agreement's specific rules of origin — and a certificate that's technically true but vague on those points can get treated as though it doesn't qualify at all. If a distributor or buyer on the other side of the border is asking you for updated documentation this month, that's very likely why.

If you export cheese made with any animal-derived cultures or rennet, don't forget the health or veterinary certification that some destinations require alongside your commercial paperwork — it's a separate document from your certificate of origin, and it's easy to let it lapse when nobody's asked about it in a while. Check the renewal date on file, not just whether you remember having one at some point.

## Where to start

Export Documentation Essentials walks through building a document packet that holds up under exactly this kind of scrutiny — invoice language, harmonized system coding, certificates of origin, and the country-specific extras that some destinations require. If you export at all, even occasionally, this is a good month to sit through it or send whoever in your shop handles your shipping paperwork. A confused customs officer costs you more than a confusing invoice ever saves you.

If you're not sure whether your current paperwork would hold up, pull your last shipment's full document packet and compare it line by line against what the course covers. Most members find the gap in about ten minutes.
