We wrote in February about the additional traceability rule members were watching move through committee, the one requiring extra recordkeeping on certain high-risk foods so a contamination event can be traced back through the supply chain faster. Two developments since then are worth an update, because they land differently depending on what you make.

First, the food-safety regulator carved cottage cheese made under the interstate milk shipment grade "A" pasteurized milk ordinance out of the rule entirely, effective this quarter. Second, and more relevant to most of our members, the general compliance deadline for the rest of the rule has moved out to July 2028, a roughly thirty-month extension from the date most of us had been planning around. If you make cottage cheese specifically, the exemption means the rule simply does not apply to you. If you make almost anything else on our roster, you now have considerably more runway than you thought you did.

## Why cottage cheese got a different answer

The exemption is narrower than it might sound. It applies to cottage cheese produced under a specific pasteurized-milk grading standard that already carries its own inspection and recordkeeping regime, and the regulator's reasoning was that layering the new traceability paperwork on top of an already-covered product wasn't adding meaningful safety value. It is not a signal that cottage cheese is safer than other fresh dairy products, and it is not a template other categories should expect to receive. Our Food Safety Committee has fielded a few questions this spring from members hoping a similar carve-out might extend to other fresh, short-aged cheeses, and the honest answer is that nothing in the record suggests that is coming.

The bigger and more useful news for most members is the extended timeline. Regulators said even entities well-positioned to meet the original date had raised concerns, largely because compliance depends on getting accurate data from supply chain partners who were not equally ready. That is a familiar problem for a lot of our smaller members, who had been putting off the lot-tracking and recordkeeping systems the rule requires because the deadline felt impossible to hit on top of everything else this spring.

Extra runway is only useful if you use it, and "we have until 2028" has a way of becoming "we'll start in 2027" without anyone deciding that on purpose. If your operation falls under the rule's scope, this is a good season to build the recordkeeping habit in pieces rather than as a single project the month before a deadline: start logging the lot and source information the rule will eventually require, even informally, so the system is already running by the time compliance is mandatory. The Food Safety Committee keeps an updated plain-language summary of which product categories the rule actually covers, since the list is more specific than most members expect and a fresh look is worth it now that the deadline has shifted. If your cheese doesn't fall under the covered list at all, the committee can help you confirm that too, rather than building a system for a requirement that was never yours.
