For most of our members, whey has always been the part of the vat you plan around rather than the part you plan for. It leaves in a truck, it gets a modest check a few weeks later, and the real conversation stays fixed on the cheese. This spring that ordering is starting to look old-fashioned. Federal price-reporting data through May shows dry whey holding well above where it sat a year ago, and the strength has been steady enough that forecasters keep nudging the full-year Class III number upward on the back of it, not on cheese alone.

The mechanics are simple even if the market chatter around them isn't. Class III pricing bundles cheese and whey together, so a strong whey market lifts the number your milk check is built on even in a month when cheese itself is only holding steady. High-protein whey products, in particular, have been the driver — demand for concentrated whey protein has been outrunning what plants can currently produce, and that scarcity shows up as price. If you're a wholesale-scale maker selling wet whey or dry whey to a processor, that line item on your statement is no longer trivial.

## What this actually changes at your operation

If you're a small farmstead operation dumping whey to feed or spreading it, this may read like someone else's windfall, and largely it is. But the ripple reaches you too. Distributors and buyers who watch these numbers closely are recalibrating what they'll pay for milk overall, and a Class III price built partly on whey strength is still a Class III price. Several members reported on last month's margin webinar that their co-op statements showed whey premiums for the first time in years — worth asking your own buyer whether that line exists on your contract and whether you're capturing it.

For the makers who do sell whey commercially, this is a good spring to revisit that contract rather than assume last year's terms still apply. A processor buying at a premium has more room to negotiate volume and quality terms than one buying at a nuisance price. If whey protein concentrate demand holds through summer — and the forecasts we're reading suggest it might — there's a real case for renegotiating rather than renewing on autopilot.

## The number to watch

We'd rather you track the actual whey price than take our word for the trend. The federal mandatory price-reporting system publishes weekly, and it's the same data feeding the Class III formula your milk check runs on. If you buy hay, feed, or packaging in bulk, that whey line is one more input into whether this is a year to lock in a supplier contract or wait a quarter. The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course walks through exactly this kind of read-through — how a component price shows up two steps removed on your own statement — and it's worth the two evenings if margin math isn't your comfort zone.

None of this changes what's in the vat. It changes what the thing that used to leave in a truck is worth to you, and after a hard couple of years, that's a number worth having on your side for once.
