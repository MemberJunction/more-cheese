A year ago, sitting for a federation credential meant driving to a testing site, showing your ID at a desk, and working through the exam in a room full of other people's pencils. This month, for the first time, every one of our seven credentials — from the Cheese Foundations Certificate through Competition Judge Accreditation — is available on a remote-proctored basis, start to finish, from wherever you happen to be.

We didn't plan to move this fast. Testing sites closing last year forced the question, and once we'd built the remote path for one credential we found it worked well enough to extend to all of them. It's not a downgrade from the in-person exam. It's the same question bank, the same passing standard, and a proctor watching your webcam feed in real time rather than standing at the front of a room.

## What actually changes for you

The proctoring is the part people ask about most, so let's be direct. You'll need a stable connection, a quiet room, and a device with a working camera and microphone — a phone propped against a mug works fine if that's what you've got. The proctor will ask you to show your ID and pan the camera around the room once before you start, the same identity and environment check a testing site would run. During the exam, they're watching for the same things a proctor in a room would flag: a second monitor, a phone in reach, someone else in frame.

What's genuinely easier: no travel, no fixed testing-site calendar, and for most credentials a wider window of exam dates each month. A Certified Cheesemonger candidate who used to wait six weeks for the next open seat at a regional site can often test within a week now.

## Setting up your space before exam day

A few members have had to restart an exam because their setup wasn't right, and every one of those was avoidable. Test your camera and microphone the day before, not the morning of. Clear your desk of notes, second screens, and anything with writing on it — the proctor will ask you to show the surface. And pick a room with a door you can close; a kitchen table works, but only if nobody else is walking through it for the next two hours.

If you're studying toward the Advanced Affinage Certificate or the Sensory Evaluation Certificate this quarter, both have practice question sets available through your member portal, and we'd rather you spend your nerves on the cheese questions than the technology.

If your worry is a credential quietly lapsing rather than a new exam, that's a separate conversation — recertification windows and continuing-education hours still work the way they always have, remote testing doesn't touch that clock. Check your portal for your renewal date, and reach out to the Education Committee if you're not sure where you stand.

We'll keep the remote option running well past whenever in-person testing sites fully reopen. For a lot of our members — especially the ones running a one- or two-person creamery who can't easily lose a travel day — this turned out to be the better way to test all along.
