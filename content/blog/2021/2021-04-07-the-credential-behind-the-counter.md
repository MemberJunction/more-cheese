If you've been rebuilding a counter schedule this spring — bringing a part-timer back, hiring someone new to cover the hours a departed employee used to work — this is a good week to think about Certified Cheesemonger. It's the credential we hear the least about relative to how useful members say it is, probably because it doesn't sound as dramatic as an affinage or sensory track. It's not meant to. It's built for the specific, unglamorous job of standing behind a case and making good decisions all day, every day, without a manager checking every call.

Retail hiring has been a live topic for a lot of our members this spring, and not just at the counter — trade reporting has been describing foodservice and retail cheese demand as still below pre-pandemic levels but picking up steadily as dining restrictions ease, which means staffing decisions members put off last year are coming due now. Staffing a shop back up after a year of running lean takes longer than posting a job listing, and a new hire's first few weeks tend to set the tone for how much a manager has to hover. A credential that gets someone confidently answering product questions and handling cheese correctly early is worth more right now than it might have been in a stable year.

## What it actually covers

Certified Cheesemonger isn't a tasting exam dressed up in a certificate. The material is split across three areas members tell us map directly onto a real shift: product handling and storage (temperature, humidity, cut-and-wrap technique, how long a paste holds once it's exposed to air), customer-facing knowledge (style families, pairing logic, answering a milk-type or rennet question without guessing), and case management (rotation, waste tracking, reading a delivery for quality before it goes into the case). None of it assumes you came up through a creamery. A lot of successful candidates started in general retail or food service and learned cheese on the job, which is exactly the profile a lot of our members are hiring right now.

The exam itself is scenario-based rather than pure recall — you're more likely to be asked what you'd do with a wheel that's sweating in the case than to be asked to name a region. Members who've sat it describe it as closer to a practical skills check than a school test, which tends to suit people who are good at the work but not naturally good at studying for a test in the abstract.

If you already hold Cheese Foundations Certificate, Certified Cheesemonger is a natural next step rather than a parallel track — the two overlap enough that members tell us the second one goes faster than the first. If you're starting from nothing, Cheese Foundations is still the right place to begin; Certified Cheesemonger assumes you already have the basic vocabulary down.

It's also a credential a member can point to when a customer asks why they should trust the recommendation they're getting, which matters more when a shop is rebuilding a base of in-person regulars after a year that pushed a lot of that trust online.

Registration details and the current exam calendar are on the certifications page of the member portal. If you're deciding between credentials or want to know whether your counter experience qualifies you to sit the exam directly, our Education Committee fields exactly this kind of question and is worth a message before you commit study time to the wrong track.
