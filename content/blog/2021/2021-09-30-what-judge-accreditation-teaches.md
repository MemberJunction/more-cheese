We get a version of the same question every fall: is Competition Judge Accreditation
worth pursuing if you don't plan to judge our flagship competition every year? Usually
the person asking already holds the Sensory Evaluation Certificate and is wondering
whether the judging credential on top of it is redundant. It isn't, and the difference is
worth explaining before this year's cohort fills, especially with the wider competition
calendar starting to open back up after two years of pandemic disruption to in-person
judging.

The sensory certificate trains you to describe what's in front of you accurately —
texture, aroma, the arc of a finish. Judge accreditation assumes you can already do that
and trains something different: how to hold a consistent standard across forty entries
in an afternoon when your palate is tired and the twelfth blue-veined wheel is starting
to taste like the eleventh one did. That's a skill nobody develops from eating cheese for
pleasure, and it's the part most first-time judges underestimate. It's also, not
coincidentally, the skill that large cheese competitions are rebuilding right now as
in-person judging returns and entry volumes climb back toward pre-pandemic levels.

## The part that surprises people

The credential includes supervised panel experience for a reason. Scoring alone, on
paper, feels objective. Scoring next to three other judges, watching where your number
lands relative to theirs, is where you learn whether your standard drifts — whether you
score generously early in a flight and get stingier as fatigue sets in, or whether you're
quietly rewarding a style you personally prefer over one you don't. Most candidates
discover at least one blind spot in their first supervised panel. That's not a failure of
the process. It's the process working, and it's a lot cheaper to find that blind spot on
a practice flight than on a flight that decides someone's ribbon.

The credential also lapses faster than most of ours — two years, and only if you keep
judging. That's intentional. A scoresheet skill decays if you're not exercising it, the
same way a knife skill does. If you accredited two cycles ago and haven't sat a panel
since, you're probably due for a refresher before you're asked to judge again. We've
started reminding lapsed holders directly rather than waiting for them to notice, because
the two-year window is short enough that it's easy to miss.

## Where to start

If you're building toward this year's cohort, our *Webinar: Judging Standards 2021*
lands on November 23 and is the standard on-ramp — it walks through scoresheet
calibration before candidates move into supervised panels. You don't need to have
entered a competition yourself to take it, and several of our most reliable judges
never have; judging and making turn out to be different skills that don't always live in
the same person. If you're already accredited and haven't judged in a while, the same
webinar works as a refresher — check the certification page for where your renewal clock
currently stands, and bring a notebook. Most judges say the calibration exercise is the
part they wish they'd had before their first real panel, not after.
