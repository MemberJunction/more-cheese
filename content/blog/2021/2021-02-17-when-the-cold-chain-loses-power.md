The storm that put a large part of the country under ice this week didn't stop at the farm gate. Milk still has to be picked up, cooled, and processed on a clock, and when the power goes out at the plant instead of the barn, the milk doesn't wait. Reports out of the hardest-hit region this week described tanks being dumped because there was nowhere for that milk to go — not a shortage of supply, but a breakdown in the narrow window between the cow and the vat, solved a day too late for the milk already in the tank.

That distinction matters for how members think about their own risk. A creamery's vulnerability to a cold-chain failure usually gets framed around the cave — humidity, temperature, what happens if the compressor trips overnight. This week is a reminder that the fragile point can just as easily sit three steps upstream, at a processor or a hauler you never see, and that your make schedule depends on infrastructure you don't control and, most weeks, don't even think about.

## What we'd ask you to check

If any part of your business depends on a single processor, hauler, or cold-storage facility in a region prone to grid strain — and after this week, that's a longer list of regions than it used to be — it's worth asking that partner directly what their outage plan looks like, rather than assuming one exists. Generator capacity, backup routing, and how long they can hold raw milk before it becomes a loss are all fair questions, and a partner worth keeping will answer them plainly. If they can't, that's information too.

For members further from this week's storm, the read-across is about pricing, not politics. A regional supply shock like this one tends to show up in Class III and Class IV pricing with a lag, not immediately, so don't be surprised if the numbers for this month look calmer than the week itself felt. We'd rather members hear that from us now than assume a quiet price report means nothing happened.

There's a second, quieter effect worth naming: members who ship or receive dairy inputs across state lines this week may be dealing with logistics networks that are also absorbing disruption well outside the dairy aisle — the same storm knocked out trucking, warehousing, and retail supply chains broadly, which means a hauler that's normally reliable may simply be stretched thin rather than at fault.

## What we're watching

We'll have a fuller picture once milk-price data catches up with this week's disruption, and we'll flag it here if the numbers move in a way members should plan around. Industry coverage from earlier this week was still describing routine dairy business elsewhere in the country — new investment, new leadership, ordinary product news — a useful reminder that this kind of shock tends to be sharply regional even when the headlines feel national.

In the meantime, if your creamery has its own outage story from this stretch of weather — good or bad — the Membership & Outreach Committee would like to hear it. Those accounts are what shape what we ask processors and haulers to plan for next time, and they're more useful to us than anything we could reconstruct after the fact from a spreadsheet.
