A year into a very different way of working, a lot of members are rethinking what's on their resume, and the Certified Cheesemonger credential has come up more than once in committee calls this month. It makes sense. Counter staff have been laid off, rehired, moved to curbside pickup, moved back, and asked to run online orders none of them were trained for. A credential that proves knowledge independent of any one job is worth more when the jobs themselves keep shifting under people.

Certified Cheesemonger is built around what a working counter person actually needs: cheese families and styles, proper storage and handling, pairing logic, and enough sales and service knowledge to guide a customer who doesn't know what they want yet. It doesn't require a culinary degree or years behind a specific brand of counter. It requires documented hours on a cheese counter or in a comparable role, and it requires passing the exam.

## What's changed, and what hasn't

The content of the exam hasn't moved. What has moved, for members who've asked, is delivery: several of our members sat a proctored version from home rather than traveling to a testing site last year, which lowered the barrier for anyone without travel budget or childcare backup. We expect a similar option to remain available this year, and we'll post exact dates and formats once the current cohort's schedule is set.

What also hasn't changed is that the credential travels. If your shop closes, if you move states, if you switch from a specialty grocer to opening your own stand, Certified Cheesemonger is still yours. Several members who earned it as counter staff have told us it made the difference in getting hired straight into a buying or department-lead role somewhere else, because it's a signal a new employer doesn't have to take on faith.

## Getting ready without a classroom

Studying for a credential exam over video calls and self-paced modules is a different discipline than studying in a room with other people, and we hear that from candidates every cohort. Pair up with another member working toward the same exam if you can — a standing weekly call to quiz each other on cheese families or storage temperatures does more than another hour alone with flashcards. Our discussion board for exam candidates is also open to anyone enrolled, and current CCP holders check in there regularly to answer specific questions.

If a shrinking counter staff has you covering more of the shop than usual, know that credential hours can often be documented from tasks you're already doing — receiving, storage, customer service — not just time spent literally slicing wheels. Talk to the Education Committee before you assume your hours don't count; more of them count than most candidates expect.

If you've been putting off registering because the last year didn't feel like the right time to add anything to your plate, this spring's cohort is a reasonable place to restart. Check the certification page for the enrollment window and exam dates once they're posted.
