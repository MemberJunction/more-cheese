Tomorrow is the ICF Annual Conference 2021, and for the second year in a row there is no parking lot to find, no name badge to laminate, and no hotel breakfast to skip. It's fully virtual again, and if that sentence lands with a shrug rather than a sigh, that's a sign of how far we've all come since last summer.

Last year's version was assembled in about six weeks flat, on a platform none of us had used before, with sessions that ran long because nobody had figured out how to mute a room of two hundred people gracefully. This year's is built on what we learned from that scramble, and it shows in three specific changes. We've been reading your feedback forms since last August, and this agenda is our answer to most of what showed up in them.

We know some of you are still weighing whether to log in at all. A conference you attend from your own kitchen table is a different commitment than one you fly to — there's no sunk cost of a plane ticket pulling you into the room, which means the content has to earn your afternoon on its own. That's the bar we tried to clear this year, and it's worth walking through exactly how.

## What's different this time

We cut the concurrent tracks down from four to two. Members told us after last year's conference that having four things happening at once meant most people watched none of them live and caught up on recordings instead, which defeats the point of a conference. Two tracks means a real choice instead of a menu you can't finish.

We also moved from a single afternoon of scattered live chat to a structured activity stream, with a poll built into every session and a five-minute open floor after each one instead of at the very end of the day. If you attended last year and remember typing a question into a box that nobody addressed, that's the specific thing this is meant to fix.

And the schedule itself is shorter. Last year ran nine hours with a lunch break nobody actually took. This year's agenda is five hours across two blocks, with a longer midday gap, because a full workday of video calls is a full workday of video calls no matter how good the content is.

We also asked ourselves a harder question this year: which parts of an in-person conference are worth trying to fake, and which parts should we just let go of for now. The trade floor browsing, the hallway run-ins, the informal comparing of wheels over a shared table — those don't survive a video call, and we stopped pretending they would. What we kept and sharpened instead were the parts that actually translate: the market outlook, the committee updates, and a chance to ask a direct question of the people running your federation.

## What's the same, on purpose

The keynote is still a market outlook, the awards session is still live rather than pre-recorded (acceptance speeches included, rind jokes not guaranteed but likely), and the Membership & Outreach Committee is still running its open office hours block at the end of the day for anyone who wants to talk through a renewal question, a chapter idea, or just say hello to a face they've only seen in a thumbnail for eighteen months.

If you haven't registered, there's still time before the opening session tomorrow. And if you're one of the members finishing a credential this month, bring your certificate to the closing session — we're reading names live this year instead of burying them in a slide.
