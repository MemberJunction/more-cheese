Mark July 20 on your calendar: the ICF Annual Conference 2021 is set, and it will be virtual again this year. If you attended last year's version, you already know what that means logistically. If you're newer to the federation, or if last year was too chaotic to make room for a conference on top of everything else, here's the short version of what's changed and what hasn't.

What hasn't changed is the core of the program: the year's competition results, updates from all six committees, and a slate of sessions built around what members have actually been asking about. What's different this year is that we're not treating "virtual" as a stopgap anymore. Plenty of associations spent last year trying to reproduce an in-person conference on a screen and finding that it just doesn't translate — a keynote works fine on video, a hallway conversation mostly doesn't. This year's schedule leans into what the format is actually good at: smaller breakout sessions capped at a size where people talk, recorded tracks you can watch on your own time if a live slot conflicts with your creamery's schedule, and a few live Q&A blocks built around specific topics rather than one long open mic.

## Why we're not rushing back to a hotel ballroom

We've heard from some members, mostly larger operations with a travel budget, that they'd prefer to be back in a room together. We take that seriously, and we're not ruling it out for future years. But the pattern showing up across a lot of industries this year is worth naming: attendance from smaller members — the one- and two-person creameries, the ones for whom a flight and three hotel nights is a real cost — tends to go up, not down, when a conference goes virtual. Our own registration numbers from last year bore that out. A conference that's easier to get to is a conference more of you actually attend, and that matters more to us right now than a ballroom.

There's also a practical reason we're planning this way rather than gambling on a hybrid format for July: running a good in-person track and a good virtual track at the same time is genuinely hard. Associations who tried it in the last year have generally found that a half-hearted hybrid event serves neither audience well — the in-person crowd feels like an afterthought to the cameras, and the virtual crowd feels like they're watching someone else's event through a keyhole. We'd rather run one format well than two formats poorly, and this year that means virtual, done properly.

## What to do between now and July 20

Registration opens next month; watch your inbox and the events page. If you've never attended an ICF conference before, the smaller breakout format is a good place to start rather than the biggest headline session — you'll get more out of a room of twenty people working through a real problem than a panel of ten talking at three hundred. And if you're weighing whether to pursue our Competition Judge Accreditation or Sensory Evaluation Certificate, the conference always includes a session walking through the credential path that's worth sitting in on before you commit to the coursework.

One more thing worth planning around now: if your creamery has been sitting out our courses this spring because your schedule was too unpredictable, a virtual conference is a low-cost way to test whether an online format works for how you learn, before you commit real hours to a full certificate program. We'll have more specifics — full session list, registration link, the usual — in the weeks ahead. For now, just hold the date.
