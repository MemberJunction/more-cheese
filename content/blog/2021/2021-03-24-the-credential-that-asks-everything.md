Of the seven credentials we administer, Certified Cheese Professional, or CCP, is the one members ask about most, and the one they're most likely to underestimate going in. It isn't a specialty exam. It's a survey exam — production, styles, service, sales, food safety, a little bit of history — and the breadth is the point.

A member who sat the exam last month put it this way in a note back to us: "I know cheese. I did not know I needed to know tariffs." She passed, but the studying rearranged her sense of what her job actually is, and she's not the first person to tell us that.

## What the exam covers, and what it doesn't

CCP tests working knowledge across the categories a working cheese professional touches: how the major style families are made and why that matters for storage and cutting; sensory vocabulary you can actually use with a customer; basic food-safety and handling standards; and enough of the business side — sourcing, pricing structure, a little regulatory context — that you can hold a real conversation with a distributor or a shop owner. It does not test recipe development or make-day technique in depth; that's what our Advanced Affinage Certificate and hands-on workshops are for, and members who want that side of the craft should look there first.

If your day job is behind a case rather than at a vat, CCP is usually the right first advanced credential, ahead of anything make-focused. If you're coming from production and want the case-facing side, it's still worth it — several members have told us it's the first time they had language for what a good monger is doing when they talk you into a wedge instead of the one you walked in planning to buy.

Eligibility runs on hours in the industry rather than a fixed prerequisite course, so the barrier to sitting the exam is mostly experience, not paperwork. That's meant a wider range of members qualify to sit it than they usually assume.

## How members actually study for it

The two resources we hear about most from members who passed on a first attempt: our Sensory Evaluation Certificate coursework, taken first, and then a study group. CCP doesn't have a required prerequisite, but the sensory credential builds a vocabulary base that makes the tasting-adjacent exam sections much less of a guessing game. A study group matters for a more practical reason — a lot of the exam rewards the kind of pattern recognition that comes from talking through style families out loud with other people who've handled them, not from reading alone. Some members supplement that with hands-on time at a working creamery when they can arrange it; one told us book study only got her so far, and a few days actually turning wheels taught her more about texture than any flashcard set.

If you're weighing whether to start this spring, the honest answer is that virtual prep has made this more approachable than it used to be, not less — you can join a study group with members you'd never have met in person, spread across three time zones, which is exactly the kind of small silver lining we keep finding in an otherwise hard year.

If you're ready to register, the Education Committee can walk you through timing and study materials — reach out through your member portal and ask for the CCP packet. If you're not sure this is the right credential for your role, that's also a good first email to send us; we'd rather point you at the right one than have you study for six months and find out halfway through that you needed a different exam entirely.
