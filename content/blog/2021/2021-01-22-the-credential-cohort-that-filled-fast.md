If you tried to register for a Certified Cheese Professional (CCP) cohort this month and found it already full, you weren't imagining it. Enrollment in our credential programs picked up sharply once everything moved online last year, and that pace hasn't slowed with the new year — a fair number of members are telling us a slower winter, spent mostly indoors, turned out to be the first real stretch of time they've had for a credential they'd been putting off since before 2020.

## What the CCP actually tests

The CCP sits a level above our entry-point Cheese Foundations Certificate, and it's built for people who already handle cheese for a living — counter staff, buyers, affineurs — and want something that verifies broader working knowledge rather than a single specialty. The exam covers production methods across styles, storage and handling standards, pairing and service knowledge, and enough regulatory literacy to know when a labeling or safety question needs to go to someone else. It is not a sensory-only credential and it is not a substitute for the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate if your role touches plant safety directly — those two get confused often enough that we'll say it plainly here.

What's changed isn't the content, it's the format. Live remote proctoring moved to a virtual setup last spring out of necessity, and enough members told us they preferred not traveling for a written exam that we've kept it that way. Testing services across a number of professional fields made the same switch around the same time, for the same reason: a candidate connects from their own device under supervision rather than driving to a testing center, which cuts a full travel day out of sitting for an exam. For someone running a two-person counter, not losing that day turns out to matter more than we expected going in.

A few members on our January calls pointed to a version of the same story: slower in-person traffic freed up hours that used to go to the counter or the floor, and a credential that had been sitting on a to-do list for two years finally got scheduled. One cheesemonger described using the quiet stretch between the holidays and spring to finally work through the CCP study guide she'd bought back in 2019. We don't love the reason business slowed down enough to make that possible, but we're glad to see members using the time this way rather than losing it.

## Deciding if it's the right next step, and getting started

If you're weighing whether the CCP is the right next step, a good gut check is what your customers or your team ask you that you can't answer cleanly yet. If it's mostly production questions, Advanced Affinage or one of our style-specific courses might serve you better first. If it's the broader "how do I talk about this case with confidence" question, the CCP is built for exactly that.

Cohorts run on a rolling basis, and while this winter's group is full, the next one opens for registration in February. The certification page on the member portal has the full exam outline, a practice question set, and prerequisite guidance if you're not sure where you'd place. If you're already partway through Sensory Foundations or Affinage Fundamentals, ask your course coordinator about stacking credit toward the CCP rather than starting the study guide from zero.
