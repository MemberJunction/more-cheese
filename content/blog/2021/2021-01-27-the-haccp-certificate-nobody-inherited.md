A member called our office last week with a familiar problem. Their food safety plan lived almost entirely in one person's head, that person had moved on to a different job over the holidays, and the binder left behind was two revisions out of date. Nobody had done anything wrong, exactly — it is just what happens when a small creamery's food safety knowledge sits with one employee instead of in a documented, repeatable system.

We hear a version of this story a few times a year, and it is the single best argument for the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate. Not because a credential replaces a written plan, but because it gives whoever picks up the plan next a shared framework for reading it.

## What the credential actually covers

The certificate walks through hazard analysis, critical control point identification, monitoring procedures, corrective actions, verification, and recordkeeping — the seven principles that any HACCP-based plan is built on, whether it was written five years ago or five weeks ago. For members newer to the space, it is worth knowing this sits alongside, not instead of, the newer preventive-controls framework that expanded what a written food safety plan needs to address for many processors: process controls, sanitation controls, allergen controls, and supply-chain controls layered on top of the classic HACCP steps.

For a small or farmstead operation, that layering can feel like a lot. The certificate is deliberately scaled to be workable at creamery size — it does not assume a dedicated quality department, and the coursework leans on templates and worked examples rather than asking you to build a plan from a blank page. Several members who took it after exactly the kind of staffing gap described above told us the templates alone saved them a full week of reconstruction work.

## Who should take it, and when

We recommend it for anyone stepping into a food safety role for the first time, whoever currently owns your plan even if they wrote it years ago, and any second person you can free up for a few hours — because the member above is right that a plan known to only one person is a risk on its own, separate from whatever is written down. If your operation handles raw milk cheese with an extended aging period, pay close attention to the module on validating your specific hazard controls for that process; it is the section our Food Safety Committee gets the most follow-up questions about.

The credential is also not a one-time badge. Like our other certificates, it carries a renewal cycle, and it is easy for that date to slip past while you are focused on the day-to-day plan itself. If you are not sure when yours is due, check your member portal profile under Certifications — it will show your issue date and status without you needing to dig up the original certificate.

If this week's story sounds like your creamery, do not wait for the next staffing surprise to fix it. Enroll a second person now, even if your plan already looks solid on paper. The certificate is built to be worked through at your own pace, and our Food Safety Committee volunteers are available through the member portal if you want a plan review alongside the coursework rather than after it.
