A member call this week started with a question we get in some form almost every summer: has
anything changed on interstate cheese shipment lately? Not federally, no — but a state just south
of several of our members loosened its own raw-milk retail rules last month, and it's a good
excuse to walk through why the federal picture and the state picture keep moving on completely
different clocks.

## One federal rule, fifty different neighbors

The federal baseline hasn't shifted in decades: a raw-milk cheese cured at least 60 days and
labeled as unpasteurized can move across state lines, full stop, while raw fluid milk generally
cannot. That part is settled and it isn't what's in motion right now. What is in motion is the
patchwork underneath it — individual states loosening or tightening their own retail and
on-farm raw-milk rules on their own timelines, sometimes several times in a single year. A state
easing its rules on raw-milk retail sales doesn't touch the federal aged-cheese exemption at all,
but it can change what your buyer on the other end of a shipment is legally allowed to do with
the wheel once it arrives, which matters just as much to a small creamery as the shipping rule
itself. Members who sell into more than one state have told us this is the part that actually
trips them up — not the 60-day rule, which is consistent everywhere, but a receiving state's
handling and disclosure requirements that changed since the last time they checked.

That's a real cost for a member with a three-person shipping operation: tracking fifty sets of
state rules on top of one federal one isn't something most of you have staff time for, and it's
exactly the gap the federation's Membership & Outreach Committee has been trying to close by
keeping a running, member-sourced log of where a receiving state's rules have shifted recently
enough to matter. It's not a substitute for your own counsel, but it's a faster first flag than
combing fifty state health department sites yourself.

## What we're asking members to do

We keep an open petition on interstate cheese shipment that any member can add their name to,
and we've heard the fair question before: does one more signature really move anything? Not by
itself. But when a state agriculture office or a legislative committee asks whether a proposed
change would actually affect working producers, a documented list of small creameries who ship
across a specific pair of state lines carries more weight than a general association letter, and
it's the kind of specific, dated record that's hard to build after the fact. If you've run into a
state-level snag on a shipment recently — a labeling requirement you hadn't seen before, a
receiving state asking for paperwork your home state doesn't require — that detail is exactly
what belongs in the petition record, not just in a complaint to us.

Add your name and your specific state pair through the Membership & Outreach Committee, and if
you've got a recent story like the one above, send it along with the signature. We'll keep
tracking state-level movement as it happens and flag anything that starts to touch the federal
aged-cheese exemption directly, which so far this year it hasn't.
