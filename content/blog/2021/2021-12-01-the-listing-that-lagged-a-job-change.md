A member wrote in last month with a small complaint that turned out to be a useful one. She'd changed creameries back in the spring, moving from a mid-size wholesale operation to a smaller farmstead outfit closer to home, the kind of lateral move that felt routine at the time. Her federation profile, however, still listed the old employer, the old email address, and a job title she hadn't held in months. She only noticed when another member tried to reach her through the directory about a rennet question and the message bounced.

She's far from alone in changing jobs this year — a record number of workers switched employers this fall, and while most of that movement was job-to-job rather than people leaving the workforce, it means a lot of member records across a lot of associations are quietly out of date right now, not just ours. It's a small failure, and an easy one to have. Dues get paid automatically. Newsletters keep arriving at whatever address is on file. It's only when someone tries to use the directory the way it's meant to be used — to find a real person doing a specific job at a specific creamery — that the gap shows up, and by then it's usually a stranger who discovers it first, not the member.

## Why the old listing kept working, until it didn't

This is a fairly common pattern for us: a member's professional life moves forward and their record doesn't, because nothing forces the update. "I didn't think of it as urgent," she told us. "The people I actually talk to day to day knew where to find me. It didn't occur to me that someone with a technical question might go looking in the directory first." That's exactly the use case the directory exists for, and it's also the easiest one to forget about, because you rarely see it happen from the inside — the failure is invisible to the person it happened to.

The fix, in her case, took about five minutes once she sat down to do it: new employer, new title, an email address she actually checks. She's also agreed to be reachable for questions about small-batch rennet handling, which is the kind of connection the directory is supposed to make possible in the first place, and one that only works if the listing is current.

## What we're asking members to check

If you've changed employers, taken on a new role at the same creamery, or moved production sites in the past year, your member profile is worth two minutes this week. Log in, check the employer and title fields, and confirm the email address on file is one you'll actually see this month, not one you'll get around to checking after the holidays. It's a small thing, and it mostly matters to someone else — the member trying to reach you, not you.

If anything looks stuck when you try to update it, the Membership & Outreach Committee can help sort it out. A directory only does its job if the people in it are still findable, and right now is a reasonable moment to check, before a January renewal notice arrives at an address nobody's reading anymore.

It's a small housekeeping item compared to everything else on a creamery's plate this month, which is exactly why it tends to slip. Nobody schedules "update my member profile" the way they schedule a make day or a delivery route. But the fix takes less time than reading this post did, and it's the kind of thing that only costs you something when someone else needs it and can't find it — usually at the worst possible moment, like a supplier trying to confirm an order or a fellow member trying to return a favor.
