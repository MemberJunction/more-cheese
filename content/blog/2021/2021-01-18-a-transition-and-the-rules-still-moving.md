This is a strange week to be tracking a comment deadline, but the comment deadline doesn't know that. A new administration takes office this week, cabinet confirmations are moving through committee, and a lot of members have asked us whether it's even worth submitting testimony into an agency that's about to change hands. Short answer: yes, and here's why.

## The deadline that isn't waiting

The federal traceability proposal we flagged back in December — the one that puts several soft and semi-soft cheeses on an expanded recordkeeping list — has its public comment window closing this Thursday. Comment periods don't reset when an administration changes; the docket stays open, career staff keep processing it, and the record you build now is the record a future rule gets built on. If you make a cheese on that list and haven't weighed in yet, this is the week.

We know the rule itself is still a proposal, not a final requirement — nothing changes at your plant on Thursday. But the shape of the eventual rule, including how much lot-tracking detail a small creamery is actually expected to keep, is still very much being argued out in these comment files. A two-paragraph account of what your current batch records look like, and what an additional tracing requirement would cost you in time, is more useful to the docket than most of what gets submitted.

## What else is moving, and what isn't

A handful of other dockets we've been watching are effectively paused through the transition — new appointees need to be seated before certain rulemakings resume in earnest, and we'd rather tell you that plainly than pretend momentum where there isn't any. Interstate raw-milk shipment questions, in particular, are likely to sit for a few more months before they move again. We'll flag it the moment that changes.

What doesn't pause is state-level activity. A few state agriculture committees have session calendars that don't care what's happening in Washington, and at least two states are expected to take up aging-period language again this spring. If your business crosses a state line, or you're watching a specific bill, the Membership & Outreach Committee keeps a running list and can tell you where your state stands.

If you've never submitted a comment before, the advice we give every year still holds: describe what actually happens at your plant, in your own words, rather than trying to sound like a policy brief. A regulator reading a hundred form letters remembers the one paragraph that sounds like a real operation talking about a real Tuesday. Say what the proposed change would cost you in hours or dollars, not just whether you're for or against it.

The advocacy page on the member portal has the open dockets, the Thursday deadline, and a short template if you want a starting point rather than a blank page. If you're not sure whether a specific rule applies to what you make, reach out to the Standards Committee before you write anything — they can tell you in five minutes whether you're covered.
