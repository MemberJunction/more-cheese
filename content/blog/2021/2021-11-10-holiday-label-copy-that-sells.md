The specialty cheese case gets crowded in November, and not just with your own wheels. Members selling into holiday gift boxes and retail counters this month are competing against every other specialty food category for the same three seconds of a shopper's attention, and the make sheet behind your cheese isn't what wins that moment — the two lines of copy on the label or shelf talker are. Industry reporting on the specialty food category this year has kept pointing to the same thing we hear directly from members: small producers are having a real moment with shoppers who want to know who made something and why, but only if the story is actually on the package where someone can read it.

That's the gap our Storytelling & Provenance in Marketing course exists to close, and this is the right week to make time for it if you haven't already.

## Write the specific thing, not the general thing

The most common label mistake we see isn't bad writing — it's generic writing. "Handcrafted with care" describes every cheese in the case and therefore sells none of them. What sells is the specific, slightly odd detail that only your creamery could write: the cave humidity you hold for the last three weeks of aging, the herd that's grazing stockpiled fall grass instead of fresh pasture, the batch number that ties this wheel to a particular week on your calendar. Members who've taken the course tell us the exercise that changes their labels fastest is writing down five true, specific facts about a single batch before writing a single word of marketing copy. The marketing copy comes out of that list — it doesn't come first.

This matters more for holiday gift purchases than it does the rest of the year. A shopper buying cheese for themselves in July already knows what they like. A shopper buying a gift box in November is buying a story to hand to someone else, and "this is from a small creamery that does X" travels through a gift far better than "this cheese won an award" — award language reads as generic even when it's true.

## Say it once, consistently, everywhere

The other lesson from the course that's easy to skip: whatever specific story you land on, it needs to show up the same way on your label, your farmers' market sign, and your website, not three different versions written by three different people at three different times of year. A shopper who reads one story on your shelf talker in November and a different one on your website in January doesn't necessarily notice consciously, but it reads as slightly less trustworthy, and trust is most of what a holiday gift purchase is actually buying.

If you're weighing whether to squeeze the course in before the holiday rush hits full force, our answer is yes — better to fix the label copy now than to rewrite it in the middle of your busiest fulfillment week. Members who complete it walk away with a one-page story brief for their own cheese, which is a small document that keeps paying off well past this season.
