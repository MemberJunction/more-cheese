If you make or sell raw-milk cheese, you already live with the rule: unpasteurized milk can't cross state lines in a package meant for direct consumption, but cheese made from it can, provided it's aged at least 60 days and clearly labeled. That exception is the only reason a raw-milk cheddar or a farmhouse tomme can reach a buyer two states over at all. This month it's back in front of federal lawmakers, and members have been asking us what's actually on the table.

A bill introduced this session would loosen federal interference with interstate movement of raw milk products more broadly. It hasn't touched the 60-day aging standard itself, and we don't expect it to move quickly. But it's put the whole framework back in the conversation, and our Food Safety Committee is fielding a fair number of questions about what the rule is for, not just what it requires.

## What the 60 days is doing

The aging window isn't arbitrary. It's the compliance mechanism for a category of product the federal government otherwise treats as unsafe to ship raw. Held at proper cave conditions, 60 days gives pathogens like *Listeria monocytogenes* and pathogenic *E. coli* time to decline to levels regulators consider acceptable in most cheese matrices — not every one, and not on a guarantee, which is exactly why your own environmental monitoring plan still matters more than the calendar. A wheel that hits day 60 without documented humidity and temperature logs hasn't actually demonstrated anything to an inspector.

## What members are asking for

The federation isn't taking a position on the interstate bill itself — that's above what our members have asked us to advocate for as a body. What we are hearing, consistently, from small producers is narrower: clearer guidance on what documentation actually satisfies an inspector during the aging period, since practice varies noticeably state to state. That's the ask several members have raised in comment periods and in direct outreach this year, and it's the one our Membership & Outreach Committee is tracking as a live advocacy topic.

If you've navigated an inspection during the aging window — smoothly or otherwise — we want to hear about it. Specific, documented experiences are what make testimony useful; general frustration, however justified, doesn't move a rule.

We're hosting a webinar on raw milk rules this Tuesday, August 24, covering the federal framework, how it interacts with state-level variation, and what a defensible aging log actually looks like. It's free and virtual, and if you can't make the live session we'll have materials up afterward. Bring your specific state's wrinkle — that's usually where the useful discussion starts.

If you want your creamery's experience folded into what we bring to a future comment period, reach out to the Food Safety Committee directly. The rule isn't changing this month. But how clearly it's explained to the people who have to comply with it — that's something we can actually move.
