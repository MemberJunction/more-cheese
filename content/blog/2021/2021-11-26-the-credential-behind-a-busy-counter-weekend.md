If you run a counter, this is one of the heaviest weekends of your year. Between Friday's foot traffic and Saturday's small-business crowd, a lot of the people standing in front of your case this weekend have never bought cheese from you before, and some of them have never bought cheese from anyone who wasn't standing behind a supermarket deli slicer. That's exactly the gap our Certified Cheesemonger credential is built around.

We get asked fairly often what separates this credential from general product knowledge, and the honest answer is that it's less about how much you know and more about how fast you can use it in front of a customer. The written and practical components both test the same skill from different angles: can you take someone from "I don't know what I want" to a confident purchase in under two minutes, using questions instead of a lecture. A good cheesemonger doesn't recite a wheel's entire production history to a shopper who's late for a dinner party. They ask two questions — what's the occasion, what does the shopper usually reach for — and narrow from there.

## What the exam actually covers

The credential leans on four areas: style families and how to talk about them without jargon, pairing logic that holds up under real kitchen conditions rather than a tasting-room ideal, safe handling and storage at the counter, and recovery — what you do when a customer is unhappy with a cut, a recommendation, or a price. That last piece surprises people. It's not on most study guides for general cheese knowledge, but it's a meaningful share of the practical assessment, because it's a meaningful share of the actual job.

Retail demand has stayed strong headed into this holiday season, with several producers and distributors telling us gift-box and wholesale orders are running ahead of where they were a year ago. That's good news for members, but it also means more unfamiliar faces at the counter this weekend, not fewer. A credentialed cheesemonger isn't just a nicer title for a badge — it's the difference between a shopper who buys one wedge because they were told what to buy, and a shopper who leaves with a plan for the whole table and a reason to come back in January.

## If you're thinking about sitting the exam

The next scheduled sitting isn't this weekend — obviously — but members who work the floor through the holidays often tell us January is when they finally have the headspace to study, because the volume drops and the two-minute conversations they had all December are still fresh. If that's you, our Education Committee keeps a running study group thread for members preparing for Certified Cheesemonger, and it's worth joining before the rush ends rather than after, while this weekend's specific customer questions are still top of mind.

For members who already hold the credential: thank you for what you're doing this weekend. A calm, well-informed counter is doing more for this industry's reputation with new customers than almost anything else we could point to. If a shopper leaves your counter today having learned something instead of just having bought something, that's the whole credential working exactly as intended.
