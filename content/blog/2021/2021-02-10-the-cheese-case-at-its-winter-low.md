Federal dairy pricing data through January and into this month shows Class III and Class IV milk prices sitting well below where they were heading into the fall, with cheese stocks running higher than a year ago and block and barrel prices soft. If you buy milk to make cheese, that's the kind of stretch you'd normally welcome. If you're also trying to move finished wheels through a case that's still adjusting to a strange winter, it's more complicated than that.

## Two members, two very different Februaries

A cheesemaker buying milk under a formula tied to Class III is watching input costs ease for the first time in months. That's real relief after a year of unpredictable ordering and a longer make schedule for anything that needs a cave. But the same reports show cheese stocks building faster than usual, which tends to mean softer finished-goods prices even as milk gets cheaper. Lower input costs and lower finished-goods prices can cancel each other out faster than either shows up on a spreadsheet, and members who only track one side of that ledger sometimes assume the whole business got easier when only half of it did.

On the buying side, a distributor or retailer member sees a different picture entirely: softer wholesale cheese prices this month, which is good for margin if volume holds, and a genuine question about whether it will. February retail traffic is typically the quietest stretch of the year for a cheese counter, virtual tastings notwithstanding, and members have told us they're watching sell-through more closely than usual before committing to a bigger spring order. A distributor we heard from this week put it plainly: cheap milk upstream doesn't help you if the case downstream isn't moving.

## What tends to happen next, and what we'd suggest

Seasonal patterns in this market are not guarantees, but they are patterns, and both this winter's outlook reports describe prices as still searching for a floor rather than clearly turning. Domestic sales and export demand are the two levers analysts are watching most closely; if either strengthens, the reports suggest milk prices could firm up again over the following months the way they typically do heading into spring entertaining season. If that holds again this year, members locked into a Class III formula now are buying at close to the best price they'll see for a while, which is worth knowing before you decide whether to build inventory or wait it out.

If you buy on formula, this is a reasonable window to think about topping up culture and rennet stock alongside milk, since those costs don't move with the federal order. If you sell finished cheese, resist discounting a slow February case just because foot traffic is soft; a softer wholesale market gives you room to hold retail pricing rather than chase it down.
