Most members budget a fall bump for the cave's heat and humidity systems and move on. This year the bump arrived before the leaves had fully turned. Federal energy reporting has residential propane opening this heating season at its highest price point in a decade, with household propane spending projected to run roughly half again what it did last winter. Natural gas hasn't been far behind. If your maturation room runs on either, the number on your October invoice is not a blip — it's the new baseline you're planning against for the next several months.

That matters more for a cheese cave than it does for a kitchen. A make room heats for an hour a day. An aging room holds temperature and humidity around the clock, for weeks or months, on wheels that don't forgive a swing. Members running smaller rooms — the ones without a second unit to fall back on if the primary struggles — are the ones telling us this is landing hardest, because there's no way to trim the thermostat without changing what comes out of the cave at the other end.

## What's actually driving the number

The immediate cause is straightforward: global demand for propane recovered faster this year than supply did, and a larger share of U.S. production is now committed to export than in past winters, which tightens what's left for domestic heating. None of that is specific to dairy or to cheesemaking — it's the same pressure showing up in home heating bills across the country. But it arrives at an awkward moment for our members, layered on top of a milk market that the federal dairy market reporting service's most recent weekly report describes as running slightly behind last year's growth pace, even as cheese and export demand both stay firm. Firmer demand is good news on its own. It's just landing next to a heating bill nobody budgeted for at this level.

## What we'd suggest doing with the number

We're not going to tell you a magic fix exists, because it doesn't — this is a real cost increase, not a rule you can appeal or a paperwork problem you can solve. What we can offer is what other members have already tried. Several smaller creameries have told us they're locking in a fixed-price propane contract now rather than riding the spot market week to week, even at a modest premium, because the volatility itself was the harder problem to plan around. Others are re-checking door seals and insulation on aging rooms that were built for a cheaper energy era and have quietly been leaking conditioned air for years — a fix with no ongoing cost once it's done. If your make sheet or price sheet hasn't been revisited since before this season's numbers came in, our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course walks through exactly this kind of input-cost shock and how to reflect it without guessing.

If you haven't already run your winter energy number against your current wholesale pricing, now is the week to do it — a contract you sign in December is a contract you're living with in February, and the earlier you know where the gap sits, the more options you have for closing it.
