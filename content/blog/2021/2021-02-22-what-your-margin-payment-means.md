If you enrolled in the federal margin coverage program for your farm's milk, you probably noticed the same thing we did this week: the payment triggered again. The income-over-feed-cost margin has stayed under the coverage threshold for months running, and members who insured lower up the scale are seeing the checks to prove it. That number is designed to smooth a farm's feed bill against milk price. It was never designed to tell a small creamery, a farm stand, or a cheese counter what to charge. That's a separate calculation, and it's one we hear more questions about every winter than almost anything else.

A firmer national milk price is generally good news, but it doesn't automatically mean your own case should reprice itself. If you buy milk on contract, a rising Class III number moves your input cost with a lag. If you make from your own herd, the margin payment offsets feed cost directly and buys you room, not a mandate, to hold price steady through a slow season. Either way, the number in the trade press this week is a signal to check your own math, not a signal to copy.

## Three questions before you touch a price tag

Start with your actual cost per wheel or per pound, not last year's price sheet. Feed, packaging, and now shipping have all moved since you last sat down with a calculator, and a lot of members are working from numbers that are a year stale.

Second, ask what the price is actually doing for you. A direct-to-consumer price at a farm stand or online store can carry margin that a wholesale account never will, because you're not splitting it with a distributor. Several members who leaned into their own storefront and shipping over the last year have told us that's where a firmer number this winter is easiest to hold, and where a softer one is easiest to absorb.

Third, decide whether you're pricing to move volume or to protect margin on a smaller run. Those are different strategies and they call for different numbers. A cave running near capacity with a waiting list for wheels is in a different spot than one competing for shelf space against a wholesale account with more flexibility on price.
