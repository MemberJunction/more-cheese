Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets opens a new cohort this week, and we expected it to fill with members brand new to selling straight to a customer. Instead the waitlist is mostly people who already built something last year and now want to fix it. That's a good sign, and it's also exactly what this run of the course is for.

A member who runs a small mixed-milk operation put it to us plainly on a committee call last month: her order form worked fine in April when she was building it in an afternoon out of necessity, and it's been quietly costing her money every week since. Flat shipping on a box that sometimes weighs four pounds and sometimes weighs eleven. No way to hold an order for a specific ship date. A single "notes" field where customers were typing allergy information nobody downstream ever read. None of that was a mistake last spring — it was the fastest version she could build. It's not the version worth keeping.

## What the course actually covers this cohort

The syllabus has shifted since the first time it ran. Less "how do you take an order online" — most members have answered that question for themselves by now — and more on the parts that get expensive if you skip them: weight-based shipping tiers instead of flat rates, a cutoff time that actually blocks late orders from a ship date your cold pack can't cover, and a returns policy written down before your first damaged-in-transit complaint instead of improvised in the moment.

There's also a full session on packaging for shipped cheese specifically, not general e-commerce packaging. A wedge that survives a truck in January doesn't survive the same box in July, and more than one member found that out the expensive way last summer.

## The part people skip: your price list didn't move with your channel

If you built your online prices by copying your wholesale sheet and adding a flat shipping line, this is worth a second look. Shipping a single order costs more per pound than a wholesale pallet, and if your margin doesn't reflect that, you're effectively subsidizing every direct order out of the rest of the business. The course walks through a simple per-order cost breakdown — box, ice pack, label, carrier rate, your time — that most members find changes their online price by more than they expected.

If your direct-to-consumer setup has been humming along fine, the course is still useful as an audit, not a rebuild. Bring your current order form, your last quarter's shipping costs, and an actual damaged-order complaint if you've had one. The instructor builds the second half of each session around real member numbers rather than a generic case study, and that's where most of the useful correction happens.

Registration is open through your member portal this week; the cohort runs across four sessions, and past cohorts have used the shared workbook well after the course ends. If shipping cheese wasn't part of your business plan a year ago and is now half your order volume, this is worth the four weeks.
