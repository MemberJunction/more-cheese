Last spring, a member we'll call the cheesemaker behind a small upper-Midwest farmstead operation packed away her market table and never expected to need it again this soon. Her market shut down for the season within weeks of opening, and she spent the rest of the year rebuilding sales entirely around a mail-order list — a shipping cooler, a subscription spreadsheet, and a lot of trial and error figuring out which of her wheels actually traveled well in a box. This month, with national vaccination coverage climbing and markets around the country announcing a return to something closer to normal operations, she's putting the folding table back up for the first time in over a year.

She's not going back to exactly what she had before. That's the part of her story that struck us most.

## What she's keeping from the mail-order year

The subscription list isn't going away. She built it out of necessity, but it turned out to solve a problem she'd had for years without quite naming it: unpredictable weekly demand. A market table depends on who happens to walk by; a subscription list is people who already decided they want your cheese before they see it. She's planning to run both going forward — the market for new customers and walk-up sales, the list as a floor under her production planning so she isn't guessing how many wheels to make in a given week.

She's also kept the packaging discipline the shipping year forced on her. Cutting and wrapping cheese so it survives two days in a box with an ice pack turned out to make her case presentation better too — tighter portions, cleaner labels, less waste from product that sat out too long. A habit built under pressure outlasted the pressure that created it. She told us she almost feels grateful for the shipping detour, which isn't a sentence she expected to say a year ago.

## What's coming back cautiously

She's staffing the table herself for now rather than bringing back the part-time help she had before, partly out of caution and partly because a year of shipping taught her she could run leaner than she thought. Markets in other regions have been reopening this month too, several with modified operations rather than a full return to normal — capacity limits still in place even as in-person shopping resumes instead of last year's pre-order-only setups. She's watching her own market's rules closely rather than assuming the whole recovery happened everywhere at once. She's also starting with a shorter product line at the market than she used to carry — the handful of styles that move fastest — and adding the rest back in as she gets a feel for how much foot traffic has actually returned.

If any of that sounds familiar — a subscription list you built out of necessity that turned out to be worth keeping, packaging habits from a hard year that are actually improvements — we'd like to hear about it. Members like her are exactly why we keep asking what changed for good and what was only ever a stopgap.

If you're thinking about setting a market table back up this spring and want a second read on staffing or product mix, our Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding a steady stream of these questions and is worth a call before opening weekend.
