We get a version of the same question every few weeks: "I've been aging cheese for years — do I actually need a certificate for that?" The honest answer is no, not to keep doing what you're doing. But if you're trying to prove your cave practice to a wholesale buyer, a retail partner, or your own newer staff, the Advanced Affinage Certificate is built for exactly that conversation. It's also a credential we get more questions about than almost any other, so it's worth walking through plainly.

## What it covers

The credential sits above our Affinage Fundamentals course, not alongside it — you'll want that course, or equivalent hands-on experience, before you take on the certificate material. The fundamentals course is about the basics of a healthy aging environment: airflow, humidity ranges, and recognizing early spoilage. The Advanced Affinage Certificate goes further, into the judgment calls that separate someone following a checklist from someone who actually understands their cave. That means learning to read a rind that's developing unevenly, telling a humidity swing that's a real problem from one that's just a normal seasonal shift, and documenting aging decisions well enough that someone else could pick up where you left off without guessing.

That last part surprises people every time we explain it. A meaningful share of the certificate's coursework is about record-keeping — not because paperwork is glamorous, but because a buyer asking "how do you know this wheel is ready" wants an answer better than "I can just tell." Members who've completed it consistently tell us the biggest shift wasn't in their technique, it was in how they talk about their technique with people who weren't in the cave with them. That's a skill that pays off well past the exam itself, in supplier negotiations and in training whoever comes after you.

The exam itself covers both a written component and an evaluation of submitted aging logs from the candidate's own cave over a defined period, so it rewards people who've already been documenting their work rather than people who are good at studying for a test.

## Who it's actually for

- **Affineurs and cave managers** who want a credential that speaks for them when they're not in the room — useful if you're supplying a retailer or restaurant group that wants some assurance behind the label.
- **Staff stepping into aging responsibility for the first time**, especially at creameries where the person who built institutional knowledge about the cave is retiring or moving on.
- **Anyone entering a judged competition category** where aging technique is scored directly. It won't make your cheese better on its own, but the vocabulary and evaluation framework overlap with how competition judges are trained to think about a wheel.

It is not required for membership, for entering competitions, or for any regulatory purpose — nothing about food safety compliance changes based on whether you hold it.

If you've already completed Affinage Fundamentals, you can enroll directly through your member portal; the next cohort opens for registration in the new year. If you haven't taken Fundamentals yet, start there — trying to skip ahead tends to mean redoing the record-keeping module later once you hit material that assumes it. Either way, the Education Committee is a good first stop if you're not sure which track fits where you are, and they can tell you honestly whether your current record-keeping habits are already most of the way there.
