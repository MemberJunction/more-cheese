The ICF Annual Conference lands in two weeks, and the registration numbers on our end look about the way they always do this time of year: strong on the production side, a little slower on the retail and distribution side, and a rush of last-minute sign-ups we can set our watch by. If you're still deciding, decide this week — the room-block rate expires Friday, and the Saturday workshop slots (the ones that fill first) are already thinner than they were this time last year.

This is the one week where we'd rather over-communicate than under-communicate, so here's the shape of it.

## What's actually on the floor

The conference program leans harder into trade and export sessions this year than it has in a while, which tracks with what members have been telling us all spring. Export volumes softened industry-wide in the first half of 2019 — federal trade data puts overall dairy export volume down double digits year over year, driven mostly by lost demand in one major overseas market and stiff competition from European and Oceania suppliers. Cheese specifically has held up better than powders or whey, and one afternoon track is built entirely around what's working in that lane: documentation, buyer relationships, and reading a shifting tariff schedule without getting burned by it.

Alongside that, we've got the usual cave-and-craft track — washed-rind troubleshooting, a hands-on affinage clinic, and a judging-calibration session ahead of this year's competition cycle. If you did the Export Documentation Essentials course this spring, the Thursday trade panel is the natural next step; bring your questions, because the panel format this year is short remarks and long open floor.

## Why the first morning matters

Registration opens at 7:30 on day one, and the line forms before that. Badge pickup and the general session both run at capacity in that first hour, and the two things members ask us about most afterward — "how do I get on the mentor list" and "where do I sign up for a cave tour" — are both first-morning, first-come tables. If you're bringing a newer team member who's never been, walk them to those tables before anything else. It saves everyone a Thursday-afternoon scramble.

One practical note on the heat: several of you have asked whether the outdoor tasting tent is staying put given the run of record-setting temperatures the eastern half of the country logged earlier this month, with local weather services reporting some of the highest heat indices in years. The tent is staying, but we've moved the afternoon sessions under it earlier in the day and added shade panels. Dress for it, and if you're transporting product to the competition tables, use the cold room check-in rather than leaving coolers in a car in the host hotel lot.

If you're not registered yet, talk to your Events Committee contact this week, not next. Room availability at the conference-rate hotels is genuinely tight, and once that block closes we can't reopen it. If cost is the obstacle, ask about the member assistance line on the registration form — it exists precisely for weeks like this one, and nobody on our end will make you explain yourself twice.

We'll see you in two weeks. Bring a jacket for the general session room — it's the one place at this conference that's reliably over air-conditioned — and bring your questions for the trade panel. That's the session most likely to change how you plan the back half of your year.
