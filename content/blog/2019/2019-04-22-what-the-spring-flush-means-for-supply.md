Every spring, milk supply climbs as pasture greens up and calving finishes, and every spring a few of our members ask the same question: is this the year the flush overwhelms the market, or the year it barely registers? Based on the federal price data that came out this month, this year looks like the quieter version. Milk production nationally was up only a fraction of a percent from a year earlier, which is a modest flush by historical standards, not the kind that floods a cheese case with cut-rate spot milk.

That matters more than it sounds like it should, because how hard the flush hits shapes decisions our members are making right now — how much extra milk to take on for a seasonal batch, whether to lock in a summer contract or ride the spot market, how much aging-room space to free up for a run of flush-season wheels.

## Why a modest flush is actually good news for quality

A hard flush is a mixed blessing even when the volume is welcome. When production surges fast, milk composition shifts quickly too — protein and fat percentages move, and the character of the milk itself changes almost week to week as animals move from stored feed to fresh pasture. Makers who buy on contract sometimes find their make sheet needs adjusting every few days just to keep a consistent wheel through the peak. A gentler flush like this year's gives you more runway to make those adjustments without chasing a moving target.

It's also a friendlier environment for anyone planning a seasonal, pasture-driven cheese. If you've been holding off on a flush-milk batch because last year's surge came in too fast to plan around, a steadier ramp this spring is a reasonable window to try it.

"Last year I was adjusting my culture dosage every three days because the milk kept changing under me," one small-creamery member told us this week. "This spring it's actually been slow enough to plan around, which sounds boring until you've lived through the other kind of April."

## What this means for pricing conversations

None of this shows up as a dramatic number on an invoice. Regional mailbox milk prices reported this month were up slightly from March and meaningfully higher than a year ago in most reporting areas, which is a reasonable environment to be having supply conversations in — suppliers aren't under the kind of pressure that makes them difficult to negotiate with, but they're also not so flush with cheap milk that they're eager to discount a summer contract. If you're weighing whether to lock in volume now or wait, this is a decent moment to have that conversation without either side feeling cornered.

We'd still caution against reading one calm spring as a signal for the rest of the year. Flush intensity varies by region and by herd, and a dry stretch in a key growing area later this season can tighten supply again faster than anyone expects. What we're watching for over the next month is whether the modest pace holds into peak flush in May, because that's the better indicator for how your summer contracts should be priced.
