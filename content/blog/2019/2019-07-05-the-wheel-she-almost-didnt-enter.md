Entries for this month's competition close in a little over a week, and one member — a small-batch producer who's entered every year since she joined — told us this week she almost skipped her strongest wheel this time around. Not because she doubted the cheese. Because her paperwork on it was thin, and she's learned the hard way that a judge can't score what isn't documented.

"I had the wheel. I didn't have the record," she said. "And I'd rather enter a slightly less exciting cheese with a complete affinage log than my best cheese with gaps in it."

## Why the documentation matters as much as the wheel

Competition judging in her category weighs the finished cheese, but the paperwork behind it — how long it aged, what humidity and temperature it held, when it was turned or washed — is part of what tells a judging panel whether a result is repeatable or a lucky batch. A wheel that shows up with a clean, dated affinage record gives judges a fuller picture of the maker's process, not just the outcome sitting in front of them. She's watched entries get marked down not for the cheese itself but for a submission that couldn't answer basic questions about how it got there.

Her fix, going into this year, was almost embarrassingly simple: a wall calendar next to the aging room where every turn, wash, and humidity check gets logged the moment it happens, not reconstructed from memory the week before entries close. "I used to trust my memory," she told us. "My memory is fine for most things. It is not fine for six weeks of exact humidity readings."

## What almost got left off the list

The wheel in question spent longer in the cave than she'd originally planned, after she noticed the rind developing more character than usual and decided to let it ride rather than pull it on schedule. That's exactly the kind of judgment call that separates a good affineur from someone just following a timetable — but it's also the kind of deviation that needs a note attached to it, or it just looks like an inconsistency later. Once she'd gone back and reconstructed the log properly, the decision to extend the aging window became a documented strength instead of a loose end.

She's entering it. She wanted other members sitting on the fence about their own entries to hear why: "If you've got a wheel you're proud of and a logbook you're embarrassed by, fix the logbook. Don't just skip the wheel."

For anyone finalizing entries before the deadline, the Awards & Competition Committee is the fastest path to a straight answer if you're unsure whether your documentation meets the bar — better to ask this week than find out at the judging table. And if this is the year you start keeping a real affinage log instead of a mental one, our Affinage Fundamentals course walks through exactly what a usable record looks like, in enough detail that six weeks from now you won't be reconstructing anything from memory either.

We'll see plenty of you and your wheels at the conference in two weeks. Bring the paperwork.
