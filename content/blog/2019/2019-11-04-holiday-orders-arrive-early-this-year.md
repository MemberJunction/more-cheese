Members running a retail case have been telling us the same thing this week: holiday orders are landing on their desks earlier than usual, and it's not entirely clear why. Some are reading it as a sign of a genuinely strong season ahead. Others suspect their wholesale buyers are simply trying to lock in product before anything else changes, given how unsettled cheese pricing has felt for the past month.

Both readings are probably a little bit true. Wholesale market reporting through late October and early November has described cheese demand as steady overall, with production and supply both healthy enough that no one's short on product. That's a good position to be selling from, but it also means an early order isn't necessarily a signal of unusual appetite — it might just be a buyer being cautious, wanting their case filled before a slower December scramble.

## What "steady" actually means for your case

Steady demand sounds unremarkable, but it's worth sitting with for a second, because the alternative headlines this fall have mostly been about tariffs and import costs. A market that's absorbing normal seasonal volume without drama is, in a quiet way, good news — it means the parts of your business that have nothing to do with trade policy are behaving the way they're supposed to. If your holiday orders are up, it's worth checking with your buyer whether that's because they want more product or because they want it sooner, since the two call for different responses on your end.

For producer members, an early order calendar is also a scheduling gift, if you treat it as one. Knowing what a buyer wants by early December instead of finding out in the second week of that month gives you more room to plan your make schedule around it, rather than compressing your busiest production stretch into the shortest possible window. A few members who supply wholesale accounts told us they've already shifted a make or two earlier in response, specifically to avoid a crunch right before the holidays.

## Watching the spread, not just the number

One thing worth tracking if you sell into the block-and-barrel market rather than direct to retail: block and barrel prices don't always move together, and the gap between them shifts through the fall as supply and buying patterns change. That gap matters more to a wholesale supplier's margin than the headline price itself, since it changes which form of cheese is more profitable to be selling into at a given moment. If you have flexibility in how you package or sell your output, this is a good season to be checking which side of that gap you're on rather than assuming it's fixed.

None of this is a forecast — cheese markets have a way of humbling anyone who tries to call the next six weeks with confidence. But if your holiday order calendar looks different than usual this year, you're not imagining it, and you're not alone. If you want to compare notes with other members on how they're reading this season's order timing, the Membership & Outreach Committee can connect you with a producer or distributor member who's already talked this through.
