Two things are true about the export picture right now, and members keep telling us it's hard to plan around both at once. Cheese shipments abroad have posted growth for eight consecutive months, with buyers in Mexico and South Korea absorbing a growing share of domestic supply. At the same time, this was the week broader trade talks between the United States and a major trading partner broke down without a deal, and tariff lines on a wide swath of goods moved in a direction nobody in the room seemed to want.

Cheese isn't the commodity sitting in the middle of that particular fight, at least not directly. But several of you have said the uncertainty changes behavior even when a specific tariff schedule doesn't touch your product line. A buyer who's nervous about margin on one SKU gets conservative about the whole order. A distributor who's absorbed a surprise cost increase somewhere else in the truck starts renegotiating terms on everything, cheese included, whether or not that cheese was ever named in a tariff notice.

## The demand side looks genuinely good

Set the politics aside for a second and the underlying trend is a real one: cheese exports have been climbing on a broad base rather than one buyer propping up the total. If you're a wholesale producer with export-ready volume and the paperwork to back it, that's an opportunity worth taking seriously, not just a line in a trade newsletter.

The catch is capacity and lead time. Export orders tend to come in larger and further out than domestic wholesale orders, which is a different cash-flow rhythm than a lot of small and mid-size creameries are built around. A member who shipped their first export pallet last year told us the paperwork took longer than the cheesemaking, and the payment terms took some getting used to. If you're weighing a first export contract, talk to someone who's already doing it before you sign anything. The Membership & Outreach Committee can make an introduction, and several export-active members have been generous about sharing what surprised them the first time.

## What we're watching, and what we're not predicting

We don't have a crystal ball on where the broader trade situation goes from here, and we're not going to pretend we do. What we can tell you is that none of the current tariff actions are aimed at cheese specifically, and the export growth trend predates this week's news by months. That's a real distinction, even if it doesn't make the uncertainty feel any smaller from where you're standing on the production floor.

If your business has export exposure and you want a second set of eyes on a contract term, a documentation question, or how to think about lead-time risk, reach out to the office. We can't give legal or financial advice, but we can point you toward members and resources who have already been through it, and toward the parts of a first export contract that are worth slowing down for.
