We field a version of the same message every January: a member who assumed their credential
renewed automatically, or forgot the date entirely, discovering the lapse when a competition entry
form or a retail employer's compliance checklist asks for a certificate number that no longer
checks out. Every one of our seven credentials — from the entry-level Cheese Foundations
Certificate through Advanced Affinage and the judge accreditation — runs on its own renewal
cycle, and none of them renew themselves. With roughly three months left in the year, this is a
better week to check your own status than the week you actually need the paperwork.

## The renewal date isn't the certification date

The single most common mix-up we see is a member checking the date they originally earned a
credential rather than the date it's due for renewal, which for most of our certificates isn't the
same calendar mark and can drift further apart if a prior renewal happened a little early or a
little late. If you can't find your renewal date offhand, the member portal keeps it under your
credential record — not buried, just easy to have never looked at since the day you earned it.

Continuing-education requirements are the second most common gap. Several of our credentials
require a set number of education hours logged within the renewal window, not just a fee paid on
time, and those hours have to come from qualifying sources — a federation course, an approved
outside program, or documented supervised practice, depending on which credential you hold. A
member who's been taking relevant coursework all year sometimes hasn't actually logged it against
the right credential, which turns a routine renewal into a scramble to gather records in December.

If you hold the judge accreditation, a lapse carries a sharper consequence than most: an expired
accreditation can't score entries, which means a judging panel short a scorer during competition
season, at the worst possible time to find out. If you hold Certified Cheese Professional standing
or the cheesemonger credential, a lapse is less immediately disruptive but still shows up on your
public credential record, which matters if a retail employer or a wholesale account checks it as
part of doing business with you. None of this is a reason to panic about a credential that's still
months from due — it's a reason to actually look, rather than assume.

## What to do this week

Pull up your credential record in the member portal and note two things: the renewal date, and
whether you're short on logged education hours against it. If you're on track, this took you five
minutes and you're done. If you're behind, three months is enough time to close most gaps through a
federation course or two before the window closes — it is not enough time if you wait until the
last few weeks of December, when course seats fill up with everyone else who had the same idea at
the same time.

This is also the season when a lot of the broader cheese trade turns its attention to competitions
and awards, between the fall judging calendar and the international results that circulate every
October. It's a fair moment to notice that the people whose names show up on winning entries are,
almost without exception, the same people who never let their own paperwork slide — not because
the two things are causally linked, but because both come from the same habit of not putting things
off. The Education Committee can tell you exactly which upcoming courses count toward which
credential if your own record isn't clear on the mapping.
