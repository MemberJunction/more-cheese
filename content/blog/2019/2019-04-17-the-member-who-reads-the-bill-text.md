Most members know Tom Reyes' name from the bottom of an advocacy update, not from a conference stage. He owns Speltmoor Farmstead, a small raw-milk operation, and for the last several years he's been one of the federation's most active members when it comes to actually showing up — reading proposed rule language line by line and submitting written testimony on the aging and labeling dockets that affect operations like his own. This spring has been a busier season than most: statehouse activity on raw-milk sales has picked up in several states, and Reyes has been tracking more than his usual share of it.

We asked him what got him started.

## "Nobody else was going to read the whole thing"

"The first time, I did it because I was annoyed," Reyes told us. "There was a proposed rule that would've changed how aging periods got measured, and every summary I read got some detail wrong. So I just read the actual text, and once you've done that once, you notice how few people have."

That habit turned into a routine. Reyes now tracks a handful of dockets each year, most of them dealing with raw-milk aging thresholds and interstate shipment rules, and submits comments when a proposal would affect small operations like his differently than it affects larger ones. "A big commercial plant can absorb a labeling change in a way a two-person farmstead can't," he said. "Somebody needs to say that part out loud, in writing, where it counts."

He's careful to note he's not a lawyer and doesn't pretend to be one. "I'm not telling anyone what's legal. I'm telling them what a rule looks like from the vat, which is a different kind of information than a regulator usually gets in a comment file."

This year, more than a dozen states have had bills in front of their legislatures that would expand or restrict the sale and distribution of raw milk and raw-milk products, and Reyes says the pattern in the language is what makes each one worth reading closely rather than skimming a summary. One state's bill this spring simply widened an existing monthly volume cap to cover an additional species of milk — a change that sounds minor in a headline but rewrites the math for anyone planning next year's herd. "A retail-sales bill and an on-farm-sales bill can look almost identical in a headline and be completely different in what they actually require of a small producer," he said. "The herd-size exemptions matter. The volume caps matter. You can't know which side of that line you're on without reading the actual sections."

## Why this matters more than it looks like

It's easy to treat advocacy work as background noise — something the federation handles so members don't have to think about it. Reyes pushed back gently on that framing. "The federation can summarize a proposed rule and tell you the deadline to comment. It can't manufacture the specific example from a real operation that makes a regulator stop and reconsider a threshold. That part only comes from us."

Reyes doesn't attend many events — he's said before that a two-person operation doesn't leave much room for travel — but his written record with regulators is longer than almost anyone else's in the federation. It's a reminder that engagement doesn't have to look like a conference badge to matter, and that some of the federation's most useful work happens in a farm office with a highlighter, not in a hotel ballroom.

If you've ever wanted to weigh in on a proposed rule but weren't sure where to start, the advocacy page on the member portal lists open comment periods and a short guide to writing testimony that regulators actually read. Reyes' advice, for what it's worth: "Say what happened to you, specifically. Don't try to sound like a policy brief. You're not one, and that's the point. A regulator reads a hundred form letters and remembers the one paragraph that sounds like an actual person."

If your state has a raw-milk bill moving this session and you're not sure how it would affect your own operation, the Membership & Outreach Committee can point you to members like Reyes who've already been through the process of figuring that out.
