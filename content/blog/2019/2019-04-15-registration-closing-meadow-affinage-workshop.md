We're a little over five weeks out from the Workshop: Meadow Affinage session in Courtenay, and registrations have picked up enough that we want to flag the deadline now rather than in a last-minute reminder. This is a hands-on, small-group session, which means the room only holds so many people before the instruction stops being hands-on.

## What the two days actually cover

This isn't a lecture about affinage in the abstract. You'll spend most of your time in a working cave, handling wheels at different stages, and the instruction is built around the judgment calls that don't show up cleanly in a textbook: when a rind is developing normally versus when it's drifting, how to read a wheel's moisture loss by feel rather than just by scale weight, and how cave humidity swings with the season change the timeline you should be planning around. If you've ever second-guessed a call on whether to turn a wheel early or leave it another week, this is the workshop built for that exact uncertainty.

We've kept the group small on purpose. Affinage is one of those crafts where watching someone else's hands matters more than hearing the explanation, and that only works if the instructor can actually get to everyone in the room over two days rather than lecturing at a crowd.

That's part of why we keep bringing this workshop back to a working facility instead of moving it to a hotel meeting room where we could seat twice as many people. The tradeoff is a hard capacity limit, but the payoff is that everyone who attends actually gets hands-on time with a wheel, not just a seat and a slideshow. A few members who came through last year's cave-based sessions have told us they didn't fully appreciate the difference until they compared notes with someone who'd only done the webinar version of similar material — the questions you think to ask change completely once your hands are actually on a wheel.

It's worth saying that the craft side isn't the only part of this industry evolving right now. Trade coverage this month has also been tracking newer cutting and portioning equipment aimed at larger-scale aged-cheese handling — a reminder that even as the hands-on, small-batch end of this business grows, the tools for the wholesale end keep changing too. Our members mostly live on the small-batch side of that line, which is exactly why a workshop like this one stays focused on judgment and feel rather than throughput.

## A practical note on cost and timing

Because this workshop runs in a working facility rather than a rented conference space, we can't hold seats past the registration cutoff the way we sometimes can for webinars — the cave space and the instructor's schedule are both fixed commitments on our end. If you've been meaning to sign up, now is the window. Members who've been through one of our previous cave-based workshops tell us the travel is worth it, and a few have said the two days changed how they think about their own aging room entirely.

For members earning credit toward the Advanced Affinage Certificate, this workshop counts toward your practical hours — check with the Education Committee if you're not sure how many you still need before you book travel. It's a cheaper mistake to ask now than to find out after the fact that a hands-on session didn't count the way you assumed it would.

We'll follow up in June with a short write-up from the room, the way we usually do, so members who couldn't make the trip still get something useful out of it. But there's no substitute for standing next to the wheels yourself, and if your spring travel budget has any room left in it, this is a good place to spend it.

Registration details are on the events page of the member portal. If you have questions about logistics, or need help making the case to a business partner for the travel, reach out to the Events Committee — they've talked more than a few members through exactly that conversation, and most of the time the hardest part is just picking the dates.
