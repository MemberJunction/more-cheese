We got a version of the same question from three different members this month: "my Competition Judge Accreditation card says it expires this year — do I need to do anything?" The honest answer is usually yes, and it usually catches people off guard, because this is the shortest-fused credential we run. It lapses in two years, and unlike most of our certificates, it doesn't just quietly expire on a clock — it lapses faster if you haven't been active on a panel.

That design is deliberate. Judging is a perishable skill in a very literal sense. A palate that hasn't scored a flight of aged cheddars or washed-rind wheels in a while drifts, and drift is exactly what a competition can't afford. We'd rather ask members to keep their hand in than hand out a card that says "still qualified" when the honest answer is "used to be."

## Why this is on our minds this week

A national trade outlet reported this month that U.S. cheese supply is running ahead of what the country is eating right now — not, by most industry accounts, an alarming glut, but enough that a pound of cheese has to work harder to earn its shelf space than it did a few years back. When the case is crowded, the cheeses that stand out are disproportionately the ones that can point to a medal, and medals are only as credible as the panel that awarded them. Every accredited judge on a competition floor is part of what makes that medal mean something to a buyer who's never met you.

That's the case for treating accreditation as more than paperwork. It's also the case for us being fussy about who holds it.

## How the pathway actually runs

Competition Judge Accreditation isn't an entry-level credential, and we don't expect it to be anyone's first stop. The usual route starts with the **Sensory Evaluation Certificate** — structured tasting, defect identification, and scoring against style standards, which is the same vocabulary a judging panel uses on contest day. From there, accreditation requires supervised panel experience: sitting alongside working judges, scoring flights in parallel, and having your scores checked against theirs before you're cleared to judge on your own.

If you already hold the Sensory Evaluation Certificate and haven't logged panel time recently, this is a good week to ask us about upcoming supervised sessions — we can usually place a member into the next available slot without much lead time. If you don't yet hold the certificate, it sits comfortably alongside a **Certified Cheese Professional (CCP)** track for members who want the fuller credential, or on its own for anyone whose main interest is judging rather than the broader CCP scope.

One thing worth knowing before you commit the hours: this isn't a credential you can bank and forget. A judge who accredits and then doesn't sit on a panel for a couple of years will find the card has quietly gone stale, exactly the way ours is built to work. We'd rather you plan for that up front than discover it the week before a contest wants your name on its roster.

For members outside a national contest's own judge-selection process, our route in is usually smaller and closer to home — regional shows, state fair cheese contests, and our own competition all need panelists, and all of them are a reasonable place to start logging supervised hours. If you're not sure where you stand on the ladder — certificate only, mid-supervision, or accredited and current — reach out to the Awards & Competition Committee. They keep the actual hour counts, not us, and they're the ones who can tell you what's left before your card is current again.
