A public comment period on a proposed tariff list closes next Monday, and it is a long one — the list under review covers roughly nine in ten of the imported cheeses that cross the counter at a typical specialty shop. The tariff idea itself has nothing to do with cheese. It grew out of a long-running dispute over aircraft subsidies, and cheese landed on the retaliation list mostly because it is easy to itemize and hard to substitute overnight. That is exactly why we think the comment record needs voices that actually run a cheese case, not just the trade lawyers on either side of the aircraft fight.

If your business carries imported wheels — a retailer with a Bleu d'Auvergne on the board, a distributor moving pallets of Comté, an affineur who ages a European wheel under contract — this is the week to say so in writing. The docket wants specifics: your typical order volume, what a duty in that range does to your landed cost, and whether a domestic substitute exists at the quality and price point your customers expect. "This would hurt" is not as useful in a public docket as "here is the invoice, here is the markup, here is what changes."

## What we are hearing from members

The mood on our advocacy calls this week has been more resigned than alarmed. Most members who carry European product have already run the arithmetic and know a duty in the proposed range would either come out of their margin or go straight onto the shelf price, and neither is comfortable in a fourth quarter that is usually the busiest stretch of the year for gift boxes and holiday trays. A few members on the production side see it differently — a couple of domestic producers competing directly with imported washed-rind and blue styles told us a tariff could finally give their pricing some room to breathe. We are not going to pretend the membership speaks with one voice here, and we are not trying to. Our job is to get both sets of facts into the same docket.

If you want language to start from, our Membership & Outreach Committee has a short template that walks through the categories the docket is actually asking about — landed cost, substitutability, employment impact — rather than general opposition or support. Send us your operational numbers by Thursday if you would like a hand turning them into something submission-ready; after that, you are on your own for the Monday deadline, and we would still rather you file something imperfect than nothing at all.

## Why we keep track of this even when it doesn't touch your case

Interstate shipment rules, small-producer exemptions, labeling proposals, now an import tariff line — none of these show up in the same news cycle twice, and none of them feel urgent until the week the deadline lands. That is the actual value of a trade association doing this work: someone is watching the docket calendar so you don't have to, and someone flags the Monday you didn't know was coming. If you have not been on our advocacy list, now is a reasonable week to join it — email the federation office and ask to be added, and we will keep you posted on where this particular proceeding lands once a final rule comes out.
