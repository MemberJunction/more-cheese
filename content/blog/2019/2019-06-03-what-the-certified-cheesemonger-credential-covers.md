We hear a version of the same question at almost every regional workshop: someone has been running a cheese counter for a year or two, they're good at it, and they want to know if there's a credential that actually matches what they do all day. For most counter staff, the answer is the Certified Cheesemonger credential, and this week's a good time to walk through what it covers and, just as important, what it doesn't.

Certified Cheesemonger is the federation's entry-level retail credential. It sits below Certified Cheese Professional in our certification pathway and is built for the day-to-day work of running a case: storage and handling, basic style families, cutting and wrapping without wrecking a paste, and enough language to describe a wheel to a customer who has never heard the word "affinage" in their life. It is not a producer credential. If your question is about make sheets, cultures, or a vat, that's a different course entirely — you want something out of the cheesemaking side of our catalog, not this one.

## What's actually on the exam

The written portion covers cheese families (fresh, bloomy, washed-rind, blue, hard/aged), safe handling and cross-contact basics, and case merchandising fundamentals — rotation, temperature logging, and why a rind that's sweating is telling you something. The practical portion is a cutting and wrapping demonstration plus a mock customer interaction, where an evaluator plays a shopper asking for a substitution or a pairing suggestion. We keep the practical short on purpose. It's meant to test whether you can talk someone through a decision at the case, not whether you can recite a textbook.

What it doesn't cover: HACCP plans, formal food-safety certification for a licensed kitchen, or anything about wholesale pricing and margin — those live in the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate and in courses like Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese, respectively. A member who wants the fuller retail-management picture usually pairs Certified Cheesemonger with Counter Culture: Retailing before moving on to the CCP track. There's no requirement to do it in that order, but it's the sequence most members land on.

## Who it tends to be for

Most candidates are counter staff at independent cheese shops or the cheese section of a larger specialty grocer, plus a smaller number of distributor sales reps who want the vocabulary to talk to their retail accounts more precisely. We also see it from people newer to the trade who are trying to figure out whether cheese is a career or a job — it's a low-stakes way to find out, and it counts toward the Certified Cheese Professional pathway if the answer turns out to be "career."

Recertification runs on a two-year cycle and doesn't require retaking the practical if your continuing-education hours are current. If you're weighing whether this is the right credential or whether you should wait for CCP, the Education Committee fields exactly that question at every regional workshop — bring it to the next one, or ask us and we'll point you to the right person.
