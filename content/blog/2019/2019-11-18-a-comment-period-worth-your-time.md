There's a federal comment window open right now that affects more of our membership than the subject line suggests. It concerns how cheese and cheese-product labels have to describe a common milk-processing step used to concentrate protein before the make begins, and whether the current labeling language actually tells a shopper anything useful. The window closes in early December, which means you have about three weeks left to weigh in if this touches your production.

If you don't use the process, this can feel like someone else's fight. But the rule as drafted would set the labeling language for a whole category of standardized cheeses, and standardized-cheese language has a way of becoming the template regulators reach for the next time they touch an adjacent category. We'd rather members who actually run the process tell the regulator what the real-world labeling looks like than let the record fill up entirely with comments from people who've never stood next to the tank.

## What the federation is asking members to do

We're not telling anyone what position to take — reasonable cheesemakers disagree on this one, and several of you have told us different things. What we are asking is that if you use this processing step, or you've made a business decision not to because of how it would have to be labeled, you put that experience into the record in your own words. A one-paragraph comment describing your actual practice carries more weight with a regulator than a form letter, and it's the kind of documentation that holds up if this exact question comes back around in a few years, which questions like this tend to do.

The Standards Committee has been tracking this rule since it first opened for comment, closed, and reopened specifically to gather more information on current industry practice — which is unusual, and suggests the agency genuinely isn't sure yet what "current practice" looks like. That's exactly the gap members are positioned to fill. If you're not sure whether your process falls under what's being discussed, or you want a plain-language summary of the actual proposed text before you write anything, email the committee and we'll walk you through it. We'd rather spend twenty minutes on the phone with you than have you sit this one out because the notice reads like it was written for lawyers, because in fairness, it mostly was.

## Why the deadline matters more than it looks like it should

Comment periods on food-standard rules don't reopen often, and this one already reopened once to ask specifically for more real-world labeling examples. That's a signal the agency wants exactly the kind of input our members can give: not opinion, but description of what a label on your product actually says today and why. Waiting until the deadline is close to start writing is the most common way members miss it entirely — the text always takes longer to get right than you expect once you sit down with it.

If you've never filed a public comment before, it's simpler than it sounds. You don't need legal language, a lawyer, or a position on the whole rule. You need a short, accurate account of your own practice and how the proposed labeling would or wouldn't match it. The Standards Committee can review a draft before you submit if you want a second set of eyes, and can point you to the docket number so you're commenting on the right rule and not an older one with a similar name still circulating in some corners of the industry press.
