We opened registration for the curd retailing workshop back in early May, and it filled faster than most of our single-day sessions do. The session ran this week, and if the room and the Q&A afterward were any indication, fresh curd is a bigger piece of a lot of members' cases than we'd assumed.

The workshop's built for anyone selling fresh, unaged curd at retail — squeaky cheese, fresh mozzarella-style product, quark and similar quick-turn items — which behaves nothing like the aged wheels most of our retail-facing content assumes. Shelf life is measured in days, not months, and the merchandising problems are different: you're managing turnover and cold-chain timing more than humidity and rind development.

It's also a growing part of a lot of members' business, not a niche add-on. Fresh, quick-turn cheese categories have been getting more retail shelf space over the past year or two as shoppers look for lighter, higher-protein options, and several attendees said that shift is exactly why they signed up. One member who runs a counter inside a larger specialty grocer said fresh curd went from a small side case two years ago to a full third of her weekly cheese order today — a pace her staff training hadn't caught up with until this week.

## What generated the most questions

Two topics ran long. The first was sourcing: several attendees buy fresh curd from a regional producer and resell it same-week, and wanted a clearer sense of what to ask a supplier about production date versus pack date before it hits their case. The second was pricing fresh product against a faster spoilage clock than the rest of the case — how to avoid either marking down too early and eating margin, or holding too long and writing product off. Neither is a solved problem industry-wide, but the instructor walked through a rotation framework a few members said they were adopting on the spot.

One attendee, who runs the cheese section at a small specialty grocer, put it plainly during the break: "Everybody trains you on how to age something. Nobody trains you on how to sell something you have four days to move." That gap is exactly what this workshop was built to close, and based on the sign-up numbers, we'll likely run it again.

## If you missed it

The slide deck and a rotation worksheet from the session will be posted to the member resource library within the next two weeks — watch your inbox for the notice. If your case includes fresh curd and you weren't able to attend, the Events Committee is the right place to ask about a repeat session or a recorded version; regional demand tends to drive whether we schedule another one this year.

We'd also rather hear from you before the next one fills up than after. If you sell fresh curd and skipped this workshop because the date didn't work, tell us that directly — a note to the Events Committee about scheduling conflicts is genuinely useful when we're setting next year's calendar, and it's a better use of your time than waiting to see if we happen to pick a date that suits you. Members who attended a fresh-cheese course through our education catalog in the past year are also worth pairing with this one; the two cover different halves of the same problem, production on one side and the retail clock on the other.
