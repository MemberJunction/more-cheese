If your creamery buys fluid milk instead of milking your own herd, you've probably already
noticed: the check that's supposed to sting a little this fall isn't stinging quite as much.
Federal dairy market reporting for the first half of October put Grade AA butter above $2.09
a pound, with 40-pound cheddar blocks holding just over $2.10 — both meaningfully firmer than
where they sat for most of the last two years. National milk production is still climbing,
up roughly 1.3% in September with another herd increase and higher yield per cow expected for
October, but demand has finally caught up to it for the first time since the slump that
started back in 2018.

We've heard from enough members over the last few weeks that it's worth saying plainly: this
is not a signal to relax your contracts. It's a signal to read them more carefully. A milk
price recovery changes the math on every input-cost line in your business plan, and if you've
been discounting wholesale to move inventory during the lean months, this is the moment to
revisit that pricing before your buyers get used to it.

## What's actually moving

The rebound is a production-and-demand story, not a policy story. Slower national output
growth through the first half of the year, combined with steadier retail and food-service
demand, is what firmed the market — not a single announcement or a single buyer. Federal
market analysts are now pointing toward milk prices climbing further through the fourth
quarter, a level of recovery the industry hasn't consistently seen since early 2018.

Some of the price firmness on the block and barrel side is also getting a push from an
unrelated story: importers have been stockpiling hard European cheeses ahead of a new round
of tariffs, which is tightening the domestic wholesale market for comparable styles right
alongside the production numbers. It's a reminder that a cheese price line can move for
reasons that have nothing to do with your own vat.

For members who are milk buyers rather than producers, that means your raw-material cost is
likely to keep climbing gently through the winter rather than easing. For the smaller
farmstead operations in our membership who both milk and make, it means the math on that
fourth cow or that extra vat finally starts to look better than it has in two years. Either
way, the direction matters more than any single week's number, and the direction right now is
up.

If you're a producer rather than a buyer, this is also worth a look at the federal Dairy
Margin Coverage program, which is currently open for 2020 enrollment. It's built to cushion
exactly the gap between what you're paid for milk and what it costs you to make it, and
several members have told us the paperwork is more approachable than it looks once you've
done it once.
