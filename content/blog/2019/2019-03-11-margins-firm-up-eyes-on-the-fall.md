If your milk check has looked a little friendlier the last couple of months, you're not
imagining it. Class III and Class IV prices have been climbing steadily since the start of
the year, after a stretch that had a lot of members quietly recalculating whether this was
still a business or a hobby. It's not a boom. But after two lean years, "not terrible" reads
like good news.

We've heard from members on both ends of the supply chain this week — a few farmstead
producers finally penciling out a positive margin on paper, and a couple of buyers noting
that wholesale cheese pricing has firmed up enough that they're locking in contracts a
little further out than usual. If you've been holding off on equipment purchases or a hire
you've been putting off, this is roughly the moment a lot of members are deciding whether
the recovery has legs.

## The trade question nobody's settled yet

Here's the part worth watching rather than celebrating. A long-running international dispute
over aircraft subsidies is working its way through the usual trade-arbitration channels, and
cheese — particularly imported European cheese — has come up repeatedly as a category that
could see new tariff lines if the dispute resolves the way some trade watchers expect later
this year. Nothing is final, and nothing changes your import costs today. But if you buy,
sell, or age imported wheels as part of your business, this is a good week to start asking
your distributor what their contingency looks like, not a good week to be caught flat-footed
in the fall.

It's worth saying plainly: we don't have a crystal ball on trade policy, and neither does
your distributor, no matter how confidently either of us sounds. What we can tell you is
what members told us this week when we asked around. A cheesemonger who buys a meaningful
share of her case from European producers said she's already sketched out which domestic
and Canadian lines she'd lean on if a tariff line landed on her usual suppliers. A smaller
producer who exports occasionally said the bigger worry for him isn't the tariff itself but
the retaliation cycle that tends to follow — a pattern he's watched play out in other
commodities before it ever touched cheese. Neither of those is a prediction. They're just
two ways members are already hedging without waiting for a headline to force the issue.

On the domestic side, the new farm bill's dairy safety net program is still being built out
at the federal level, with enrollment details expected later this year and coverage running
retroactive to January 1. We don't have a firm signup date yet — when the window opens, we'll
put it in front of you here and flag it to the Membership & Outreach Committee mailing list.
If margin volatility has been part of your winter, it's worth getting familiar with how the
new coverage levels work before the paperwork lands on your desk.

## What we're telling members

Nothing here is investment or legal advice, and we're not going to pretend we can call a
tariff decision or a milk future. What we can say: this is a reasonable week to review your
supply contracts, ask your bank about the interest rate on anything variable, and — if you
buy imported cheese for resale — have a plan B supplier lined up before you need one. As one
distributor-side buyer on our advisory list put it this week, the worst time to find a
backup supplier is the week you actually need one, and it costs nothing to make a phone call
in March that saves you a scramble in October.

The federal dairy safety-net signup we mentioned above will matter more to some members than
the trade question does — if most of your revenue is wholesale milk rather than a cheese
case with imported lines in it, that's the piece to watch closely once enrollment opens. If
you want to talk through what any of this means for your specific business, the office is a
good first call. We can't set your prices for you, but we can usually tell you what other
members in a similar spot are doing, and we'd rather have that conversation with you now
than after a decision's already been made for you.
