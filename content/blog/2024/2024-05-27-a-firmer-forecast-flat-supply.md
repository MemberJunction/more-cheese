The federal government's monthly supply-and-demand estimate, released just ahead of this week, raised its butter, cheese, whey, and nonfat dry milk price forecasts from the previous month "on recent price strength," while holding the overall 2024 milk production estimate essentially flat — up less than a billion pounds from last year. Read together, that's a market where output isn't really growing, and product prices are edging up in response. It's a different picture from earlier this spring, when a bigger herd and stronger output per cow were doing most of the talking.

For our producer members, that shift matters more than the headline number. When production is flat and product prices firm, the story usually isn't "more cheese is worth more." It's closer to "the same amount of cheese is being fought over a little harder," and the milk that goes into it doesn't get any cheaper just because the wheel sells for more. A firmer cheese price forecast is genuine good news if your milk costs are already locked in. It's a wash, or worse, if your supply contract resets before your product contract does.

## Specialty holds its own

Agricultural reporting out ahead of this week carried a related but separate thread: Wisconsin's specialty cheese output hit a new record last year, continuing a run of steady growth rather than a single good season, even as the broader commodity market shifts underneath it. That distinction matters for members who compete on craft and provenance rather than price per pound. A tightening commodity cheese market can actually work in your favor if your customers are already choosing you for reasons that have nothing to do with the futures board.

It doesn't mean specialty producers are insulated from the underlying cost pressure. Milk is milk, and a herd that isn't expanding constrains everyone's supply eventually, commodity and artisan alike. But it does mean the conversation with your buyers this quarter can reasonably be about quality and story, not just about matching a spot price you don't actually compete on. Several of our retailer members have told us this spring that shoppers trading down from imported wheels are landing on domestic artisan cheese rather than commodity block, which is exactly the kind of substitution a strong specialty category is built to catch.

## What to watch before your next price review

Two things worth tracking through June: whether the flat production estimate holds in next month's report, and whether your own milk supplier's pricing follows the same "recent price strength" logic the federal forecast is citing. If your milk cost moves before your wholesale price does, you're absorbing the gap in the meantime, and it compounds the longer a stale price sits on your sheet.

If you haven't run a fresh cost-per-wheel model since the spring numbers came in, this is a reasonable week to do it, especially if your milk contract is up for renewal before your next scheduled price review. Members who've been through a cycle like this before generally say the mistake isn't holding a price too long on purpose — it's not noticing that the inputs moved until a full quarter has already gone by underneath the old number. A quarterly reminder on your own calendar costs nothing and catches exactly this kind of drift before it becomes a real gap.
