If you milk your own herd, there's a decision landing on your desk in the next few days that's easy to put off and expensive to skip. The federal dairy margin safety-net program that pays out when the gap between milk prices and feed costs gets too thin opens its enrollment window at the end of this month, and members who sat out last year's signup told us afterward they wished they hadn't. We'd rather you hear it from us now, while there's still time to decide, than from a neighbor in April.

Here's the plain version of how it works. The program compares your milk price against your feed costs each month and pays a benefit when that margin drops below whatever coverage level you've chosen. You pick your coverage level and how much of your production history to insure, pay a premium scaled to that choice, and then the program does the rest automatically — no claims process, no proving a loss happened. For a small or mid-sized farmstead operation, that's meaningful: feed costs don't always move with milk prices, and the months when they diverge are exactly the months cash flow gets tight enough that a delayed claims process would hurt.

## What changed for this signup

Two things are worth knowing before you sit down with the paperwork. First, this year's program includes an option some longtime enrollees haven't had before: producers who previously carried supplemental production history from an earlier version of the program can now combine it with their regular coverage history in a one-time adjustment. If you've been enrolled for several years and never revisited how much of your production is actually insured, this is the year to check — you may be able to cover more of your herd's output than your current paperwork reflects. Second, the price forecasts we've been tracking this winter show milk prices firming somewhat for the year, which changes the math on how much coverage actually makes sense at each level. A margin program is cheap insurance in a bad year and a real expense in a good one; nobody can tell you in February which this year will turn out to be, but knowing the current forecast is at least a starting point for the conversation.

"We treat the enrollment decision like we treat a hay contract," one farmstead member who's been through several signups told us. "You don't wait until you're sure you need it. By the time you're sure, the window's closed." That's the instinct we'd encourage across our farmstead membership this week, whether you've enrolled every year or you're weighing it for the first time.

## What we're asking of you, and what we can do

None of this is advice about which coverage level to choose — that depends on your herd, your feed contracts, and your own tolerance for a bad month, and it's worth a conversation with whoever handles your operation's numbers rather than a blog post. What we can do is point you toward the enrollment window itself and flag the production-history adjustment before it's easy to miss.

If you want to talk through how other farmstead members are approaching this year's decision, our advocacy volunteers have been comparing notes since the forecast came out, and the Membership & Outreach Committee can connect you. And if raw-milk and margin policy generally is something you want a say in beyond your own operation, our advocacy program tracks exactly this kind of dairy pricing question — a short note to that committee is how you get looped in before the next comment period, not after.
