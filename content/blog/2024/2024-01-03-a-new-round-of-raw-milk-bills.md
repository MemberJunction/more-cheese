State legislative sessions are starting back up this month, and raw-milk sales are on more than one opening-week docket. It happens most Januarys — a handful of states take up direct-to-consumer raw-milk bills early in the session, before committee calendars fill up with everything else. This year is no exception.

The shape of these bills tends to be familiar to members who've followed this issue for a while. Most permit registered producers to sell raw milk directly to consumers — often on-farm only, sometimes through farmers' markets — while adding new labeling, storage, and handling requirements. A container has to say "unpasteurized," carry the producer's name and address, and usually a printed warning about the risk of foodborne illness. None of that is new territory for members who already sell raw-milk cheese; it's the milk itself that most of these bills are reaching toward, and the rules tend to move fast wherever a chamber sees a real shot at passage.

## Why we track this even if you don't sell fluid milk

If you make raw-milk cheese but not fluid raw milk, a bill like this can still touch you. Interstate shipment rules, on-farm sale definitions, and the labeling language legislatures settle on for raw milk have a habit of getting borrowed wholesale for raw-milk cheese rulemaking a session or two later. Our Food Safety Committee reads every bill in this family for that reason, not just the ones naming cheese outright.

**Interstate shipment** remains the harder problem. A state permitting on-farm raw-milk sales doesn't touch the federal rule against shipping raw milk across state lines for retail sale — the two operate on entirely separate tracks, and we've heard from more than one member confused about why a state-legal sale still can't cross a border. If your customer base spans state lines, that distinction is worth confirming with whoever handles your compliance before you plan around a new state law.

## What we're asking members to do

Public comment periods on bills like these are usually short — sometimes a single committee hearing window — so waiting for a final version to comment on often means missing the window entirely. If your state has a bill moving, two things help:

- **Read the actual bill text**, not a summary. Labeling and handling language varies more between states than the headlines suggest, and the difference between "on-farm sale only" and "direct delivery permitted" changes what a bill means for your operation.
- **Tell your Membership & Outreach contact** if a bill in your state reaches a hearing. We track these federally, but a member testifying locally, in their own words, about their own creamery, carries more weight in a state capital than anything we could submit.

We'll keep updating this space as bills move through committee this session. If you want the short version delivered as it happens rather than reading it here first, the advocacy mailing list is the fastest route — sign up from the member portal.

Members who've watched several sessions of this pass through tell us the same thing every year: a bill that clears its first committee vote with strong support in January is not the same as a bill that becomes law. Session calendars are long, other priorities crowd the floor schedule, and a bill can stall in a second committee for months after a promising start — sometimes carrying over to the next session entirely, sometimes dying quietly without a final vote either way. That's not a reason to disengage. It's a reason to stay engaged past the headline, because the version of a bill that eventually passes, if one does, is rarely the version that got the early press.

For members newer to advocacy work, the easiest way in is small: read the committee summary when a bill in your state gets a hearing date, and if the labeling or handling language would change something in your own operation, say so in writing before the hearing, not after. The Food Safety Committee keeps a running file of bill language by state specifically so members don't have to start from scratch each session — ask for the current file through the member portal if your state is one we're tracking this year.
