For most of the spring, members told us the same thing on committee calls: the margin looked
good on paper, but nobody was ready to call it a trend. That's shifted. The federal
farm-milk margin over feed costs has now come in above the top Dairy Margin Coverage tier
for five months running, and it landed at its highest point of the year in the most recent
reporting period. For a program that exists to catch producers when feed costs outrun milk
income, five clean months in a row is the kind of run that changes what a member is willing
to sign up for next season.

The mix underneath it is worth a second look, not just the headline number. The July advanced
Class I base price rose to its highest level since January 2023, while Class II and Class IV
edged up slightly and Class III — the class that prices most cheddar and Monterey-style
cheese — slipped a little on the month. Butterfat values firmed even as protein values
softened. Cultured products have been a bright spot on the retail side too; trade coverage
this spring pointed to volume and pricing gains in cottage cheese and other cultured lines,
which is part of why Class II has held up even as commodity cheddar cooled slightly. None of
that is dramatic on its own. Together, it reads like a market that's found a floor rather
than one that's about to run.

## What this means at the vat, not just the ledger

If you sell fluid milk or wholesale cheese into a Class III or Class IV pricing structure,
the story is steady rather than exciting — welcome, after two volatile years, but not a
reason to change your production plan. If more of your volume runs through Class II — soft
ripened, cultured, or specialty lines sold outside standard commodity contracts — you've
likely felt the firmer pull more directly this summer.

The margin coverage number matters most for enrollment decisions, and that window doesn't
stay open all year. If your farmstead has been sitting on the fence about whether Tier 1
coverage is worth the premium, five months of margins clearing that line is the kind of data
point worth bringing to whoever does your books before the next sign-up period opens. We are
not in a position to tell you what to sign — that's between you, your lender, and your own
cost structure — but we can tell you what the number has actually done, and it has been kind
this summer.

## A market that rewards patience, this time

We've spent a fair number of these updates over the past few years walking members through
volatility — a tariff line moving, a feed-cost spike, a pricing rule under review. This one
is a quieter kind of news, and quieter is not nothing. A cheesemaker who called in for our
last member office hours put it simply: "I stopped checking the price board every morning
around May. That's new for me." For a market that's spent the better part of five years
lurching from one disruption to the next, a few months of a margin sitting comfortably above
the line members actually plan around is worth noting, even if it doesn't make for dramatic
reading.
