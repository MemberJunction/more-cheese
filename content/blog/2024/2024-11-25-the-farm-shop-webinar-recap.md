Webinar: Farm Shop Design drew one of the fuller virtual rooms we've had this fall, and the timing wasn't an accident. A lot of members run their retail case out of the same building where the cheese gets made, and late November is exactly when that case has to do double duty — regular customers plus a wave of once-a-year holiday shoppers who don't know your layout, your labeling, or which wedge is the one to try first.

If you missed the session, here's the short version, and a few things worth doing with it before this Saturday.

## The case is the argument

The recurring theme was that a farm shop case has to make its own argument, because there usually isn't a clerk standing at every register explaining what's what. That means legible labeling — style, milk type, and one sentence about the make, not a paragraph — placed at eye level, not buried at knee height where only regulars know to look. Presenters also pushed back on the instinct to cram the case full before a busy weekend. A case with breathing room reads as curated; a case stacked to the edges reads as clearance, even when the cheese inside it is the same.

Lighting came up more than we expected. Cool white light flattens color and makes a rind look tired by midafternoon; a warmer bulb, angled slightly downward, does more for a wheel's appeal than almost any other single change a shop can make in a weekend. A gift-guide piece making the rounds this month, cataloging advent calendars and specialty boxes across the country, made the same point from the buyer's side: the shoppers browsing those lists are judging a cheese almost entirely on presentation before they ever taste it.

## Small Business Saturday is this coming weekend

This Saturday is Small Business Saturday, and independent retailers nationally are expected to see meaningful foot traffic from shoppers specifically looking to buy local rather than from a big-box store. For members who sell direct — whether that's a farmstead store, a counter inside a larger shop, or a table at a holiday market — that's worth planning around now, not Friday night. A short tasting flight by the register, three cheeses instead of ten, gives a first-time shopper a reason to linger and a story to repeat when they're buying the wedge as a gift.

If you're taking online orders alongside the physical case this season, the same clarity principle applies. Gift buyers researching cheese online are doing it fast, often from a phone, often deciding in the time it takes to read one product description. A listing that says "aged six months, washed rind, pairs well with a dark ale" does more work than a listing that just says "artisan cheese." It's also a season when milk pricing rules are in the news — federal regulators finalized changes to the formulas behind Class I and Class III pricing this month — which is a reminder that your own cost basis for a wheel can move for reasons that have nothing to do with your case. We know late November is a hard week to clear an hour for a webinar, and attendance data bears that out — this was one of our better-attended virtual sessions of the year, but plenty of members still couldn't get away from the make room or the counter. A recording is available to members through the education portal, along with the slide handout on case lighting and label sizing that a few attendees asked us to share separately.

The Events Committee is already gathering ideas for next year's in-person track at the ICF Annual Conference, and a farm-shop retail session is on the shortlist based on how this webinar landed. If you have a specific question your case is fighting with this weekend — layout, pricing signage, how much to hold back for walk-ins versus preorders — reach out to the committee directly; several members who attended have already offered to compare notes with anyone still finalizing their setup before Saturday.
