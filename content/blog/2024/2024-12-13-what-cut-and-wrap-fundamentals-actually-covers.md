Cutting cheese sounds like the easiest part of the job right up until you're doing it fifty times a day in the two weeks before the holidays, under time pressure, with a customer standing at the counter waiting for a gift wedge to look as good as it tastes. That gap — between "I can cut cheese" and "I can cut cheese fast, cleanly, and consistently, at volume, while someone watches" — is exactly what our Cut & Wrap Fundamentals course is built to close.

We get asked about this one every December, usually by a counter manager who just watched a new hire struggle through their first gift-box rush. It's a fair question, because the course title undersells what's actually in it — proper technique goes well beyond the basic fold-and-tuck wrap most people learn on their own.

## What the course trains

The core of Cut & Wrap Fundamentals is portioning technique: how to read a wheel's structure before you cut it, how to minimize waste on an irregular shape, and how to keep a cut face from drying out or oxidizing between the knife and the wrap. That last part matters more at this time of year than any other — a wedge that sits under case lights for two days before it's picked up needs a different wrap approach than one that's sold within the hour, and the course walks through both.

The second half is presentation and packaging: how to build a gift box that holds its shape in transit, what wrap materials actually extend shelf life versus just looking nice, and how to label a cut portion so it carries the same information as the whole wheel it came from — origin, lot, allergens, weight. Export showcases this fall reminded us that specialty cheese is being judged on presentation as much as flavor these days, and the same is true at a domestic counter: the wrap has to do real work, not just look finished. That labeling piece connects directly to material we also cover in our food-safety and labeling courses, and it's the part that most often gets skipped when a shop is moving fast during a rush.

The course does not cover cheesemaking, aging, or wholesale pricing — if you're looking for guidance there, our Cave Management and Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese courses are better fits.

## Who it's for

This one is aimed squarely at retail and counter staff, not producers. A cheesemonger who's new to a counter, a shop owner training seasonal holiday hires, or a farmstead operation that's started selling direct out of a small on-site shop are all a good match. Members who've taken it tell us the most useful part isn't the technique itself so much as the pacing drills — learning to hold quality steady when you're cutting your fortieth wedge of the afternoon instead of your first.

If you supervise seasonal staff, it's worth running this before the holiday rush rather than during it. A few members have told us they schedule it for early November specifically so new counter hires have the muscle memory in place before December volume hits. If that window's already passed for you this year, the course still runs on a rolling basis, so signing up now gets your team ready for the next busy stretch — the run-up to a spring gift-box season comes around faster than people expect.
