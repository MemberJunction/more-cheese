Two things worth putting next to each other this week: national milk production has held up better than forecasters expected heading into winter, and dairy trade coverage this fall keeps pointing to the same pattern — flavor-forward, higher-margin dairy categories growing faster than the commodity middle of the case. Neither is dramatic on its own. Together they describe a category that's growing at the higher-margin end while staying well supplied at the vat — which is a genuinely healthy combination, and also one that tends to get taken for granted right up until it changes.

We mention this now because January is when a lot of our members renegotiate annual wholesale terms, and the mood a buyer or distributor brings into that conversation is shaped by exactly this kind of macro backdrop. A distributor who's watched steady production and rising demand all year is likely to come to the table assuming supply isn't the issue — which means the conversation defaults to price and volume, not availability. If your own production has been anything other than steady this year, that's worth naming explicitly rather than letting a buyer assume you're riding the same curve as the national numbers.

## What steady numbers hide

Category-wide data smooths over a lot. A national production figure doesn't know that your feed costs went up, that you lost a hand for six weeks, or that your cave ran hot in August and cost you a batch. Flavor-forward dairy gaining shelf space nationally doesn't mean it's gaining ground for your specific style or your specific account — a rising profile for one seasonal flavor doesn't necessarily lift a small-batch washed rind. Before you walk into a January renewal, it's worth pulling your own numbers for the year rather than assuming the national trend describes your business. If it doesn't, that gap is exactly what you want to be able to point to when a buyer opens with "the market's been strong all year, so."

The flip side applies too. If your year actually has tracked the broader trend — steady output, growing sell-through — that's useful leverage in its own right, particularly with a distributor who's counting on your account to be a dependable line rather than a variable one. Reliability is worth naming in a renewal conversation just as much as growth is.

## Before you sign

A few practical things worth checking before any January paperwork gets finalized: confirm your volume commitments still match what you can actually produce given this year's realities, not last year's; check whether your pricing terms reference an index that's moved since you last signed; and if you're enrolled in any federal margin support program, verify your enrollment carried over rather than assuming it did — a lapsed enrollment is one of the more avoidable headaches we see every January. None of this requires urgency this week. It does reward getting your own numbers in order before someone else's numbers set the terms of the conversation.
