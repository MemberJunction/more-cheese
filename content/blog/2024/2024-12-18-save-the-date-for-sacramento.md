Next year's Annual Conference is set: mid-July in Sacramento, and early registration opens this week, well before most members are thinking past their holiday orders. We know December is an odd month to ask anyone to plan a July trip. We're doing it anyway, because the members who book early are consistently the ones who get the workshop tracks and hotel rooms they actually wanted, and the members who wait until spring are consistently the ones writing in asking why a session is full.

This isn't a new pattern for us. Early registration has opened in December or January for each of the last few conferences, quietly, without much fanfare, and the members who've caught it early have told us the same thing every year: locking in a flight and a hotel room in winter, while the room block is still wide open and fares haven't climbed for peak summer travel, saves real money on top of the seat.

## What's different about a summer conference

Sacramento in July runs hot and dry, which matters more for planning than it sounds like it should. If you're bringing product for a cave tour add-on or a competition entry, you'll want to think through cold-chain logistics earlier than you would for a spring or fall location. It also means the conference schedule leans harder into indoor, air-conditioned session blocks during the afternoon, with any outdoor components, welcome receptions, evening tastings, scheduled for after the heat breaks. None of that changes what the conference covers, but it changes how you pack and how you plan your travel days.

Workshop tracks for next year are still being finalized, but early signals from the Events Committee point toward a continuation of what's worked the last two years: a hands-on affinage and cave-management track, a food-safety track scaled for small and mid-sized operations, and a retail track for members on the counter side. If a specific track filled fast last year and you want back in, that's the strongest argument for registering now rather than waiting for the full schedule to post.

## Booking now versus waiting

Registering early doesn't commit you to a specific set of sessions. It reserves your seat at the conference generally and gets you into the room block at the conference-rate hotel before those rooms release to the general public closer to the date. You can pick your actual workshop tracks once the full schedule is posted in the spring, the same as every year.

If travel budget is the obstacle rather than timing, reach out to your Membership & Outreach Committee contact about what support exists for smaller operations before you decide to sit next year out. And if you've hosted a cave tour or a tasting for a past conference and Sacramento's geography makes that logistically difficult this time around, we'd still like to hear from you; there's more than one way to be part of the program besides opening your own cave.

Early registration pricing typically holds for a limited window, and it isn't a token discount. Members who've compared notes across a few conference cycles now tell us the gap between the early rate and the rate closer to July has widened each year, in step with what venues and hotels are charging generally. Locking in your seat this month, while the rate is at its lowest and the hotel block still has rooms at every price point, is a genuinely different cost than waiting until spring.

We'll send a reminder as the early window gets close to closing, but the members who've been to a few of these know not to wait for it. If Sacramento in July is on your calendar at all, this week is a good one to make it official.
