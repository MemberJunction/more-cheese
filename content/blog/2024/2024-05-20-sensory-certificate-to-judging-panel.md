With the annual conference eight weeks out and this year's call for entries about to open, we've had the same question land in the inbox three times this month: how does a member go from dropping off wheels at the competition to sitting on the panel that scores them? The short answer is the Sensory Evaluation Certificate. The longer answer is that most people start the process later than they should.

The certificate itself is not exotic. It covers structured tasting, defect identification, and scoring cheese against defined style standards — the same skills a working cheesemaker already uses when deciding whether a wheel is ready to ship, just written down in a way a competition can standardize across dozens of judges. It's valid for three years, which sounds generous until you notice how many members let it lapse quietly between conference cycles and then have to requalify the year they finally have time to volunteer.

## What the certificate actually unlocks

Holding the Sensory Evaluation Certificate is the usual route onto a judging panel, but it isn't automatic entry. Competition Judge Accreditation is a separate credential layered on top, and it requires the sensory certificate plus supervised panel experience — you don't just take an exam and get handed a scoring sheet. Members who've been through it describe the supervised rounds as the part that actually teaches you the job: sitting next to an experienced judge, comparing notes on the same wheel, and learning where your palate agrees with the standard and where it drifts.

The accreditation also has a shorter shelf life than most of our credentials — two years, and it lapses quickly without active judging. That's by design. A judge who hasn't scored a panel in two cycles is judging from memory, and memory is exactly what a structured tasting standard is supposed to correct for.

## Where to actually start

If you're aiming for a seat on this year's panel and you don't already hold the sensory certificate, the timeline is tighter than it looks from here — exam scheduling and the required panel hours don't compress well into a single summer. The Judging & Competition Preparation course is built for exactly this runway: it walks members through what the exam expects, how scoring sheets are structured category by category, and what a first supervised round actually feels like from the judge's side of the table rather than the entrant's.

We hear from members every spring who assumed judging was reserved for people who'd been in the federation for a decade. It isn't. Several of the newer names on recent panels came up through this same path — sensory certificate, supervised hours, accreditation — inside two or three years of joining. What it actually rewards is starting the sensory work early, not seniority.

If competition entries are part of your summer already, worth asking yourself the flip side of the question: are you interested in being on the other side of the table next year? The Education Committee can point you to the next Judging & Competition Preparation cohort, and the Awards & Competition Committee can tell you what this year's panel still needs.
