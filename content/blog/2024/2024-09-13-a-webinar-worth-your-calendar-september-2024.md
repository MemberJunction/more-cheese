Next Tuesday, September 17, the Events Committee is running Webinar: Digital Storefronts 2024 — an hour built for members who've been selling online for a while and members who are still deciding whether it's worth the trouble. It's free, it's virtual, and you don't need a shipping department to get something out of it.

We scheduled this one for the fall on purpose. This is the stretch when a lot of smaller producers start thinking seriously about holiday gift boxes and mail-order, often for the first time, and the questions we hear every year are close to identical: what actually ships well without arriving as a puddle, how do you price for cold-chain packaging without quietly eating your own margin, and is it worth building your own storefront versus listing through an existing specialty retailer that already has the logistics figured out.

## What's actually on the agenda

The session walks through three things members have asked us about most this year. First, packaging that survives two or three days in a truck without your rind suffering for it — insulated boxes and ice packs sound simple until you are the one paying for a box that failed. Second, how to write a product listing that sets accurate expectations about a cheese that is genuinely handmade and therefore genuinely variable from batch to batch. Third, a plain look at fulfillment costs so you are not pricing a wheel as though shipping were free, when refrigerated carriers rarely are. None of it is theoretical — it is built from what members already running online storefronts have actually run into this year.

Specialty cheese has had a strong run by most trade reporting this year, with premium and artisanal categories pulling ahead of the broader grocery case. That is good news for members thinking about a bigger online push, but growth in interest does not automatically become a good experience for your customer — someone still has to get a hand-cut wedge to a doorstep in one piece, on time, and at a temperature that does not embarrass the cheese.

## If you can't make it live

We record every webinar and post it to the member portal within a few days, so a Tuesday afternoon conflict is not a reason to skip it entirely. But live sessions get a question-and-answer window the recording does not, and this is a topic where the specific question — what do I do about a cheese that needs to breathe versus one that needs to stay cold — is exactly the kind of thing worth asking in real time, to someone who has actually shipped one.

Registration is open through the member portal now. If online sales are becoming a bigger part of your plan this year, this is a low-cost way to skip a few of the mistakes other members already made for you, on their own dime.

Bring a question if you have one. Past sessions have run long on the Q&A precisely because someone in the audience had a shipping problem nobody on the panel had thought to cover in the prepared material, and that's usually the most useful ten minutes of the hour.
