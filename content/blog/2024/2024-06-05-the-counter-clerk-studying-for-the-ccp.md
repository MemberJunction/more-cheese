Every summer a handful of members write in with some version of the same question: they've been behind a cheese counter for a few years, they've taken one or two of our courses, and they want to know whether they're close enough to attempt the Certified Cheese Professional credential to bother preparing seriously. There's no single member behind this post — we hear the question often enough that it's really a composite of a few dozen members at any given time — but the shape of the answer is consistent enough to be worth writing down.

## The hours matter as much as the knowledge

The CCP isn't gatekept by cleverness. It's gatekept by accumulated hours in the industry, and that trips up more members than the exam content itself does. A candidate generally needs several thousand hours of hands-on cheese work over the preceding several years, or a mix of hands-on hours and formal coursework, before they're even eligible to sit the exam. We've had members assume that a strong showing in one of our courses substitutes for years behind a counter or cave. It doesn't, and finding that out a month before a registration deadline is a rough way to learn it.

What we tell members weighing this is to start counting now, honestly, rather than guessing generously later. Log the hours as you go: counter shifts, cave work, formal training days, anything documentable. A candidate who's been careless about tracking their own experience often has more of it than they think, and just as often has less of it than they assumed, and neither surprise is a good one to discover during an application window.

## Studying for a 150-question exam behind a working counter

Once eligibility is settled, the exam itself rewards the kind of member who treats a slow Tuesday shift as study time rather than downtime. It's a three-hour, wide-ranging test covering everything from production technique to sensory evaluation to the practical business of selling cheese, and members who've passed it tend to describe the preparation less as cramming and more as finally organizing knowledge they already had scattered across a few years of practice. Reading a style's production method the same week you're actually selling that style at the counter tends to make the material stick in a way a textbook alone doesn't.

If this sounds like where you are — a few years in, a course or two under your belt, wondering if you're eligible — the honest first step isn't studying. It's counting your hours accurately and checking them against the current eligibility requirements before you build a study plan around an exam you might not qualify for yet. Our certification page has the current hour thresholds, and our Education Committee can point you toward which of our own courses count most directly toward the material the exam covers.

**Next step:** if you think you might be close to CCP-eligible, start with an honest hour count before you start a study schedule — the certification page lists exactly what counts.
