A small retail data point crossed our desk in January, and it's worth more attention than a milk-aisle statistic usually gets: whole milk grew its retail volume again last year, and plant-based alternatives fell for a second consecutive year running, down to their lowest level since before the pandemic. Nobody's declaring a trend off one year of numbers, but it lines up with something a lot of our members in the specialty case have been telling us anecdotally for a while — shoppers who drifted toward alternatives a few years back are drifting back, and they're bringing a preference for "the real thing" with them into other categories too, cheese included.

## Small producers are treating this as real, not a fluke

We don't usually put much stock in a single data point, but this one is consistent with what we're hearing from members directly. A handful of smaller creameries in our network spent part of this winter adding capacity rather than trimming it — new equipment, larger batch sizes, one member who told us she'd finally invested in a proper affinage room after three years of borrowing cave space from a neighbor. That's not the behavior of an industry bracing for a downturn. It's the behavior of one that expects the shopper who wants a real answer instead of a substitute to keep showing up.

None of this means every category is booming equally. A recovery in consumer preference for dairy generally doesn't automatically show up as a recovery in your specific wholesale account, and plenty of members are still fighting for shelf space against private-label competition that has nothing to do with whether shoppers prefer real cheese. But it's a genuinely different backdrop than the one a lot of us were planning around three years ago, when the safe assumption was that every category needed to defend itself against the alternative next to it on the shelf.

## What we'd actually do with this

If you've been holding off on an investment — a piece of equipment, a new hire, a slightly larger batch size — because you weren't sure demand would hold, this is a reasonable month to revisit that math with fresher numbers rather than the assumptions you built two or three years ago. That doesn't mean every expansion pencils out; it means the demand side of the equation looks a little more forgiving than it has in a while, which changes what a reasonable bet looks like.

It's also worth remembering that a favorable retail trend doesn't do your storytelling for you. Members abroad have been leaning hard into provenance and small-batch narrative on their own labels this winter — not because the product changed, but because a shopper who's choosing real dairy over an alternative is often also the shopper most willing to pay attention to where a wheel actually came from. If your label or your counter card hasn't been updated in a while, this is a good season to make sure it's actually telling that story, not just naming an ingredient list.

We'll keep watching this one past a single winter's numbers before calling it anything more than encouraging.
