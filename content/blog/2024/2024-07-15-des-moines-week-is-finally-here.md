If you're reading this from an airport gate, welcome to conference week. The ICF Annual Conference opens Tuesday in Des Moines, and for the fourth summer running, we're watching registration numbers land at the highest count we've had since the in-person calendar came back. Four weeks ago we asked you to save the date. This week we're asking you to check your itinerary — and pack lighter than you think.

Central Iowa has been running well above normal this month, with the mid-month stretch bringing a run of upper-80s and low-90s afternoons and a forecast heat episode later in the week, part of a heat wave that national coverage was already tracking marching across the country earlier this month. It's not the kind of thing that changes a conference agenda, but it does change what you should wear between the hotel and the convention hall, and it's worth checking your registration confirmation for the shuttle schedule if you'd rather not stand outside hailing a car in that kind of heat.

A few housekeeping notes before you arrive. Badge pickup opens at the host hotel at 7 a.m. Tuesday, a full ninety minutes before the first session, specifically so the line doesn't back up into the coffee cart. If you registered a guest or a second staff member from your creamery, bring the confirmation email — the desk has had trouble matching last-minute add-ons to the master roster in years past, and we'd rather you not stand there re-typing an email address on someone else's phone.

The session worth rearranging a flight for, if you can only make one, is Wednesday afternoon's panel on pricing a wheel through a year of uneven milk costs. Class III prices dipped slightly this July after climbing for most of the year, and margins over feed cost are the best they've been all year, but that doesn't mean every creamery's numbers moved the same direction at the same time — a lot of the room will be comparing notes on how differently a bulk producer and a small aged-cheese operation felt the same twelve months. Bring your own cost sheet if you're willing to talk numbers; nobody on that panel is going to ask you to share more than you want to.

## What's different from last year

This is the first conference where our Certified Cheese Professional track runs as a full parallel day rather than a single afternoon block. If you've been chipping away at that credential by correspondence, Wednesday and Thursday are set up so you can sit for a proctored session on-site instead of scheduling one separately later in the year. Bring a photo ID and check in with the credentialing table by 8:30 the morning you plan to test.

The competition wing also moves this year — entries and judging happen in a separate hall from the main sessions, partly because last year's overlap meant half the judges missed the opening keynote trying to get from one room to another. If you're planning to watch any part of judging, budget the walk.

## If you can't make it

Not every member can get away from a summer make schedule for four days in Iowa, and that's fine — a farmstead in full production doesn't stop because the trade association is meeting. Recordings of the general sessions go up within two weeks for anyone registered, virtual or in-person, and the pricing panel above will be one of them. If cave humidity or a milking schedule is what's keeping you home this week, we'd still like to hear how the season is going; drop a line to the Events Committee and someone will call you back after the dust settles.

Safe travels to everyone headed to Des Moines, and see you Tuesday morning.
