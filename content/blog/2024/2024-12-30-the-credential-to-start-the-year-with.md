The calls start the same way every year, usually the week between Christmas and New Year's: a member wants to know which credential to pick up next, and whether it's worth waiting until January to start. Our answer is almost always the same. Start now, in the odd, quiet week you already have, rather than waiting for a January that will fill up with everything else waiting to fill it.

Vague resolutions rarely survive contact with a busy February. The ones that do tend to be specific and tied to something already on your calendar — "I want to finish Competition Judge Accreditation before entries open for the flagship competition" is a plan, not a wish. "I want to grow professionally this year" is not.

## Why the judging credential, specifically

Competition Judge Accreditation is one of the credentials members underestimate the most. It doesn't sound as immediately useful as a food safety certificate or a sensory certificate, and it's easy to assume it's only for members who want to sit on a judging panel someday. But the material underneath it — structured tasting vocabulary, defect identification, and scoring consistency across a flight of dozens of wheels in a single sitting — sharpens the same judgment you're already using every time you decide what goes in your own case.

The training most judge-accreditation programs are built around follows a similar shape industry-wide: modules on scoring rubrics, a working vocabulary for describing flaws versus preferences, and practice sessions that force you to defend a score to someone who disagrees with it. That last part is the underrated benefit. Explaining why you scored a wheel the way you did, out loud, to a skeptical colleague, is a different muscle than tasting alone in your own cave, and it's one that transfers directly to pricing conversations and buyer meetings.

Members who've gone through it consistently tell us the same thing: it changed how they taste their own product, not just how they evaluate someone else's. That's a return on the credential whether or not you ever sit behind a judging table.

## Timing it against the calendar you already have

Entries for the flagship competition open in the next few weeks, and judging panels get built out over the following months. If accreditation is something you've been meaning to pursue, starting now means you're credentialed well before panel selection, not scrambling to finish coursework during entry season when your own production calendar is also busiest.

The credential doesn't require you to give up your day job to complete it — it's designed around exactly this kind of quiet-week start, with self-paced modules you can work through between now and when panels are actually selected. Check the credential requirements page first to confirm what, if any, prerequisite coursework you still need, then block two or three hours this week rather than in January, while your calendar is genuinely quiet. If you're not sure whether judging accreditation fits your goals better than a different credential on the ladder, ask us directly — we'd rather talk you into the right one than watch you start the wrong one out of new-year momentum.
