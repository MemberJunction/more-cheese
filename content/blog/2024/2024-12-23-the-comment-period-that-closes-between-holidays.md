Most of the federation's inbox goes quiet this week. Out-of-office replies, a skeleton crew answering the phones, caves running on a holiday checklist instead of a normal one. It's also, every year, when one of our longer-running comment periods comes down to its final stretch — and this year that's the docket on interstate shipment of aged raw-milk cheese, which closes the first week of January.

The rule members keep asking us about hasn't changed this month: cheese made from unpasteurized milk can still cross state lines if it's aged at least 60 days and labeled as unpasteurized. What's live right now is a federal request for comment on whether that aging floor should move, and on what "clearly labeled" should mean in practice for a wheel that gets cut and repackaged three times between the cave and the shelf. If you make, age, or sell raw-milk cheese and have an opinion about either question, this is the window.

## Why the federation is asking members to write, not just us

We've filed our own comment, and it says roughly what you'd expect: any change to the aging requirement should be grounded in the microbiology, not just the calendar, and any relabeling standard needs to survive contact with a real supply chain, not just a spec sheet. But a federal docket reads differently when it has forty submissions from people who actually run affinage rooms attached to it, versus one from a trade association's letterhead. If you've got twenty minutes over the holiday and a specific example — a batch that hit 60 days and one that didn't, a label that confused a distributor, a state inspector who reads the rule differently than the one three counties over — that's exactly the kind of detail that makes a comment useful instead of generic.

You don't need a lawyer's voice to write one. Say what you actually do, say what the rule change would actually mean for your cave and your case, and say it in your own words. The docket link and a short template are on the advocacy page; the template is a starting point, not something to submit as-is; we'd rather see fifteen comments that sound like fifteen different creameries than one form letter copied fifteen times.

## What else is worth an hour this week

If raw milk isn't your product, the interstate shipment docket may still be worth a read — it's one of the clearer examples this year of a rule that started as a food-safety question and turned into a labeling and traceability question, which is a pattern showing up in a few other dockets we're tracking into next year. Our Membership & Outreach Committee has also flagged that petition and comment participation tends to dip sharply between Christmas and New Year's, then never fully catches back up before a deadline — so if you've been meaning to add your name to something, this week, not the first week of January, is the better time.

Committee contact information and a running list of open comment periods live on the advocacy page. We'll post a wrap-up once this docket closes and we know what, if anything, moves.
