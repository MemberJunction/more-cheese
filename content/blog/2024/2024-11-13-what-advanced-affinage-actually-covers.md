Our member line gets some version of this question every fall: is the Advanced Affinage Certificate worth adding once you already hold Cheese Foundations? Short answer: if you are the person actually making cave decisions — humidity, turning schedule, rind treatment, when a wheel gets pulled early — the credential is built for exactly that job, not for a generalist.

Cheese Foundations covers the basics of milk handling, culture selection, and make-day fundamentals. Advanced Affinage assumes you already know that and tests something narrower: how you read a cave and act on what it tells you. That includes managing humidity and airflow across different wheel geometries, recognizing early signs of an undesirable mold bloom versus a house culture doing its job, and making the call on rind-washing frequency as a wheel ages. It is less about knowing the right answer from a textbook and more about demonstrating judgment under a set of cave conditions that are never quite identical twice.

Trade reporting this fall has been full of supply-side wrinkles — a butterfat shortage abroad affecting how some imported styles are made, and a domestic herd that keeps inching up even as demand patterns shift. None of that changes what happens inside your own cave, but it does mean more members are experimenting with new milk sources, new supplier relationships, or new wheel formats to manage input costs. Every one of those changes shows up first in the cave, not on the label. A credentialed affineur is better equipped to catch a problem in week two rather than week eight, when the batch is already a loss.

## Who tends to get the most out of it

Members who have been running a cave solo for a year or two, without a formal credential to point to, are the most common candidates. So are apprentices who have moved past their first full season and are ready to take over cave calls rather than just executing someone else's schedule. If you are earlier than that — still learning to read a make sheet, not yet trusted with cave decisions — Cheese Foundations and a season of supervised affinage will serve you better than jumping ahead.

The credential also pairs well with entering our annual competition. Judges tell us, informally, that entries from Advanced Affinage holders tend to show more consistent rind development across a batch, which is one of the things a scoring panel actually notices even when it isn't a named criterion on the sheet.

## Getting started

The credential path runs through a mix of coursework and a practical assessment; our Affinage Fundamentals course is the usual on-ramp if you have not taken formal instruction in this area before. If you are already confident in your cave practice and just need the structured assessment, contact the Education Committee through the member portal and they can tell you whether you are ready to sit for it directly. Either way, do not rush a fall application if your cave is mid-crisis with a batch — book the assessment for a calmer stretch so it reflects your actual judgment, not a bad week. Recertification timelines and prerequisites are on the certifications page of the member portal if you want the specifics before you commit.
