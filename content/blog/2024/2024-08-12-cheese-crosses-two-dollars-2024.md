Forty-pound Cheddar blocks broke two dollars a pound in the middle of this month, the highest
level in more than a year on the wholesale exchange members watch for pricing cues. If you sell
by the wheel rather than the block, the number itself may not move your invoice, but it moves
the conversation you're having with a buyer this week, and it's worth knowing where it came from.

## Tighter milk, steadier demand

Production has come in a touch below last year's pace for several months running — federal data
put milk output down roughly 166,000 tons over the first half of the year — and the gap has been
enough to keep both cheese and butter firm without any single dramatic event behind it. Average
milk per cow was still edging up nationally, so this isn't a herd-health story; it's a story about
plants competing harder for the milk that exists. Spot milk has been trading over Class price in
a lot of regions lately, which tells you the competition is real rather than seasonal noise. For a
farmstead operation, that's the kind of quiet tightening that shows up as your milk hauler
mentioning a better mailbox price before you see it reflected anywhere official.

Export demand has held up its end too. Mozzarella and Gouda have moved well overseas this year,
and a domestic market that's absorbing higher block prices without much flinching suggests buyers
built room into their forecasts rather than getting caught by it. Cheddar itself had been sitting
just under two dollars back in June before this month's move past it, which is a faster climb
than the summer's earlier, steadier pace. None of this tells you what to charge for a wheel of
washed-rind or a wedge of aged cheddar at your own counter — that's a conversation with your
buyer, not a market report — but it's useful context when that conversation happens.

## What this means at your case

If you've been holding a price from earlier in the year, this is a reasonable week to revisit
it. Buyers who watch the wholesale numbers as closely as you do will not be surprised by an
adjustment right now; they may already be expecting one. If you're the one buying, ask your
supplier what's driving any change before you agree to it — "the block market moved" is a fair
answer, "costs are up" without a number behind it is not, and you're entitled to see which one
you're getting.

It's also worth remembering that a strong wholesale number doesn't automatically translate to a
strong year for every member. The weekly trade press covering this same window noted spot milk
running over Class in region after region — a tightening block market can just as easily squeeze
a small buyer working thin margins on imported styles as it lifts a producer selling direct. If
your business model depends more on what you're paying for milk than what a distributor pays for
block Cheddar, the export and production trends matter more to you than the headline price.

We don't publish dues, wholesale contract terms, or margin targets — those are yours to set with
your own numbers — but the market data behind this post is public, and it's worth five minutes
with your own spreadsheet before your next order goes out. The federation's markets page links to
the same wholesale reports we used here, updated as new numbers come in each week, and the
Membership & Outreach Committee is a good place to ask if a particular number doesn't match what
you're seeing in your own region.
