We asked a specialty cheese buyer at a regional grocery chain — she asked us not to use her name, since she was speaking about her own habits rather than her employer's — to walk us through her membership renewal, and she laughed before we'd finished the question. "I treat it like a dentist appointment. I know it's coming, I know I should just deal with it early, and every year I somehow open the reminder email with a handful of days left in the grace window."

That's not because the membership isn't useful to her. She checks the member directory constantly before placing an order with a new producer, and she's pulled up standards documents mid-negotiation more than once when a supplier's paperwork didn't quite match what they were claiming on a call. This year the renewal notice landed in the middle of back-to-school buying season for her stores, and the timing could not have been worse. Trade coverage this week has been full of cheese-snacking trend pieces — portable, high-protein cheese formats are having a real moment in the lunchbox aisle right now — and that's exactly the category she manages. Her days this month are almost entirely producer calls and shelf resets, not admin.

## What a buyer actually gets from membership

For someone in her seat, the case for renewing isn't sentimental — it's transactional in a way we think more members should hear about. She checks a producer's certification status before including them in a new program. She reads the standards updates because a retail buyer who gets caught recommending a mislabeled product loses trust fast, and that trust is most of what her job actually runs on. None of that requires her to attend a single conference — she told us she's been to exactly one in six years of membership, and mostly for the trade-show floor rather than the sessions.

What almost tipped her renewal into an actual lapse this year, she said, was simple bad timing rather than any doubt about value. "I keep AutoRenew off because I like reviewing the invoice myself before it goes through, which is usually fine, except this year I was buried in orders right when the notice came due. I renewed with about a week of grace left. If it had landed one week later, during the actual back-to-school crunch, I honestly don't know."

## A timing problem, not a value problem

That's a useful data point for us. A member who clearly gets value from the directory and the standards library can still drift close to a lapse purely because of when, in their working year, the renewal notice happens to land. Buyers on the retail side tend to have a predictable crunch every August and again around the winter holidays — exactly the weeks a flat anniversary renewal date is most likely to catch them off guard.

We're taking a look at whether buyer-heavy members would be better served by a renewal window tied to their own sales calendar rather than a fixed anniversary date. Nothing is decided yet, but if that pattern sounds familiar in your own membership, tell your account contact. Small timing fixes are often the easiest churn problem to solve, and they don't require anyone to want membership more than they already do — they just require us to stop asking for a decision during the one month a member has no time to make one.
