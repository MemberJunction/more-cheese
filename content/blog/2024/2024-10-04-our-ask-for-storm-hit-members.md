Federal food-safety rules already carve out real flexibility for small producers — the kind of exemption language that matters enormously to a farmstead creamery that isn't running a facility the size of a national processor. It only helps, though, if the people administering it in the field understand it and apply it consistently. That's the gap we're spending our advocacy time on this week, and it's directly tied to the storm we wrote about earlier this week.

A small-producer exemption written for a normal year doesn't automatically account for what happens when that same producer is rebuilding a barn, running a generator off a trailer, and trying to document a make sheet from memory because the paper copy went under two feet of water. The damage from this storm went well beyond structures — the topsoil itself washed off some fields, which tells you how long a full recovery actually takes and why "back to normal by spring" is not a realistic timeline for everyone. Members in the affected region have told us the paperwork burden of proving continued compliance is, right now, harder than the compliance itself. That's the specific ask we're carrying to the regulator's small-producer liaison desk: temporary, documented flexibility on inspection timing and recordkeeping for operations in a declared disaster area, without touching the underlying safety standard.

## Why we're not asking for a safety rollback

We want to be precise about this, because it matters. We are not asking anyone to relax what actually keeps a wheel of cheese safe to sell. We are asking for practical accommodation on inspection scheduling and paperwork deadlines for members who lost their generator, their internet, or their entire records room to a flood. Those are different requests, and conflating them would cost us credibility we need for the next fight too.

Recordkeeping expectations for small producers have grown more detailed in recent years, including traceability requirements that assume a functioning office, a working printer, and a filing system that didn't just go underwater. None of that is unreasonable in an ordinary year. It becomes a genuine barrier to compliance — not a shortcut around it — when the operation trying to comply is also mucking out a barn.

This sits alongside our longer-running work on interstate cheese shipment rules, where inconsistent state-by-state paperwork already slows a small creamery's ability to sell across a state line in an ordinary week — let alone a week when that creamery is also rebuilding. The storm has made a slow-moving frustration into an urgent one, and we're using this window to push harder on both fronts at once.

## How to add your voice

If your operation has direct, current experience with either the storm's aftermath or the interstate shipment paperwork problem, we want your account for our next petition round — specific, dated, and yours. General support matters too: a signature from a member who hasn't been personally affected still tells a regulator this is a shared industry concern, not one operation's complaint. Reach the Membership & Outreach Committee to add your name, or to flag your own storm story for the record.

We'll keep members updated as this moves. If the liaison desk gives us a real answer one way or the other, you'll hear about it here before you hear about it anywhere else.
