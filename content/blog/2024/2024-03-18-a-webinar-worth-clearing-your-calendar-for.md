Insurance is not a topic anyone joins a cheese federation to talk about, and it is also one of the two or three subjects that generate the most calls to our office every spring. Wednesday's webinar, "Insurance for Creameries," is our attempt to get ahead of that call before it happens, rather than after a claim has already gone sideways.

The session is free and virtual, which was a deliberate choice. Insurance conversations tend to happen member by member, on the phone, after something has already gone wrong — a walk-in cooler compressor failure, a slip-and-fall at a farm stand, a shipment that arrived damaged and the carrier's coverage didn't apply the way anyone assumed it would. A webinar can't replace a broker who knows your operation, but it can make sure everyone in the room is asking their broker the right questions before the next renewal, not the one after that.

## What the hour will actually cover

The session is built around the coverage categories that come up most often in members' calls: general liability for the manufacturing side of the operation, product liability specific to a food business, and the business-owner's-policy bundling that a lot of small creameries default into without checking whether it fits a facility that also runs a retail counter or ships direct to consumers. If your operation does any of those three things — production, retail, and shipping — under one roof, the coverage gaps tend to hide in the seams between them, and that's where this webinar is aimed.

It will not cover crop insurance, dairy revenue protection, or the federal Dairy Margin Coverage program; those are milk-price risk-management tools for producers, a different animal from the liability and property coverage a creamery business needs, and conflating the two is one of the more common mix-ups we hear on the phone. Dairy revenue protection, for instance, sizes its payout off a quarterly coverage endorsement tied to expected milk revenue and a declared share of production — useful if you're worried about a price swing, useless if what actually worries you is a compressor failure or a customer injury at your retail counter. If margin coverage is what you're after, the Farm Service Agency's enrollment window for this cycle is open now through late April, and that's a conversation for your local FSA office, not this webinar.

## Who gets the most out of it

Newer members and members who've recently added a retail or shipping line to an existing production operation will likely get the most out of the hour — that's exactly the moment when an old policy stops matching the actual business. Members who've been through a renewal cycle or two with their broker already may find less new information here, but the Q&A portion tends to surface specific scenarios worth bringing to your own coverage review regardless of how long you've been at this.

Registration is free and the session records for members who can't make the live time. If you've been meaning to ask your broker a specific question and haven't found the right moment, Wednesday's an easy one to build around — bring the question to the Q&A, or bring it to your broker the following week armed with better language for what you're actually trying to cover.

One more thing worth saying plainly: nothing in this webinar, or in this post, is a substitute for a licensed broker reviewing your actual policy. The goal is narrower than that. It's to make sure you walk into your next renewal conversation knowing which categories of coverage apply to which part of your operation, so you're asking about the right gap instead of discovering it later. If your operation has changed in the past year — a new retail counter, a new shipping lane, a piece of equipment that would be expensive to replace — that change is reason enough to sit in on Wednesday's session even if your last renewal felt fine.
