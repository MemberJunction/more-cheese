Registration for this year's Annual Conference opened back in May, and if you registered that first week, you can probably stop reading here and go check your hotel reservation instead. For everyone still deciding, four weeks out is exactly the point where "I'll get to it" starts costing you money and options.

Room blocks at the conference hotel are the first thing to go, and this year is no exception — the discounted block has been thinning out for the last two weeks. If you haven't booked, do it this week rather than next. The overflow hotels a short walk away are fine, but they add fifteen minutes to your morning and they don't come with the block rate.

## What's already decided, and what still needs a decision

The general sessions and the flagship competition awards are set; those don't require any action from you beyond showing up. What still needs a decision is your workshop track. Conference runs concurrent sessions across affinage, food safety, and business-of-cheese tracks, and the popular slots — anything touching cave management or pricing strategy — have historically filled before the doors open. If there's a specific session you don't want to miss, put it on your personal schedule now rather than deciding in the hallway.

If you're planning to enter the flagship competition, the entry window we wrote about last month closes before conference itself, not at check-in — double-check that deadline separately from your travel planning, because they are not the same date and treating them as one has tripped up more than one first-time entrant.

This year's trade press has been full of talk about specialty cheese regaining shelf space after a couple of quieter years, and conference programming leans into that: several of the business-track sessions are built around what a growing case means for a small producer's pricing and wholesale relationships, not just for the large brands that usually get the headlines. If that's the conversation you came for, it's worth building your Friday around the business track rather than splitting your day evenly across all three.

## The parts people forget until they're already there

A few practical notes from past years, for anyone attending for the first time. Bring a way to keep samples cold if you're carrying product for informal tasting outside the competition hall; the venue doesn't provide cold storage for anything that isn't an official entry. If you're presenting at a workshop or bringing materials for a table, confirm shipping deadlines with the events team directly — conference week is the wrong time to discover a box is still in transit.

For members who've never been, conference is also simply the best place all year to put a face to the names on a mailing list. Committee members are easy to find between sessions, and if you've been meaning to ask someone on the Standards Committee a question about a certification pathway, or introduce yourself to the Events Committee about a workshop idea for next year, this is the week to do it.

Four weeks is enough time to still get a good rate, a workshop seat that matters to you, and a plan for what you're bringing. It's not enough time to leave all three until the week before. Book the room this week, pick your track this weekend, and we'll see you at conference.
