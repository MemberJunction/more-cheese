Members keep telling us the same thing this spring, from a farmstead in the upper Midwest to a
shop counter on the coast: buyers are trading up. Not everywhere, and not on every shelf, but the
specialty case is pulling a bigger share of the cheese dollar than it was a year ago, and it's
holding through a season that isn't normally a growth season for artisan product.

Trade press has been circling the same story for a few months now — specialty and artisan brands
gaining meaningfully across grocery, club, and independent channels, even as the broader cheese
aisle grows more slowly overall. Retailers who've been most successful at capturing that growth
are the ones organizing their cases around an occasion — a grilling weekend, a gathering, a gift —
rather than just grouping wheels by type and letting a shopper sort it out alone. The federation
doesn't publish market-share figures of its own, so we won't put a decimal point on any of this.
What we can say, from what members are reporting on calls and at regional meetups, is that it
tracks with what's happening at their registers: smaller formats, higher price points, and repeat
buyers who ask about affinage and provenance instead of just price per pound.

## Why this trade-up looks sturdier than the last one

The federation has seen "artisan is having a moment" stories before, usually tied to a single
holiday push or a magazine feature that fades by February. What members are describing this year
reads differently — steadier reorders, buyers who come back asking for the same maker by name, and
counters that are willing to carry a slower-moving wheel because the margin on it justifies the
shelf space. If your case has room for one more slow seller this summer, this is probably the
season to try it.

There's a milk-price backdrop worth naming plainly, without dressing it up. Farm-level prices have
been firmer this year in several markets we track through public reporting, which changes the math
on how a small creamery prices its own wheels relative to commodity cheese. If your cost of milk
has moved, your price list should have moved with it — and if it hasn't, this is a good week to
sit down with your margin numbers before the summer rush picks up further.

## What we're asking members to watch

A trade-up in specialty cheese is good news for member creameries, but it also means more scrutiny
from buyers who are spending more per pound and expect more in return: clean labeling, a
consistent flavor profile batch to batch, and a story they can repeat to their own customers
without embellishing it. If your labeling or your make-sheet documentation hasn't kept pace with
your growth, our Standards Committee has fielded a steady stream of member questions on exactly
this over the past year — reach out through the member portal if you want a second set of eyes.

One member who runs a small retail cheese program told us she's already planning her fall order
around the assumption that this trade-up sticks rather than fades, which is a bet, but not a
reckless one given what she's seeing at her own register week over week.

We'll keep tracking this one. If your creamery or shop has a trade-up story — good or otherwise —
we'd like to hear it for a future post.
