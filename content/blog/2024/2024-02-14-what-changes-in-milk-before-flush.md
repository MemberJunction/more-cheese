If your make sheet has been fighting you for the last couple of weeks and you can't figure out why, check your calving calendar before you check your culture. A lot of farmstead operations concentrate calving in late winter so fresh milk lines up with spring pasture, and that means the milk coming into your vat right now is coming from a herd with a lot of animals in early lactation at once. Early-lactation milk runs differently — higher in some solids, lower in others, and more variable cow to cow — and a recipe that's been dialed in all fall can suddenly need adjusting for reasons that have nothing to do with your technique.

This is exactly the gap that Milk Quality & Herd Health is built to close, and if you've never taken it, this is the season it pays off fastest. The course doesn't teach veterinary medicine — nobody walks out of it managing herd health themselves — but it teaches cheesemakers to read the handful of signals that actually show up in a vat: somatic cell count trends, colostrum carryover in early pooled milk, and the composition swings that come with a herd moving through a concentrated transition period. One member who took the course last year told us the single most useful thing it gave her was permission to stop assuming a bad set was her fault when the culprit was three weeks of transition-period milk she hadn't accounted for.

## The transition period is a herd-wide event, not a per-cow one

The three weeks after calving are when a cow's system is under the most strain, and it's also when the biggest share of herd health issues tend to show up — retained placenta, milk fever, the metabolic stuff that farm managers are watching closely but that a cheesemaker two buildings away might not hear about until the milk arrives different. If your farmstead runs its own herd, close the loop: ask whoever manages calving to flag which pens are in early lactation this week, and treat that milk as its own batch mentally, even if it's not physically separated. If you buy from a co-op or a specific supplying farm, ask the same question of them. A five-minute conversation now saves a ruined vat later.

## What actually to watch for on your end

Elevated somatic cell counts and small shifts in protein-to-fat ratio are the two things most likely to move during a concentrated calving window, and both change how a curd sets. If your coagulation times have been drifting longer or your curd has felt softer than usual at cut, that's worth checking against your supplier's calving schedule before you start adjusting culture percentages or rennet dose — you might be solving the wrong problem. The course spends real time on this exact troubleshooting sequence: what to check first, second, and third, in an order that doesn't waste a batch guessing.

This winter's milk production numbers came in a little lower than forecasters expected, even as the year's overall price picture has looked more encouraging than the last two winters. For most of you that means slightly tighter supply from your usual sources right when a good share of that supply is also mid-transition — a combination worth watching rather than one to panic over.
