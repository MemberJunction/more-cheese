Federal rules on interstate shipment of raw-milk cheese have sat mostly still for decades: cheese made from unpasteurized milk can cross state lines as long as it's aged at least sixty days and labeled accordingly, a compromise struck long before most of our current members joined the trade. That rule is under real discussion again this year, with more than one legislative proposal circulating that would loosen it further for small producers, and a high-profile enforcement dispute working through the courts that's kept the underlying question in the news. We're not going to weigh in on that specific case here — it's a live legal matter, not our lane — but the broader shipment question is squarely ours, and it's one our members have strong, and sometimes conflicting, views on.

## Why this one is harder than it looks

Most of our advocacy work has an easy consensus behind it. This doesn't, entirely. Producers making aged raw-milk cheese for out-of-state buyers want a more workable shipment rule, full stop — the current sixty-day floor already covers most classic styles, but a small producer trying to ship something aged less than that has no legal path at all, regardless of testing record. On the other side, some of our members who buyer-side or run retail counters have told us they'd rather see any loosening paired with clearer, standardized testing requirements than trust labeling alone. Both positions are reasonable, and we've heard both loudly enough that we're not pretending there's a single "member position" to hand a legislator.

Tom Reyes, one of our longest-active members on the advocacy side, has sat through more of these hearings at the state level than almost anyone we know, and his read on this round matches something he's said about past raw-milk fights: proposals that pair looser shipment rules with specific, written-in testing schedules tend to hold up better than ones that just remove a restriction and leave the safety question to a later regulation. That's not a position we're asking every member to adopt, but it's worth hearing from someone who's watched more than one version of this argument play out.

## What we're actually asking for

Rather than pick a side our membership doesn't agree on, we're asking the federation's comment to reflect the range of member experience honestly: real appetite among small producers for a workable shipment path, real caution from the retail and distribution side about testing consistency, and a request that whatever passes be specific enough to survive a first legal challenge rather than getting written broadly and litigated for years afterward. If you make, ship, buy, or sell aged raw-milk cheese across state lines and have a view — either direction — we want it before the comment window closes.

Submitting testimony doesn't require a lawyer or a polished statement. A short account of how the current rule actually affects your operation, in your own words, carries real weight with a committee that mostly hears from people with a financial stake dressed up as a policy argument. The Membership & Outreach Committee can walk you through how to submit, and the advocacy page has the current filing deadline.
