We get a version of the same question a few times a year: "I know cheese. Why would I need a credential to sell it?" It's a fair question, and it's also a slightly wrong one, because the Certified Cheesemonger credential isn't really testing what you know about cheese. It's testing whether you can run a case.

That distinction matters more this year than it might have a few years ago. Retail buyers keep telling us their case has gotten more competitive — more producers wanting shelf space, more customers who've read about a cheese online before they ever walk in, more pressure to turn product before it's past its best. A counter person who can talk knowledgeably about a wheel but can't rotate stock, manage shrink, or build a case that sells itself is leaving money on the table. That's the gap this credential is built to close.

## What's actually on it

The Certified Cheesemonger curriculum breaks down into four pieces, and only one of them is what most people expect. There's cut-and-wrap technique, which sounds basic until you've watched someone lose margin to sloppy portioning for a year without noticing. There's storage and rotation — humidity, temperature, first-in-first-out discipline, and how to read a wheel that's starting to go past its window before a customer does. There's customer guidance, which is the part people assume is the whole credential: how to ask the right two or three questions and land on a recommendation the customer actually likes, rather than the recommendation you'd pick for yourself. And there's building a balanced case, which is closer to merchandising than tasting — price points, styles, and turnover working together instead of against each other.

Four years of validity is on the shorter side of our credential ladder, which is intentional. Case management practices shift with what's selling, and a credential that never asks you to update your thinking isn't worth much to the member or to the customer standing at the counter.

"I'd been mongering for six years before I got certified," one member told us this winter, "and I still learned things about rotation I'd been doing wrong the whole time." That's a common reaction, and it's not a knock on experience — it's what a credential built around a specific job, rather than general cheese knowledge, is supposed to do.

## Who it tends to fit

This credential has no prerequisite, which makes it one of the more accessible entries in our program, but that doesn't mean it fits everyone the same way. It's built for people whose job is the counter, not the cave — retail staff, shop owners, and the newer hires you want handling your case with confidence inside their first year. If your interest runs more toward make procedures and affinage, the foundation certificate followed by our broader professional credential is probably the better path; think of Certified Cheesemonger as the credential for the side of the business that faces the customer.

If you're deciding whether now's the time, the market conditions we've been watching this winter make a decent case for sooner rather than later. Cases are getting more competitive, and a credentialed team is one of the cheaper ways to compete without cutting price. Reach out to the Education Committee if you want to talk through timing, or look at the current course listings if you're ready to enroll a member of your staff this spring.
