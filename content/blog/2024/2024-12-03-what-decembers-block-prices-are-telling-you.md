If your case looked busier this week than your invoices felt heavier, you're not imagining it. Block cheese prices have been feeling downward pressure into this week even as butter and nonfat dry milk prices moved higher, a split that's been unusual enough that market watchers have flagged it. Meanwhile the cheese boards are out, the gift boxes are moving, and the counter traffic is doing exactly what it does every December. Softer block prices and strong sell-through aren't a contradiction — they're just two different clocks, and it's worth being clear about which one you're reading before you plan January.

## Why the two numbers are moving apart

Cheese is still the category holding up American dairy consumption overall — per-capita cheese consumption rose again last year, one of the few dairy categories still climbing while categories like ice cream slipped. That's the encouraging backdrop. But federal livestock and dairy outlook data released heading into this week points to a softer near-term picture for block prices specifically, with cheese values under more pressure than other dairy commodities even as overall demand for the category stays healthy. Steady household demand meeting a softer wholesale price is part of why block prices have looked weak even while other dairy commodities firmed up.

For members with a wholesale block business, that combination has meant slimmer, harder-to-predict margins the last few weeks. For members leaning into specialty and direct sales — the segment where most of ICF's membership growth has landed over the past two artisan-boom years — holiday cheese-board demand looks to be carrying the quarter regardless of what the commodity numbers are doing. Export volumes have also stayed strong this year, a reminder that "flat domestic demand" doesn't mean flat demand everywhere. It tracks with what we're hearing from members: the wedge business is fine, the block business is the one that needs watching.

## What to do with a soft block market in December

A few practical notes we'd pass along:

- **Don't let a soft commodity print talk you into a soft holiday price.** Your cheese-board customer isn't pricing against the wholesale block quote; they're pricing against the wedge next to yours on the shelf.
- **If you sell any raw milk or bulk cheese on a formula tied to a commodity index, model out where January and February land**, not just this week. Wholesale block softness can persist well past the holidays even when household demand stays strong.
- **Watch your own margin, not the headline.** Flat commodity cheese demand is a wholesale-side problem first; it doesn't automatically show up in a specialty counter's numbers.

None of this is a call to panic — flat commodity demand alongside strong holiday specialty sales is a split we've seen before. But December numbers get read wrong more often than any other month's, mostly because two different markets are moving in opposite directions at once. Worth separating them before you close out the year.
