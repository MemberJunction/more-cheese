We've had more questions about the Certified Cheesemonger credential this month than any point in recent memory, and the timing makes sense. Cases are busier, customers are asking harder questions, and counter staff are the ones fielding them in real time. So this week's post is the plain answer to the question we keep getting: what does this credential actually cover, and is it the right one to start with.

## What it tests, and what it doesn't

Certified Cheesemonger is built around the counter, not the cave. It covers handling and storage at retail, cutting and wrapping technique, pairing knowledge across major styles, and the customer-facing skills that turn a browsing shopper into someone who trusts your recommendation. It does not test make-day production, herd management, or plant food-safety systems — that's deliberate. The credential is scoped to what happens after a wheel arrives at your case, not before.

That scoping is also what separates it from Certified Cheese Professional (CCP), which sits a level up and expects broader business and production knowledge alongside the retail skills. Members sometimes ask whether they should skip straight to the CCP. Our answer is almost always no, unless you already have production-side experience elsewhere — Certified Cheesemonger is the credential that matches what a counter role actually demands day to day, and the CCP tends to reward people who've already spent time on both sides of the operation.

## Why this is a good year to start

The specialty case has been growing faster than usual this year, by most accounts we've seen in the trade press, and growth at the counter tends to bring more first-time specialty shoppers who don't know what they're asking for yet. That's exactly the moment a credentialed monger earns their keep — not by knowing the rarest cheese in the case, but by asking the right two questions and landing on something the customer takes home happily. If your shop has been busier without adding staff, the people you already have are the ones absorbing that gap, and the credential gives them a structured way to close it rather than learning entirely on the fly.

The most common path we see is a counter employee with six months to a year of hands-on experience enrolling alongside a manager who's already credentialed, so there's someone on shift to talk through the study material in real terms rather than in the abstract. Our Education Committee's course catalog includes a self-paced prep track built specifically for that pairing, and it's worth checking whether your shop already has someone who could mentor a newer hire through it this fall.

One honest note: this credential, like our others, tests knowledge and judgment, not food safety compliance for your specific facility. It's a good complement to your Food Safety & HACCP training, not a substitute for it, and the two are meant to be held together rather than treated as either-or.

If you're deciding whether now is the right time, we'd say yes, mostly because the demand is already showing up at your counter whether or not your staff feels ready for it. Registration details and the current exam window are posted in the member portal under Certifications, and our Education Committee can answer questions about which track fits a specific role.
