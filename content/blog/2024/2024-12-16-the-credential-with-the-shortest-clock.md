Every credential in our ladder has its own expiration rhythm, and members mostly notice the one that's about to lapse on them. Right now that's the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate. It's the shortest-validity credential we offer, good for two years instead of the Certified Cheese Professional's three or the Certified Cheesemonger's four, and if you earned yours in a busy December two winters ago, this is the month it's asking for attention.

The short cycle isn't an oversight. Hazard analysis and environmental monitoring practice actually moves: new pathogen data, new testing protocols, new recall patterns show up faster in food safety than they do in, say, affinage technique. A credential that certified you once and left you alone for five years would be teaching you less than the industry has learned since. Two years keeps the material honest, even when it means more paperwork on your end.

## What recertification actually checks

The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate covers hazard analysis, environmental monitoring, and recall readiness, scaled for small and mid-sized creameries rather than industrial plants. Recertification isn't a rubber stamp. You'll be asked to show that your monitoring plan reflects your current facility, not the one you had when you first tested, and that you can walk through a mock recall without reaching for a script.

If your plan hasn't been touched since the last time you sat for this, that's the tell. A cave that changed layout, a new washed-rind line, a second employee handling raw milk receiving, all of that should show up in an updated hazard analysis before you resubmit. Our Listeria Control in Small Creameries course is built to walk directly into this recertification; it's not a substitute for the exam, but it will save you from relearning your own monitoring plan from scratch under deadline pressure.

Two-year and three-year certificates don't expire in neat annual waves; they land whenever they land. But a lot of members earned their first HACCP credential in a December cohort, back when the flagship conference calendar pushed a run of food-safety sessions into late fall. If that's you, the notice in your portal isn't random timing. It's this cohort's turn.

Don't let a holiday-week inbox bury it. Recertification takes longer than people expect when it's rushed, mostly because pulling together a year's worth of monitoring records in one sitting is its own project. Give yourself the two or three weeks before your expiration date, not the two or three days.

If you're not sure when your certificate expires, that's a five-minute look at your member portal, not a guess. And if you're holding a HACCP certificate for the first time and wondering what's next, the Certified Cheese Professional pathway builds on it rather than duplicating it.

It's worth saying, too: none of this is happening against a backdrop of members pulling back. Specialty cheese has kept growing through 2024, and members tell us the counter conversation now includes shoppers actively asking about production practices, not just flavor. A credential renewal isn't just a compliance chore in that environment; it's one of the few concrete things you can point to when a buyer or a customer asks how your operation actually handles risk.

## The part that's easy to skip

The piece members most often shortcut isn't the paperwork, it's the mock recall walkthrough. It feels like the least consequential part of the exam because nothing is actually wrong; you're narrating a hypothetical. But a recall you've only ever rehearsed on paper moves differently than one you've talked through out loud, under a clock, with someone asking "and then what" after every step. Treat that section like the rest of it counts, because it's usually the part that surfaces gaps a written monitoring plan hides.

It's also worth reading this cycle's material even if you're confident nothing in your operation changed. Regulatory guidance around cheese-specific risk categories has kept shifting in small ways over the past couple of years, and a monitoring plan that was airtight two years ago can be quietly out of step with current expectations without anyone in the building noticing. Recertification is the one built-in moment where you're required to check.

One more practical note: if your certificate expires between now and the end of January, don't wait on scheduling your exam slot. Our proctoring calendar thins out over the holidays the same way everyone else's does, and the last thing you want is a lapsed credential because the only open slot was three weeks past your deadline. Book it now, do the review at your own pace, and treat the deadline as the backstop rather than the plan.
