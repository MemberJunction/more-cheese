Cheese has become a gift category in its own right this year, not just a hostess add-on, and the shops carrying our members' wheels are reorganizing their cases to match. A widely shared holiday gift guide published in mid-November catalogued advent calendars and curated multi-wheel boxes from grocers and specialty shops alike, and the roundup itself is a signal: cheese is being covered as the gift, not as a side dish to one.

For a specialty producer or a counter that sells direct, that's a real opening this week, and also a real logistics question.

## What "gifting demand" means for a small producer

Reporting on the season has been consistent: buyers are choosing gifts around experience and connection more than novelty, and a well-made regional cheese fits that instinct better than most impulse items on a shelf. That's good news for members whose whole business is a specific style done well — but it also means a first-time gift buyer is judging your product on presentation as much as flavor, because they're often buying for someone whose palate they don't fully know. If you sell direct, this is the week to make the gifting use case obvious rather than assumed. A short card that says "good with a dark ale, keeps two weeks wrapped in the fridge" does more to move a gift-box sale than a technical tasting note aimed at someone who already knows cheese. Buyers this season also skew toward researching gifts on a phone before they walk in or order online, so if your online listing photo shows the wheel loose on a board rather than boxed and ready to give, it's worth a quick update before the weekend rush.

## Production side: this is not the week to guess on inventory

Federal regulators also finalized changes to the milk pricing formulas that set Class I and Class III prices this month, a reminder that a wheel's cost basis can move for reasons that have nothing to do with your case. That's a mixed signal worth watching rather than reacting to. It doesn't change what's already aging in your cave, but it's a reasonable prompt to double-check supplier lead times if you're buying in cultures, rennet, or packaging materials for a holiday production push, since a broader market tightening can show up first as a supplier backorder rather than a headline. If you're deciding how much of a seasonal favorite to hold back for walk-in gift sales versus committing to a wholesale order, the general guidance from members who've been through a few holiday seasons is to err toward holding a little more back than feels comfortable. A sold-out gift case two weeks before the holiday is a worse outcome than an early wholesale shipment that's slightly smaller than requested.

This Saturday is Small Business Saturday, which is typically one of the strongest single days of the year for members who sell direct to the public. If your case, your online listings, and your staff's pitch aren't already telling a clear gifting story, this is the week to fix that — not the week after. And if you're weighing a price change on a seasonal item in response to any of this, remember that dues, program pricing, and your own cost structure are separate conversations; nothing here is a pricing recommendation, just a read on where demand is pointed. For members deciding how to stage a gift case for the first time, the recording from last week's Farm Shop Design webinar is a good next stop — it covers case layout and labeling in more depth than we have room for here.
