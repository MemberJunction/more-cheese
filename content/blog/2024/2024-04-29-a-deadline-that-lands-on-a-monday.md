If you make cheese from your own herd's milk, today is the day the federal margin protection program closes its enrollment window for the year. We've said it twice already this month in the member newsletter, and we're saying it again because every spring a handful of farmstead members call us the week after the deadline, not the week before.

The program pays out when the gap between the national all-milk price and feed costs narrows past a threshold the farm chose when it signed up. It isn't dairy insurance in the way a building policy is insurance — you're not filing a claim after a bad month, you're locked into a coverage level you picked back in February, and the payment, if any, arrives on its own schedule. Two of the four payment months already triggered this year at solid margins, which is exactly why we keep saying: sign up even in a year that looks fine. Last year the margin fell below the $4-per-hundredweight mark for two straight months for the first time since the program began, and coverage triggered across most of the year — the kind of stretch that makes a farm glad it filled out the form back when nothing looked wrong yet. The years that look fine in January are not always the years that look fine in October.

This cycle's enrollment also carries a genuinely useful change for the smallest operations: a farm producing under five million pounds a year can make a one-time adjustment to its production history, so the coverage reflects what the herd is actually milking now rather than a number set years ago. There's also a no-cost coverage tier for beginning, limited-resource, and veteran producers, which several of our newer farmstead members have used to get a baseline of protection while they're still building up cash reserves for anything more.

## What our advocacy members have been telling us

Members with the least slack in their books are usually the ones who put off the paperwork longest, and that's backwards. A small farmstead operation with five people wearing six hats doesn't have a full-time bookkeeper checking a federal deadline calendar; it has whoever's free between milking and the next make. Our grassroots advocacy volunteers — the members who show up to comment periods and county hearings on their own time — have been the loudest voice pushing us to send more reminders, not fewer, because they're the ones who see a neighbor miss it by a week and lose a year of protection over nothing more than a full inbox.

If today is the day you're reading this and you haven't filed, the enrollment window closes at your local Farm Service Agency office at the end of business, not at midnight online, so don't assume you have until you fall asleep. Existing coverage from a prior year doesn't automatically roll forward at the level you'd choose today; the production history adjustment available this cycle can matter a lot for a smaller operation, and it's worth five minutes with your local office to check before you skip it.

## Beyond today

For members whose window has already closed one way or the other, the conversation doesn't end here. Our advocacy work at the federal and state level is a standing effort, not a once-a-year push, and testimony from working cheesemakers is the thing that actually moves a hearing room, more than any position paper we could write on our own letterhead. If you've testified before, or you've been meaning to, our Membership & Outreach Committee keeps a running list of the state agriculture committees taking public comment this year — reach out and we'll get you on it.
