A quality manager we'll call Renata has been a federation member for six years, and for most of that time her profile has been accurate the way most people's profiles are accurate: without anyone thinking about it. That changed at the start of this year, when she moved from a mid-sized wholesale creamery to a quality manager role at a smaller farmstead operation two states over. Eight months later, her federation listing still names her old employer.

"It's not that I forgot," she told us. "It's that updating it has never once been the most urgent thing on a Tuesday." That's a familiar shape of problem for anyone who manages membership data at scale — a directory listing doesn't break anything the day it goes stale. It breaks something three months later, when a different member is trying to find a quality manager with her exact background and the search turns up an employer she hasn't worked for since winter.

## Why the small update matters more than it looks

Renata's day job gives her a useful vantage point on this. As a quality manager, she spends a good part of her week reconciling records that drift the same way her own membership listing did: a supplier certification that expired but wasn't flagged, a batch code that got entered wrong once and then copied forward for months. "The failure mode is always the same," she said. "Nobody decided the record should be wrong. It just stopped getting looked at." She said updating her own listing has been on a mental list since roughly the week she started her new job, filed somewhere just behind everything else that had an actual deadline attached to it.

What finally moved it up the list, she said, was a smaller and slightly embarrassing moment. A fellow member reached out through the directory hoping to ask her about an environmental monitoring protocol, using her old employer's name, and the message sat unanswered for a week because it routed to an inbox she doesn't check anymore. "That's the cost," she said. "Not a system failure. Just a conversation that didn't happen when it should have." She's since told two colleagues at her new employer to check their own listings, on the theory that if her profile drifted unnoticed, theirs probably did too.

## What we'd ask every member to check

Renata's situation isn't unusual, and it isn't really about her specifically. Job changes, title changes, and even a new email domain are common enough across a membership our size that we'd rather ask every member to spend five minutes checking their own listing than assume the gap is rare. Log into the member portal, confirm your current employer, title, and preferred contact email are correct, and update anything that's drifted since you last looked. If you've moved recently and you're not sure what's changed on your record, Membership & Outreach can pull your current listing and walk through it with you.

None of this requires a certification or a course, and it isn't a data problem unique to any one member. It's exactly the kind of five-minute task that, as Renata would be the first to admit, is easy to keep meaning to do — right up until the week a message goes unanswered because of it.
