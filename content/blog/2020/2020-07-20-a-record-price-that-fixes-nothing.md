Block cheddar on the commodity exchange touched a price this month that nobody on our advisory calls had seen before — a level that would have sounded absurd back in February. Several members have asked us, half-joking, whether this means the hard year is suddenly over. It doesn't, and it's worth walking through why.

The short version: the number on the board is a spot price for standardized block cheddar, set by a small number of trades in a thin, jumpy market. It moved because federal order milk pricing lurched upward in June after months of the opposite problem, and because buyers scrambling to restock after the spring's food-service collapse hit a market that hadn't rebuilt normal inventory. It is real, it is dramatic, and it is not the price most of our members are actually getting for their cheese.

## Two prices, still not talking to each other

If you're a small or mid-size producer selling direct, through a regional distributor, or into retail on a standing contract, this spike mostly passed you by. Your price is set by whatever agreement you signed months ago, and if anything you're now facing a higher milk cost without a matching bump in what you're paid for the finished wheel. We heard this from more than one member this week — the futures board says one thing, the invoice says another, and the gap between them is coming straight out of margin.

If you're selling into a channel that prices off the spot market — some bulk cheese, some private-label contracts — you may see a short-term benefit, but it will likely be just as short-lived as the spike itself. Commodity board watchers we've spoken with this summer describe the swings as a market still trying to find a floor after food service disappeared and came back unevenly, not a durable repricing of cheese.

Milk production itself hasn't slowed to match any of this. Farm-level milk prices have recovered from spring's lows as a temporary production-discipline mechanism dairy cooperatives had been using earlier in the year wound down, which means more milk is moving through the system right now, not less. That's part of why a headline-grabbing block price and a soft cash market for surplus cheese are both true at the same time.

## What we're telling members

Don't restructure a contract, a staffing plan, or a big capital purchase around a single week's commodity print. If you sell direct or on contract, keep asking your buyer what they're actually paying and why, rather than accepting the board number as a proxy for your reality. If you're negotiating a new contract this quarter, this is a reasonable moment to ask for a review clause tied to an index you both trust, rather than a fixed number that will look wrong again within a season.

The Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding pricing questions all summer and can connect you with members in a similar channel to compare notes — informally, without disclosing anyone's actual numbers. If your cash flow is getting squeezed by the gap between milk cost and contract price, say so sooner rather than later; we'd rather help you renegotiate in August than watch you absorb it silently through the fall.
