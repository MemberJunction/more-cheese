A member on our Membership & Outreach Committee put us in touch with a wholesale sales rep
this week who's spent about a decade building a route out of white-tablecloth restaurants
and a handful of hotel banquet kitchens — the kind of accounts that order in cases and
don't blink at a premium wheel. In March, most of that route closed within about ten days
of each other.

"I had a list of around sixty accounts," she told us. "By the end of March I could count on
one hand who was still placing orders."

## Building a new list from scratch

Rather than wait for restaurants to reopen, she spent April doing something she says she
hadn't done since her first year in the job: cold-calling grocery buyers she'd never worked
with. Independent grocers, a couple of regional chains, one co-op she'd driven past for
years without stopping in. Retail cheese demand held up through the shutdown in a way
foodservice didn't, and that gap is exactly where she went looking for a new list.

It wasn't a clean pivot. Grocery buyers order differently than restaurant chefs — smaller
individual cuts, tighter margins, a much longer conversation about shelf placement and
label design before anyone commits to a first order. She says the learning curve cost her
most of April in frustration and a few orders she underpriced just to get a foot in the
door. By May the newer accounts were starting to catch up to what she'd lost, and June has
been stronger still, as the cheese market itself has tightened up considerably since the
spring glut — a sharp reversal from where things stood two months ago. Industry risk
reporting circulating this month has also been pushing wholesale buyers toward suppliers
who can show a steadier, more diversified customer base, which hasn't hurt her case with
a couple of the larger chains.

## What she'd tell another member doing the same thing

Her advice isn't complicated, but she says it took her longer than she'd like to admit to
believe it: the sales skills transfer, but the relationship doesn't. You can't treat a new
grocery buyer like a restaurant chef you've known for a decade, and trying to rush that
familiarity backfires. She also admits she undercharged out of anxiety more than once this
spring, quoting a nervous discount nobody asked her for, and had to walk a couple of prices
back up once she saw what the account was actually worth.

She isn't predicting her restaurant accounts will fully return this year, and she's not
waiting around to find out. She's kept every grocery contact from this spring in a separate
list from her old restaurant book, on the theory that whichever channel recovers slower,
she wants a foothold in the other one already built.

If you're rebuilding a sales territory from scratch the way she did, the Membership &
Outreach Committee keeps a running list of members willing to compare notes on channel
shifts — reach out if you want an introduction, or if your own pivot looked nothing like
this one and you think other members should hear about it.
