If your case looks a little more picked-over than usual this week, you're not imagining it. The stretch before Valentine's Day has become one of the busiest small windows on the specialty calendar, and the last couple of years have made it busier still, because the thing most people are actually buying isn't a single wedge — it's a board. Pre-built or build-your-own, the cheese-and-charcuterie board has quietly become one of the fastest-growing categories at the counter, and members who've leaned into it are telling us it's no longer a seasonal blip they staff around once a year.

The shift has been building for a while. Coverage heading into this year has pointed to a specialty cheese market that keeps expanding on the strength of exactly this kind of pairing and presentation, not just straight wedge sales, and retail trade writers have been tracking the same pattern from the other side of the counter: charcuterie moving from a niche deli offering to a category retailers are actively investing shelf space and staff training in. Put those two views together and you get a pretty clear picture — the growth in specialty cheese over the past several years has ridden the board trend at least as much as it's ridden any single style or region.

## What that means for how you staff this week

For members running a retail counter, the practical question isn't whether boards are popular — it's whether your case and your staff are set up to sell them well under a deadline. A customer buying for Friday night wants guidance, not just inventory: what pairs with what, how much per person, what holds up if it sits out on a table for two hours. If your counter staff can answer those questions quickly and with confidence, you'll move more product and send people home happier than if they're left guessing in front of the case. This is a good week to make sure everyone on the floor, not just your most experienced cheesemonger, can walk a customer through a simple pairing without hesitating.

For members who wholesale into retailers rather than sell directly, this is also a useful moment to ask your accounts what they're featuring for the holiday and whether your product is positioned for it. A wheel that photographs well and cuts into clean wedges has an advantage in a board-driven display that a technically excellent but visually plain cheese doesn't always get credit for — worth knowing if you're deciding what to push into this particular week's orders.

None of this is a reason to chase a trend you don't actually believe in. If boards aren't where your business lives, a two-week spike around Valentine's Day isn't worth restructuring your case for. But if you've noticed board-style purchases creeping up as a share of your counter sales over the past year or two, this week is as good a confirmation as any that it's not a fluke — it's where a meaningful part of the growth in this category is actually coming from. We'd rather you plan around that on purpose than notice it only after the case is empty on a Thursday afternoon.
