A new comment period on raw-milk aging requirements opened earlier this month, and it's already generating more mail to the Food Safety Committee than anything else on the docket. The proposal, at its core, is about how aging periods for raw-milk cheese get measured and documented — not whether raw-milk cheese is allowed, which is a separate question decided state by state.

For members who've been through a rulemaking cycle before, this will feel familiar: a technical-sounding change that reads as minor in a summary and turns out to matter a great deal once you get to the paperwork. The proposal would tighten how a facility has to log and verify the start date of an aging clock, with more frequent temperature and time documentation than the current baseline. For a large commercial plant with a dedicated quality team, that's an adjustment. For a two- or three-person farmstead operation, it can mean rebuilding a recordkeeping system from scratch in the middle of a production season.

## Why this one is different from past cycles

Most aging-threshold proposals we've tracked have moved slowly, with comment windows stretching well past the original deadline. This one has a tighter clock — the window closes in six weeks, not the usual twelve — which has caught a few members off guard. If you make raw-milk cheese and haven't started drafting comments yet, now is the time, not next month.

We'd also note that dairy pricing has been unusually soft to start the year, driven by a slower move in cheese prices generally. That's not directly related to the aging-threshold proposal, but it's part of why the timing feels tight for smaller operations: a compliance cost lands harder in a month when margins are already thinner than they were in the fall.

Members who've filed comments before tell us the same thing every cycle: specificity beats polish. A regulator reading a hundred submissions remembers the one that describes an actual make schedule, an actual cave, an actual complication — not the one that restates the proposal back in formal language. If you've never submitted testimony, the advocacy page on the member portal has a short guide and a list of what the comment needs to include to be considered.

## What we're hearing from the docket so far

Early submissions have focused on two things: the herd-size threshold below which the new logging requirement wouldn't apply, and whether an existing paper-based system (rather than a digital one) would still satisfy the rule. Neither question has a clear answer yet, which is exactly the kind of gap a well-written comment can help close. A proposal that's ambiguous about paper-based systems is a proposal that a working farmstead can still shape.

The Food Safety Committee is compiling a summary of member concerns to submit alongside individual comments, but a committee letter doesn't replace an individual one — regulators weigh volume and specificity together, and a form letter from an association carries less than fifty operators describing fifty different aging rooms. If your operation would be affected and you haven't drafted something yet, the committee can help you find the right section of the proposal to respond to; reach out before the window narrows further.
