Right before members scattered for the holidays, a new continental trade agreement cleared its first legislative hurdle with a wide margin, and a final vote is expected early this year. We've been tracking this one since spring because the dairy language in it, once it's fully in force, touches a fair number of our exporting members directly. If you sell cheese across either northern or southern land borders, this is worth five minutes now rather than five minutes when the paperwork changes under you.

## What actually changes for cheese

The headline dairy provision reworks how our northern trading partner prices certain milk components used to make products like skim milk powder and milk protein concentrate — the kind of ingredient-grade dairy that competes with U.S. exports in global markets. Under the new language, that country commits to retiring its current pricing class for those products within six months of the agreement taking effect, replacing it with a formula tied to a published U.S. reference price. In plain terms: a pricing mechanism that made it cheaper for a competing product to undercut U.S. exports goes away, at least on paper. New quota categories also open gradually over several years, giving U.S. dairy products, including a defined list of cheese types, more room to move north without hitting the tariff wall that stops shipments once a quota fills.

To the south, the agreement preserves duty-free treatment for most U.S. dairy exports and — this is the part our cheese members should actually read — locks in market access for a specific list of thirty-three recognized cheese varieties, including cheddar, mozzarella, and gouda, through a side letter. If what you export isn't on that list, it's worth checking with your customs broker or an export compliance advisor about whether it's covered by the agreement's broader dairy terms or needs separate handling. We are not in a position to give you that answer for your specific product — this is a "know before you ship" flag, not legal advice.

## What we're asking members to do

Nothing urgent yet, but two things worth doing before spring:

If you export dairy products north or south, pull your current shipping documentation and confirm which tariff classification you're using today. When the new quota categories open, being ready to apply for them early matters more than being first to notice they exist.

If you have a story about how the current rules have helped or hurt your export business — a shipment held up, a tariff line that closed a market, a quota you couldn't get into — the Membership & Outreach Committee is collecting those for the comment period we expect once implementing regulations are published. Real examples carry more weight than general support when an agency is drafting the rules that will apply the agreement's language day to day.

We'll follow up with specifics once the agreement clears its final vote and implementing rules start to take shape. In the meantime, the committee is a good first call if you have questions about whether your product line is affected.
