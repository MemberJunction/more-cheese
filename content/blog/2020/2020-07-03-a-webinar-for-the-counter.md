Most of what we've put together virtually this year has aimed at owners and
managers: pricing decisions, relief-program paperwork, wholesale contracts. Next
Tuesday, July 7, we're doing something different. Webinar: Counter Culture 2020 is
built for the person actually standing at the case — the cheesemonger, the new hire
three weeks in, the counter lead who's been improvising all spring because nobody
handed them a playbook for any of this.

We heard the request loud and clear from members in June. Retail traffic patterns
have been strange all year, and counter staff have had to relearn basic parts of the
job at the same time: how to guide a customer through a case when they can't linger
and sample the way they used to, how to talk through a cut over a phone order, how to
keep a rotation moving when delivery schedules from distributors like Great Lakes
Gourmet Distributors and Heritage Curd Transport & Logistics have gotten less
predictable.

Sampling is a good example of how unsettled the basics still are. Most shops paused
it outright back in March, and as of late June only a handful of larger retailers had
started cautiously bringing it back, in modified form, behind a shield and handed out
one piece at a time rather than left out for self-service. Our members' shops are
mostly still deciding what, if anything, to do about sampling for the rest of the
summer, and that decision touches everything from staffing to signage to how a
cheesemonger talks a customer into trying something new without a taste to back it
up.

## What's on the agenda

The session runs just under ninety minutes and stays practical. We're covering
case merchandising when foot traffic is unpredictable and sampling is limited or
paused, phone- and order-ahead etiquette for a counter that didn't used to take phone
orders at all, and a short segment on recognizing when a wheel's condition has
changed enough that it needs a second opinion before it goes back on display. There's
a live Q&A block at the end, and we're deliberately leaving more time for it than
usual — the chat during our May sessions ran long, and members told us afterward they
wanted more of it, not less.

This is free and open to all current members, and it's virtual, so anyone on staff
can join from a shop's back room or a laptop at the kitchen table. If you run a
counter team, we'd encourage you to treat this as a staff meeting rather than
something to skip because it isn't aimed at you directly — the questions your newer
staff bring to it are often the ones you stopped noticing you needed answered.
Grocery data through late June still showed double-digit growth over last year
across the perimeter departments cheese sits in, which is good news for volume but
means a lot of that traffic is landing on counter staff who are newer and thinner
on the ground than the department is used to handling.

## How to join

Registration is open now through the Events Committee. If you can't make the live
session, a recording will be available to members afterward, though the live Q&A is
the part most people tell us they got the most out of. Bring a specific case problem
if you've got one — the format works best when the questions are concrete.
