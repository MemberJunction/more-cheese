Nobody plans a credential renewal around the holidays, which is exactly why this is the week to check one. Our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate carries the shortest validity in the program at two years — shorter than the Certified Cheesemonger's four or the flagship Certified Cheese Professional's three — because hazard-analysis practice keeps moving and a certificate frozen from two years ago stops reflecting current expectations. If you earned yours in early 2019, it is either already lapsed or close to it, and that is worth a five-minute check before the new year gets busy.

## Why the short clock is the point, not a flaw

It is tempting to read a two-year cycle as an inconvenience, but the logic holds up: environmental monitoring practice, recall procedures, and hazard-analysis documentation are the parts of a food-safety plan most likely to have shifted since you last studied them, especially this year. A member who batched up production to fill holiday gift-box orders is running a different risk profile than the one on file from a routine year, and a recertification cycle is a built-in moment to make sure your paperwork and your actual practice still match.

That matters more than usual heading into 2021. Several members told us this fall that they changed batch sizes, added a co-packer relationship, or shifted from wholesale to direct shipping, and each of those changes touches the hazard analysis a food safety plan is built on. A supply-chain control that made sense when your cheese moved through three wholesale accounts does not automatically make sense when half your volume is now boxed and shipped straight to a customer's door. Recertifying is a reasonable trigger to revisit the plan itself, not just the paperwork behind it, and we would rather members treat it that way than as a box to check.

Dairy markets themselves have been unsettled this month too, with wholesale cheese and butter prices easing back after a strong November, and a few members have mentioned adjusting production schedules in response. Any change to batch size or run frequency is worth a quick look against your existing hazard analysis, even outside a formal recertification cycle — the plan is supposed to describe what you are actually doing, not what you were doing when you wrote it.

## What recertification actually involves

The process is lighter than the original certificate: a refresher module covering current guidance, plus a short assessment. Members who let a credential lapse briefly do not lose their standing entirely, but a longer gap means starting closer to the original coursework rather than the refresher track, so it is worth acting before the expiration date rather than after. Set aside an evening for the refresher module itself, and expect the assessment to take less time than you remember the original exam taking — it is scoped to what has changed, not a repeat of the whole credential.

If you are not sure when yours expires, check your member portal record before the new year — it is a two-minute look now versus a scramble in February when your buyer or a wholesale account asks for current documentation. Our Food Safety Committee also holds office hours most months for members working through a recertification alongside a changing production setup; reach out through the committee page if your situation is more complicated than a straight renewal.

Here's to closing out a strange year with your paperwork in order, at least.
