There's an odd silver lining a few members have mentioned to us this summer, almost apologetically, because it comes wrapped in a bad year for their sales: with wholesale volume down and some wheels sitting longer than planned while distribution catches up, they've had more actual time in the cave than usual. Not more cheese moving through it — more time watching the cheese that's already there.

A handful have turned that into progress on the Advanced Affinage Certificate, the credential above CCP that covers rind management, cave environment control, and maturation planning across styles. It's not a credential people usually rush. It rewards patience and repeated observation more than it rewards intensity, which makes it a strange fit for a normal year and, it turns out, a decent fit for this one.

## What the credential actually asks for

Advanced Affinage is open to CCP holders and goes deeper into judgment than into procedure. A candidate needs to demonstrate they can read a rind's condition and adjust humidity, airflow, or turning frequency in response, across more than one style, over an extended period rather than a single snapshot. That's always meant sustained access to a working cave and a body of wheels to track — which is exactly what a slower wholesale summer has handed a few members without them asking for it.

The assessment format has shifted the same way the rest of our credentials have this year: candidates document their observations and adjustments in a structured log with dated photos, submitted in stages rather than reviewed in one in-person sitting. Reviewers say the staged format is actually producing better documentation than the old single visit did, because candidates are recording their reasoning in the moment rather than reconstructing it afterward for an assessor standing next to them.

That slowdown behind it is real, not incidental. Restaurant and food-service closures this spring pulled a large share of wholesale demand out of the market almost overnight, and while retail sales picked up some of the slack, the net effect for a lot of mid-sized producers was slower turnover than usual. Product that would normally have left the cave in six or eight weeks has, for some members, been sitting closer to ten or twelve. That's not a situation anyone chose, but it does mean more wheels at more stages of maturity to actually observe and compare, which is the raw material this credential is built around.

## Worth it if the timing lines up

We're not going to tell members that a slow sales quarter is a good thing — for most operations it isn't, and we know several are looking at real revenue pressure this year, not spare time to enjoy. But for the specific situation of an affineur with product sitting longer than planned and hours that would otherwise go unused, this credential is a reasonable way to turn that time into something that outlasts the season. One member candidate told us she'd been meaning to start the log for two years and only actually opened it the week her usual buyer cut an order in half.

If you're a CCP holder with a cave and more time in it than you'd like right now, reach out to the Education Committee about the current cohort. If your situation is the opposite — busier than ever and barely keeping up — this one can wait for a calmer season without losing anything.
