Two weeks ago, most of you were making cheese for a market that included restaurants, hotel
breakfast buffets, and school cafeterias. That market is largely gone for now. With dining rooms
closed and campuses empty across most of the country, a channel that by some estimates carried
close to half of U.S. dairy output has narrowed to takeout and delivery. We've heard from members
this week who are looking at coolers full of wheels that were cut, sized, and priced for a
foodservice account that isn't placing orders.

The imbalance is showing up furthest upstream first. Milk is perishable in a way that finished
cheese isn't, and when a processor can't move it, the only release valve some farms have found is
dumping raw milk they can't get picked up or paid for. That's a fluid-milk problem before it's a
cheese problem, but it reaches you fast: co-ops under that kind of pressure are re-pricing loads,
and spot milk has gotten cheaper and less predictable in the same week that packaging capacity for
retail-sized formats has gotten scarcer. If your case runs on block or wheel product that needs to
be cut down for a grocery shelf instead of a deli counter, you already know why that matters.

## What's actually changing right now

A few things worth having straight before you make a pricing or production call:

- **Retail demand hasn't collapsed — it's shifted.** Grocery cheese sales are, by most accounts,
  holding up or even running ahead of a normal spring, which is not the same story as the
  foodservice side. If you can sell into that channel, the demand is there; the friction is
  packaging, not appetite.
- **Federal relief moved fast, but is not tailored to food manufacturing yet.** The relief package
  signed into law on March 27 opens payroll-support lending to small employers, cheese producers
  included, with a fairly wide eligibility bar and the possibility of loan forgiveness tied to
  keeping staff on payroll. It is not sector-specific dairy support, and it does not touch milk
  pricing. Treat it as a payroll bridge, not a market fix.
- **Nothing about your make sheet needs to change.** This is a demand and logistics shock, not a
  food-safety or quality event. The instinct to cut corners under financial pressure is
  understandable and one we'd ask you to resist.

## Where we'd point your attention

If a chunk of your volume depended on a foodservice account that's gone quiet, the fastest lever
most members have is repackaging and re-routing what you already have toward direct and retail
channels rather than discounting into a wholesale market that's also under pressure. Our
**Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese** course walks through exactly this kind of repricing
decision — what a shortened channel does to your real margin, and how to reset a price list
without guessing. If foodservice was a meaningful share of your business, it's worth an hour this
week.

We don't have a clean read yet on how long this lasts. What we can tell you is that the federation
is tracking relief-program eligibility questions as they come in and will keep this space updated
as the picture firms up. If you have a specific question about your situation, reach out — we'd
rather hear it now than after a decision's already made.
