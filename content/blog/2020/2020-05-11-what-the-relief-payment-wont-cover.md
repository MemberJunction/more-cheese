Members keep asking us the same question in different words: is help actually coming, and how much. The short answer is yes, something is coming, and the federal farm-support agency has said producers will be able to apply for a new relief payment program starting near the end of this month. The longer answer is worth walking through, because the program is narrower than the headlines about it.

The payment is built around a single number: how much milk your operation produced in the first three months of this year, multiplied by a fixed rate per hundredweight. That's it. It doesn't look at April or May production, it doesn't look at what you actually sold versus what you had to dump, and it doesn't distinguish between a bottling plant that kept every account and a small producer who lost a farmers market season overnight. If your first quarter was strong and your second quarter collapsed, the payment will look generous relative to your current reality and thin relative to what you've actually lost.

## Why the two prices stopped agreeing

The other thing worth saying plainly, because several members raised it on last week's advocacy call: the price you're being paid for raw milk and the price a shopper pays for a wedge of cheese are not moving together right now, and that's not a rounding error. Class III milk — the classification used for most hard and semi-hard cheese — has fallen sharply since January as food-service and school demand disappeared almost overnight. At the same time, retail dairy cases have been busier than usual, because home cooking is up and grocery is absorbing demand that used to go to restaurants. Two real things are true at once: your milk check is smaller, and the case at the end of the supply chain looks fuller than it has in years.

That gap is exactly what the relief payment is meant to soften, not solve. If you're a producer who sells fluid milk into a co-op, talk to your co-op about how the payment will be distributed before you assume it lands the same way for everyone in the pool. If you make and sell your own cheese direct, the calculation is simpler but the amount is smaller relative to your actual losses, because the formula doesn't account for the wholesale and food-service accounts a lot of our smaller members lost first.

## What we're telling members to do this week

Start gathering your first-quarter production records now, even before applications open — county offices are expecting volume, and a complete file moves faster than one built from memory on the phone. If you're a co-op member, ask in writing how the payment will flow to individual producers, not just to the co-op. And if your operation doesn't fit neatly into the formula — a mixed producer, a recent start-up without a full quarter of records, a creamery that buys milk rather than milking its own herd — say so on our advocacy line this week. We're compiling exactly those edge cases to raise with the federal food-safety and farm-support agencies, because a program built around one clean number is going to miss some of you, and we'd rather document that now than after the application window closes.

None of this is legal or financial advice, and every operation's situation is different enough that we're not going to guess at your numbers here. What we can do is keep asking the questions on your behalf and keep this space updated as the details firm up.
