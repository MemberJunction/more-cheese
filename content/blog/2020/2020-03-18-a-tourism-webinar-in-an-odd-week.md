We opened registration for "Webinar: Cheese Tourism 2020" back in January, when the plan
was to talk about farm-stay bookings, cave tours, and how to build a tasting-room season
around a summer of visitors. It runs this Thursday, and almost nobody registered for it is
thinking about any of that right now.

We talked about pulling it. We're not going to. We are changing what's on the slide deck.

## The session is staying, the topic is shifting

Nobody is planning a farm visit this spring, and pretending otherwise on a webinar would
waste your Thursday afternoon. So we've asked our presenter — a member who's run tasting
tours and farm-stay bookings for years — to spend the first half on what she's actually
doing right now: what she's telling visitors who already had reservations, how she's
handling refunds versus credits for a summer that may or may not happen, and what she's
keeping ready so that when tours do come back, she isn't rebuilding the program from
scratch. The second half is still yours, live, for whatever's actually on your mind this
week — the chat has been more useful than the slides in most of our sessions lately.

If you registered expecting a marketing session and this isn't that anymore, we understand;
you're welcome to skip it, and we'll have the recording. If you run any kind of
visitor-facing program — tours, tastings, farm stays, retail events — we think the
Thursday version is more useful to you than the January version would have been.

## What this means for the rest of the events calendar

We've been watching what other associations and conference organizers are doing this
month, and the pattern is consistent: postpone the ones that can wait, convert the ones
that can't into something remote. We're applying the same test event by event rather than
canceling everything on the calendar at once. Nothing changes this week for the July
Annual Conference in Portland — registration stays open, and we'll update members directly
the moment that changes rather than let it sit unaddressed.

For webinars and workshops already scheduled through the spring, assume they run as
planned unless you hear otherwise from us directly. We'd rather send you a specific notice
about a specific date than a general "things may change" email that tells you nothing.

A few members run their own tasting-room events, release parties, or farm tours and have
asked us how we're deciding what to keep on our own calendar. Our answer: ask whether the
value of the session survives if nobody can travel to it. A hands-on affinage workshop
doesn't survive that test — you need hands in the room. A conversation about what you're
doing right now, with people who are in the same spot, usually does survive it, because the
value was always the conversation, not the room. Use that test on your own spring calendar
before you cancel anything you didn't have to.

We've also noticed something we didn't expect going into this month: attendance for
virtual sessions is running well ahead of what we'd normally see for an equivalent webinar.
Part of that is simply that fewer members are on the road right now, so a Thursday
afternoon slot is easier to protect. But part of it looks like members wanting a
half-hour where the topic is cheese and not the news. If that's part of why you're joining
Thursday, you're not the only one, and we'd rather build more of these sessions than fewer
while that's true.

Come with a question, not just an ear. We're keeping the second half open specifically
because the members-helping-members portion of these calls has been more valuable lately
than anything we could put on a slide. If your tasting room, farm-stay program, or retail
calendar has a decision on it you haven't made yet, bring it. Somebody on the call has
probably already made a version of that decision this month, and would rather tell you
what they learned than watch you learn it the slow way.
