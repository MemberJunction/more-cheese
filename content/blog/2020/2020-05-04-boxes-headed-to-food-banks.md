The federation's inbox this week has been full of one question in different phrasings: is anyone actually going to buy this cheese? For the last two months, the honest answer has mostly been "the freezer." Foodservice orders collapsed when dining rooms closed, and processors kept running because a shutdown line is its own kind of expensive, which meant cheese and butter went into cold storage instead of onto a plate. The most recent federal cold-storage report, covering stocks through the end of March, already showed natural cheese inventories climbing; end-of-April figures, due out later this month, are expected to push that higher still. This is one small corner of a much larger disruption — the same weeks that filled our members' warehouses also wiped out billions of dollars in cancelled conventions and trade gatherings across the broader economy, which is the same demand collapse showing up in a different industry's numbers.

That's the backdrop for a new federal food-box purchase program moving through its first solicitation rounds this spring. The structure is straightforward on paper: the program is buying dairy, produce, and meat in bulk from distributors, packing it into boxes, and routing it to food banks and nonprofits rather than grocery shelves or restaurant kitchens. Cheese is squarely in scope — bid solicitations already out cover process cheese, cheddar shreds, string cheese, and sliced product, with contracts expected to start moving boxes out of warehouses later this month.

## What this does and doesn't fix

For members who sell primarily to co-ops or larger processors, this is likely to show up as a modest, welcome bump in a commodity line that's had almost nothing but bad news since March. It is not a substitute for restaurant demand, and it is not going to touch the specialty end of the case. A purchase program built around bid solicitations for shelf-stable commodity formats has no obvious slot for a small-batch washed rind or a raw-milk tomme, and members making those cheeses shouldn't expect this round to move their inventory.

There's also a timing question worth flagging honestly: contracts are still being awarded, and the gap between "solicitation issued" and "truck loaded" has run longer than announced in earlier emergency programs. If your cash-flow planning assumes this demand lands in May, build in some slack rather than counting on a check that hasn't been mailed yet.

It's also worth separating two different kinds of relief that keep getting talked about in the same breath. This purchase program moves finished product; it doesn't touch a farm's milk price directly, and it doesn't do anything for a member whose problem is a processing contract that's paying less than it did in February. Members carrying both kinds of pain this spring shouldn't expect one announcement to resolve both.

## What we're doing

The Membership & Outreach Committee is compiling a plain-language summary of which dairy categories are actually named in current solicitations, updated as new rounds are announced, so members aren't parsing federal contract notices to find out if their format qualifies. If you distribute product through a co-op or processor that might bid into this program, ask them directly whether your cheese is part of what they're offering — the program buys from distributors, not from individual creameries, so your leverage is in that conversation, not in a federal portal.

We're also hearing from members who assume, reasonably, that a program this large must have some slot for smaller-volume specialty product further down the line. We don't have anything to point to on that yet, and we'd rather say so plainly than let a hopeful guess circulate as if it were confirmed. If a later round opens a lane that fits smaller producers, we'll say so specifically, with the actual solicitation language, not a summary of a summary.

We'll keep tracking inventory data as it's released this month. If storage numbers come in as high as expected, this won't be the last purchase program we write about this year, and it likely won't be the last one that helps some members more than others.
