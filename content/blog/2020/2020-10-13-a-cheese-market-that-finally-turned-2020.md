If your wholesale numbers looked strange this fall, you weren't imagining it. Milk pricing that had fallen hard by late summer has climbed sharply into October, and members who lock contracts around this time of year are asking us what changed and whether it will hold.

The short version: cheese demand did not behave the way anyone expected in a pandemic year. Retail cheese sales have been running well ahead of last year's pace since spring, and that pull-through has moved cheddar block and barrel prices up from where they sat in late summer. Dry whey has firmed too, on export demand that federal trade data shows running meaningfully ahead of last year — several members who sell into ingredient channels have mentioned it as the quiet story of their fall books. Butter, by contrast, has stayed soft, which is a reminder that "dairy is up" is never one number. It's several markets moving at different speeds under one label.

## What this means for your contracts

For members selling wholesale or into distribution, a market like this cuts two ways. If you're negotiating fall or early-winter pricing now, current numbers are stronger than they were even a month ago — worth revisiting a quote you sent in August. If you're on the buying side, a supplier who locked in over the summer may be sitting on a contract that looks unfavorable to them today, and a little flexibility in how you handle that conversation tends to pay off over a longer relationship than one season.

We'd also gently flag: none of this is a forecast, and we're not in the business of telling members what to charge. What we can say is that the swing from July's highs to September's lows to October's recovery has been unusually sharp this year, and members who build in a wider pricing-review cadence — monthly rather than seasonal, for as long as this volatility lasts — have told us it's saved them from being caught flat either direction.

## Retail demand is doing real work here

The other half of the story is on the shelf. Natural cheese has been one of the stronger performers in the grocery aisle all year, with both dollar and unit sales up meaningfully over last year's pace. Specialty and artisan cheese specifically has kept pace with that growth, which matters for members whose whole business lives in that segment rather than commodity block. If you've noticed your retail accounts reordering faster or your online orders climbing, you're part of a pattern showing up in the national numbers, not an outlier.

None of this changes the basics of running a small operation through a volatile year: know your cost to produce a wheel, know your margin at today's price, and don't let a good month convince you to extend credit you'd normally decline. If you want a second set of eyes on your cost sheet before you sign anything for November or December delivery, the Membership & Outreach Committee can point you toward a member who's willing to compare notes — anonymously, if you'd rather keep the specifics private.
