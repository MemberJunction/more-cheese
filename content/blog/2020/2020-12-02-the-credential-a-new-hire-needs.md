A few members told us this fall that they'd hired help for the first time all year, not because business was booming, but because a smaller staff running longer shifts finally caught up with them. A counter clerk who used to cover for a sick coworker now has no one to cover for, and a make-day helper brought on to cut hours elsewhere is suddenly cutting and wrapping cheese for a holiday gift-box program nobody planned to run this big. If that's your December, the question isn't whether to train someone. It's what to actually train them on, fast, without pulling you off the floor for a week.

That's the gap the Cheese Foundations Certificate is built for. It's our entry-level credential, and it's worth being specific about what it covers, because the name alone undersells it.

## What's actually in it

The certificate walks through four areas: how cheese is made at a level that lets someone talk to a customer or handle a wheel without guessing, the basic vocabulary of styles and categories so a new hire isn't inventing descriptions on the spot, safe handling and storage fundamentals that matter whether someone's stocking a case or packing a shipping box, and a short module on reading a label — moisture, aging claims, allergen statements — well enough to answer a customer's question correctly instead of confidently.

It is not a food-safety credential on its own, and we're clear about that distinction every time someone asks. If your new hire is going to be doing hands-on production work — cutting curd, monitoring a vat, anything that touches the make itself — the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate is the one that actually covers that ground, and it's a longer commitment. Cheese Foundations is the right starting point for counter staff, seasonal packing help, and anyone customer-facing. Layering HACCP on top later, once someone's staying past January, is the usual path.

## Why this month, not next

The honest answer is that a lot of members ask about training in January, once the holiday rush is over and there's finally time to think about it. By then the seasonal hire who most needed the training has often moved on, and the member who's staying has already learned the job by trial and error, sometimes the wrong way. The certificate is self-paced and takes most people six to eight hours spread over a week or two, which fits around a holiday schedule better than it looks like it should on paper.

Federal dairy price reports released this week showed cheese and milk prices pulling back some from November's run, which is a reminder that this holiday bump, wherever it lands for your business, isn't a permanent shift in the market. It's a good month to get more out of the staff you already have than to assume you'll need to keep them at this pace into the new year.

If you've got someone at the counter or in the packing room right now who's learning the job as they go, the certificate is a structured way to close that gap before the busiest weeks are behind you rather than after. Enrollment is open through the member portal, and the Education Committee can point you to the right starting module if you're not sure where your hire's gaps actually are.
