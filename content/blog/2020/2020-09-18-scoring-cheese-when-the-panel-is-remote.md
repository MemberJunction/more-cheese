We've had more members ask about the **Sensory Evaluation Certificate** in the last two months
than in any stretch we can remember, and we think we know why. With most tastings, panels, and
judged flights moved to video calls this year, members who used to lean on a shared table and a
shared nose are now scoring cheese alone, on their own counter, second-guessing themselves without
anyone next to them to compare notes with in real time. The credential turns out to be exactly the
thing that steadies that.

## What the certificate actually tests

The Sensory Evaluation Certificate isn't a "can you describe a cheese nicely" credential. It tests
whether you can apply a consistent vocabulary and a consistent scoring method to a wheel
regardless of who else is in the room — texture, aroma, flavor balance, finish, and defect
identification, scored the same way on your worst day as your best one. That consistency is
exactly what a remote panel needs, because the thing that usually keeps a panel honest — four
people tasting the same piece at the same table, catching each other's blind spots out loud in the
moment — is mostly gone right now.

A certified taster scoring alone still has the discipline of the method even without the room.
That's the whole point of the credential, and it's why members who hold it have told us the switch
to remote judging felt like a smaller adjustment than they expected. The scoring language was
already doing the work the room used to do.

## What's different about doing it from home

A few practical notes for anyone scoring remotely this fall. Temper the sample properly before you
start — a cheese straight from a cold case scores differently than one that's had twenty minutes
to come up to a fair tasting temperature, and a cold sample will read falsely firm and falsely
muted on aroma. Score in writing before you compare notes with anyone else on the call, not after.
The whole value of a shared method is that it should get you to a similar answer independently,
and comparing scores too early erases the evidence that the method is actually working rather than
everyone just agreeing with the first person to speak.

Lighting and camera framing matter more than people expect, too, if you're scoring alongside a
video call rather than purely alone — a rind's true color reads differently under a webcam's white
balance than it does under kitchen light, so don't let a screen talk you out of what your own eyes
are telling you in front of the actual wheel.

Home tastings and virtual judged events have become a real part of how the specialty cheese trade
is reaching customers this year, not just a stopgap for internal panels. Members who've built
comfort with structured remote scoring this year are finding it useful well beyond judging — it's
the same discipline that makes a virtual customer tasting feel credible instead of improvised.

Registration for the next Sensory Evaluation cohort is open through our education calendar. If
you're already scoring cheese informally for your shop, your co-op, or your own house standard,
the certificate mostly formalizes work you're already doing — it just makes sure the method holds
up when nobody else is at the table to catch what you missed.
