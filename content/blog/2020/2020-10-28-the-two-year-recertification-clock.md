Of the seven credentials on our ladder, the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate has the shortest shelf life: two years, against three or more for most of the others. That's not an accident, and this fall it's worth explaining why, because we're hearing from a number of members whose two-year clock is about to run out.

## Why this one recertifies faster

Food-safety practice moves. Regulators updated their federal HACCP guidance materials again just this month, and a credential that stayed static for four or five years would quietly go stale against that kind of change. The federation set the HACCP certificate's validity at two years specifically so the content behind it gets revisited on a schedule, not just when something goes wrong.

This year has made the case for that on its own. Creameries that pivoted to curbside pickup, farm-stand sales, or new packaging formats picked up new points of contact between product and hands, and a hazard analysis written in 2018 may not reflect how your operation actually moves cheese out the door in 2020. If your make room, your packaging line, or your sales channel has changed since you last certified, your written plan should reflect that even before your two years are up.

Recertification isn't a full retake of the original coursework. It's a shorter review focused on what's changed since your last certificate — updated hazard categories and a check that your documented plan still matches your actual process. Members who've been through it describe it as a few focused hours, not a redo of the whole credential. If your certificate lapses before you recertify, you're not locked out of the federation, but you lose the credential listing on your member profile, and a few of our wholesale-facing members have told us buyers do ask.

## Building a plan from scratch, and where to start

If you've never held the credential and you're building your first written food-safety plan this fall — which several new and smaller members are doing, given how much has changed about how they sell — treat it as the foundation piece, not an afterthought. A plan that only exists because a buyer asked for one tends to be thin. A plan built because you actually walked your process end to end tends to hold up when someone asks a follow-up question.

The federation's coursework covering this ties directly into the credential: members preparing for either their first HACCP certificate or a recertification have been leaning on our Listeria Control in Small Creameries course this fall, which walks through environmental monitoring for exactly the kind of small-batch, hands-on operations most of our membership runs.

If you're not sure when your certificate expires, your member profile has the date on file — check it before the holiday season gets busy, since recertification tends to get pushed to the bottom of the list once make schedules pick up in November and December. If you have questions about what's changed since your last certification, the Food Safety Committee is the right place to ask.
