Of the seven credentials we administer, the Advanced Affinage Certificate has always leaned hardest on someone else watching you work. Rind management and cave environment control are skills you demonstrate with your hands in a real room, not on a written exam, and that's exactly the part of our credential pathway that's been hardest to run this year. We've had a steady trickle of members asking what's changed, so here's where things actually stand.

We're not alone in working through this. Conformity-assessment bodies well outside cheesemaking spent the spring building out remote-auditing protocols for standards ranging from food production to aerospace, and payment-card assessors were issuing formal guidance on remote validation by early April. None of that is directly about affinage, obviously, but it's a useful reminder that "how do you verify hands-on competence without being in the room" is a problem a lot of industries have had to solve fast this year, and we've borrowed more than a little from how they approached it.

## The credential itself hasn't moved

The scope is the same as it was in January: rind management, cave environment control, and maturation planning across styles, open to members who already hold Certified Cheese Professional standing. We haven't loosened what the certificate represents, and we don't plan to. A credential that gets easier during a hard year is worth less to everyone who holds it, including the members who earned it before this one.

What's changed is how the practical component gets verified. Where an assessor would normally walk your cave in person, we've moved to a documented-and-reviewed model: you submit a maturation log covering a defined period, photograph key rind stages at set intervals, and walk an assessor through your environment controls over a video call. It's more paperwork than the old model. It's also, several members have told us, a genuinely useful discipline even for cheesemakers who've been aging wheels for a decade — writing down what you're watching for forces you to notice things a habit lets you skip.

## What this means if you're mid-pathway

If you were partway through the practical component when in-person assessment stopped being available, you don't need to restart. Your logged hours and prior sign-offs carry forward; you only need to complete the remaining documentation under the current model. If your CCP is close to its five-year mark and you're deciding whether to pursue Advanced Affinage now or wait until assessment is fully in-person again, our honest answer is: don't wait on our account. The documented model isn't going away even after travel opens back up, because several assessors have told us it actually surfaces more detail than a single walk-through visit used to.

One practical note on timing: cave conditions vary by season, and an assessor reviewing your maturation log wants to see how your controls hold up through a stretch that includes some variability, not just a calm week. If you're planning to start the documentation window, late summer into early autumn — when outside temperature swings put real pressure on a cave's climate control — is a genuinely informative period to log.

We've also heard from a few members that the video-call component feels more exposed than an in-person visit, oddly enough. An assessor standing in your cave is looking at the whole room; an assessor on a call is looking at whatever your camera shows them, which can feel like it's putting a single narrow angle under a magnifying glass. Our advice there is practical rather than reassuring: walk the space slowly, narrate what you're showing and why, and don't try to frame out the messy corner. Assessors have seen plenty of working caves, and a tidy staged shot reads as more suspicious than a real one.

If you're ready to start, or you're not sure whether your current logs qualify, the Education Committee can walk you through what the documentation packet needs before you're deep into it. And if you're holding a Cheese Foundations or Food Safety & HACCP credential and wondering whether your own renewal cycle is affected by any of this, the short answer is no — this documented-review model is specific to Advanced Affinage's practical component for now, though we're watching how it performs before deciding whether to extend it elsewhere in the credential ladder.
