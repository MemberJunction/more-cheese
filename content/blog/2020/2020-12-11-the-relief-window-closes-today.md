If you've been putting off the paperwork on the second round of federal pandemic assistance, today is the day it stops being an option. The application window closes tonight, and the local farm service offices we've heard from expect a late rush, the same as it always is with a deadline like this one. If your production numbers from earlier in the year still need to go in, don't wait for a confirmation email before you call.

We've fielded a fair number of questions about this program since the fall, and the most common one is simple: is it worth the paperwork for a smaller producer? For most of the creameries and farmstead operations we've talked to, the answer has been yes, even when the payment itself is modest. The program is calculated off actual production earlier in the year, with an estimate covering the last few months, and the per-entity cap is generous enough that it rarely binds for a member-sized operation. The bigger risk isn't the size of the check. It's missing the window entirely because the form felt like more trouble than it was worth.

## A market that's actually cooperating, for once

The timing lands in an unusual spot for cheese. Block cheese prices have firmed meaningfully over the past month, and the federal all-milk price forecast for the year was revised upward as a result, with a noticeably stronger fourth-quarter number built in. That's a real reversal from where the market sat over the summer, when plenty of members were selling into a case nobody wanted to fill. None of that makes the relief program less useful — market strength and program eligibility aren't the same conversation, and a good December doesn't erase a rough spring on anyone's books — but it does mean this deadline is landing at a moment when a lot of our members are cautiously optimistic rather than purely defensive.

That's a strange combination to sit with: a firming market and a closing relief window in the same week. Our advice hasn't changed either way. File if you're eligible, whether or not this quarter looks better than the last one did. The form doesn't ask how your November went, and neither should you when you're deciding whether it's worth twenty minutes at a keyboard.

## If you're still not sure you qualify

Dairy production, including cheese made on-farm, is treated as an eligible commodity under this round, calculated on actual and estimated marketings rather than a flat headcount. If your operation has any uncertainty about how milk used in your own cheese production should be reported, your local farm service office is the right first call, not a guess. We are not able to file on any member's behalf, and nothing here should be read as advice on your specific eligibility — but we can tell you that "I wasn't sure I qualified" is the single most common reason members have told us they almost skipped this one, and most of them didn't need to.

There has also been talk of a technical correction to part of the program's rule, which in past rounds has meant a short extra window for anyone caught by the fix after a deadline already passed. That's not something to plan around today. If tonight's cutoff is real for your operation, treat it as real, file what you can support with your existing records, and let any later correction be a pleasant surprise rather than the reason you waited.

A few callers this week have asked whether it's worth filing if the number looks small once it's calculated. Our answer hasn't changed since the fall: a modest payment that took twenty minutes to request is still a modest payment, and skipping it doesn't buy you anything. The one exception worth flagging is if your bookkeeping for the relevant months is genuinely incomplete — filing something inaccurate to make a deadline is worse than filing nothing, and a rushed number tonight can cause more paperwork in January than it saves. If that's your situation, call your local office directly and ask what a reasonable estimate looks like before you submit.

Whatever you decide, don't let a firming cheese market talk you out of a program built for a rougher stretch of the year. The two aren't in conflict, and taking the relief you're owed for last spring doesn't say anything about how you expect the next quarter to go.
