We keep writing some version of this post, and we'll keep writing it as long as it stays true: the record block cheddar price from mid-July has not stopped sliding. Blocks are trading into the low $1.70s this week, and the federal all-milk price for August came in nearly a dollar below July's number. Three weeks ago we called the July spike a market reacting to a government purchase program rather than steady demand. Late August looks like the same market working its way back to whatever "steady" means in a year like this one.

## The school cafeteria that isn't quite a cafeteria

The other piece of news this week matters more to a lot of our members than the board itself. A federal decision announced this past Monday extended free-meal eligibility for all students through the end of the calendar year, reversing an earlier plan that would have pushed many districts back to charging for lunch starting this month. That's good news for the families it covers, and it keeps a meaningful channel of institutional cheese demand from disappearing outright — a channel that looked, back in the spring, like it might simply vanish once summer feeding programs wound down.

It's also a reminder of how much of this year's demand picture has been decided on a rolling, month-to-month basis rather than settled once and left alone. Members who supply institutional accounts have gotten used to checking back in every few weeks rather than assuming a spring agreement still holds by fall, and this extension is one more data point in that pattern rather than a new one.

But "the channel stays open" isn't the same as "the channel looks like it used to." Districts running grab-and-go bags and classroom-delivered trays instead of a traditional cafeteria line are buying differently — more individually wrapped and pre-portioned product, less of the bulk block and shred that a full-service kitchen would run through a slicer or a pizza oven. If your creamery has historically supplied food-service distributors that serve schools, this is worth a direct conversation with your account rep about format, not just volume, before you assume this fall looks like last fall.

## Reading the two numbers together

A softening commodity board and a preserved-but-reshaped institutional channel are two separate stories that are easy to blur into one. The board tells you what a pound of commodity cheese is worth on a given week; it says nothing about whether your specific customer — a school district, a hospital kitchen, a corporate cafeteria that's mostly empty — actually wants what you make in the format you currently make it. Several members selling into food-service accounts have told us this month that their invoices are down on price and format both, which compounds faster than either change alone would.

If you sell wholesale on an index-linked contract, expect the September settlement to track this month's softer board. If you sell into institutional accounts specifically, ask your buyer now what their fall service model actually looks like, rather than finding out when a purchase order comes in smaller or shaped differently than you planned for.

That conversation is worth having even if your account seems stable on paper. A district that's still buying the same total volume but splitting it across more, smaller deliveries changes your production planning and your cold-storage turnover in ways a flat purchase-order number won't show you until it's already happened. A member who supplies two mid-sized districts told us this week that her delivery frequency has roughly doubled since spring even though her per-month volume is close to flat — more trips, smaller loads, tighter windows for drop-off.

We don't have a clean answer for what fall institutional demand settles into, and we'd rather say that plainly than guess at a number. The Membership & Outreach Committee has been collecting notes from members navigating both a softer commodity board and a reshaped institutional channel at once, and can connect you with someone who's already had the format conversation with a school or hospital account.
