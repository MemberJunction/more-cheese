Labor Day usually marks the moment members stop watching the summer case and start planning the fall one. This year the handoff felt uneven. Grocers spent late August leaning hard into back-to-school shopping, and sliced and snack cheese rode along with the lunch-box staples. Retailers told us they saw steady movement on the everyday case even as families budgeted carefully for the rest of the shopping list. That is a good problem to have if you sell wedges by the pound at a counter, and a different problem if your business lives on the wholesale board.

On that board, the summer's modest recovery in the all-milk price gave members something to point to after a rough spring. The federal price forecast was nudged up for the first time in months, a small but real signal that the worst of the year's margin squeeze may be behind some producers. We heard from a few members who described it less as good news and more as "finally, a number that isn't falling." That is a fair read. A price forecast ticking upward after months of declines is relief, not a rally, and members who lock in supply contracts this month should treat it that way.

## Two different customers, two different weeks

The gap between the retail case and the wholesale board is not new, but it has been unusually visible this year. Members selling direct — at a counter, a farmers market stand, or through a subscription box — describe a fall that looks a lot like last fall: steady interest, a little more price sensitivity than 2022, and shoppers who ask more questions about where a wheel was made before they ask what it costs. Members selling into distribution describe something flatter. Contracts renegotiated this summer landed closer to where they started the year than anyone predicted in January.

If you sit in both camps — a farmstead producer with a retail case up front and a wholesale account out back — this is the month to look hard at which side is actually carrying your margin. We have said this before and we will keep saying it: a softer wholesale number does not mean your retail price has to move in the same direction. The two markets are reading different signals right now, and pricing them identically is how members leave money on the table in either direction.

## What to watch through September

A few things worth putting on your own calendar this month. First, if your creamery runs a seasonal make schedule, September is typically when cow's milk components start climbing back up after the summer dip, which changes yield calculations on the same volume of milk — worth rechecking your make sheet rather than assuming August's numbers still hold. Second, keep an eye on your own distributor relationships; a member who called us last week had renegotiated a contract in June that already looks stale against where wholesale demand has settled. Third, if you have not looked at your fall staffing for the retail case, back-to-school patterns suggest the lunch-box rush doesn't fully let up until closer to the holidays — plan coverage accordingly.

None of this is dramatic. It is the kind of week where the smart move is a spreadsheet, not a strategy shift.
