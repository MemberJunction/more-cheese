Members have been asking us to say something plainly this month: is the news actually good? Mostly, yes, with the usual caveats. The federal milk-margin support program has now triggered a payment for several months running this year, which is either a remarkable stretch of thin margins or a sign the safety net is doing exactly what it was built for, depending on which end of the phone you're on.

Wholesale block cheese has also had a firmer few weeks heading into August, after a soft spring and early summer. If you sell into that market directly, you already know the number. If you don't, the short version is: prices are still below where they sat two years ago, but the direction lately has been up, not down, and analysts are pointing to milk production running a little behind last year's pace as one reason cheese and milk prices have firmed together.

## Why a decent month doesn't change the plan

We keep hearing from members who assume a good month means the safety net programs matter less. It's the opposite. The reason margin coverage has stayed useful all year is that milk prices and feed costs have both been unsettled — the broader global dairy price index, which tracks butter, cheese, and milk powder together, sat essentially flat in July even as the domestic cheese block firmed. Those two facts aren't in conflict; they're describing different markets moving at different speeds, and a member selling into the domestic wholesale block market will feel this stretch very differently than a member watching export demand.

If you haven't looked at your own coverage election since enrollment closed in January, this is a reasonable week to do it. Not because anything is wrong, but because a member who checks the math only when the number looks bad ends up making decisions under pressure instead of ahead of it. A ten-minute review against your actual feed costs this month beats a scramble later in the fall, and it's a much shorter conversation to have with your accountant now than during a margin squeeze.

## What we're watching into fall

Two things worth keeping an eye on:

- **Export demand out of Asia.** Slower economic growth there has been weighing on dairy import volumes broadly, and a soft fall for exports would put pressure back on the domestic cheese price even if the recent firmness holds for now.
- **The next quota and tariff conversation.** We've flagged before that international tariff lines shape what specialty producers can sell abroad and what shows up on your own store shelf from elsewhere. Nothing new landed this week, but the conversation hasn't gone away, and we'll keep flagging it as it moves.

None of this is a forecast — we don't have one worth printing, and anyone who tells you they do is selling something. What we can tell you is that the members riding this out best are the ones treating margin coverage as an ordinary part of the year's paperwork, not an emergency lever. If you want a second set of eyes on your election or your feed-cost assumptions, our membership office can point you to the right federal resources; we don't administer the program, but we hear from enough of you each month to know where the common mistakes are.

If your creamery is small enough that a single milk check still swings your month, this is also a good week to look at the Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course. It won't change what the market does, but it will change how fast you notice when it does, and members who've taken it tell us the biggest shift is simply knowing which number to watch first each week.
