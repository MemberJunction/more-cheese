The ICF Annual Conference lands in Louisville, Kentucky, on July 25, and we are four weeks out. That is the point in the calendar where a conference stops being a save-the-date and starts being a list of things you actually have to finish, so this week we are running through the ones members ask about most.

If you entered the competition through last week's Birch cohort workshop or on your own, your wheels have a firm submission window and we are not extending it this cycle. Categories like Alpine Styles and Soft-Ripened tend to fill early, and judges need enough lead time with each entry to give it a fair evaluation rather than a rushed one. If you are still on the fence about entering, the honest answer is that a first entry rarely wins, but it does tell you exactly where your make sits against a room full of peers you would not otherwise get to compare against.

## What is actually different about this year's room

Attendance at in-person events has kept climbing back since the pandemic years, and this conference is shaping up to be one of our fuller rooms yet. That is a good problem, but it changes a few practical things: hotel blocks near the venue are moving faster than in past cycles, and session capacity for the hands-on affinage and cheesemaking workshops is genuinely limited rather than a formality. If a specific session is the reason you are coming, register for it directly rather than assuming you can walk in.

We are also hearing from more members who are treating this conference as a supplier meeting as much as an educational one. "I go for the judging feedback, but honestly half my calendar there is meetings with distributors I only see once a year," one longtime member told us recently, and that tracks with what we are seeing in registration notes — more members flagging specific vendor conversations they want to have on-site. If that is you, reach out to the exhibitors you want to meet before the conference rather than hoping to catch them at a booth between sessions.

## Before you leave home

A few members have asked whether the conference counts toward continuing requirements for credentials like the Certified Cheese Professional or Advanced Affinage Certificate. Session attendance can count depending on the specific requirement, so check your credential's renewal page before you build your schedule, not after you get home and try to reconstruct which sessions you sat through.

This has been a genuinely hot summer even by regional standards — the heat wave that settled over Texas and parts of the Southwest this month broke long-standing temperature records in more than one city, and the pattern shows no sign of easing before conference week. If you are hand-carrying competition entries or cave samples to Louisville, plan your transport insulation for a real heat load, not a mild one — a wheel that traveled fine in May will not necessarily survive an afternoon in a parked car in late July. Pack it like you mean it, and build in a cooler stop if your drive runs more than a few hours.

If you have not registered yet, do it this week. If you have questions about the competition rules, the Awards & Competition Committee is the right place to ask, and if you want help matching your schedule to a specific credential's requirements, our membership team can walk through that with you before the conference, not during it.
