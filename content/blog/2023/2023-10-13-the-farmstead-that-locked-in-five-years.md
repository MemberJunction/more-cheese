Most of our farmstead members treat their federal margin coverage election the way they treat their crop insurance: revisit it once a year, adjust the number if last year felt tight, move on. One member on our Membership & Outreach Committee did something different this fall, and when we asked why, she gave us an answer worth passing along to anyone else weighing the same decision this quarter.

She runs a small farmstead operation with her own herd, makes most of her own cheese on site, and has been an ICF member for a little over six years. This year, instead of renewing her margin coverage election for another single season, she locked in a five-year term. "I've done the year-to-year thing since I started," she told us. "Every fall I'd guess at what the number should be based on how the last twelve months went, which mostly meant I was always reacting to the year that already happened instead of planning for the one coming."

## Why five years instead of one

Her reasoning came down to two things: the discount for a longer commitment, and the fact that a farmstead operation, unlike a larger commercial dairy, doesn't have the staff to redo this analysis carefully every single fall. "I have one bookkeeping afternoon a month if I'm lucky," she said. "Doing this once and being done with it for five years is worth more to me than optimizing the number every single September." She was careful to add that the decision made sense for her specific situation — steady herd size, no plans to expand or contract, and a coverage level she'd already tested against two rough years — and that a member in a different position, especially anyone actively growing or shrinking their herd, should think it through with their own numbers rather than copy hers.

We want to be equally careful here: this is one member's account of her own decision, not a recommendation from the federation about what level of coverage any other producer should carry, or whether locking in a longer term makes sense for a different operation. Margin coverage elections have real financial consequences and belong in a conversation with your own lender or accountant, not a blog post.

## What she'd tell a newer member

What she does think is broadly useful advice, and not specific to her own farm: read your own last three years of milk and feed numbers before you make any coverage decision, not just the headline margin figures that get published nationally. "The national number is an average across a lot of different regions and a lot of different feed rations," she said. "My own feed costs don't move exactly the same way the national index does, so I built my own version of that chart before I decided anything." That's roughly the same exercise our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course walks members through, and she said the course was where she'd first learned to build that worksheet for herself, well before this fall's decision.

If you're on the fence about your own coverage election this season, the Membership & Outreach Committee is a reasonable place to ask what other farmstead members have been weighing, even if nobody there can tell you what number is right for your particular herd.
