The ICF Annual Conference 2023 is three weeks out, and this is the week we start hearing from
members who are still deciding whether to go. If that's you, here's the honest pitch: this is
the one week a year where the person who judges your competition entry, the distributor who
might carry your wheel, and the committee volunteer who can actually answer your labeling
question are all in the same building.

## What's actually happening there

Beyond the general sessions, the Awards & Competition Committee runs the judged competition
that this spring's entry-prep workshops have been building toward all year — the conference is
where that work finally gets scored. Industry outlooks published earlier this year pointed to
exactly this kind of in-person judging and networking as the thing cheesemakers said they
missed most and were most eager to get back to. The Education Committee is also
running hands-on sessions tied to several of this year's course tracks, including affinage and
sensory evaluation, so if you're partway through a certification, this is a good place to close
a gap before the fall cohort starts.

If you've never attended before, budget more time than you think for the exhibit floor. It's
where a lot of members find their next supplier or distributor relationship, in the kind of
five-minute conversation that a phone call never quite replicates. It's a pattern that holds
across the trade, not just at our own event: a major specialty-food trade show sold out its
exhibit floor and drew thousands of buyers this June, a sign that in-person sourcing hasn't
lost any ground to catalogs and cold email even years after it briefly went virtual.

What still needs a decision from you? A few things have real deadlines attached, not just a
general "the sooner the better":

- **Competition entries.** If you're submitting a wheel, the entry window closes well before
  the event itself — check the competition page rather than assuming you can register on-site.
- **Course credit.** If you're mid-certification and want conference attendance to count toward
  it, confirm with the Education Committee before you travel, not after.
- **Shipping vs. carrying.** Wheels traveling any distance need a real plan for temperature
  control in transit. A member who shipped an unrefrigerated box two summers ago still brings
  it up as the mistake she doesn't repeat.

Veterans of past conferences tell us the same thing every year: bring a notebook you'll
actually use, comfortable shoes for a concrete exhibit floor, and a business card even if you
think your operation is too small to need one. The member who ends up on a distributor's short
list is usually the one who handed over a card, not the one with the biggest booth.

If you haven't registered yet, do it this week. If you have questions about the schedule or
the competition rules, the Events Committee and the Awards & Competition Committee are both
the right place to ask.

## If you're still on the fence

We hear a version of the same hesitation every year: "I'm not sure my operation is far enough
along to belong there." That's a familiar mix of confidence and caution — plenty of optimism about demand, tempered by
real worry about costs and coverage — for anyone who's been watching this year's dairy-margin
numbers alongside us. That mix is exactly the room you want to be in. The member
sitting next to you at a session on affinage may be three years ahead of you on the exact
problem you're trying to solve, or three years behind and asking the question you wish
someone had answered for you back then.

If travel budget is the real obstacle, say so to the Membership & Outreach Committee before you
count yourself out. It costs nothing to ask what support exists, and a quiet conversation now
beats a missed conference and a fuller inbox of "wish you'd been there" messages in August.
