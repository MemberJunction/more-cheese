Holiday shipping season starts earlier every year, and this week our phones started ringing with a question we hear every November: why does a wheel that clears one state's inspection sometimes get held up crossing into the next one? It is not a new problem, but the volume makes it a louder one right now, and it is why interstate cheese shipment rules have moved back to the top of our advocacy list.

The short version is that raw-milk and aged-cheese shipments are subject to a patchwork of state-level labeling and cold-chain documentation requirements that do not always match what a producer's home state requires. A gift box that sails through a home-state dock can sit for an extra day at a distribution point in a state with a different documentation format, and around the Thanksgiving-to-Christmas run that extra day is the difference between a wheel arriving in good shape and one that arrives soft at the edges. Several of you have told us it is the single biggest source of holiday-season customer complaints that has nothing to do with the cheese itself.

## What we're asking for

We have joined a coalition push asking for a single standardized interstate shipment form that any state inspector would recognize, instead of the state-by-state variations currently in circulation. We are not asking anyone to change what gets inspected — the safety substance stays the same — just to stop requiring producers to fill out six versions of the same information depending on where the truck happens to cross a line. A federation member testified on this exact point in front of a state agriculture committee earlier this fall, and the response was encouraging enough that we think a pilot standard form is realistic for a handful of states next year.

In the meantime, if you ship across state lines during gift season, keep a folder — physical or digital — of every state's current cold-chain documentation requirement for the states you actually sell into, and check it again before your first December shipment, not your last. Requirements shift more often than anyone expects, usually with little notice, and a form that was current in the spring is not something you want to be relying on in December.

It also helps to build a little slack into your shipping calendar this time of year. A pallet that would normally clear in two days can take three when carriers are running holiday volumes, and a documentation mismatch on top of that delay compounds quickly. Members who ship nationally tell us the safest habit is to treat every unfamiliar destination state as a fresh check, even if you shipped there without trouble last year — the requirement on file this December is not guaranteed to be the one you used twelve months ago.

## Where this sits with other priorities

Interstate shipment is one of four issues we are actively tracking this cycle, alongside labeling standards, raw-milk aging rules, and the ongoing conversation about FSMA's small-producer exemptions. Dairy margins have also been easing after a difficult stretch of feed costs earlier in the year, which is good news for producers weighing whether to expand shipping capacity heading into next season — a steadier margin makes it easier to invest in the packaging and cold-chain upgrades that reduce these holdups in the first place.

If you have a specific shipment horror story from this season — a hold-up, a form nobody recognized, a state that asked for something surprising — send it our way. Specific, dated examples are what make the case to regulators; general frustration, however justified, does not move a rulemaking docket the way one clear anecdote does.

If you want to add your name to the coalition letter or just want the current documentation checklist for the states you ship to most, reach out to our advocacy team before your next outbound pallet goes out the door.
