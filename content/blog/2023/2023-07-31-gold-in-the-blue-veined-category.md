We're back from Louisville, still smelling faintly of cave and diesel from the load-in, and ready to talk about what happened at this year's Annual Conference. If you were there, thank you for the conversations at the booth and for not letting us skip the last session on a Tuesday afternoon. If you weren't, here's the short version: a full competition floor, a trade show that ran out of chairs by Friday, and a Blue Veined category that produced the best flight of entries we've judged in years.

## What the judges rewarded

Rowanbrook Tomme took gold in Blue Veined this year, ahead of a strong Bronze finish from Quernstone Tomme in the same category — two very different approaches to the style sitting next to each other on the judging table, which is exactly the kind of range we like to see. Aged Cheddar & Territorials went quieter, with Foxcombe Blue picking up Silver in a category that's gotten more competitive every year the boom has run.

Not every entry placed, and that's worth saying plainly: a lot of Alpine Styles and Washed Rind entries went home without a ribbon this year, including some wheels that generated real buzz on the show floor before judging. A no-placement result isn't a verdict on your make. Categories get deep fast when membership grows the way ours has, and the judges are working from a rubric, not a gut feeling about who tried hardest.

## The floor, not just the stage

The trade show side of the conference is where a lot of the actual business happens, and this year that meant longer conversations than usual about input costs. With milk prices down sharply from last year's highs, several members told us they were using the conference to renegotiate supply terms in person rather than over email — a distributor rep at one booth joked that he'd had more frank pricing conversations in three days than in the previous three months. That tracks with what we're hearing generally: margins are tight enough this summer that people want to look each other in the eye before they sign anything.

We also noticed more conversation than usual about traceability and labeling technology — nothing you'd call a trend yet, but enough members asking about provenance labeling and cold-chain verification that it's worth a proper look in a future post.

Recorded sessions from the general track will be posted to the member portal over the next couple of weeks; competition results in full, including every category and every entry, are already up. If your make didn't place this year and you want a second read before next year's cycle, the Sensory Evaluation Certificate coursework covers exactly the kind of structured tasting the judges are doing at the table — it's worth a look if you want to understand the rubric from the inside rather than guessing at it from the results sheet.

Thanks again to everyone who came out to Kentucky. Next year's conference details will go up on the events page as soon as the venue is locked, and we'll flag it here the moment it does.

A few members asked us on the way out whether next year's competition would add categories to relieve the crowding we saw in Blue Veined this year. That's a question for the Awards & Competition Committee, not for communications, but we've passed it along, and we'd encourage anyone with a strong opinion on category structure to reach them directly rather than waiting for us to relay it secondhand.
