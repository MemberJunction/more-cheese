Every December we hear some version of the same thing from members: "I keep meaning to get my HACCP paperwork current" or "I've been saying I'll sit for the cheesemonger credential for two years now." Neither of those is a small ask, and neither is something you finish between now and January 1. But this is the week people actually plan it, so we'd rather talk about it now than wait until the excuse has had another twelve months to grow teeth.

The federation offers seven credentials, and they cover different problems. It's worth being honest with yourself about which one you actually need before you commit a winter to it.

## The floor, and the longer game

The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate exists because federal rules increasingly assume you can document a hazard analysis, not just perform one in your head. Programs that meet the standard equivalency generally run in the range of a two-day course, whether taken in a room or online in self-paced units, and they're built for the person actually responsible for the plan — a plant manager, a production supervisor, a head cheesemaker who's never had anyone check their homework. If you're the person who wrote your creamery's food safety plan by copying a template you found somewhere, this is the credential that closes that gap. It is not optional risk management dressed up as a certificate; it's closer to a floor everyone making cheese for sale should be standing on.

Certified Cheesemonger and Certified Cheese Professional sit on the other end of the spectrum — broad exams covering production, sensory evaluation, business practice, and enough regional and historical detail that cramming doesn't really work. If you're the retail or wholesale side of this business rather than the production side, one of these two is usually the better use of a winter than a second run at a make-technique workshop. Members who've been through the Certified Cheese Professional exam describe it less as a test of trivia and more as a test of whether you can connect the dots between a make decision and what ends up in the case — useful for exactly the job most of you actually do.

## When you already know what you don't know

If you've been aging cheese for years and you're past the basics, the Advanced Affinage Certificate is built around the judgment calls — humidity swings, rind development timing, when to intervene and when to leave a wheel alone — that a beginner course won't touch. Competition Judge Accreditation is its own animal: it trains you to score someone else's cheese against a standard, not just your own taste, which turns out to be a harder skill than most experienced cheesemakers expect the first time they sit on a panel. Members who've judged a competition after earning the accreditation say the scoring discipline changed how they taste their own wheels, too, not just other people's.

None of these credentials get easier by thinking about them longer. If one of the seven is the thing you've been meaning to start, use the quiet week between now and the new year to check the enrollment calendar and put a date on it — even if the date is three months out. A credential with a start date attached to it survives January. A credential that's still "sometime this year" on December 28 usually isn't.
