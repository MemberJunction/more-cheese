Kathy OLeary has worked the counter at Orchardmere Cheese & Provisions for a few years now, but her federation membership is only a couple of months old. She joined in June, in the middle of the shop's busiest stretch of the year, which she says was either terrible timing or exactly the right push. "I kept meaning to sign up and kept not doing it," she told us. "Then a supplier rep asked if I had a member number for a discount code, and I didn't have an answer."

That small, practical moment is a common one. People who work in cheese every day, at a counter, in a cave, on a truck, often assume membership is for the owner or the head cheesemaker, not for them. Orchardmere has long carried a strong roster of specialty cheeses and a loyal case of regulars, and Kathy has been part of building that case for a while without a membership card of her own.

## What she has used so far

Two months in, Kathy says the directory is what she has opened the most, mostly to check who supplies what before a customer asks her to track something down. She has also started reading the federation's tasting and provenance notes more closely, something she says has changed how she talks to customers about where a wheel actually came from rather than just what it tastes like. Retail-facing members tell us this kind of provenance conversation is increasingly what shoppers expect, not an extra, and that shops with a genuinely engaged counter staff tend to hold onto regulars even when a competitor undercuts on price.

That tracks with something we hear from busier specialty shops generally: the difference customers notice is rarely the fixture or the signage, it is whether the person behind the counter actually knows the cheese and seems glad to talk about it. A counter staffed by people who treat the shift as a chore reads differently to a regular customer than one where the staff are visibly invested, and Kathy says that is part of what pulled her toward finally joining rather than just clocking in. She wanted the vocabulary to match the enthusiasm she already had.

She has not yet been to a conference or signed up for a course, and we are not going to pretend she has. New members settle in at their own pace, and the honest version of a spotlight admits that most of the value in the first few months is quieter than a competition ribbon: a directory that answers a question fast, a newsletter that flags a supplier change before a customer does, a reference note that saves her from guessing at a rind she has not seen before.

## If you are the newest one on your own team

If you work behind a counter, in a cave, or on a route and have assumed membership is somebody else's line item, it is worth checking whether your employer already carries a membership that covers you, or whether an individual membership makes more sense for how you actually use the federation day to day. Either way, the directory and the member newsletter are open to you from day one, not after your first conference, and Kathy's advice to anyone in her position is not to wait for a supplier rep to ask the question for you.
