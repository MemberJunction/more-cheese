If your distributor rep has sounded a little more upbeat on recent calls, you're not imagining it. After more than a year of soft prices and tight milk supply working against each other, several dairy categories firmed up this fall, and members who buy on contract are already asking what it means for the next two quarters.

The short version: demand held up better than the supply side expected, and a run of tighter milk output through the summer meant processors weren't sitting on the cushion they'd had in 2022. That combination has a way of turning a sleepy market fast, without a single headline event to point to. It's the kind of shift that shows up first in a wholesale invoice, not a press release.

## What this means for your ordering calendar

For members buying inputs — cultures, rennet, packaging — a firmer dairy market usually shows up as slower-moving quotes and shorter validity windows on pricing your suppliers offer. If you've been running month-to-month on a handshake price, this is a reasonable stretch to ask for a 60- or 90-day lock instead, especially heading into the holiday production push. It costs you nothing to ask, and a supplier who's watching the same market you are will usually understand why you're asking.

For members selling finished wheels, firmer category pricing is generally good news, but it's worth being deliberate about how much of it you pass through right away. Buyers remember a price increase longer than they remember why it happened. A short note with your next invoice — "input costs are up across the category, here's what we adjusted and why" — tends to land better than a silent bump.

None of this happened in a vacuum. Dairy Margin Coverage payments have been flowing for most of the year on the strength of a margin that's stayed tighter than usual, which tells you the underlying cost pressure on the production side was real even before this fall's price movement. Producers we've talked to this year describe it less as a windfall and more as a program finally doing the job it was built for — smoothing a stretch that would otherwise have been a lot rougher.

There's also a calendar deadline worth flagging. The current farm bill's authorization for dairy programs is due to lapse at the end of the year unless federal lawmakers extend or replace it, which is a live thread we've been tracking separately through the federation's advocacy work. A lapse wouldn't switch anything off overnight, but it would leave next year's Dairy Margin Coverage signup sitting on uncertain footing right when producers are trying to plan around it. If you produce milk yourself and haven't looked at your own Dairy Margin Coverage election for next year, now is a reasonable time to pencil it in alongside your fourth-quarter budgeting — before the holiday rush eats the calendar entirely, and before any lapse forces a scramble.

It's worth saying that none of this is showing up as a slowdown at the consumer end. Regional cheese festivals this year have reported strong walk-up turnout, which lines up with what our retail members are telling us about foot traffic at the case. Members on the buying side should treat the farm bill deadline as a reason for a little extra slack in December ordering, not a reason to panic. Farm bill lapses have historically been followed by short-term extensions more often than not, but "historically" isn't a guarantee, and a supply contract that assumes a program will definitely be renewed on schedule is making a bet it doesn't need to make.

## What we're watching

We don't make price predictions, and neither should you base a production plan on ours. What we can tell you is that a market moving this much on this little public notice is worth checking against your own numbers rather than assuming your last quote is still good. A firming market also tends to reward members who ask early rather than late — a supplier fielding its first few lock-in requests of the season has more room to say yes than one who's already promised out its capacity by Thanksgiving.

If you want a second set of eyes on what a firming market means for your specific inputs, the Membership & Outreach Committee can point you toward members in your category who've already had that conversation with their suppliers this month. And if the farm bill timeline affects your operation directly, our advocacy team keeps a running summary of where the dairy title stands — worth a look before you finalize a 2024 budget around assumptions that might not hold.
