Two virtual sessions land on the calendar for April 20, one built around allergen disclosure on
finished cheese and one built around rennet alternatives, and both are the kind our members tend
to ask about the week after rather than the week before — so this year we're getting the notice
out early. Both are free, both virtual, and both scheduled for the same afternoon, which means
members with a real stake in both subjects have a scheduling decision to make sooner than they'd
like.

## The allergen disclosure session

This one is aimed at anyone selling finished cheese directly to consumers or through retail
accounts, especially members handling nut-adjacent ingredients — think a walnut-crusted rind or
a fig-and-almond pairing box — where a labeling gap creates real liability, not just a compliance
headache. It's also a timely session: sesame joined the list of major allergens requiring
disclosure at the start of this year, and food-safety trade guidance has been reminding
processors since that any product already in the supply chain before the new requirement took
effect doesn't have to be pulled and relabeled, which has left some retail accounts confused
about what's actually on the shelf. The session walks through what has to appear on a label, what
counts as adequate allergen warning language, and where members most often get tripped up when a
seasonal recipe changes mid-run without anyone updating the label copy to match. If you've
changed a recipe since your last label print run, put this one on your calendar before you put
anything else there. Trade coverage this week is still tracking how unevenly that requirement has
rolled out across smaller processors, which is exactly the gap this session is meant to close for
our members.

## The rennet alternatives session, and picking one if you can only make one

The second session is aimed squarely at the production side: a comparison of animal, microbial,
and fermentation-produced coagulants, with attention to how each behaves differently in a
washed-curd or long-aged style. Members who've been asked by a buyer whether their line has a
vegetarian-rennet option — a question we're hearing more of this year than last, alongside wider
interest in fermentation-derived dairy ingredients generally — will get a straight answer about
what actually changes in the vat when you swap coagulants, not just a marketing pitch for one
option over another. It's a narrower audience than the allergen session, but for members running
a washed-curd line, it's the more consequential hour.

If you only have the bandwidth for one, match it to where your exposure actually sits this
quarter: a recipe or packaging change points you toward the allergen session, a coagulant
question from a buyer points you toward rennet. If your role touches both — a lot of our
smaller-creamery members wear both hats — ask whether someone else on your team can sit in on the
other session and swap notes afterward. Both webinars record and post to the members' resource
library within a few days, so nothing here is truly a hard choice between the two, just a
question of which one you want live.

Registration for both goes live through the events calendar; if you're not sure which session
fits your schedule, the Events Committee can point you to whichever time slot runs closest to
your time zone.
