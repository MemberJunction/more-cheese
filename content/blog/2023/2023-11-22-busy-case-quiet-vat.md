Two things are true about this week at once, and they pull in opposite directions. Commodity cheese plants are running lighter holiday schedules, which is normal for this stretch of the calendar and has put some downward pressure on spot milk handling. At the same time, several members tell us their retail case has never looked busier, between gift-box orders, holiday charcuterie boards, and shoppers stocking up before next week's gathering. Both of those things being true in the same month is exactly the split that makes late November hard to read from the outside if you're only watching one side of it.

For a commodity-focused operation, a quieter production schedule this week isn't a warning sign so much as the ordinary seasonal rhythm — plants run lighter, milk handlers absorb the shift, and the pattern corrects itself once the holiday stretch passes. If your business sits closer to that side of the industry, this is a normal week, not a concerning one, and we'd rather say that plainly than let a quiet production number sit unexplained in a members' inbox.

## Where the actual growth is sitting

For members on the specialty and artisan side, the story reads differently. Holiday gift boxes, aged wedges, and anything with a story behind it have been a genuine bright spot, and several members running their own retail counter or farm stand describe November as one of their strongest months of the year, gift-box orders especially. That tracks with the broader pattern the specialty cheese category has shown for a couple of years running now: shoppers trimming elsewhere in the cart while still paying up for something they consider worth the occasion. It's also visible outside our own membership this month — several holiday gift guides published this week feature cheese boxes and subscriptions alongside the usual food-gift lineup, which wasn't a given even a few years back.

A few members flagged the same operational wrinkle this week: gift-box demand front-loads a lot of packing and shipping work into a short window, which stresses a small staff more than the equivalent dollar volume spread evenly across a quarter would. If you're filling holiday orders with the same crew that runs your everyday counter, plan your labor for the crunch weeks specifically rather than averaging it across the whole month. A few extra hours in the first week of December, planned for in advance, tends to cost less than a rushed temp hire once the backlog is already visible on the shelf.

## What to watch through the new year

None of this changes the margin conversation we've been having with members all fall — feed costs are still elevated, and a strong retail week doesn't erase a squeezed production month. What it does mean is that a creamery with both a wholesale side and a retail counter may see genuinely different pictures on each side of the same ledger this week, and it's worth tracking them separately rather than blending them into one number that tells you less than either piece on its own. Treat your holiday retail results as their own line item, not a rounding error inside a bigger annual number.

If your holiday order volume caught you by surprise this year, good or bad, we'd like to hear about it — not for a case study, but because it helps us know what to ask about when we talk with members about next year's planning.
