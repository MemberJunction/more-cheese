Competition Judge Accreditation is not our biggest credential by enrollment, and it was never meant to be. Most years it runs a fraction of the size of the Cheese Foundations Certificate or Certified Cheesemonger cohorts. But as our competition calendar has filled back in over the last two seasons, more members are asking what it takes to sit on a judging panel instead of just entering one, and the honest answer is: more than a good palate.

The credential assumes you can already taste critically. What it actually trains and tests is something narrower and, frankly, harder to fake: consistency. A judge who scores a washed-rind wheel a 34 out of 40 on Tuesday and a nearly identical wheel a 29 on Thursday, with no defensible reason for the gap, is a liability to a competition's credibility even if their palate is excellent. The Sensory Evaluation Certificate builds the vocabulary for describing a defect precisely. Judge accreditation builds the discipline to apply that vocabulary the same way, wheel after wheel, category after category, without your mood or your lunch or the judge next to you shifting your number.

## What the coursework covers

The program leans on scoresheet mechanics as much as sensory theory. You'll spend real time on how a category's scoresheet is weighted — a fresh chèvre and an aged alpine-style wheel are not judged against the same rubric, and a judge who applies one style's expectations to another style's category produces scores nobody can defend later. There's a module on calibration exercises: tasting the same reference set as a room full of other candidates and comparing your scores against the panel average, which is a genuinely humbling way to spend an afternoon. And there's a section most candidates underestimate going in, on documentation — writing feedback a maker can actually use, rather than a number with no explanation attached.

Prerequisites are lighter than people expect. You don't need the flagship Certified Cheese Professional credential to start, though most candidates who pursue judge accreditation already hold the Sensory Evaluation Certificate or are close to it, since the two build on the same tasting fundamentals. What the program does ask for is documented tasting hours — enough repetitions that the calibration exercises mean something.

## Why it matters beyond the judging table

Members sometimes assume this credential is only useful if you want to work a competition circuit. In practice, the discipline transfers directly to your own cave or your own case. A cheesemonger who's been through calibration training tends to catch a slipping batch earlier, because they've built the habit of comparing what's in front of them against a fixed standard instead of against whatever they tasted last. A head cheesemaker who's judged a category knows exactly what an outside evaluator is going to notice first, which is a useful thing to know before you submit a wheel yourself.

If you're weighing whether to pursue accreditation this cycle, the Awards & Competition Committee keeps a running list of judging slots that open up as the calendar fills, and accredited members get first notice. It is not a fast credential — most candidates spend a full season on the calibration work before they feel ready to sit a panel — but for members who already spend their days tasting critically, it is one of the more directly useful things the federation offers.
