The multi-year federal farm law that underwrites a lot of the dairy sector's safety net is due to expire at the end of this month, and federal lawmakers have not agreed on what replaces it. That includes the margin-support program a lot of our members with their own herds have leaned on this year — the one that pays out when the gap between milk prices and feed costs gets too thin. It has triggered a payment in most months of the current program year, which tells you how real that gap has been for producers who bought feed at a bad time.

We want to be careful about what we are and are not saying here. We are not a lobbying shop, and we are not going to tell you how to feel about farm policy in general. What we can tell you, because members have told us directly, is that a program lapse — even a temporary one bridged by a short-term extension — creates real uncertainty for anyone budgeting a creamery's next two quarters around it. If your farmstead operation has counted on that payment showing up when your milk check comes in light, a gap in coverage is not an abstraction.

## What our advocacy volunteers are watching

Our Membership & Outreach Committee has been tracking this alongside interstate shipment and raw-milk aging rules, which have taken up more of our advocacy bandwidth this year. Dairy pricing reform hasn't had the volume of petition activity those other topics have gotten, and honestly, we think that is partly because it is easy to assume someone else in the industry is already making this case. Someone is. But a federal program renewal is exactly the kind of decision where a comment record full of only large-scale operators tells an incomplete story. Small and mid-sized creamery voices belong in that record too.

If you want to add yours, the fastest path is a short letter to your own representatives describing your operation in plain terms: herd size or milk volume, what a payment gap would mean for your cash flow this fall, and whether you have used the margin program before. You do not need policy language. A specific, honest account of your own numbers is more persuasive than a form letter, and it is the kind of testimony our advocacy volunteers tell us actually gets read.

## What we are not saying

We are not predicting whether the program lapses, gets bridged, or gets renewed on schedule — nobody outside the process actually knows yet. We are not offering legal or tax guidance on how to plan around a gap in coverage; that is a conversation for your own accountant or lender, who can see your specific numbers. What we can do is keep members informed as the picture changes and make sure the record includes creamery-scale voices, not just the largest operations.

If you want to add your name to the letter-writing effort this program year, or if you simply want to understand how the margin-support program has worked for members who have used it, reach out to the Membership & Outreach Committee through the member portal. We will keep this post updated if the picture changes before the end of the month.
