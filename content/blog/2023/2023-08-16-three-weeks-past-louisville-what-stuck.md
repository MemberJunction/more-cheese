The badges are unpacked, the sample coolers are back in storage, and by now most of you have had three weeks to let the ICF Annual Conference in Louisville settle. It landed in a steadier stretch for the broader dairy market than last year's conference did, which probably explains why the mood in the hallways was closer to planning-ahead than white-knuckling the next milk check. We've spent this week going back through session feedback and hallway conversations, and a few things kept coming up enough that they're worth saying out loud.

The affinage track was the room people mentioned first, unprompted. Not because the content was new — cave humidity and rind management aren't reinventing themselves overnight — but because this was the first year the sessions filled with makers who'd only ever learned the craft from video calls. A member told one of our staff, half-joking, that she finally got to argue about washed-rind timing with another human being in the same room, and it was better than she remembered.

## The conversation that kept happening in the hallway

The other recurring topic was less about technique and more about the shelf. Several members brought up the same tension: specialty cheese counters keep growing, but so does the pressure to keep a rotating cast of unusual, attention-getting cheeses next to the staples that actually pay the bills. That tension shows up in the wider grocery data too — fresh cheese categories have held a big share of specialty sales this year precisely because they're dependable, even while buyers keep asking for something new to talk about at the counter. Nobody at the conference had a clean answer, which is honestly the more useful outcome than a tidy one — it means the Membership & Outreach Committee has real material to work with instead of a slogan.

Competition results are still worth a second look if you missed the announcement stage. Categories were tighter than most years, and more than one exhibitor told us they changed their entry between the schedule closing and the actual drop-off after tasting a competitor's wheel in the holding room. That's either gamesmanship or good sportsmanship depending on how you tell the story, and we're choosing to call it good sportsmanship.

## What to do with a good conference besides remember it fondly

A conference is only worth what you do with it in the following month, so here's the practical version:

- If you took notes on a technique question you didn't get answered, the Standards Committee and the Food Safety Committee both take member questions between meetings — send yours in rather than waiting for the parking-lot conversation to fade.
- If you met a distributor or a fellow member you want to keep talking to, do it this week. Conference contacts have a short half-life once the harvest and fall production schedule takes over everyone's calendar.
- If competition judging made you want to understand the scoring better, the Competition Judge Accreditation credential walks through exactly what the panels are weighing, category by category.

We'll be back with a full announcement on next year's conference location once the Events Committee finalizes it, but for now: thank you to everyone who showed up, asked a hard question in a session, or helped a first-timer find the right room. That last part matters more than it sounds like it should — several members told us their first conference only felt worth the trip because someone experienced sat with them at lunch.
