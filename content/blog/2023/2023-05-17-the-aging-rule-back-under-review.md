The federal rule requiring raw-milk cheese to age at least 60 days before sale has been in place since the middle of the last century, and it comes up in our advocacy inbox almost every year. This spring it's come up again, as regulators continue an ongoing microbiological sampling program on aged raw-milk cheese and members ask, reasonably, whether 60 days is still the right number or just the old one.

We've seen a steady run of letter-campaign activity from members under our raw-milk aging rules advocacy effort this year, and it's picked up again over the past month. That's not a sign of new legislation moving. It's a sign that our raw-milk members are watching the federal sampling program closely and want their own experience on the record before anything changes.

## Where the rule actually stands

Nothing about the 60-day requirement has changed this spring, and we're not aware of a specific rule change pending right now. What is happening is continued federal sampling and testing of aged raw-milk cheese, the kind of ongoing data collection that eventually feeds into whether an old rule gets revisited. A previous multi-year sampling round tested well over a thousand raw-milk cheese samples and found contamination rates for the pathogens regulators care most about running under one percent — a result raw-milk producers have cited for years as evidence the current aging window works. Whether that evidence translates into any near-term change to the rule itself is a separate question, and one nobody at the federal level has answered yet.

For a requirement written decades ago, the honest position — and the one most of our raw-milk members hold — is that the science should get another look periodically, whichever direction it points. That's a more nuanced position than "loosen it" or "keep it," and we think it's worth saying plainly: members who make raw-milk cheese are not asking regulators to skip food safety. They're asking for the rule to reflect the best current data about which cheeses, aged how, actually pose a risk — which may mean some categories could responsibly age less, and may mean others need more scrutiny, not less.

## What we're asking members to send us

If you make raw-milk cheese and have production records, batch testing history, or aging-and-outcome data going back several years, that's exactly the kind of member evidence that carries weight whenever a formal review opens a comment window. Recordkeeping you've been doing anyway for your own quality control turns out to double as advocacy material, and it's a lot easier to pull together now than to reconstruct later under a deadline.

We're also hearing from newer raw-milk producers who assumed this was a settled question they didn't need to track. It isn't settled, and it's worth tracking even if you don't plan to submit anything yourself — a rule change in either direction would reshape what's possible for your cave and your release schedule with fairly short notice once it's finalized.

We'll flag a formal comment window here the moment one opens. In the meantime, the Food Safety Committee is keeping a running file of member questions and production data so we're ready to respond quickly rather than starting from scratch when that day comes. If you've got real experience with the 60-day rule — good or bad — send it our way; the file is more useful with more voices in it, not fewer.
