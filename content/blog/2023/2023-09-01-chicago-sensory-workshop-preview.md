Registration for our Chicago sensory workshop opened in July, and with the event six weeks out, seats are filling in the way they usually do at this point in the cycle — steadily, and mostly with members who waited to see how their fall production schedule shook out before committing a day of travel. If that's you, this is a good week to decide.

## What the day actually is

The workshop is built around structured tasting practice, not a lecture with cheese on the side. Small groups work through flights of samples together, calibrating language against each other before anyone sees a reference sheet, which is deliberately the reverse of how most people first learn to describe cheese. It's the same in-person format the federation has run for several years now, and it pairs naturally with the Sensory Evaluation Certificate for anyone working toward that credential, though it isn't a requirement — plenty of attendees come just to sharpen a skill they use every week at a counter or a bench.

We've noticed the room tends to split roughly three ways: cheesemongers who want a shared vocabulary to use with customers, assistant cheesemakers who want a second, more disciplined way to evaluate their own batches, and buyers who taste for a living but rarely get to compare notes with people outside their own company. All three groups report the same thing afterward, which is that tasting next to strangers who use slightly different words for the same sensation is more useful than tasting alone ever is.

## What to bring, and what not to worry about

You don't need prior sensory training to get value out of the day — the morning session is built for people who've never used formal tasting vocabulary before, and it moves at a pace that assumes that. Bring a notebook. Leave your assumptions about what a "good" wheel tastes like at the door; a fair amount of the exercise is learning to describe what's actually in front of you rather than what you expect to taste.

Part of why we keep running this workshop in the fall is timing. This year's national judging season drew well over a thousand entries across more than a hundred categories, and the case at the retail level has kept diversifying to match — traditional styles alongside newer entrants experimenting with different bases and cultures, including a wave of dairy-adjacent product development that's still finding its footing on flavor. A shared vocabulary for describing rind, paste, and finish isn't just a competition-judging skill in a season like that — it's a sales skill, and one that's harder to build alone than most people expect.

A crowded, more varied case also means more members are the ones standing behind the counter or answering the phone when a customer asks what something actually tastes like before they buy it. The workshop won't teach you every style you might carry this fall, but it will give you a consistent way to describe whatever's new on the table, which turns out to matter more than memorizing every variety in advance.

Seats are capped to keep the tasting groups small, and travel discounts through our partner hotel block hold for another two weeks. If you've been meaning to register and haven't, the events page has the remaining spots and the group hotel code.
