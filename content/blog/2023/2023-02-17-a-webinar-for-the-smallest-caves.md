Not every member reading this has a cave. A good number of you are aging wheels in a converted closet, a corner of a walk-in, or a basement room that started life as something else entirely and got repurposed once the humidity numbers looked promising. That's the audience for this month's webinar, Small-Room Affinage, going out live on the 23rd.

We hear from small-room producers often enough that this webinar has become something close to an annual fixture, and the questions rarely change much year to year: how do you hold a stable humidity in a space too small to have much thermal mass of its own, what do you do when the room shares a wall with something that generates heat, and how do you keep a wash schedule consistent when the room's own conditions swing more than a purpose-built cave's would. This year's session spends extra time on that last one, since inconsistent washing is the single most common cause of the rind problems small-room members bring to us.

## What's different about a small room

A large cave has enough air volume and thermal mass to smooth out a lot of small mistakes — a door left open for ten minutes barely registers. A converted closet does not have that forgiveness built in. The temperature and humidity swings happen faster and harder, which means the margin for error on your monitoring schedule is smaller too. The session covers practical fixes that don't require a capital investment: passive humidity buffers, placement strategies that keep wheels away from a room's coldest or warmest corner, and a simplified logging cadence built for someone checking a room once or twice a day rather than running continuous sensors.

The demand for this kind of session tracks the broader picture in specialty cheese right now. Industry coverage this year has kept describing steady growth in artisan and small-batch cheese, with new producers entering even as market conditions stay uneven — which in practice means more of our newer members are starting exactly where this webinar is aimed: small, improvised, and figuring out affinage by trial and adjustment rather than by design.

## If you can't make it live

The webinar is free and virtual, which has been the format for most of our smallest-audience sessions since the pivot to online programming a couple of years back — it's simply easier for a one- or two-person operation to carve out ninety minutes on a screen than to travel for a workshop. If the 23rd doesn't work, a recording goes up in the member portal within a few days, and the Q&A submitted during the live session gets answered in writing for anyone who watches later. If you're building your own small-room setup and want more structured material afterward, the Rind Development & Washing course covers the same fundamentals at more depth over several weeks.

Members can register through the events calendar in the member portal; there's no fee and no cap on attendance for this one.

Feed and packaging costs have stayed elevated across the dairy trade this winter, which is exactly the kind of pressure that pushes more members toward a converted room instead of a purpose-built cave in the first place — a smaller footprint costs less to climate-control and less to build out. If that's the position you're in, this webinar is written for you specifically, not as a smaller version of a session aimed at a bigger operation. Bring your own room's numbers to the live Q&A; the presenters have found that a specific humidity swing or a specific wall-sharing problem gets a more useful answer than a general one.
