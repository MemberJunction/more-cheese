Two pieces of federal news landed close together this fall, and members have been asking us to sort out what each one actually does. The short version: the federal margin support program that catastrophically triggered twice this summer has kept improving, and the current farm bill got a one-year extension instead of a rewrite. Neither is a solution. Both are worth understanding before your next renewal cycle or your next call to a state committee.

## What actually moved

The federal milk-to-feed margin calculation has climbed for four straight months, from a summer low that broke the catastrophic coverage threshold for the first time since the program began, up into single digits per hundredweight by November. That's real relief for members enrolled in the program, and it shows up as a payment, not a policy change. The formula, the coverage tiers, and the annual enrollment window are all exactly what they were in January. If your creamery's margin position improved this fall, it's worth checking now whether your current coverage level still makes sense before the next signup, rather than waiting until the numbers turn again.

The farm bill news is separate and, honestly, less dramatic than the headlines made it sound. Rather than passing a new bill with new dairy provisions, the extension keeps the existing programs running for another year. For members who were hoping this cycle would finally rework how the margin program's feed-cost calculation works, or widen the coverage tiers that small producers keep telling us feel too coarse, that conversation gets pushed another year down the road.

## Why we keep saying "keep the testimony coming"

We know a one-year extension can feel like nothing happened. It's closer to the opposite: it means the window where changes get made hasn't opened yet, and won't close either. Members who've filed comments or shown up for state hearings on margin coverage tiers, feed-cost formulas, or the paperwork burden of enrollment are the reason those specific fixes are even on a list anywhere. Tom Reyes, whose testimony record on shipment and safety questions is a fixture around here, has said more than once that the boring years are when the actual language gets written — the loud years just decide whether it passes.

If you've never filed anything with a state agriculture committee or a federal comment docket, this is a reasonable year to start small. A short written account of how a coverage tier or a feed-cost assumption plays out on your own books carries more weight than you'd think, and it's a lot less intimidating than testifying in person. Trade coverage of the extension has mostly framed it as lawmakers buying time rather than resolving anything, which tracks with what members closer to the process have told us directly: the arguments over feed-cost formulas and coverage tiers didn't go away, they just moved to next year's calendar.

That's worth remembering the next time a comment period feels too small to matter. The margin program didn't get its coverage tiers from nowhere — someone's specific, dated example of how a tier failed to match their operation is sitting in a docket somewhere from an earlier cycle. This is the quiet year to add yours.

Two concrete things follow from all of that. First, if you're enrolled in margin coverage, take twenty minutes this month to check whether your current tier still fits, now that the margin has moved. Second, if there's a piece of the program that doesn't work for a creamery your size, write it down in plain language and send it to us — we route member input to the right committee, and specific, dated examples travel further than general complaints. The Membership & Outreach Committee can point you to the current open comment windows if you're not sure where to start.
