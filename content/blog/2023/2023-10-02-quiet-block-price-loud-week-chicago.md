Cash cheese barely blinked as the month turned over. Forty-pound blocks opened October within a
few cents of where they closed September, and federal market news is describing the tone as
"steady," which is the dairy market's way of saying nobody wants to make the first move. Class
III milk has been drifting lower most of the fall, and the latest federal supply-and-demand
estimate trimmed its full-year price outlook again this month, a few dimes lower than September's
call. None of it is dramatic. All of it adds up over a quarter.

We've had more calls than usual this week from members asking what a quiet market actually costs
them, and the honest answer is: it depends how you're contracted. If your creamery sells mostly on
spot, a flat few weeks reads as calm. If a chunk of your volume is tied to a formula that resets
off these same federal reference prices, calm this month can mean a smaller check two or three
months out, after some lag rolls through the calculation. Read your contract's pricing basis
before you decide whether a quiet market is good news or bad news for your specific operation.

## The Chicago factor

The bigger story for the next two weeks isn't the price — it's the guest list. A large
international dairy gathering opens in Chicago in the middle of the month, the kind of event that
pulls processors, cooperatives, and trade delegations from dozens of countries onto one convention
floor for several days, with closing remarks expected a few days later once sessions wrap. It's
not an ICF event and we're not running programming there, but enough of our members work in
specialty and export-adjacent roles that we expect a fair number of you to be in the building
anyway, or at least fielding calls from someone who is.

For members who aren't going, the practical thing to watch is what comes out of the gathering in
the weeks after, not the week itself. International trade conversations at that scale tend to
produce commentary on tariff lines, import quotas, and standards harmonization that eventually
filters down into the rules a smaller creamery has to follow when it ships a wheel across a
border. We'll be reading the output and flagging anything that touches artisan and specialty
producers specifically, rather than the commodity side of the business that usually gets the
headline in the trade press.

## What we're telling members this week

Three things, in the order we've been asked them most often. First, a flat cash market is not the
same as a flat farm-gate price for everyone on a formula contract — check your own basis before
you assume the quiet applies to you. Second, if you export or are seriously considering it, the
next month of trade commentary out of Chicago is worth more of your attention than the daily
block-and-barrel print, which moves on its own schedule regardless of what gets discussed on a
conference floor. Third, if you've been putting off a hard look at your own cost structure because
the market felt too unsettled to plan around, a quiet week is actually a reasonable one to use for
that instead of waiting for a signal that may not come.

Members with active export lines can find our current documentation guidance through the
education calendar; we'll flag anything concrete that comes out of the Chicago gathering again
next week, once there's something specific worth passing along rather than just atmosphere.
