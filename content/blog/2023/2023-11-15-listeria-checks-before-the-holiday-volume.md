Every November we hear the same thing from members: the gift-box orders and the counter traffic are both climbing at once, and that's exactly when environmental monitoring gets skipped. More product moving through the same drains, the same slicer, the same aging room, on a schedule that has less slack in it than usual. It's the worst week to let a swab schedule slide, and the easiest week to let it happen anyway.

## Why this is a volume problem, not a hygiene problem

Most small creameries we talk to aren't doing anything wrong day to day. The risk shows up when volume goes up without the monitoring schedule going up with it. A drain that gets swabbed weekly in September is carrying more moisture and more foot traffic by mid-November, and a slicer blade that's fine at forty wheels a week starts telling a different story at seventy. Listeria doesn't need a mistake. It needs an environment that stayed the same while everything running through it changed.

Our Listeria Control in Small Creameries course spends most of its time on exactly this gap — not the theory of the organism, but the practical version: where in a small facility it actually tends to establish (drains, mats, wet equipment, condensate lines), how to build a swab map you'll actually keep using instead of one that looks good on paper and gets abandoned by February, and how to read a corrective action plan so it fixes the root cause instead of just re-cleaning the same spot. It's built for creameries running with two or three people in production, where "add another QA hire" isn't a real option this season.

It won't tell you your cheese is safe to eat, and it won't tell you what your legal exposure is if a test comes back positive — that's a conversation for your own food safety consultant and, if it comes to it, your attorney. What it will do is help you build the kind of documented, defensible monitoring program that makes those harder conversations less likely to happen in the first place, and easier to walk through if a customer or a regulator asks. A trade digest we follow flagged a raw-milk cheese warning out of the mid-Atlantic this same November, tied to a positive Listeria test on an aged wheel — the kind of notice that tends to make every cave-running member a little more attentive to their own swab schedule for a week or two, whether or not it touches their own supply chain.

## Do this before Thanksgiving week

If your creamery hasn't touched its environmental swab map since early fall, this is the week to pull it out and check whether the sampling points still match where your product actually travels now that volume is up. The next Listeria Control in Small Creameries cohort is open for enrollment through the education portal, and it runs short enough to finish before the holiday rush is in full swing. If you took it earlier in the year, a refresher pass through the swab-map section takes less than an hour and is worth the hour.
