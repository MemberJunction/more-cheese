The ICF Annual Conference opens in Louisville on Tuesday, and if you're driving instead of flying, this is the week to check your route against the heat advisories rather than assume the interstate will be fine. Kentucky and the surrounding region have been under sustained heat warnings this month, and a wheel that spends six hours in a trunk in July needs more than a cooler bag between it and a ruined entry. Pack it like you mean it: an insulated box, ice packs frozen solid the night before, and a thermometer you actually check at your first fuel stop.

This is our flagship in-person event, and after a few years of rebuilding it, this year's registration numbers look like the biggest gathering we've had since before the pandemic pause. Other cheese trade gatherings around the country are reporting similar rebounds this summer, which lines up with what our own Events Committee has been seeing in registration trends since spring. If you haven't been to a conference since the virtual years, expect a different rhythm than you remember: more hallway time built into the schedule on purpose, because that's consistently what members have told us they valued most on the feedback forms.

## What's actually on the schedule

Beyond the general sessions, three things are worth planning your days around. The competition judging runs across two full days this year, with results announced at the closing dinner - if you've got an entry in, get it checked into the staging room the moment doors open rather than waiting until your assigned slot. The Standards Committee is holding an open comment session on the current draft language, which is genuinely useful if you've been meaning to weigh in but haven't found the right venue for it. And the Education Committee has expanded its short-course track this year, with sessions timed so you can catch two without missing a meal in between.

If you're presenting or exhibiting, double-check your booth or table assignment against the confirmation email now rather than at check-in - a printer error moved two exhibitors last year, and both only found out standing in the registration line. And if this is your first conference, look for the first-timer meetup on the first morning; it's kept small on purpose, and it's a much easier way to meet people than trying to work an entire ballroom cold.

A few members ship entries ahead through a refrigerated carrier rather than hand-carrying them, and if you're driving through the current heat, that's worth considering even at the added cost. If you are driving, plan your route with fuel stops timed for shade if you can, and never leave a cooler in a parked car longer than you have to - cab temperature climbs fast in the kind of heat the region has seen this month, and it climbs faster than most coolers can fight.

## If you're not going this year

Not everyone can make the trip, and that's fine. We'll post a recap the week after with competition results and the top takeaways from the general sessions, and the Education Committee's short-course materials are typically made available afterward for members who couldn't attend in person. If Louisville isn't in the cards this year, keep an eye on our events calendar - there's usually a regional workshop within driving distance of most members before the year is out.

Safe travels, and see you there.
