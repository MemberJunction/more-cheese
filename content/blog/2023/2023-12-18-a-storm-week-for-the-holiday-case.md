A major winter storm moved through the Northeast this week, and it picked about the worst possible days to do it. Flash flooding, flight cancellations, and downed trees are inconvenient any week; landing them on the shipping window between now and Christmas is a different problem entirely for anyone with wheels on a truck instead of already sitting in a case. We heard from several members Monday who were rerouting orders, pushing pickup times, or simply telling customers a delivery would run a day late. "Our usual truck couldn't get out of the county until Tuesday afternoon, so I spent Monday morning calling every customer with a gift order instead of packing more boxes," one shop owner told us.

None of this shows up in a federal price report, which is worth saying plainly, because the numbers that do get published this month tell a calmer story. Wholesale cheese and butter prices have both softened compared to November, and milk supply has stayed steady enough to keep plants running through the holiday push. That's a market with plenty of product moving. It says nothing about whether your specific truck made it out of the specific county that got the worst of the storm.

## The gap between the market and your loading dock

This is the distinction we try to remind members of every December: a soft or steady commodity market is a supply-and-demand story, and a missed delivery window is a logistics story, and the two don't always move together. A specialty distributor can have a full truck, a willing driver, and a customer waiting, and still lose a day to road closures that have nothing to do with cheese at all. If you sell direct-to-consumer gift boxes this time of year, a one-day slip between now and December 24 is the kind of thing a customer remembers longer than almost anything else you do right.

A few practical notes members have passed along this week, for what they're worth with one shipping week left before the holiday:

- Confirm delivery windows with your carrier rather than assuming last week's schedule still holds — several members found their usual pickup time had already shifted before they called to ask.
- If you ship perishable gift orders, build in a buffer day before promising an arrival date to a customer. A cold-chain delay that adds a day is manageable; a delay that blows past a promised date is a refund conversation.
- If a wholesale account is waiting on a holiday order and your usual distributor is stretched thin, this is a reasonable week to ask whether a second carrier in your region has room, rather than finding out the hard way that your only option is fully booked.

## What we're watching after this week

Once the roads clear and the last of the holiday orders ship, the more useful question for members is whether this month's calmer wholesale numbers hold into January, or whether they were partly a function of plants building supply ahead of exactly this kind of disruption. We don't have that answer yet, and neither does anyone publishing a forecast this week. What we do know is that a market report can tell you the state of cheese in general; it can't tell you whether your own truck is running on time. That part is still a phone call, not a chart.

If a carrier issue this week left you scrambling and you'd like to compare notes with other members who route through the same region, our Membership & Outreach Committee keeps an informal list of members willing to talk through their own distribution setups. It's a smaller conversation than a market report, but for a delivery due before December 24, it's usually the more useful one.
