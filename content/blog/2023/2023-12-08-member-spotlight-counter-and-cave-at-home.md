Most weeks, this space goes to a producer or a program director whose name is on a business
license. This week we wanted to talk about a member whose cheesemaking happens after her
shift ends, not during it. She works the counter at a small specialty shop in the upper
Midwest, joined the federation this year as an individual member, and has quietly become one
of the more active voices in our online workshop threads — not because anyone asked her to,
but because she keeps showing up with questions.

By day, she is answering the same holiday-season questions most of our retail members are
fielding right now: which wheel travels well as a gift, which pairing note actually helps a
nervous shopper decide, how to keep a case looking full when a gift-box promotion has
cleared out half your best sellers by the second week of December. Specialty food and
beverage sales have kept growing as a category through this stretch, and gift-focused cheese
purchasing is a real part of that seasonal bump — she's living that math at the register
every day this month.

## What happens after the shift

At home, in a converted closet she insulated and fitted with a small humidifier, she's
working through her first real batch of a washed-curd style, using a make sheet nearly
identical to the ones our commercial members keep, just built for a much smaller volume.
She posts about it in the member forum in plain, first-attempt language — a bandage-wrapped
wheel that cracked under too much humidity, a rind that came in patchier than she wanted, a
second attempt that finally held. Working cheesemakers have started commenting with fixes of
their own, which is exactly the kind of cross-pollination between the retail floor and the
production side of this community that we don't get to plan. It just happens when someone
keeps posting honestly about what didn't work.

It's also a reminder that engagement and revenue aren't the same axis. A member paying an
individual-tier fee can still be doing more to strengthen the community — answering a
question in a workshop chat, sharing a make-day photo that convinces a newer member to try
their first wheel — than plenty of members writing much larger checks. We don't think that
means dues tiers should change. We do think it means we should say thank you publicly once
in a while, and this month, that's her.

## If this sounds like you

We hear from counter staff and home cheesemakers fairly often who assume membership is only
for people running a production business. It isn't. If you're building a home cave in a
closet or a basement, working through your first batch of a washed-curd or bloomy style, or
you just want a community that will tell you honestly whether your rind looks right, the
individual membership tier exists for exactly that. Several of our winter workshops are open
regardless of whether cheese is your job or your evening hobby — reach out to the
Membership & Outreach Committee if you want help finding the right one to start with.
