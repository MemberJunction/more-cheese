## A letter from the federation

2023 was the year the federation ran out of chairs.

We mean that literally. The Annual Conference in Louisville was built for the room we had booked
eighteen months earlier, when nobody was confident that a July in a convention hall was a safe bet
again. Two hundred and seventy-six of you came anyway, and the five workshops we ran across the year
averaged 153 registrations each. It is a good problem, and it was not the problem we spent 2020 and 2021
planning for.

The headline is straightforward: the federation ended 2023 with 924 members holding an active
membership period, up from 661 at the end of 2022. Two hundred and seventy-one of those members
joined us for the first time. Growth of that size in a single year changes an association whether
it wants to be changed or not, and much of this report is an honest accounting of what it changed —
the programme calendar, the competition, the committee workload, and the small operational seams
that a smaller federation could get away with ignoring.

What did not change is where the work actually happens. It happens in a make room at five in the
morning, in a cave with a hygrometer that disagrees with the one next to it, and behind a counter
where somebody has to explain for the ninth time that yes, the rind is meant to look like that. Our
six committees met twenty-four times last year to argue about standards, safety, education, awards,
events and outreach on your behalf. We are grateful to every volunteer who sat through those
meetings, and to the members who wrote to us when we got something wrong.

*Office of the Chief Executive*

## Membership

At 31 December 2023, 924 members held an active membership period with the federation, compared
with 661 at the same point in 2022 — an increase of 263 members, or roughly 40 per cent. It is the
largest single-year gain in the period this report series covers, and it follows a 2022 that was
itself up sharply on 2021.

Two hundred and seventy-one of those members were joining the federation for the first time. The
remainder are renewals, and the renewal picture is the number we watch hardest. Of the 924
membership periods that began during 2023, 814 were renewed, 84 lapsed, and 26 were cancelled
outright — a renewal rate of 88.1 per cent. That is a healthy figure for an association of this
size and mix, and it is worth saying plainly that it was not obviously going to be. Members who
joined during the virtual years of 2020 and 2021 had, in many cases, still never met us in a room.

The tier mix at year end was 655 Individual members, 148 Small Business members, 80 Enthusiast
members and 41 Corporate members. Individual membership continues to be the spine of the
federation, and the Small Business tier — creameries, shops and small distributors — grew in step
with it. The Enthusiast tier, which we introduced for home cheesemakers and serious amateurs,
remains small in revenue terms and disproportionately loud in engagement terms, which is exactly
what we hoped for when we created it.

The 84 lapses deserve a sentence rather than a footnote. Lapses cluster, and they cluster in
predictable places: members whose employer changed, members whose only contact with us was a single
webinar two years ago, and members whose renewal notice went to an address that stopped working. We
have begun treating a lapse as a question about our record-keeping before we treat it as a question
about the member's interest, and we expect to say more about that in next year's report.

## Programs and education

The federation held 13 events in 2023: one Annual Conference, five in-person workshops and seven
webinars. Across all of them we took 2,244 registrations and recorded 1,535 attendances, an
attendance rate of about 68 per cent. Webinar no-shows account for most of the gap, which is
normal and which we would rather have than a smaller registration number.

The ICF Annual Conference 2023 ran on 25 July in Louisville, Kentucky. Two hundred and ninety-eight
members registered and 276 attended. The workshop programme ranged more widely than usual —
Alpine styles in Greensboro in February, competition preparation in Guelph in June, sensory work in
Chicago in October, bloomy rinds in Burlington in November, and a second competition-preparation
session in Green Bay in December. Running workshops in five locations across two countries is more
than we have attempted before. The two competition-preparation sessions, in June and December, took
160 and 148 registrations respectively.

The webinar track carried the topics that members ask about between events: allergen labelling,
export paperwork, small-room affinage, rennet alternatives, packaging sustainability, and Listeria
control. Listeria control drew the largest webinar registration of the year at 191, with packaging
sustainability just behind at 188 — against a webinar average of 169.

Eight course instances began during 2023: Listeria Control in Small Creameries, Cut & Wrap
Fundamentals, Advanced Sensory Evaluation, Blue Cheese Production, Cheese Chemistry, Milk Quality &
Herd Health, Rind Development & Washing, and Cave Management. Those courses took 506 enrolments
during the year, of which 360 have since been completed; 257 course completions were recorded
during 2023 itself, some of them by members who had enrolled the previous year.

Credentialing moved more slowly, and we are not going to dress that up. Twelve members enrolled in
a certification pathway during 2023 and two credentials were awarded, both Sensory Evaluation
Certificates. Thirteen members held an unexpired credential at year end across the federation's
seven certifications. Our credential ladder is the part of the programme that has not yet caught up
with the growth in membership, and the Education Committee has it as a standing item.

## Competitions

The 2023 competition drew 47 entries from 31 member organisations across all six categories: Blue
Veined (12 entries), Alpine Styles (10), Soft-Ripened (8), Fresh & Pasta Filata (7), Washed Rind
(5), and Aged Cheddar & Territorials (5). Fourteen medals were awarded.

Three cheeses took Gold. Rowanbrook Tomme, a blue from Fromagerie Saint-Rémille, won Blue Veined.
Dapplewick Blue from Mossgate Farm took Fresh & Pasta Filata — a result that caused some cheerful
confusion at the announcement, since the cheese is named for a farm and not for its style. Maplefen
Tomme from Winterfen Fine Cheese won Alpine Styles.

Fromagerie Saint-Rémille had a notably good year, taking three of the fourteen medals: the Blue
Veined Gold, a Silver in Aged Cheddar & Territorials for Foxcombe Blue, and a Bronze in Blue Veined
for Quernstone Tomme. Henri Dubois, who runs the maturation room there and joined the federation in
January 2019, entered eight cheeses across five categories — the largest single-maker entry of the
year, and a useful reminder that the competition rewards people who are willing to be judged more
than once.

Blue Veined was the most contested category at 12 entries and also the most decorated, taking five
of the fourteen medals. Washed Rind and Aged Cheddar & Territorials were the thinnest fields at
five entries each. The Awards & Competition Committee has asked us to say, on its behalf, that a
five-entry category is not a category anyone should feel intimidated by.

## Advocacy and standards

Members took 88 recorded advocacy actions through the federation in 2023: 49 letter campaigns, 20
petition signatures, 17 coalition meetings and two formal testimonies.

Five topics accounted for all of it. Interstate cheese shipment drew the most activity at 22
actions, followed by dairy pricing reform (20), raw-milk aging rules (19), small-producer
exemptions under federal food-safety rules (16), and labeling standards (11). The shape of that
list has been stable for three years, which tells us the underlying questions have not been
settled rather than that members have lost interest.

Two testimonies is a small number and a significant one. Preparing a member to testify takes weeks
of work from the Advocacy & Standards function and from the member's own business, and the members
who do it are rarely the ones with time to spare. The federation's position on raw-milk aging rules
continues to be that any revised rule should scale its requirements to the size and risk profile of
the operation, and that small farmstead producers should not be asked to fund compliance
infrastructure designed for plants a hundred times their size.

On standards, the Standards Committee met four times and continued its work on aging, labeling and
raw-milk production requirements for member producers. The Food Safety Committee met four times.
Each of the federation's six committees met four times in 2023, for 24 meetings in total. Members
who want to know what was discussed can read the published minutes for any of the six.

## Financial summary

These figures are unaudited operating revenue and reflect orders and payments recorded in the
federation's membership system; they are not a complete financial statement. The federation does
not publish an expense statement or a balance sheet in this report.

Gross orders booked in 2023 came to $490,358, against which $479,915 was applied, leaving $10,443
outstanding at year end. Cash captured during the calendar year was $462,776; the difference
between cash captured and revenue applied is timing, principally December registrations that paid
in January.

Membership across the four tiers contributed $236,698, or 48 per cent of the total. Events —
conference and workshop registration together — contributed $228,412, or 47 per cent. That is a
materially more event-dependent revenue mix than the federation carried before 2020, and it is the
single most important thing on this page. An association whose events revenue is nearly the size of
its dues revenue is an association with a concentrated risk, as we learned expensively in 2020.

The remaining five per cent comes from publications, credentials, competition entries, merchandise
and donations. Certification exam fees at $4,704 reflect the credential volume described above:
real, but not yet a programme of scale.

Prices were unchanged in 2023. Membership runs from $150 for the Enthusiast tier to $1,000 for
Corporate; conference registration is $450, workshop registration $150, a certification exam $425,
and a competition entry $65 per cheese.

## Looking ahead

The ICF Annual Conference 2024 is scheduled for 16 July in Des Moines, Iowa, and on current
registration behaviour we expect it to be larger than Louisville. We would rather book a room that
is slightly too big than repeat 2023's cheerful scramble.

Three priorities carry into 2024. The first is credentialing: the gap between 924 members and 13
current credential holders is the clearest programme gap in this report, and closing it means
making the pathway easier to start rather than making the credential easier to earn. The second is
the event revenue concentration described in the financial summary — not by holding fewer events,
but by growing the parts of the programme that do not depend on a room. The third is the
record-keeping question behind the 84 lapses, which is a data problem before it is a retention
problem.

We are also going to keep saying the unglamorous thing about competitions: enter. Five-entry
categories exist because good cheesemakers assume the field is stronger than it is. It usually
isn't.

Thank you for a genuinely good year.

## About the federation

The International Cheese Federation is a membership association for cheesemakers, affineurs,
cheesemongers, distributors, educators and enthusiasts. It runs an annual conference, a workshop
and webinar programme, a course and credential ladder, an annual competition across six categories,
and an advocacy programme directed by its members through six standing committees.
