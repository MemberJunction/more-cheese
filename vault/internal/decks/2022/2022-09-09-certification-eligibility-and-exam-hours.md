## Why the hours gate exists

- CCP tests broad working knowledge across production, sensory and sales
- Written tests alone cannot replace practical experience
- Regulatory nuances like identity standards require on-the-job exposure
- Hands-on time ensures candidates recognize faults by smell under pressure

> notes: Candidates often ask why they cannot sit the test immediately after studying. The exam assumes real cave and counter familiarity.

---

## Where the hours come from

- Retail counter work builds customer-facing and service knowledge
- Cave and production floors build technical and affineur expertise
- Formal coursework covers part of the requirement but not all
- Sensory Foundations and Cheese Foundations bridge the educational gap

> notes: Production-side candidates need counter exposure; retail candidates need production context. Neither path bypasses the total hours threshold.

---

## Common applicant mistakes

- Trying to sit the exam on coursework alone with zero hours
- Failing to document shift logs or supervisor sign-offs in advance
- Scrambling for verification records the week before application deadlines
- Assuming informal training counts without employer validation

> notes: We need to push members to assemble documentation two years out rather than two weeks before.
