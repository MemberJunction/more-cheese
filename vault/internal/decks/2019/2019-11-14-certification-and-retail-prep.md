## What the exam actually tests

- Tasting and style identification component is substantial
- Retail operations make up roughly half the exam
- Cutting, wrapping, and waste reduction techniques
- Case rotation, temperature management, and allergen safety

> notes: Staff often assume it is pure tasting. We need to make sure study guides emphasize the operational half.

---

## Who takes it and why

- Most candidates have one to two years of counter experience
- Used by shop owners as a structured onboarding tool
- Stepping stone for staff eyeing buying or management roles
- Formalizes daily knowledge and fills operational gaps

> notes: The credential turns daily counter work into a recognized professional resume credential.

---

## Preparing without overdoing it

- Sensory Foundations coursework helps with identification and pairing
- Retail operations require deliberate self-quizzing on safety protocols
- Avoid neglecting less glamorous hygiene and wrapping standards
- Education Committee available for ten-minute guidance calls

> notes: Candidates happily taste tomme wheels but skip studying cross-contact procedures. We must correct that balance.
