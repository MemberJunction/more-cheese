**Subject:** Wholesale contract volatility and the case for monthly pricing reviews

Wholesale milk pricing has climbed sharply into October following a volatile summer, generating anxious inquiries from members locking in fall and early-winter contracts. The public blog post published yesterday addresses the shift in cheese demand and retail pull-through, but internally we need to examine how members are restructuring their pricing calendars. The swing from July highs to September lows and the subsequent October recovery has broken normal seasonal rhythms. Members who continue to use annual or seasonal pricing cadences are finding themselves exposed to margin compression.

## What the current data shows on membership and orders

Our weekly snapshot for the week ending 18 October 2020 records 5 membership purchases grossing $750, bringing our current member count at week-end to 424. Confirmed orders for the week stand at 6, generating $1,163 in gross orders with zero cancellations. Total cash captured for the week is $1,929. While these figures reflect steady operational baseline movement, they mask the severe pricing pressure our wholesale and farmstead members are communicating to us when they call for guidance.

The recovery in block and barrel prices, coupled with firm export demand for dry whey, means suppliers who locked contracts during August lows are currently delivering under unfavorable terms. Conversely, buyers who committed earlier are facing pushback from producers whose production costs no longer align with stale price sheets.

## The shift to monthly review cadences

In our discussions with members this week, those operating on a monthly pricing-review cadence have weathered the volatility significantly better than those tied to traditional quarterly or seasonal agreements. A monthly review allows small producers and cheesemakers to adjust quotes as whey, block, and barrel prices move, preventing them from extending credit or locking inventory under terms that threaten their solvency.

We must ensure our advisory messaging remains practical: reminding producers to calculate exact make costs per wheel and maintain strict discipline on credit terms. Extending credit on hope during a volatile market is the single greatest risk reported by farmstead operations this season.

## What we need

- Communications: reinforce the monthly review recommendation in upcoming member touchpoints without crossing into prescriptive pricing advice.
- Advocacy & Standards: monitor federal dairy market reports for signs of further pricing instability that could impact small-producer margins heading into November.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that membership operations and outreach will continue fielding contract-comparison queries through the end of the month.
