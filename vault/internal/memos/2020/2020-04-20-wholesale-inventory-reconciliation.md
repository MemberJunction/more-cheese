**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Membership Operations; Communications
**From:** Advocacy & Standards
**Date:** 20 April 2020
**Subject:** Wholesale inventory accumulation and cooler capacity

Farm milk prices have fallen to levels most members have never priced against, with some producers reporting figures below fourteen dollars per hundredweight. At the same time, cheese and butter are piling up in coolers with nowhere to go because accounts that used to move inventory — restaurants, school cafeterias, and hotel breakfast lines — closed at once. This memo outlines what our members are experiencing and how we should frame federal relief messaging without making promises the department cannot fulfill.

## The supply chain split

Retail cheese counters are busier than they have been in years, while the wholesale and food-service side of the same supply chain has fallen off a cliff. A creamery selling into both channels can watch one line of its business boom and the other collapse in the same quarter, and neither number tells the whole story on its own. Cheese does not spoil the way fresh milk does, so when demand disappears faster than a plant can shift its output, milk goes into cheese and cheese goes into cold storage rather than getting dumped. That is why stocks are elevated even as farm prices are historically weak.

Some processors have asked farms to pull back volume by ten percent or more. For small creameries selling wheels directly off the farm, the picture is different: tasting rooms or farmers market crowds may have increased, but wholesale accounts have gone quiet, and distributors report overstocking in categories our members do not even produce. Both halves belong to the same national picture.

## What relief funding actually covers

Federal disaster relief passed at the end of March includes a dedicated pool for producers hurt by the pandemic, and dairy is explicitly named in it. Mechanics are still being worked out at the department level as of this week, and we do not have confirmed payment formulas to share yet. When confirmed payment formulas arrive, they will come through the Advocacy Committee channel, not as a guess from us. The relief is aimed at farm-level and producer-level losses tied to the collapse in food-service demand, not retail-side businesses currently experiencing increased volume.

## Capacity and data integrity

Membership Operations reports current membership standing at 394 members at the end of the week, with seven membership purchases booked for a gross of 1432 dollars, alongside nine confirmed orders grossing 1699 dollars and cash captured at 1983 dollars. We must ensure our advocacy comments rely strictly on verifiable member submissions rather than anecdotal panic. The picture we build for federal comments is only as good as what members actually report.

## What we need

- Communications: align blog messaging with the principle that relief targets wholesale collapse rather than retail gains.
- Membership Operations: flag any member reporting anomalous pricing or inventory issues so Advocacy can review them for the federal comments.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the distribution of our updated talking points to committee chairs by Wednesday.
