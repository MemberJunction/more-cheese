**To:** Communications; Events Team; Education & Credentialing
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 20 November 2020
**Subject:** Production shifts and cave capacity during the November lockdown wave

The commodity cheese price held its recent gains through this week, extending a run that began in October. At the same time, renewed indoor dining restrictions across multiple states have created a sharp split in our membership's order books. Members relying on restaurant and foodservice accounts are seeing orders drop or go quiet, while those selling through direct-to-consumer routes and grocery channels are busier than they have been all fall. This memo reviews how members are managing that shift, what our course data tells us about where they are looking for help, and how our communications should reflect these pressures without overstating them.

## The retail and direct-to-consumer pivot

Our order data for the week ending 22 November shows 5 confirmed orders grossing 681 USD, with 3 membership purchases totaling 426 USD (2 Individual tier and 1 Enthusiast tier) bringing total current membership to 425 at week end. Cash captured for the week stands at 847 USD. While gross orders are modest, members in the field report that their actual volume is shifting rapidly from wholesale accounts to direct pickup routes and retail-ready formats. A farmstead member in the upper Midwest told us this week that her roadside stand has turned into a regular text-order list with sixty households and a second chest freezer. Members making this change are cutting wheels into smaller household-sized formats rather than holding large wholesale blocks. This requires more labor at the cutting table, but it provides predictable demand that does not vanish when a restaurant closes overnight.

## Cave capacity and humidity drift

Pushing more wheels into maturation to meet retail and holiday demand is creating a secondary operational challenge in association caves. When a maturation room is packed tighter than usual, humidity creeps up and airflow stops reaching the back shelves. Members working with bloomy and washed-rind styles are reporting slow rinds and uneven moisture drift. The Cave Management course and our recent advisory material emphasize that a room holding seventy wheels behaves differently than one holding forty. Members are adding small circulating fans and walking their rooms with handheld probes rather than trusting wall gauges near the door. Education & Credentialing reports strong interest here, which matches our course enrollment numbers.

## Course enrollments and upcoming schedule

Education enrollment remains robust despite the broader economic uncertainty. We took 26 course enrollments this week, and the Listeria Control in Small Creameries course (Willow cohort) starts today, 20 November. Looking further ahead, the Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course starts on 8 December, and Sensory Foundations (Alpine cohort) opens on 25 December. Two webinars are scheduled before year-end: Digital Storefronts 2020 on 3 December and Costing a Make 2020 on 31 December. None of these free webinars carry paid registrations in our data, so we must not quote attendance figures for them in public or internal summaries.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure blog posts and member updates acknowledge the retail and direct-sales pivot without pretending foodservice accounts have disappeared permanently.
- Education & Credentialing: keep course materials focused on practical troubleshooting for packed caves and smaller-format cutting.
- Events Team: verify that upcoming December webinars are promoted to members who are actively restructuring their order books around direct sales.
