**To:** Communications; Events Team; Education & Credentialing

**Date:** 3 December 2020
**Subject:** Storefront cleanup, holiday cutoffs, and the December support queue

The digital storefront webinar is running live today, and our support queue has spent the last four days handling the exact questions the session is built to address. Members who threw together online order forms back in March are reaching out now because volume is picking up just as carrier delays and case spikes make order-cutoff messaging critical. This memo reviews what the weekly figures show so far, where the support inquiries are clustering, and how we handle the rest of December without burning out the team.

## What the numbers show so far

For the week ending 6 December 2020, membership purchases total 233, generating 56,674 USD in gross sales. Of those purchases, 154 are Individual, 53 are SmallBusiness, 15 are Corporate, and 11 are Enthusiast, with 6 first-time members included in the total. Confirmed orders stand at 278 representing 53,111 USD in gross value, alongside 38 cancellations. Cash captured for the week is 6,677 USD. Course enrollments taken this week are 16, with 0 completions recorded so far. On the advocacy side, 1 coalition meeting on labeling standards was logged. These figures are preliminary as of today and will close on Sunday.

The order volume confirms that retail and direct channels are carrying the weight while foodservice accounts remain thin. Members are running longer shifts with fewer people, which explains why training inquiries are arriving alongside technical storefront questions.

## Where the friction sits

The support emails coming into Membership Operations and Communications are heavily concentrated around three operational bottlenecks. First, members are struggling with inventory flags: sold-through wheels are remaining active on patched-together order forms, resulting in oversold stock that creameries cannot fulfill. Second, order-cutoff dates are being communicated too late or not at all, leaving customers uncertain whether gifts will arrive before the holidays. Third, seasonal hires brought on to cut wrapping and packing hours are struggling with basic handling and storage questions because training was skipped in the rush.

The Cheese Foundations Certificate, which covers safe handling, storage, and label reading, is the association's standard answer for these seasonal hires. Yet members keep asking whether they can put temporary holiday help through a six-to-eight-hour self-paced course while running full shifts. We need to be clear in our replies that while December is a tight window, starting the credential now helps staff avoid costly labeling and storage errors.

## Capacity and coordination

The free webinar on digital storefronts today has 0 paid registrations, which is expected since free webinars carry none in our data, but attendance based on member inquiries is running high. We must ensure that the recording is posted within a few days as promised, because members working through evening packing shifts cannot attend live. Communications should coordinate with Education & Credentialing to make sure the storefront webinar pointers link directly to the Cheese Foundations enrollment page for members who realize their new hires need structured training.

## What we need

- Communications: publish the webinar recording by Monday and verify that all links point to active course and certification portals.
- Education & Credentialing: provide a short guidance note for members asking how to split the Cheese Foundations Certificate across two weeks for temporary holiday staff.
- Events Team: flag any early schedule conflicts for the upcoming webinars, particularly the cost-making session scheduled for later in the month.
- Everyone: treat all weekly figures cited here as preliminary until Sunday's close.
