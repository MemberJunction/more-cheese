**Subject:** Food box advocacy and the small-producer on-ramp

The federal food-box purchasing program moved into its second round of contracts this month, widening the eligible list beyond the standard cheddar cuts that dominated the first pass to include Gouda, Gruyère, Provolone, Romano, and other hard, semi-firm, and semi-soft styles. While the expanded product list is a meaningful improvement for specialty producers, our advocacy work must now focus on the administrative on-ramp. A two-person farmstead creamery cannot absorb the compliance paperwork required to get listed as an eligible supplier without dedicated administrative support.

## What members are telling us

Our position, which we have put in writing to the program's contracting office, centers on two straightforward asks: keep the eligible-cheese list broad in any future rounds, and simplify the documentation a smaller producer needs to submit. A large distributor can manage the compliance burden of getting a product added to an approved list; a small creamery cannot. Members who have tried to apply report that the greatest barrier was not the cheesemaking standards but figuring out which regulatory forms applied to their scale of operation in the first place.

In practice, a farmstead operation's path into the program runs through existing distributor relationships rather than direct federal agreements. We are advising members to contact distributors already holding a contract rather than attempting to apply as individual producers, which the program structure does not accommodate at that scale. Specific examples carry more weight with contracting officers than general frustration, and we are collecting concrete stories from members who hit administrative walls to fold into our next round of comments.

## Committee activity and broader priorities

As recorded in our Q3 meetings, our advocacy and standards work spans several parallel fronts. The Standards Committee completed its Q3 meeting on July 6, reviewing compliance and labeling questions, and the Education Committee met on July 9. Across our overall advocacy record, we track 110 total actions, led by 28 actions on interstate cheese shipment, 27 on labeling standards, 22 on dairy pricing reform, 19 on raw-milk aging rules, and 14 on FSMA small-producer exemptions. The food-box purchasing feedback aligns directly with our ongoing push for proportionate compliance requirements.

## Capacity and next steps

We must ensure our communications and membership teams direct inquiries effectively. Members should report specific documentation hurdles to the Membership & Outreach Committee so we can incorporate them into our regulatory submissions.

## What we need

- Communications: reinforce in member updates that farmstead operations should approach existing contractors rather than applying independently.
- Membership Operations: forward any member-reported compliance roadblocks directly to Advocacy & Standards.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that our next federal submission will emphasize plainer-language guidance for specialty cheese suppliers.
