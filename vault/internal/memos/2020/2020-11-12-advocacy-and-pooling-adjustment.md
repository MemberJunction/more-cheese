**Subject:** Advocacy priorities, milk-check adjustments, and small-producer pooling

Member inquiries regarding negative adjustments on milk checks have increased notably over the past two weeks. The pricing gap between cheese-milk and fluid classes is producing two-to-three-dollar negative adjustments per hundredweight across multiple regions, creating significant confusion among producers who observe a strong retail cheese market simultaneously with degraded milk returns. This memo outlines our current advocacy posture, reviews the recent coalition meeting on labeling standards, and establishes how Membership Operations should coordinate with the Food Safety Committee to assist members navigating pooling decisions.

## The pricing gap and pooling realities

The current divergence between cheese prices and pooled blended prices is not a demand failure but a formulaic artifact. When cheese-milk values run high, the pooled price drops relative to cheese alone, generating the negative adjustment. Furthermore, cooperative depooling decisions exacerbate the disparity. Producers shipping through the same regional order find divergent checks simply because their respective cooperatives made different pooling choices. We have confirmed through member contacts that those shipping to cooperatives that depool entirely face smaller deductions than those whose milk remains fully pooled.

Our current advocacy actions reflect this split. We recorded one coalition meeting on labeling standards this week, complementing our broader tracking of federal dairy pricing reform and interstate shipment rules. However, members must understand that pricing formulas are not subject to immediate regulatory correction. Our immediate value lies in helping members understand whether their cooperative pools or depools, rather than promising a regulatory fix that will not arrive this quarter.

## Interstate shipment and the sixty-day rule

Parallel to the pricing gap discussions, member confusion regarding the sixty-day rule for raw-milk cheese crossing state lines persists. Despite decades of precedent allowing raw-milk cheese aged sixty days or more to move interstate where raw milk itself cannot, members continue to turn down out-of-state wholesale inquiries out of an abundance of caution or improper compliance checks. Our current letter campaign is asking federal regulators for clearer, plain-language guidance and a straightforward verification mechanism for specific batches.

We must ensure our advocacy communications do not conflate state unpasteurized retail sales with federal interstate shipment thresholds. The Food Safety Committee is available to review individual product line aging protocols, and Membership Operations is collecting concrete accounts of wholesale orders lost due to regulatory ambiguity.

## Capacity and committee coordination

The Advocacy & Standards team is currently handling multiple parallel friction points, including dairy pricing reform, FSMA small-producer exemptions, and interstate shipments. While advocacy actions total 110 year-to-date across all topics, staff bandwidth is constrained. We must rely on committee structures to manage direct inquiries from members.

- Food Safety Committee: prioritize reviews for members needing assistance with make-date documentation for sixty-day compliance.
