## The Traceability Template and Small-Scale Realities

The Food Safety Committee has been reviewing the draft traceability rule published in late September. As noted in association commentary, the regulation assumes a level of inventory infrastructure—automated lot tracking, conveyor systems, and receiving docks—that simply does not match how a forty-wheel batch is made in a small creamery. Members are asking whether their existing paper lot logs, dated wax marks, and delivery receipts are enough. Our task this month is to finalise a three-question template that asks producers about their actual batch sizes, current lot logging methods, and the time required to stand up a compliant digital or manual trail. We need to collect these examples before the holidays consume December. The strongest comments are not general complaints about regulatory burden, but specific descriptions of existing batch records that prove compliance is already happening in a different format.

## Margin Protection Versus Commodity Index Swings

The weekly dairy market report confirmed a pullback in block and barrel cheddar prices following months of wide swings. For members pricing holiday gift boxes and wholesale renewals, chasing the spot price down would erode margins built on higher input costs. As highlighted in our member spotlights this week, counters building mail-order gift programs are successfully pricing shipping and packaging into the box rather than eating courier costs or discounting into soft margins. Our internal guidance to members negotiating with regional grocers and distributors is to bring production cost data rather than spot indices to the table. Comfortable cold storage inventories mean December will likely be quieter than the rest of 2020, but volatility remains high enough that every supply contract floating with the market needs a mid-December re-price clause.
