**Subject:** Membership lapse grace window and the second federal relief round

As the second federal relief program opened its signup window this week and our margin reports continue to show volatile gaps between feed costs and milk prices, Membership Operations is dealing with a parallel operational pressure: a cluster of small-producer members whose annual dues payments have lapsed during the ongoing market turbulence. This memo reviews our current lapse counts, proposes an extended grace window for operations caught in the pricing crunch, and coordinates how our outreach ties into the relief and advocacy messaging published this week.

## The lapse-risk pattern and current standing

The association's total membership count sits at 418 at week end, supported by three new membership purchases booked this week (two Individual and one Enthusiast, totaling $426 gross). However, the number of members reaching the end of their twelve-month rolling period without an immediate renewal has crept upward since summer. For small-scale cheese producers and farmstead operators facing the same milk price volatility detailed in this week's blog posts, paying annual dues often slips behind feed bills and cave power costs.

We have resisted cutting off lapsed members automatically because the disruption prevents them from accessing peer networks and staff guidance precisely when they need it most. Yet, holding them indefinitely without payment skews our operational data. We need a defined administrative boundary that offers relief without creating an unrecoverable accounts-receivable tail.

## Aligning membership grace with relief support

This week's public blog posts emphasized that small producers should not assume they are ineligible for the fourteen billion dollar federal relief package, and highlighted the fifteen-minute costing sheet routine recommended by the Education Committee. We propose tying our membership lapse grace policy directly to this advocacy and educational push.

Rather than issuing hard termination notices to members whose renewals are thirty to sixty days overdue, Membership Operations will reach out directly with a combined offer: assistance with navigating the federal relief paperwork through the Membership & Outreach Committee, paired with a thirty-day grace extension on their annual dues. This approach reframes the renewal conversation around practical financial survival rather than an administrative penalty.

## Capacity and communication constraints

Our team is currently handling both day-to-day membership renewals and the influx of inquiries regarding the new advocacy letter campaign on dairy pricing reform. We cannot absorb an intensive, individual phone campaign for every lapsed account without dropping our response times on general member inquiries. We must rely on automated reminder templates supplemented by targeted calls to our SmallBusiness and Corporate tiers.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: confirm whether the letter campaign templates can include a brief footnote directing members to Membership Operations if dues hardship is preventing them from participating.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the temporary thirty-day grace extension for members affected by feed-cost volatility through the end of the fourth quarter.
- Communications: ensure our upcoming weekly digests point small producers to both the federal relief signup and our membership support desk without overpromising staff availability.
