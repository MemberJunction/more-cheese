**Subject:** Export and federal purchasing coordination following the September moves

Two federal policy developments landed this month that affect our membership base in opposite directions: a tightened quota administration notice for dairy products crossing into Canada, and the opening of the third contract round for the federal food-box purchasing program. Communications covered both in yesterday's blog post, but we need an internal read on what these shifts mean for our casework and how we route member inquiries. Neither program was designed with our typical small-to-midsize producer in mind, and the friction our exporters are encountering is already generating calls.

## The export side and quota friction

The quota notice affecting cheese moving north across the border has not altered the total allowable volume, but the administrative procedures enforced by customs authorities have tightened. Exporters who previously relied on familiar paperwork are finding shipments delayed or held up for additional verification. This matches the broader advocacy picture for the year, where trade and labeling standards remain our most active files—accounting for 27 labeling actions and 28 interstate shipment actions out of our 110 total recorded advocacy engagements so far.

When members call about export delays, they are not looking for general reassurance; they need direct contacts with customs brokers and a clear understanding that last month's clearance procedures no longer apply. The Events Team's trade contacts have been collecting notes on where shipments are stalling, and we need to make sure those insights reach exporting members before their next dispatch rather than after a hold-up occurs.

## The domestic purchasing expansion

On the other side of the ledger, the third round of the federal food-box purchasing program has expanded its eligible cheese categories beyond standard cheddar to include hard, semi-firm, and semi-soft styles. For members sitting on growing inventories while wholesale and food-service demand remains unpredictable, this purchasing channel offers an outlet for volume they might otherwise struggle to move.

However, the gap between eligibility and actual participation remains wide. Small producers often lack the volume thresholds or federal contracting experience required to bid. Membership & Outreach has been compiling notes from members who navigated earlier rounds, and we should direct inquiries to those peer resources rather than assuming every member can jump into federal procurement without preparation.

## Milk price volatility as the backdrop

Neither of these programs operates in a vacuum. The monthly figure from the federal dairy margin program jumped by more than two dollars per hundredweight between June and July, introducing severe cost volatility just as producers try to plan their holiday maturation schedules. When margins swing that sharply within a single month, a producer's decision to chase an export order, bid on a government contract, or hold inventory in the cave can flip in weeks.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: route all member inquiries regarding federal purchasing contracts to the notes compiled by Membership & Outreach.
- Events Team: share the latest customs broker feedback with exporting members before their next scheduled shipments.
- Communications: ensure future updates maintain the distinction between export-side administrative friction and domestic purchasing opportunities.
