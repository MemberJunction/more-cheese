This memo provides an operational readout on membership numbers, cash capture, and the impact of federal food-storage purchasing on our wholesale and small-producer membership base. As of this week, our active membership sits at 398 members. This figure reflects qualifying annual purchases over the preceding twelve months. While we secured two new membership purchases this week—one SmallBusiness tier and one Individual tier, totaling $490 in gross sales—the broader financial picture across the association requires careful executive management as we approach the midpoint of 2020.

## Membership trends and cash capture

The association recorded 21 confirmed orders this week, generating $3,116 in gross order value against 3 cancellations. Cash captured for the same window reached $4,108. These figures reflect a modest weekly tick compared to our baseline, but they sit against a much larger, more volatile background. As our public blog post on warehouse inventories noted this week, natural cheese stocks are sitting well above seasonal norms due to the food-service demand collapse back in March. That glut is trickling down to our members, particularly those holding aged or contract-bound inventory who are facing higher storage quotes and tighter warehouse availability.

It is vital that our public-facing comms and internal advocacy stay aligned on what the federal purchasing program can and cannot do for these members. The contracted buying program is offering real volume, but its specifications target commodity block and barrel rather than specialty cheese. Members selling small-batch artisanal wheels are not seeing direct benefit at their receiving docks, even if the broader commodity market is finding some temporary relief.

## Education and workshop activity

On the education side, demand remains robust despite the broader economic friction. We logged 35 course enrollments this week, alongside 3 course completions and 1 certification enrollment. Following yesterday's completion of the Workshop: Lark Goat & Sheep Milk on 29 May in Ayrshire, where 21 registrations were taken against 3 refunds with 19 attending and 2 no-shows, our operational focus shifts to managing remote reviews. This surge mirrors the era-pandemic-shock-2020 trend where online coursework and remote credentials are absorbing the hours that members used to spend on canceled physical workshops.

## What we need

- Communications: verify that any public references to cold storage inventories or federal purchasing programs explicitly distinguish commodity block and barrel from specialty cheese.
- Advocacy & Standards: track member complaints regarding tightening warehouse contracts and storage fee hikes so we can brief the board next month.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the adjusted timeline for our Q2 membership retention review.
