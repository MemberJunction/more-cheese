**Subject:** Retail counter pressures, charcuterie consolidation, and margin reality

This memo reviews current specialty case pressures as the run-up to Valentine's Day heightens board-style and charcuterie purchases across our retail membership. Communications published a piece on the board trend this week, but behind the counter, members are navigating tighter margins on in-demand wheels and early consolidation among charcuterie suppliers. We need to understand how these counter-level realities affect our retail membership base before planning our spring advisory content.

## Counter trends and the board-style shift

Our public commentary notes that boards and charcuterie have become the fastest-growing part of the specialty case. Members running retail programs report that customers buying for holiday windows require extensive pairing guidance rather than simple wedge sales. While this increases case turnover, it also places a heavy burden on floor staff who must manage questions about cheese pairings, portion sizing, and table endurance under tight deadlines. Members who have trained their entire counter staff on basic pairings are moving significantly more product than those who rely solely on an experienced head monger.

However, volume growth does not automatically translate into healthier margins. As more retailers chase a limited selection of small-batch artisan wheels, acquisition costs have risen, and wholesale pricing on sought-after cheeses has tightened. Retailers attempting to compete purely on price find themselves squeezed between rising wholesale costs and consumer price sensitivity.

## Supply chain consolidation

Compounding the margin pressure at the counter is recent consolidation among charcuterie producers. Over the winter, several mid-sized charcuterie suppliers combined operations, reducing vendor diversity and giving larger distributors increased market strength over smaller specialty accounts. Retail members who built their programs on direct relationships with regional producers are insulated to some extent, but those relying on broadline distributors report fewer options and less favorable terms. This dynamic mirrors the broader specialty cheese market where small producers struggle for counter space against consolidated brands.

Our current financial snapshot for the week ending 16 February shows stable association operations with 3 membership purchases (2 Individual, 1 Corporate) totaling 1176 gross, 4 confirmed orders totaling 1538 gross, and total cash captured of 10116. While association cash flow remains healthy, member businesses are clearly absorbing margin compression that our dues structures do not directly measure.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure future retail features continue to emphasize staff training and direct producer relationships rather than volume alone.
- Advocacy & Standards: monitor distributor consolidation impacts on small-batch artisan distribution channels.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that retail member margins will likely be a central topic during spring committee calls.
