**Subject:** Advocacy response strategy for the raw-milk aging threshold docket

The federal comment period on raw-milk aging requirements has drawn more incoming mail to the Food Safety Committee than any other docket item this month. The regulatory proposal aims to tighten temperature and time documentation standards for aging clocks. While large operations absorb these administrative shifts via existing quality assurance teams, two- and three-person farmstead members face rebuilding their recordkeeping systems entirely from scratch during an active production season. With a compressed six-week window closing much faster than the usual twelve, our members need immediate guidance rather than general reassurance about regulatory intent.

## Docket dynamics and member feedback

Early submissions to the docket focus heavily on two major structural ambiguities: the specific herd-size threshold below which the new logging requirement would not apply, and whether traditional paper-based record systems will satisfy verification standards. Neither question has a clear answer in the current text published by regulators. Furthermore, dairy pricing has opened the year on a soft note, with Class III futures dropping due to slower wholesale cheese prices. This economic squeeze means compliance costs hit small farmstead producers when cash flow and operating margins are already paper-thin.

We know from past rulemaking cycles that form letters carry very little weight with regulators, who instead evaluate submission volume alongside operational specificity. A committee letter helps summarize broad concerns, but individual filings describing actual make schedules and aging rooms remain essential. The advocacy page on the member portal provides a basic guide, but committee leadership must actively help members draft precise comments before the window shuts permanently.

## Capacity and engagement

The advocacy team is currently managing 110 total tracked advocacy actions for the year across our core legislative and regulatory topics, including 28 on interstate cheese shipment and 27 on labeling standards. Adding a heavy push on raw-milk aging rules strains our current bandwidth. Communications has already published a helpful overview on the blog, but we need to ensure our members move rapidly from reading about the rule to filing substantive comments in the official docket.

## What we need

- Food Safety Committee: finalize the template guidance for farmstead operators by Wednesday.
- Communications: highlight the compressed six-week deadline and paper-system ambiguities in the upcoming member briefing.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a dedicated webinar slot next month to walk small producers through comment submission.
