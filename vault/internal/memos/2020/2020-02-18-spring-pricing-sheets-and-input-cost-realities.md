**Subject:** Spring pricing sheets and input cost realities — balancing softer milk forecasts against farm margins

Recent federal projections have trimmed the benchmark milk price forecast for hard and aged cheese down to just under seventeen dollars per hundredweight, marking the second consecutive monthly reduction. The blended all-milk price has dropped by a similar forty cents. While neither drop is dramatic on its own, their persistence across two consecutive releases is prompting members with spring price sheets on the calendar to ask how fuller supply and larger dairy herds should factor into their wholesale and retail numbers. This memo outlines what our current membership data and member inquiries suggest we should communicate as part of our ongoing guidance.

## What the market data shows

Production estimates are running close to two percent ahead of last year's total, creating a supply picture that outpaces current demand. Cold storage figures confirm this trend: natural cheese held in refrigerated warehouses has increased modestly from the prior month, and butter stocks have climbed considerably more. These increases are typical for winter months when production outpaces retail season, but the larger build reinforces the softer price outlook.

Despite the softer benchmark, regional dairy economists report that feed costs have ticked up slightly. This creates a margin squeeze for the small-scale farms supplying our members' milk. Members who buy directly from local producers are already reporting that input savings on fluid milk are partially offset by rising feed expenses on the farm side of the ledger. We should be careful not to present the price drop as a clean windfall for every cheese producer.

## Member inquiries and pricing plans

Membership inquiries this week center on how to handle spring contracts. Producers selling on annual or seasonal terms view the federal outlook as background context rather than an immediate trigger for contract changes. However, makers buying fluid milk on shorter cycles or issuing spring wholesale price sheets are evaluating whether to hold retail prices steady while improving margins or invest in packaging, new SKUs, and turning labor.

Our current week-ending numbers show steady engagement, with confirmed orders totaling seven against gross revenue of one thousand one hundred fifty-seven dollars, and cash captured reaching five thousand seven hundred forty-two dollars. Membership purchases for the week stand at three, comprising two individual tiers and one small business tier, generating six hundred ninety-eight dollars in gross revenue. Total current members at week end rest at three hundred seventy-five. These figures indicate stable baseline activity, but they also show that members are operating in a tight margin environment where pricing decisions require precise internal accounting.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure any public-facing summaries of milk price trends explicitly acknowledge the farm-side margin pressures flagged by regional economists, avoiding any oversimplification of input cost savings.
- Advocacy & Standards: maintain coordination with the Membership & Outreach Committee's running list of members willing to compare informal notes on pricing strategies.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the distribution of a brief operational note to regional committees regarding spring contract timing and input cost tracking.
