> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT** — International Cheese Federation (ICF), a fictitious trade association created for the MemberJunction More Cheese demonstration environment. All names, figures, events, and decisions are invented.

**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 3 June 2020
**Subject:** Managing reopened order volume and small-creamery cash flow

As states loosen dining-room restrictions and wholesale cheddar climbs off its April floor, our distributor and creamery members are facing a different kind of whiplash. The operational crisis six weeks ago was what to do with cheese nobody was buying; this week it is how to cover orders they did not expect to fill until autumn. This memo outlines what our preliminary numbers show for the week ending 7 June 2020, how wholesale restocks are colliding with farmstead cash flows, and what we need from our public and advocacy teams as members recalibrate their summer production.

## What the preliminary numbers show

With data running through 7 June 2020, we recorded 26 confirmed orders totaling $6,173 in gross sales, alongside 4 cancellations. Cash captured for the week stands at $7,605. On the membership front, we logged 6 total purchases generating $1,257 in gross revenue, bringing our current member count at week's end to 401. Of those 6 purchases, 3 were first-time members, distributed across Enthusiast (1), SmallBusiness (2), and Individual (3) tiers. Course enrollments also saw a strong week with 51 new enrollments and 9 completions, while advocacy actions focused entirely on two coalition meetings concerning labeling standards.

While these order figures are encouraging compared to the lows of April, we must be careful how we interpret them. Several distributor members report that restaurant reorders look like a frantic restock of empty walk-in coolers rather than a return to pre-pandemic cover volume. A buyer calling for a full pallet this week is refilling a gap left since March, not signalling guaranteed baseline demand. Recommending an immediate ramp-up of make schedules based on a single good order risks a whipsaw if food-service demand corrects again.

## Retail stability versus wholesale temptation

Many of our smaller producer members shifted successfully to direct-to-consumer and retail channels when food service collapsed. Those retail relationships continue to outperform pre-pandemic wholesale accounts in many reporting regions. Members are asking whether they should abandon direct retail channels the moment a traditional food-service buyer calls. Our operational guidance is clear: do not choose prematurely. You can maintain the retail channels built under pressure while servicing returning wholesale accounts, provided your make schedule matches what you can reliably sell over the next several weeks rather than the size of one isolated restock.

Furthermore, the federal relief loan rules updated this week provide vital breathing room for creameries struggling with liquidity during this transition. With the spending window extended to twenty-four weeks and the payroll threshold dropping to sixty percent, members have far more flexibility to cover non-payroll overhead such as cold storage and packaging without jeopardizing loan forgiveness.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure upcoming blog posts and newsletters remind members to treat food-service restocks cautiously and protect their retail channels.
- Advocacy & Standards: continue tracking feed-through from the federal loan rule changes and update our guidance on non-payroll cost allocations.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that membership operations and education teams are operating at peak capacity managing the 51 course enrollments alongside membership processing.
