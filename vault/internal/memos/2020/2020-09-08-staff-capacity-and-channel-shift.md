**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Events Team; Communications
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 8 September 2020
**Subject:** Staff capacity constraints and the grocery channel shift

Retail natural cheese sales for the 52 weeks ending early September show dollar sales up close to 16 percent and unit sales up nearly 9 percent, with processed cheese showing double-digit growth as well. While these numbers reflect a busy summer for members selling into grocery channels, our own operational capacity is currently stretched thin. Members whose businesses relied on tasting rooms, restaurant accounts, or farmers-market sampling are not seeing this lift, and several have approached us for help repackaging restaurant-format wheels into smaller retail cuts and renegotiating terms with regional distributors from scratch. Supporting these complex transitions requires intensive one-on-one advising, which is directly competing with our routine event planning and course administration. This memo addresses how we manage staff capacity during this busy stretch without dropping member support.

## The reality of the channel gap

The gap between retail growth and depressed foodservice volume is driving an unprecedented volume of inbound inquiries. Members are struggling with nutrition-panel work, re-labeling requirements, and finding new distributor relationships in a difficult economic environment. Our Membership & Outreach Committee has been pairing members who successfully made the shift with those still struggling, but coordinating these introductions takes staff time that we simply do not have allocated. Furthermore, upcoming educational offerings such as the Prairie cohort for Blue Cheese Production starting on 8 November require administrative bandwidth that is currently being consumed by fire-season member inquiries and emergency documentation requests.

## Rescheduling the webinar and shifting priorities

Tuesday's free webinar on costing a make drew three times the expected sign-ups, reflecting intense anxiety over feed costs, soft block markets, and margin coverage. The questions following that session demonstrated that members are wrestling with difficult pricing decisions and wholesale negotiations that cannot be resolved with generic advice. Because our team is split between supporting these pricing inquiries, handling wildfire smoke documentation for West Coast farmstead creameries, and preparing for the upcoming Cave Cheesemaking workshop in Bra on 22 September, we must formally move the non-essential publication deadlines back by one week. Attempting to maintain our normal publishing cadence while handling emergency member inquiries is resulting in rushed work and missed follow-ups.

## Capacity allocation and dropped work

We cannot maintain full service across advocacy, events, education, and retail transition advising simultaneously with our current staffing levels. Routine committee support and optional newsletters must take a lower priority while we triage member inquiries regarding pricing worksheets and emergency cave management. The Events Team and Membership Operations must agree on a unified intake queue so that members asking about grocery repackaging and those dealing with smoke-tainted or high-humidity caves receive prompt attention. We also need to decide whether to pause incoming non-critical inquiries regarding wholesale restructuring until the end of the month.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a temporary deprioritization of non-essential committee reports for the remainder of September to free up administrative hours.
- Events Team: confirm that the timeline for workshop logistics can absorb a three-day review delay without impacting participant communications.
- Communications: adjust the publishing schedule for upcoming member spotlight features to account for reduced editorial availability.
- Membership Operations: establish a triaging protocol for all incoming retail transition and pricing inquiries by Friday.
