> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT** — International Cheese Federation (ICF), a fictitious trade association created for the MemberJunction More Cheese demonstration environment. All names, figures, events, and decisions are invented.

**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Communications; Advocacy & Standards
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 30 December 2020
**Subject:** Member lapse and grace-window exposure readout

As we close out 2020, our current membership count stands at 430 at week end. Behind that number sits a collection of failed payments, expiring credit cards, and requests for grace windows that we cannot resolve under our standard operating rules without intervention. This memo sets out the extent of the lapse exposure we are carrying into January, why payment failures are clustering in the SmallBusiness tier, and what we need from leadership before the first billing cycle of the new year runs.

## The current membership base and payment friction

The association finished the year with 390 members on December 31, and our weekly snapshot for the week of December 28 shows 43 membership purchases, bringing gross membership sales for the week to $11,043 across 43 confirmed orders with zero cancellations. Of those purchases, 9 are first-time members, while the tier breakdown shows 29 Individual, 9 SmallBusiness, 2 Corporate, and 3 Enthusiast purchases. Cash captured for the week reached $20,944.

However, those gross figures mask a growing friction on renewals. We have tracked a cluster of failed automatic credit card charges among SmallBusiness members whose annual renewals fell due during the December holiday slowdown. Because many farmstead creameries and small cheese shops are managing cash flow tightly right now, expired cards and declined transactions are happening at twice the rate we saw a year ago. When an automatic renewal fails, our current system moves the account into an immediate 30-day grace window. Without proactive outreach, these accounts lapse entirely after thirty days, which threatens our baseline retention numbers heading into 2021.

## Why the grace window needs an operational exception

The standard grace window assumes a normal operating environment where a phone call or a polite automated email resolves a billing hiccup. Right now, our members are dealing with winter shutdown work, cave maintenance, and navigating the new federal relief package discussed in this week's industry blog posts. A rigid thirty-day cutoff risks dropping accounts that want to renew but simply missed a bank notification while tending vats.

We are currently handling these cases individually through the membership portal contact form, but that manual routing is consuming hours that should be spent on onboarding new first-time members. We need a clear policy decision on whether to extend a temporary 60-day grace window for accounts affected by year-end cash flow strains, or whether to hold the line at thirty days and risk losing small producers who would otherwise stay.

## Capacity and next steps

The Communications team has been fielding inquiries about relief fund interactions all week, and several members with billing questions are being routed to us via the portal. We cannot absorb both the relief-related inquiries and a manual grace-window review process without dropping our response times. We need Advocacy & Standards to confirm whether any federal assistance rules touch association dues deductibility, and we need the Office of the Chief Executive to approve the temporary grace-window extension so we can communicate it clearly.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve or reject the temporary 60-day grace-window extension for SmallBusiness and Corporate accounts by Friday.
- Advocacy & Standards: verify whether any year-end tax or relief guidance impacts how we structure delayed payment arrangements for commercial members.
- Communications: hold off on publishing any broad statements about membership renewal flexibilities until we confirm the grace-window rule.

*Membership Operations*
