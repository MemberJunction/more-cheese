**Subject:** January margin and trade readout — pricing pressures and the export horizon

January is closing with a sharper price drop in the cheese case than most member creameries anticipated coming out of 2019. While block and barrel prices typically soften after the holiday run, barrels have given up roughly a third of their November peak and Class III milk pricing has followed suit. This memo pulls together what our order numbers and member interactions show as the month wraps, and how the recent public communication on pricing and trade aligns with what we are hearing from members.

## What the order book shows

For the week ending 2 February 2020, membership activity remains quiet as producers adjust to lower margins. We recorded 2 membership purchases for the week, both in the Individual tier, generating 298 gross USD. Total confirmed orders for the week stand at 2, with 370 in gross order volume and zero cancellations. Total cash captured across all open accounts for the week is 11,497 USD. Total current members at week's end stands at 371. These figures reflect a standard post-holiday lull compounded by cost anxiety, not a broad association churn event.

## The margin reality

Members selling on formula pricing tied to Class III or NDPSR are absorbing the shock of January's drop directly on their make sheets. Our blog post this week correctly advised members to pull February pricing sheets now rather than waiting for invoices to land, but the internal feedback is that retail buyers are already pushing back on spring quotes. Cost inputs for feed, labor, and packaging have not dropped nearly as fast as the wholesale shelf price has. A creamery holding its price on a February quote needs to lean heavily on provenance and make-style quality, because a stale margin assumption is worse than no assumption at all.

## The trade horizon

Underneath the short-term price softness, the passage of the new North American trade agreement gives export-minded members a longer runway. As noted in our industry coverage, the agreement widens U.S. dairy access into Canada and retires the contentious pricing mechanism that undercut exports. It also introduces mechanisms to push back on geographical-indication restrictions that complicate labeling for small producers. However, staff must remind members that these provisions will take months to translate into actual shipping volume. Export documentation questions are starting to trickle into Membership & Outreach, and we should be prepared for rising interest in international shipping rules as spring approaches.

## What we need

- Communications: maintain the current guidance urging members to review their February pricing sheets and avoid locking in stale margin assumptions.
- Advocacy & Standards: compile a brief reference sheet on export documentation inquiries for members exploring first-time international shipments.
- Membership Operations: continue tracking weekly purchase patterns to identify any creameries showing prolonged grace-period distress.
- Everyone: recognize that January's spot-price dip is currently perceived as seasonal oversupply rather than a systemic structural slide, provided export demand picks up in the spring.
