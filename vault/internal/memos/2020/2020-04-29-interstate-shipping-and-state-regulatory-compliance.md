**Subject:** Interstate shipping and state regulatory compliance

As small creamery members rapidly transition toward direct-to-consumer order forms to offset the ongoing food-service contraction, our advocacy team is receiving a significant volume of inquiries concerning interstate shipping rules. The patchwork of state-level licensing requirements, registration fees, and specialized package labeling mandates is creating a major operational bottleneck for producers who previously relied entirely on wholesale distributors or local farmers markets. While our recent public communications have highlighted the retail cushion and the grassroots shift toward home delivery, members attempting to cross state lines are encountering administrative roadblocks that were never designed for low-volume direct shipment.

## The scope of the compliance hurdle

The primary friction point involves individual state departments of agriculture enforcing out-of-state dairy plant registration requirements and separate retail food license applications. Producers who launched online order forms over a single weekend in March are now discovering that fulfilling a residential order two towns over in a neighboring state can trigger multi-week approval processes and disparate labeling standards. Members with established wholesale accounts have historically been insulated from these hurdles because their logistics were handled by certified distributors. Small-scale producers operating direct-to-consumer car deliveries or local pickup circles are suddenly facing the full weight of interstate regulatory compliance without preparatory guidance.

## Federation advocacy posture and member outreach

Our advocacy team is actively compiling state-by-state requirements to identify where rules are contradictory or unreasonably burdensome for low-volume direct sellers. We are using these member reports to engage state agriculture officials directly, advocating for streamlined registration pathways and temporary enforcement discretion during the pandemic emergency. Concurrently, we must ensure that our communications to members do not provide improper legal interpretations or suggest that compliance can be bypassed. Every member inquiring about out-of-state shipping must be directed to verify requirements with the destination state department of agriculture prior to taking payment, rather than attempting to clean up a compliance violation after a chilled package has already left the creamery dock.
