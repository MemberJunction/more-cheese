**Subject:** Cold-storage inventories and federal food-box solicitations

The federation's inbox this week has been dominated by a single, repetitive inquiry from processors and co-ops: whether the new federal food-box purchase program is going to move the substantial cheese inventory currently sitting in cold storage. With dining rooms closed since March and foodservice orders collapsed, processors kept manufacturing because a shutdown line is cost-prohibitive. As a result, natural cheese inventories climbed through March, and end-of-April figures due later this month are expected to push those stocks even higher. This memo reviews what the current federal solicitations cover, who actually benefits, and where our messaging needs to remain disciplined.

## What the solicitations actually cover

The Farmers to Families Food Box Program structure is straightforward on paper, purchasing dairy, produce, and meat in bulk from distributors and routing them to food banks rather than commercial channels. Cheese is included, with active bid solicitations covering process cheese, cheddar shreds, string cheese, and sliced product. Contracts are expected to begin moving boxes out of warehouses later this month. However, members must understand that this is built around shelf-stable commodity formats. It has no obvious slot for small-batch washed rinds or raw-milk tommies. Specialty makers carrying specialty case inventory should not expect this round to move their product.

Furthermore, there is a distinct timing gap. Contracts are still being awarded, and the interval between solicitation issuance and trucks loading has run longer than announced. Cash-flow planning that assumes demand will materialize immediately in May carries significant risk, and we should advise members to build in slack rather than counting on delayed checks.

## Separating purchase relief from milk pricing

We are also seeing considerable confusion among members who conflate finished-product purchase programs with raw milk pricing relief. The food-box program moves finished cheese inventories; it does not touch a farm's milk price directly, nor does it rescue a member locked into an unfavorable processing contract paying less than February rates. Members carrying both operational pressures must understand that a single federal announcement will not resolve both problems simultaneously.

Our current week-ending figures show total confirmed orders at 14 with a gross of 1609 USD, cash captured at 3662 USD, and 2 membership purchases totaling 277 USD across Individual and Enthusiast tiers, bringing current members at week's end to 395. Course enrollments stand at 9 for the week, and event registrations include 4 for the late-May Lark Goat & Sheep Milk workshop and 3 for the annual conference.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure public messaging and blog follow-ups clearly distinguish between commodity surplus relief and specialty case realities without offering speculative timelines.
- Advocacy & Standards: maintain a plain-language summary of active commodity categories in solicitations so members avoid parsing federal contract notices directly.
- Events Team: hold steady on the late-May workshop planning as monitored in our separate logistics review.
