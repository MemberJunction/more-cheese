**Subject:** Holiday distribution triage and routing constraints

This week's public spotlight on Midwest Specialty Dairy Distribution highlights a severe driver shortage and tightening refrigerated trailer capacity across the region. With holiday ordering shifting heavily toward smaller-format cuts and varied assortments rather than single large wheels, our distribution-side members are facing a severe bottleneck that requires early triage rather than a late-December scramble. This memo reviews our current week-to-date position and outlines what we need from our committee networks to support members through the December surge.

## The logistics bottleneck and retail shift

As noted in our public communications, retail members are reporting pre-Thanksgiving and December orders skewed toward quarter wheels and wedges with higher variety per order. While this grazing-board trend is a positive revenue driver for small producers and specialty shops, it multiplies the stop count for refrigerated delivery routes. With regional distributors short on drivers and struggling with rotating quarantine schedules, delivery windows are tightening faster than in any prior holiday season.

Our current figures for the week of Monday, 23 November 2020, show total cash captured at 1176 USD, with 5 confirmed orders grossing 414 USD, 1 cancellation, and 3 membership purchases (2 Individual, 1 SmallBusiness) grossing 639 USD, bringing total current members at week end to 426. Course enrollments stand at 14, and advocacy actions reflect 1 coalition meeting on raw-milk aging rules. No events are scheduled this week, but two webinars are queued for December: Webinar: Digital Storefronts 2020 on 3 December and Webinar: Costing a Make 2020 on 31 December.

## Member impact and capacity coordination

The distribution crunch means that fixed-date gift-box programs and time-sensitive holiday case restocks are competing directly with routine wholesale deliveries. Distributors who fail to separate fixed-date shipments from flexible replenishment risk severe delivery failures by 22 December. We need to ensure the Membership & Outreach Committee's informal capacity-sharing network is active and visible to members who are currently scrambling for backup refrigerated storage or alternative carrier options.

Furthermore, food safety controls cannot slip while delivery schedules compress. With the Willow cohort of our Listeria Control in Small Creameries course underway, members are actively mapping facility moisture points and drainage high-traffic areas during peak volume. Distribution and creamery storage facilities are facing identical stress tests as double batches move through aging rooms.

## What we need

- Events Team: coordinate with the Events Committee to promote the upcoming Digital Storefronts webinar and ensure distribution logistics questions are routed effectively.
- Advocacy & Standards: maintain coalition engagement on raw-milk aging rules and small-producer compliance without adding reporting burdens during the peak shipping window.
- Membership & Outreach Committee: activate the backup refrigerated storage and distribution capacity sharing list for members before December congestion peaks.
