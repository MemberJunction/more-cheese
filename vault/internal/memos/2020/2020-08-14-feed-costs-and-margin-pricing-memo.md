While forty-pound cheddar blocks sit in the mid-$1.70s per pound and barrels run lower in the high $1.30s, and federal milk prices for August dropped under $20 per hundredweight, producers now face unquantified corn and soybean crop damage spanning tens of millions of acres. ## The reality of pricing blind in a soft market

We see the same pattern whenever block prices slide and storms disrupt regional feed budgets: members panic-cut retail and wholesale prices to match neighboring cheese cases without verifying whether those prices cover actual operating costs. Conflating wholesale market fluctuations with individual make costs is a fast track to insolvency. In a month where milk prices have fallen by nearly a dollar per hundredweight from July and regional feed contracts are in turmoil, pricing by feel or by comparison is a liability.

Our webinar walks through the foundational spreadsheet: true milk costs including quality or volume premiums, culture and rennet overhead, labor calculated at an honest rate rather than discounted founder time, aging shrink by cheese style, and packaging materials. Crucially, the session covers what to do once that number is known. If the math shows a specific cheese style is underwater at current input and feed costs, operators need the confidence to adjust wholesale pricing, have difficult conversations with distributors, or drop the style entirely. None of this is comfortable, but continuing to produce at an uncalculated loss is worse.
