The gap between grocery case demand and stalled restaurant orders continues to define members' operating reality this autumn. Members who spent the summer building direct lists are surviving the plateau, while those relying entirely on foodservice wholesale accounts are facing a tight quarter. ## The divide between retail and foodservice

Public reporting and member calls this week highlight a sharp bifurcation. Grocery cheese sales are seeing robust double-digit growth, whereas restaurant orders remain depressed and flat following a stalled summer recovery. For small producers, the farmers' market table has functioned as a reliable lifeline, allowing them to collect customer emails and build standing pre-order lists for the holidays. However, members jumping into direct shipping for the first time face immediate packaging hurdles. Cheese shipped without proper thermal insulation or tested transit times can sweat and spoil, leading to expensive returns. Our educational offerings, specifically the Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course and Listeria Control in Small Creameries, are addressing these exact operational friction points. We must ensure our course reminders align with the urgency members feel right now.

Communications is tracking the double-digit retail growth narrative without losing sight of the members stranded in the foodservice sector. We need to tighten our internal coordination so that inquiries about shipping mechanics or distributor bid lists are routed correctly without dropping routine operational tasks.
