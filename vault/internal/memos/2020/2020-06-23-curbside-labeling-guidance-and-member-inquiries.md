Following last week's member queries regarding curbside pickup and hand-cut wedges, the Standards Committee has reviewed what federal temporary flexibility actually covers. Members shifting rapidly from traditional counter service to direct-to-consumer bags and deli tubs are frequently assuming that emergency administrative rules broadly waive standard identity and allergen labeling. This memo outlines what our advisory response will be, details the exact regulatory boundaries, and establishes what Membership Operations and Communications should provide to members asking for workable packaging and labeling formats during this challenging operational period.

## What the temporary policy covers and what it does not

The federal guidance issued this spring provides real flexibility for minor formulation substitutions and bulk restaurant shipments when regular suppliers are unavailable. However, it does not alter the fundamental legal requirements for retail sale directly to households. A product sold in a curbside bag still requires an explicit statement of identity, an accurate net weight, an ingredient list, allergen disclosures, and the maker's full name and address. Furthermore, aging disclosures for raw-milk cheeses remain strictly enforced without exception. We must ensure our guidance actively discourages members from relying on informal rumors about relaxed labeling standards, as shortcuts taken now will create significant compliance liabilities once regulatory reviews resume in full force.

## Practical formats for in-house printing

The primary friction point for small producers is not the legal minimum itself, but the operational gap of printing workable adhesive labels or bag inserts in-house for unaccustomed packaging formats. The Membership & Outreach Committee has been collecting improvised examples from members across various regions, ranging from adhesive rounds sized for deli tubs to half-sheet paper bag inserts containing all mandatory disclosures. We need to consolidate these examples into a brief, practical guide before more members make preventable compliance errors during the busy summer direct-sale rush.

## Broader channels and definitions

Beyond immediate curbside needs, this summer's sales shift has exposed longer-standing ambiguities around how terms like "farmstead" and "aged" are defined and enforced when specialty products move through nontraditional distribution channels. Our advocacy log shows 27 distinct actions recorded around labeling standards this year alone. These individual member queries are exceptionally valuable inputs for the longer-term discussions we are maintaining with regulatory bodies about establishing modern, channel-agnostic definitions that protect artisan makers without imposing impossible administrative burdens.
