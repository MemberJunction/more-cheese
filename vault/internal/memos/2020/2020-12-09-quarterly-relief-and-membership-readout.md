**Subject:** Quarterly relief window closure and membership engagement readout

As we approach the end of the week of 7 December 2020, we are facing two converging pressures that require coordination across our teams: the closing window for federal pandemic assistance on December 11, and the operational insights emerging from our member outreach efforts. Membership currently sits at 390 active accounts, with 6 new individual membership purchases booked so far this week generating $928 in gross sales. While the broader dairy market has seen block cheese prices firm up and a modest upward revision in fourth-quarter milk price forecasts, our internal accounts show continued strain among small producers who are balancing tight aging room humidity against reduced wholesale movement.

## The relief deadline and market reality

The federal assistance filing deadline closing this Friday has generated a late surge of inquiries to our team. Many smaller creameries have hesitated to file, questioning whether the paperwork is worth the effort for modest production volumes. Our guidance, aligned with recent policy advisory messaging, is clear: eligible members should file using their actual production figures rather than forfeit support. However, we must ensure our communications do not inadvertently suggest that firming fourth-quarter prices negate the hardships endured during earlier months. Market strength and program eligibility are separate matters, and taking owed relief does not signal negative expectations for the upcoming quarter.

## Insights from direct member outreach

Recent volunteer-led outreach to accounts that went silent since the spring lockdown wave has provided valuable qualitative data. Rather than purely commercial friction, conversations revealed significant operational isolation and fatigue among small-business owners running skeleton crews. Accounts that stopped opening newsletters or logging into courses were not necessarily disengaged from the industry; many simply lacked the bandwidth to engage with broadcast communications while managing curbside operations and single-operator caves. This feedback confirms that passive email campaigns are insufficient for at-risk accounts, and personalized, low-pressure conversations yield better long-term stability.

## Education and cave management alignment

Operational strain is also visible in our aging room management inquiries. With heating systems activating for the winter, humidity and airflow control has become an urgent topic for members holding inventory longer than usual to capture holiday sales. Course enrollments reflect this shift, with 15 new enrollments taken this week and cohorts such as the Summit cohort for Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets starting on December 8, alongside preparations for the Alpine cohort's Sensory Foundations course on December 25. Communications must ensure that our educational offerings and advisory notes address these winter cave management realities directly.

- Communications: reinforce the December 11 federal relief deadline across all remaining outreach channels without conflating filing eligibility with current market pricing.
- Advocacy & Standards: compile a brief summary of farm service office feedback regarding small-producer reporting ambiguities to inform our January committee briefs.
- Membership Operations: finalize the weekly transaction reconciliation and track retention metrics for the remaining three weeks of the fiscal year.
