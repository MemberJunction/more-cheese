## The scope of our record discrepancies

During our weekly audit, we tracked two primary data-quality failures that directly impact member communications. First, a series of newsletter and event notices sent to established professional accounts have returned as undeliverable due to obsolete domain listings. Several corporate and small business profiles continue to house email contacts belonging to former employers, a residue of staffing shifts that occurred earlier this year when pandemic disruptions forced widespread personnel changes across the artisan sector. Second, we have flagged duplicate profile pairs where individual members registered under alternative spellings or secondary email addresses, resulting in split payment histories that complicate our annual renewal tracking.

Furthermore, payment status reconciliation remains bogged down by manual entry errors. ## Impact on federation communications and outreach

These database flaws are not merely administrative annoyances; they directly undermine federation initiatives. When Communications promotes upcoming educational programming—such as the Listeria Control in Small Creameries cohort starting on November 20 or the Blue Cheese Production cohort beginning on November 8—bounced addresses prevent key technical notices from reaching the creamery staff who need them most. Similarly, public advocacy campaigns concerning raw-milk aging rules and labeling standards rely on clean organizational hierarchies to tally support accurately across small producers.

We must establish a protocol to verify contact data before launching large-scale outreach for winter course cohorts and the upcoming digital storefront webinar scheduled for December 3. Without clean records, our engagement metrics will continue to reflect database decay rather than actual member participation.
