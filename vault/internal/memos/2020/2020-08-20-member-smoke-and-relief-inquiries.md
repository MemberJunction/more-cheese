**To:** Advocacy & Standards; Communications
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 20 August 2020
**Subject:** Member smoke inquiries and relief document support

This week has brought two distinct waves of calls to our functional mailboxes that do not fit our normal operating rhythm. The first comes from members on the West Coast dealing with hazardous air quality from dry lightning and wildfire smoke, asking whether caves with outside air exchange will taint aging cheese or endanger staff. The second comes from mixed producers trying to untangle federal direct-payment relief documentation after the commodity list expanded to nearly five dozen items and the deadline moved to mid-September. Neither topic is something our routine support scripts cover.

## What members are asking about smoke and caves

Several creameries several counties away from active fires have contacted us because outdoor air readings are among the worst in the country this week. They are trying to decide whether to temporarily seal air intakes on natural airflow aging caves and what fine particulate exposure does to a wheel over weeks of maturation. We do not have a confident product-side answer, and we are not going to invent one. What is clear is the personnel side: working a cave or a make floor in this air is a real hazard, and standard cloth masks do not stop fine particulates.

The Membership & Outreach Committee is fielding these calls and connecting affected creameries to compare notes. We need Advocacy & Standards to ensure our guidance remains aligned with what we publish on the blog without overstepping into definitive food safety pronouncements on smoke taint, where hard data simply does not exist yet.

## The document hurdle in relief applications

On the advocacy front, members looking at the expanded commodity list are running into the same documentation bottleneck: reconstructing a certified first-quarter production figure from a mix of hauler pickup receipts, handwritten logs, and settlement statements that include quality premiums, hauling deductions, and component pricing. Members shipping through cooperatives are finding that settlement numbers do not match the clean volume figures the program requires.

Our advice to callers has been straightforward: call the cooperative member services line before starting the paperwork. Cooperatives can generate production-only figures in ten minutes, saving members an afternoon of frustration. Advocacy should highlight this specific tip in upcoming updates so we cut down on inbound support friction before the September deadline.
