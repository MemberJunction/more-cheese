The industry study on US artisan and specialty cheesemakers published this week counts nearly a thousand active makers, with roughly three-quarters producing under 50,000 pounds annually. That figure matches our active membership profile closely, but our curriculum has not always reflected the operational realities of small-scale production. This memo reviews how we align our course offerings with the actual shape of our membership base before the spring and summer cohorts open.

## What the study changes in our course design

When three out of four members are working at under 50,000 pounds a year, standard industrial pricing models and multi-shift facility guidelines do not apply. A maker running a small room faces distinct distribution and margin constraints that commodity-scale processors never encounter. We need to ensure our courses address these realities directly.

Our Storytelling & Provenance in Marketing course and our Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course both target these exact distribution channels. However, enrollment figures for the week ending 9 February show 0 course enrollments taken so far, with 2 course completions recorded for the broader year-to-date. Engagement is quiet while members navigate winter production challenges.

## Food safety and seasonal realities

In tandem with marketing and distribution, our Listeria Control in Small Creameries course addresses the operational blind spots that appear when winter weather changes plant conditions. Propped doors, condensation from supplemental heaters, and tracked-in salt brine create harborage risks that standard warm-weather monitoring plans miss. Connecting our food safety materials to the study's profile of small creamery operations is essential if we want members to adopt these protocols.

We also note that federal value-added producer grant opportunities are active this month, offering a lower cash barrier for small operations planning capital investments. While we do not administer grants, our pricing and margin guidance should acknowledge how producers use them.

## Capacity and cash flow

Membership growth for the week shows 2 membership purchases totaling $490 in gross sales, consisting of 1 SmallBusiness tier and 1 Individual tier, bringing current members at week's end to 372. Cash captured across all association activities for the week stands at $6241. With revenue constrained, we must ensure our educational offerings are practical and directly tied to the immediate margin and distribution challenges small makers face.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: cross-reference the small-producer study's volume distribution against our current SmallBusiness and Individual tier renewal lists.
- Communications: verify that upcoming blog posts and newsletters point clearly to the Storytelling & Provenance and Direct-to-Consumer course descriptions.
- Education & Credentialing: finalize syllabus updates for the upcoming spring cohorts to emphasize small-scale make-sheet labor and direct distribution.
