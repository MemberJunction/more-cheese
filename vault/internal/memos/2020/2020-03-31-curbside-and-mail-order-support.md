**Subject:** Curbside and mail order support for members facing wholesale loss

The collapse of the restaurant, campus, and hotel breakfast channels over the last three weeks has left several of our small creameries with walk-ins full of cheese that was cut and priced for foodservice accounts that are no longer placing orders. Inquiries coming through the member portal since Monday indicate an urgent, widespread pivot toward curbside pickup and direct-to-consumer mail order. This memo outlines what our members are facing, how our current educational offerings address these operational hurdles, and what we need from communications and education teams this week.

## What members are encountering on the ground

Members attempting to stand up a direct channel overnight are running into predictable friction. A curbside pickup operation requires a structured ordering window, a payment method that minimizes physical contact, and strict order-accuracy controls so that family orders are not mixed up during peak hours. Mail order introduces entirely separate physical challenges: insulated packaging, appropriately sized gel packs, and contingency plans for packages stalled on porches in rising spring temperatures.

Furthermore, members report that pricing models developed for farmers' market stalls or wholesale deli counters fail once packaging, shipping supplies, and third-party delivery overhead are factored in. Without guidance, creameries risk underpricing their direct sales or drowning in administrative chaos.

## Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course alignment

Our **Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets** course covers the exact operational framework members need right now: setting up a reliable ordering system, designing porch-safe packaging, and building a pricing worksheet that accounts for absorbed wholesale costs. Because this course is scheduled in our catalog for later in the year, we should evaluate whether we can make relevant modules or practical checklists available to members immediately through the portal, rather than forcing them to wait.

Our membership numbers for the week ending 5 April reflect a modest intake — 5 total membership purchases grossing $989, including 4 Individual tier purchases and 1 SmallBusiness tier purchase, with 2 first-time members joining. Total confirmed orders stand at 7 for $1,757 gross, and cash captured is $1,906. While membership demand is holding reasonably steady for late March, cash flow for producers dependent on foodservice remains severely compromised by unsold inventory.

## Advocacy and committee engagement

Our advocacy team has been active on related fronts, recording 1 testimony on dairy pricing reform and participating in 1 coalition meeting on interstate cheese shipment during this same week. While federal relief packages enacted on March 27 provide essential payroll-support lending under wide eligibility bars, they do not offer sector-specific dairy market fixes. Consequently, direct consumer sales remain the fastest revenue valve available to independent makers.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure upcoming blog posts and member touchpoints direct inquiries regarding mail order and curbside logistics toward the Membership & Outreach Committee and our practical coursework.
- Education & Credentialing: review the curriculum materials for the Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course to see which practical worksheets can be excerpted and shared directly with members via the portal this week.
- Office of the Chief Executive: authorize staff coordination between membership ops and education to handle the incoming surge of direct-sale inquiries without delaying advocacy responses.
