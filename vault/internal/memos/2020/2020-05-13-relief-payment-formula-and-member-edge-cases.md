**Subject:** Relief payment formula and member edge cases

The federal relief payment program for dairy producers, which opens for applications later this month, is narrower than initial press coverage suggested. Because our advocacy line has received several inquiries following our recent update, this memo summarizes the formula, the exact gaps in coverage, and what we are asking members to submit so we can document edge cases for federal agencies before the application window closes.

## The payment formula and its limitations

The entire relief calculation rests on a single metric: how much milk an operation produced in the first three months of this year, multiplied by a fixed rate per hundredweight. It does not account for April or May production volumes, does not adjust for milk dumped due to processing bottlenecks, and does not differentiate between fluid milk operations and direct-to-consumer farmsteads whose retail channels collapsed overnight. For producers whose first quarter was strong but whose second quarter suffered severe contraction, the check will look generous on paper and thin in reality.

This discrepancy mirrors what we are hearing from members regarding Class III milk prices. While retail dairy cases remain full due to increased home cooking, Class III prices used for hard and semi-hard cheese have fallen sharply since January as restaurant and school demand dried up. The relief payment is designed to soften this gap, not solve it. Co-op members must ask in writing how payments will flow through their pools, while farmstead cheesemakers face a calculation that ignores lost wholesale and food-service accounts.

## Edge cases and data collection

Not every operation fits neatly into a formula based on three months of herd production. We are actively compiling reports on mixed producers, recent start-ups lacking a complete first-quarter record, and creameries that purchase milk rather than milking their own herds. Membership Operations should ensure our incoming support logs capture these specific operational profiles so we can present concrete examples to the farm-support agency rather than abstract complaints.
