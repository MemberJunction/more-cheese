This memorandum details the operational status of our membership accounts and order logs for the week of Monday, July 27, 2020, with particular focus on an ongoing invoice dispute with a small business member. As our year-to-date figures show 411 current members at week end and $1,892 in cash captured so far, we are facing friction regarding how historical tier changes and membership tier listings are reconciled against cash receipts.

## The current financial picture

During this week, we logged 3 membership purchases (all first-time members, consisting of 2 Individual tier and 1 SmallBusiness tier), generating a gross amount of $698. Total confirmed orders for the week reached 10, with $1,342 in gross orders and 1 cancellation. Cash captured stands at $1,892. These figures are preliminary as we approach the end of the week, but they reflect the ongoing adjustments small-scale members are making in response to shifting wholesale and retail channels highlighted in recent industry discussions. When members straddle different sales channels, their cash flow fluctuates, leading directly to billing inquiries.

## The invoice dispute

The immediate trigger for this review is a dispute raised by a SmallBusiness tier member regarding their annual dues invoice. The member claims that the tier upgrade processed earlier this year did not reflect their actual operational capacity under current pandemic conditions, where their wholesale accounts have fallen off while direct-to-consumer sales have required a complete pivot. They are requesting a retroactive adjustment to their billing record and a credit against their invoice.

Our database indicates that the membership purchase was recorded correctly according to the standard list price for the SmallBusiness tier ($400), but the member argues that the association's published pricing and membership tiers should accommodate businesses undergoing severe channel disruptions. We have reviewed the order lines and payment logs, and while our records are technically sound, the member's frustration mirrors the broader market volatility where block cheddar prices swung widely this spring from a dollar a pound to record highs, leaving annual contracts badly out of step.

## Reconciliation and next steps

We cannot unilaterally alter historical invoice records without creating discrepancies in our cash capture and order reconciliation logs. However, we also cannot afford to alienate small creameries that are currently fighting to stay afloat through driveway sales and online order forms rather than traditional restaurant distribution.

We recommend establishing a transparent review process for disputed invoices that separates administrative billing errors from pandemic-related hardship adjustments. For the current dispute, we propose offering a credit toward upcoming educational courses—such as our Maturation Planning for Small Rooms course or Counter Culture workshops—rather than a direct cash refund on membership dues. This preserves our revenue integrity while providing tangible value to the member.
