We are also tracking 2 upcoming events in the next eight weeks, specifically the upcoming Webinar: Costing a Make 2020 scheduled for December 31 and the Webinar: Cheese & Cider Pairing 2021 on February 9, 2021. This memo reviews how member operations are coping with the extraordinary channel shifts documented across our small-creamery base this December and outlines where our capacity is currently strained.

## The channel shift reality

The gap between a full holiday retail cheese case and soft wholesale markets is dominating the close of 2020. Members running small creameries have reported that wholesale accounts dropped sharply while direct-to-consumer mailing lists expanded from dozens of names to several hundred. Revenue ended up roughly flat for many producers, but the labor cost of packing mail-order boxes and managing direct customer logistics broke every projection made last January. Our course enrollments reflect this scramble, with 14 new enrollments taken this week as members look for structured guidance on pricing and margins before setting next year's rate cards.

## Costing the make before the year turns

Normally, make-sheet costing and pricing reviews wait until late January. This year, the pressure from members forced us to schedule the Webinar: Costing a Make 2020 for December 31. This session will walk through building a real cost per wheel from milk price, yield, labor, packaging, and overhead, then comparing that against wholesale, farmers market, and direct-to-consumer channels. Because federal dairy market reporting this month put block cheese prices below November levels even as retail cases held their ground, members need to inspect their numbers immediately rather than guessing at next year's prices.

## Capacity and staff coverage constraints

Handling the year-end close while supporting an unprecedented volume of direct-to-consumer inquiries has stretched our shared staff thin. Communications and Membership Operations are absorbing the double duty of compiling year-end reports and fielding member questions about federal traceability rules and soft cheese recordkeeping. We cannot take on additional surveys or data collection projects before the new year without dropping routine membership processing tasks. Team leads must protect their analysts' time through the holiday break.
