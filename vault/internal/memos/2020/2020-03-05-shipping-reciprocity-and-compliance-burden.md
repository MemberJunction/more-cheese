**Subject:** Shipping reciprocity proposals and small-producer compliance burden

The Membership & Outreach Committee has been logging a steady increase in member inquiries regarding the regional standards proposal on interstate dairy shipping reciprocity. This memo summarizes what is moving through state agriculture departments this quarter, how our current advocacy tally looks as of this week, and what we should and should not say in public communications.

## What the regional proposal entails

The current system relies on reciprocal recognition of state plant inspections, allowing a creamery certified in one state to ship across state lines without duplicate inspections. The new proposal under consideration introduces an annual attestation filing between full inspection cycles. While the intent is to close gaps between multi-year inspection windows, small producers with limited compliance staff view the annual filing as a real administrative burden, especially given the fragmented ways different states plan to implement the form.

This discussion arrives at a difficult moment for dairy margins. Federal price forecasts for 2020 have trimmed milk price outlooks, and cheese prices are not expected to firm up until later in the year as stocks work down. Members are entering spring with very thin financial cushions, making any new procedural requirement feel heavier than it might in a growth year.

## Advocacy tally so far

Our advocacy records for the week ending 8 March show active engagement from members across multiple fronts. Specifically, we have logged 4 PetitionSignature actions and 1 LetterCampaign action centered on FSMA small-producer exemptions, alongside 1 Testimony action regarding raw-milk aging rules. These figures are preliminary and reflect the initial wave of member outreach before the comment window closes.

Members who ship interstate are telling us their primary fear is not any single state's paperwork, but the administrative chaos of juggling multiple divergent forms across several destination states. Our committee submission focuses on requesting a single standardized form and an extended implementation runway.

## Communications boundaries

Communications published a blog post on 4 March outlining the interstate shipping paperwork debate. That post correctly identifies the core tension between inspection reciprocity and annual attestations, and it links members to portal resources. However, we must ensure that subsequent blog posts and staff emails do not overstate our formal influence on regional standards boards, nor should we promise specific timeline modifications before regulatory bodies issue their final rulings.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: continue directing inquiring creameries to our advocacy portal before the comment window closes.
- Communications: maintain the current measured tone on regulatory matters, ensuring no unverified enforcement dates are published.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that margin pressures on small producers will likely increase advocacy volume around labeling and interstate shipping through the end of Q1.
