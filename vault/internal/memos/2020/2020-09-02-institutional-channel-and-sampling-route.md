**Subject:** Institutional demand shifts and quality sampling route adjustments

Block cheddar prices have continued their relentless slide into the low $1.70s this week, and the federal all-milk price for August came in nearly a dollar below July's reported level. Compounding this broad commodity price softness, the federal decision announced this past Monday to extend free-meal eligibility through the end of the calendar year is reshaping institutional orders rather than restoring them to historical pre-pandemic patterns. School districts and institutional buyers are moving away from bulk blocks and shred products in favor of pre-portioned and individually wrapped items to support grab-and-go meal distribution models. Members supplying these accounts report significantly higher delivery frequencies with smaller loads, which substantially increases operational complexity, transportation overhead, and cold-storage inventory turnover across their facilities.

## Sampling routes under shifting production schedules

In parallel with institutional format shifts, our creamery members are navigating production schedules that refuse to fit fixed quarterly calendars. As highlighted in our member outreach this week, a quality manager at a mid-sized farmstead creamery in the upper Midwest had to completely redesign her environmental sampling route around active operating shifts rather than a rigid calendar. Traditional fixed schedules fail when staggered shifts, split crews, and fluctuating wholesale orders mean testing an empty room instead of an active production run. Moving the testing schedule to match active operations preserves the scientific integrity of the microbiological data, but it requires daily, hands-on coordination between quality managers and production leads to ensure swabs capture actual floor conditions.

## Committee alignment and member support

The Food Safety Committee has been fielding questions all summer regarding shift-based rotation worksheets and documentation standards, while the Membership & Outreach Committee is actively collecting notes from members navigating softer commodity boards alongside altered institutional formats. We need to ensure our technical guidance remains practical and does not assume stable purchasing patterns that no longer exist in the marketplace. Members require concrete worksheet templates for shift-based environmental monitoring rather than generic compliance advice that assumes traditional five-day production weeks.
