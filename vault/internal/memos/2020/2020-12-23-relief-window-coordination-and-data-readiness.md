> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT** — International Cheese Federation (ICF), a fictitious trade association created for the MemberJunction More Cheese demonstration environment. All names, figures, events, and decisions are invented.

**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 23 December 2020
**Subject:** Relief window coordination and data readiness

Late this week, federal lawmakers advanced a second round of small-business relief funding that will directly impact our membership base of creameries, small shops, and affineurs. As Communications prepares the public-facing guidance and blog posts for our members, we need to ensure our internal messaging is aligned with the exact figures and limitations we can support. Members will be looking for clear guidance on how to prove a quarter-over-quarter revenue drop against 2019, especially those who pivoted their business models toward direct-to-consumer shipping during the spring shutdowns and subsequent holiday rush. This memo outlines what our current data shows, what our advocacy team is tracking, and how we must frame our advice without promising answers that federal agencies have not yet finalized.

## What the year-end data shows and where the gaps lie

Looking at our association-wide figures for the year, we closed 2020 with 390 current members on December 31, backed by 424 total membership purchases generating $94,969 in sales. Across the entire year, confirmed orders reached 762 against 97 cancellations, producing $147,811 in gross revenue and capturing $187,828 in cash. While these association figures demonstrate our own operational resilience during a turbulent year, they also mirror the underlying volatility our members faced. Creameries that shifted wholesale volume into mail-order gift boxes and curbside pickup are finding that their 2019 baseline comparisons do not cleanly align with current accounting practices.

During this specific week (December 21 to December 27), we recorded modest administrative activity: exactly 4 membership purchases (2 first-time, 3 Individual, 1 Corporate) totaling $1,333 in gross sales, alongside 5 confirmed orders grossing $1,184 with 1 cancellation, and total cash captured of $2,606. Course enrollments saw 5 new sign-ups, 4 completions, and 1 upcoming cohort starting on December 25 (Sensory Foundations, Alpine cohort). Meanwhile, event activity remains paused, with no events this week and only two scheduled over the next eight weeks (Webinar: Costing a Make 2020 on December 31, and Webinar: Cheese & Cider Pairing 2021 on February 9). Free webinars carry no registrations in our data, so we must refrain from quoting attendance figures for them.

## Aligning public blog messaging with internal realities

Communications has drafted a blog post advising members to gather their 2019 and 2020 quarterly revenue comparisons and review their employee counts. We fully support this advice, but we must be careful not to overstate what we know. The federal guidance has not yet clarified how direct-to-consumer sales shifts interact with wholesale baselines, nor how seasonal holiday hiring affects small-business employee caps. Members who took on debt in the spring and experienced a brief holiday surge are anxious about whether a strong December erases a difficult year on paper. We should tell them plainly that documentation discipline matters more than optimism, but we must avoid speculating on how lenders will interpret mixed revenue streams.

Furthermore, our Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding one-on-one calls from members trying to make sense of relief eligibility. We should direct members to these committee office hours rather than attempting to provide definitive legal or financial interpretations ourselves. Our advocacy work this year—spanning 110 total actions, including 46 petition signatures, 38 letter campaigns, 19 coalition meetings, and 7 testimonies focused on topics like FSMA exemptions, labeling standards, interstate shipping, raw-milk aging rules, and dairy pricing reform—gives us credibility, but it does not grant us authority over SBA loan adjudications.

## What we need

- Communications: Ensure the upcoming blog posts emphasize bookkeeping preparation and document gathering while explicitly noting that federal program guidance is still evolving.
- Advocacy & Standards: Continue tracking regulatory updates regarding seasonal hiring counts and direct-to-consumer revenue weightings so we can update our advice as soon as final rules drop.
- Membership Operations: Prepare staff to handle an expected surge in member inquiries regarding membership verification and historical dues receipts for loan applications.
