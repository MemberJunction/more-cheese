**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 11 June 2020
**Subject:** Managing pricing volatility and invoice discrepancies

Block cheese prices have more than doubled off their spring lows, creating an unprecedented wave of member inquiries regarding distributor invoices, formula-pricing contracts, and wholesale agreements. While our blog post this week focuses broadly on the market swing and why April pricing no longer exists, this memo addresses the operational fallout: members are calling our desks because their distributor invoices for June do not match what they budgeted under their standard 30-day terms, and a few are panicking over whether they should sign new standing orders. This volatility is hitting wholesale and retail accounts with severe friction, and staff need a unified line on how to advise callers without giving formal financial or legal counsel.

## The reality of formula pricing in a surging market

Our order logs and cash receipts show stable transaction volumes, but the gross values attached to confirmed orders are climbing rapidly as members re-negotiate. The core problem is formula pricing tied directly to the block cheese market. In April, when processors had surplus milk and block prices plummeted, a formula-priced invoice felt generous to buyers. In June, that exact same formula is punishing buyers who locked in or accepted variable distributor terms without a ceiling. Members calling Membership Operations report that the number given on Monday is already wrong by Wednesday. We must advise members to review their distributor contracts immediately and avoid signing any agreement past 30 days right now if they can possibly avoid it. Nobody has a confident read on where July cheese pricing settles, and locking into a long-term formula in the middle of whiplash pricing is a severe business risk.

## Direct-to-consumer versus wholesale exposure

Members who diversified into farm stands or online direct sales this spring are experiencing a different set of pressures. A regular at a farm stand is not tracking dairy commodity futures; they expect predictable pricing. Members who tried to peg their direct-to-consumer pricing directly to the volatile block market are seeing pushback from local retail customers. We need to reinforce the message that direct channels and wholesale channels require separate pricing models. Wholesale accounts follow the formula; direct channels follow local market tolerance. Blurring the two is what triggered several cancellation requests and billing disputes over the last two weeks.

## What we need

- Communications: Ensure our weekly guidance continues to warn members away from long-term supply contracts exceeding 30 days until July stabilization data is visible.
- Advocacy & Standards: Compile any additional federal dairy market reports describing tight cheddar supplies so our advisory team can reference official statistics when members dispute distributor pricing.
- Office of the Chief Executive: Acknowledge that Membership Operations call volume regarding invoice discrepancies is elevated, and approve an extra advisory note for next week's member dispatch.
