**Subject:** Membership lapse management and grace-window requests under margin pressure

As community illness clusters widen and regional food-service orders soften, Membership Operations is seeing an uptick in inquiries from small business and individual members asking for grace windows on pending renewals or attempting to unwind recent tier commitments. With the current member count standing at 382 as of this week, retaining every active account is more critical than usual, but our automated payment processing is issuing hard lapses exactly when creamery operators are least positioned to absorb administrative friction. This memo outlines the operational reality behind this week's renewal escalations and proposes a controlled grace-window policy to prevent preventable churn.

## The renewal friction point

Our standard billing rules terminate a membership automatically when an annual renewal payment fails or misses the thirty-day window. In a normal operating environment, that hard stop functions cleanly. In the current week, however, several small creamery operators have reported cash-flow interruptions driven by sudden channel shifts—specifically, the sudden softening of restaurant and wholesale accounts offset only partially by retail pantry buying. When an automatic payment attempt bounces against an account tightened by these shifts, a standard lapse notice arrives like an administrative penalty rather than a routine reminder.

This week's data shows 7 membership purchases completed, grossing 1427, with 4 first-time members joining across Small Business and Individual tiers. Against those additions, however, we are tracking a rising volume of failed payment notifications and requests for extended grace periods from members whose annual renewals fell due this month. If we treat these delinquent accounts under our standard zero-tolerance schedule, we risk permanently severing relationships with businesses that intend to renew once their near-term cash-flow stabilizes.

## What members are telling us

On outreach calls this week, member-owners have been remarkably frank. Those straddling both retail and wholesale channels describe being pulled in opposite directions: grocery counter sales are moving quickly, but wholesale accounts are delaying settlements. Asking these operators to execute a twelve-month dues commitment at the standard list price while their buyers sit on invoices is triggering resistance. Several have asked if they can drop a tier temporarily or defer payment by sixty days without losing access to member benefits or course discounts.

Our current system does not handle temporary tier downshifts gracefully, and creating ad-hoc exceptions manually creates an unsustainable tracking burden for our small team. Yet refusing the request outright risks driving long-standing members into lapse status precisely when they need association resources—such as our educational courses and food-safety guidance—the most.

## Capacity and risk

Membership Operations is currently managing these escalations alongside routine payment capture reconciliation, which stands at 2729 in cash captured this week across 9 confirmed orders and 1 cancellation. Handling individual grace-window approvals by hand pulls staff away from intake reconciliation and advocacy tracking. We need an association-wide stance that provides relief without dismantling our revenue baseline.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a temporary 60-day grace window for any renewing member requesting payment deferral due to channel disruption, effective immediately through the end of Q2.
- Advocacy & Standards: review our proposed grace-window communication template to ensure it aligns with the tone we are setting in our public updates regarding market volatility.
- Communications: ensure that any outbound renewal messaging points members to Membership Operations rather than triggering an automated hard lapse.
- Membership Operations: track all deferred accounts in a dedicated spreadsheet rather than altering primary billing records.
