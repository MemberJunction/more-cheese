**To:** Communications; Office of the Chief Executive; Advocacy & Standards
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 20 August 2024
**Subject:** Fall contract timing, specialty cheese margins, and member renewal friction

This week's blog post rightly highlights that feed costs and steady exchange pricing have created the quietest margin picture producers have seen in years. Class III milk sitting at $20.66 per hundredweight and Class IV at $21.58 give our farmstead and wholesale members a stable footing heading into fall harvest. However, behind that calm wholesale surface, our operational data shows a recurring friction point that costs us renewals: the collision between fixed anniversary billing dates and seasonal retail crunches.

## The renewal timing bottleneck

Retail specialty cheese buyers face an intense back-to-school purchasing window every August. When their ICF membership renewal notice lands during this exact month, it arrives precisely when they have the least amount of discretionary time. As a buyer noted in our recent member spotlight, waiting until the final week of the grace window is not a sign of waning value—she relies on our member directory and standards library constantly—it is a casualty of bad calendar alignment.

Our week-ending figures show 13 membership purchases totaling $3,081, with 8 first-time members joining across individual, enthusiast, small business, and corporate tiers. While new acquisition numbers remain healthy, we are spending unnecessary staff hours chasing renewals from established buyers who simply need their billing anchor moved to a quieter operational month.

## Aligning market data with member operations

We need to reconcile what the market data tells us with how members experience our administration. The blog suggests that wholesalers firm up fall contracts now rather than waiting for rallies that data does not support. If wholesale and retail buyers are locking in fall commitments this week, they are doing so while juggling administrative notices from our system that threaten to lapse their accounts over an unread invoice.

Furthermore, our course enrollment figures show 63 new enrollments taken this week alongside 5 completions, and Workshop: Curd Food Safety (Querétaro) has already absorbed 54 registrations ahead of its 28 August start date. Engagement across education and events is strong, proving that the value proposition is secure. The friction is entirely structural.

## Capacity and next steps

Membership Operations cannot unilaterally alter anniversary structures without breaking the underlying billing logic, but we can offer a policy adjustment for buyer-tier members whose primary crunch falls in August or December. Allowing members to request a shifted renewal anchor without losing their historical tenure would remove a friction point that has nothing to do with whether association membership is worth keeping.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a formal review of flexible renewal windows for buyer and retail tier members.
- Communications: ensure future member spotlight follow-ups explicitly ask about administrative pain points alongside directory usage.
- Advocacy & Standards: note that operational friction in member accounts can mimic churn in our monthly metrics even when member satisfaction is high.
