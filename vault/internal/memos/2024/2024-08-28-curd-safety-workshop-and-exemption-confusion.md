Today's opening of Workshop: Curd Food Safety in Querétaro has coincided with a surge of calls from members asking whether qualifying for small-producer food-safety exemptions means they can drop their hazard analysis records. The workshop has 196 registered, with 178 attending and 18 marked no show, and at least three quality managers have arrived with two-year-old sanitation logs that they are actively rewriting after comparing notes in the room. This memo outlines the gap between what members think the exemption does and what regulators expect, so our advocacy files and our workshop messaging point the same direction.

## What members assume versus what the rule requires

The exemption thresholds are built around annual sales, offering relief on the format of paperwork for smaller farmstead operations. However, soft and semi-soft cheese sits squarely in a product category with a known pathogen history. Regardless of sales thresholds, the underlying obligation to run a hazard analysis and keep records that hold up under inspection does not disappear. Members frequently assume the exemption applies automatically once they cross under a revenue line, only to discover during an audit that their paperwork was incomplete or never formally filed.

Our advocacy work this cycle has centered on asking regulators for plain-language guidance specifically tailored to dairy processing caves and make rooms, rather than generic produce-farm rules that do not map cleanly onto a cheese vat. When members call in with stories about reinterpreting produce guidance for a dairy facility, those are the exact examples we need in our comment files. Specifics carry weight; generic complaints do not.

## Workshop alignment and calibration

The timing of today's workshop in Querétaro provides a direct test of our materials. Participants are reviewing sample logs from real plants, and attendees who brought their own plant records are finding that routine cleaning definitions look very different when subjected to peer review. We need to ensure that the Food Safety Committee's running FAQ captures these specific friction points so that members asking about compliance timelines receive consistent answers.

Milk has been tight and cheese prices have been running unusually strong this year, leaving creameries with little margin for error. In a strong market where every wheel is spoken for, a recall resulting from a lax curd-handling step carries severe financial penalties. The workshop is serving its purpose by forcing operators to confront vague swab schedules before an inspector finds them.

The overlap between education events, workshop follow-ups, and ongoing advocacy comment collection is placing heavy demands on shared staff. Communications published an exemption piece on August 26 that generated several incoming calls, but our team cannot chase every individual exemption story without coordinated intake.
