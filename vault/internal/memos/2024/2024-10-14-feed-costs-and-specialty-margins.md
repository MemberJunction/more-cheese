The federal wholesale dairy price forecast moved again late last month, rising on stronger block and barrel prices through August and September before cooling slightly toward year-end. Meanwhile, premium alfalfa hay has continued climbing through the same stretch, leaving a widening gap between output price projections and actual input costs. This memo reviews how that divergence affects our membership base, how our public communications are framing the risk for creameries, and why mixed-channel producers need separate ledger reviews rather than blended averages before holiday price lists lock.

## Mixed-channel creameries and holiday price lists

For specialty and artisan producers selling through both wholesale commodity channels and direct-to-consumer retail wedges, the read-through is more immediate. A commodity block price movement does not apply equally to an artisan wheel with a distinct flavor profile and provenance story. However, supply costs across packaging, transport, and ingredients are not moving in tandem with wholesale milk forecasts.

Producers selling across both books must separate them rather than relying on a blended average. The commodity side requires a tighter watch on cost this quarter, while the specialty side often holds more pricing room than a blended ledger indicates because customers buying differentiated cheese are not shopping strictly on a per-pound comparison. Waiting until January to reconcile these accounts risks locking in unprofitable holiday pre-orders.
