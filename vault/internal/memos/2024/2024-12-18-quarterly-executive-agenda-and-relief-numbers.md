This memorandum outlines the preliminary data and agenda items for the upcoming executive review, focusing on membership performance, advocacy actions, and the structural pricing reform discussions currently moving through the regulatory pipeline. With the federal milk marketing order amendments reaching their final decision stage this month, our members are evaluating their positions ahead of the producer referendum expected next year. Operating in an environment where 2024 margins have held mostly steady changes the tone of what our small and mid-sized creameries can credibly request from regulators.

## Membership performance and year-end baseline

As of the close of this week, our current membership stands at 1,052 active members. This reflects a stable retention trajectory through the final stretch of the year. Membership purchases for the week totaled 11 transactions, comprising 8 Individual tier purchases, 1 Enthusiast tier purchase, and 2 SmallBusiness tier purchases, generating a gross amount of 2,271 USD. Among these, 1 was a first-time membership purchase. Confirmed orders across all categories reached 20 for the week, with a gross value of 3,664 USD, while total cash captured for the period reached 11,396 USD. One cancellation was recorded.

Looking back across the full year 2024, our membership base closed at 1,051 members, supported by 305 first-time members and 727 members retained from the prior year against a prior year base of 859. The tier distribution at year-end settled at 756 Individual, 88 Enthusiast, 162 SmallBusiness, and 45 Corporate accounts, driven by 1,217 total membership purchases yielding 281,716 USD in gross sales. These figures demonstrate that specialty cheese demand has remained robust despite broader economic headwinds.

## Advocacy context and pricing reform

Recent federal announcements regarding milk pricing reform create an opening for our advocacy team. The regulator's marketing service issued its final decision on amendments to all eleven federal milk marketing orders earlier this month. Because 2024 has provided our producers with comparatively steady margins, our working groups can argue for structural formula changes—specifically better reflection of the price premiums commanded by small-batch and aged specialty cheese over commodity benchmarks—rather than relying purely on defensive survival arguments. The advocacy record shows 134 total actions for the year, led by 64 letter campaigns, 41 petition signatures, 21 coalition meetings, and 8 testimonies, with topics spanning FSMA small-producer exemptions, raw-milk aging rules, and dairy pricing reform.
