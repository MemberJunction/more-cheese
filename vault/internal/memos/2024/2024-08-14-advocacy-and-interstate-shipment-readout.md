**Subject:** Advocacy campaign readout and interstate shipment rules

This week's member response to our interstate cheese shipment action alert gives us a clear signal on where small creameries are feeling regulatory pressure. While block cheese prices on the wholesale exchange crossed two dollars a pound this month—a quiet tightening that hauler mailboxes are already reflecting—smaller farmstead producers are dealing with the separate headache of shipping rules across state lines. The current letter campaign has brought in five formal letter submissions and one petition signature so far this week, which is a solid engagement rate for an August push but leaves several key regional questions unresolved.

## The interstate shipment pushback

The feedback arriving through the advocacy inbox points directly to inconsistency between state-level agricultural departments and federal transit guidelines. Members who sell direct or through regional distributors report that small-batch shipments are frequently held up or subjected to redundant testing demands that larger commercial dairies avoid by scale. Our campaign text focuses on standardizing transit criteria and protecting small-producer exemptions under FSMA, but respondents are telling us that the bigger barrier is local interpretation at state borders. If we want the comment letter we are drafting next month to carry weight with regulators, we need concrete examples from members who have actually absorbed financial losses from border holds, not just theoretical concerns about transit delays.

## Alignment with current market trends

It is worth noting how this intersects with broader industry numbers. With spot milk running ahead of Class prices in several regions, producers operating on thin margins cannot afford transit delays that tie up inventory in transit. The wholesale price movement reported on the blog this week gives us useful external context, but it also highlights the split within our membership: a rising block price helps direct-milk sellers while squeezing small makers who buy spot milk for specialty styles. The advocacy team has been careful not to mix pricing commentary with transit advocacy, but the two conversations happen on the same phone calls with our members.

## Capacity and next steps

We cannot chase every individual shipping dispute without pulling legal and regulatory staff away from our core standard-setting commitments. We need a clean boundary between Association advocacy—which targets overarching rule changes—and member compliance assistance, which belongs in the education catalog or through direct consultation.
