**Subject:** Standards Committee labeling comment and member examples

The Standards Committee has been drafting its response to the active federal labeling comment period, which closes at the end of the month. As noted in the public blog post published on Monday, the committee wants specific member examples rather than general objections, particularly around aging descriptions and moisture-content disclosures for washed-rind and bloomy styles. This memo outlines what we have received so far, where the gaps are, and what we need before the draft goes final.

## What the committee is receiving

We have seen a handful of informal notes from members whose labels use aging ranges instead of fixed numbers because their caves operate on judgment rather than calendar entries. One farmstead producer wrote in to explain that her decade-old label uses a range precisely because her ripening room does not run on a fixed clock, and a strict numerical requirement would make her technically noncompliant on her own product. That is the exact kind of concrete detail the committee needs in the formal comment.

However, we are still short on examples for the moisture-disclosure provision affecting washed-rind and bloomy styles. Only a couple of members have confirmed whether their current packaging already complies or if they would need a complete packaging run. Without more concrete data from that segment, the comment risks reading as a theoretical critique rather than a practical industry assessment.

## Advocacy and market context

This comment period is landing against a backdrop of modest stability in the broader dairy sector. Milk production forecasts for the year came in a touch lower this month, but prices have held up better than the previous two winters, easing the severe feed-cost pressure that squeezed small producers through 2022 and 2023. Advocacy metrics for the current period show active engagement across our core topics, including one coalition meeting specifically on labeling standards this week, alongside ongoing work on raw-milk aging rules and small-producer exemptions under FSMA.

Because members have slightly more bandwidth this winter than they did last year, we are pushing for a stronger response on this notice than we usually secure for minor labeling updates. Office hours held by committee members this week are intended to help producers translate their real packaging practices into regulatory language that holds up under scrutiny.

## Capacity and next steps

The deadline for final committee review is fast approaching. Communications has agreed to highlight the office hours and the comment portal, but we need to ensure that the intake form on the member portal remains stable as submissions increase. Membership Operations should flag any member inquiries regarding labeling so we can route them directly to the committee members holding office hours.
