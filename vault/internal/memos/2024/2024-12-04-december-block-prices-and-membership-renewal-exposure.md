Federal market data released heading into this week points to a softer near-term picture for block cheese prices specifically, while household demand for specialty cheese holds firm. That gap between wholesale commodity pressure and retail cheese-board traffic is causing confusion among small-business members. This memo reviews our current renewal exposure across tiers and recommends how we frame our inbound margin guidance before January budgeting locks.

## The block price split and what members are seeing

Our current member count stands at 1,051 at week end, with 631 membership purchases recorded so far in this week's data pull grossing $154,749 across all tiers (446 Individual, 115 SmallBusiness, 29 Corporate, 41 Enthusiast). Within that buying volume, 23 are first-time members. While total cash captured across all confirmed orders this week sits at $28,011 from 641 confirmed orders, small-business members relying on wholesale block contracts report that their invoices feel heavier even as their cases move stock.

As noted in our public commentary on December block prices, wholesale block cheese prices softened into the month even as butter and nonfat dry milk firmed up. For small-business creameries tied to commodity index formulas, this split is squeezing margins. We need to ensure our guidance distinguishes between the wholesale block business, which requires careful monitoring of formula pricing, and the direct-to-consumer specialty counter business, which is benefiting from holiday cheese-board demand.

## Advocacy and membership alignment

We also logged 9 petition signatures and 14 letter campaign actions on dairy pricing reform this week. These advocacy actions cluster heavily among small-business members who feel exposed to wholesale price swings. If we let commodity softness dictate our advice on holiday pricing, we risk driving members toward unnecessary discounting. Our message must remain focused on unit margins and specialty differentiation rather than headline commodity quotes.
