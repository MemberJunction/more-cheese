**Subject:** Certification administration and foundations inquiries following the autumn wave

The recent surge of new members this fall has generated a high volume of inquiries regarding credential entry points, specifically concerning the Cheese Foundations Certificate and its prerequisite structure. With 27 certification enrollments recorded year-to-date and 616 total course enrollments across the association, staff are spending significant time answering baseline questions that should be handled by automated documentation or clear directory routing. This memo outlines the current administrative bottleneck in our credentialing pipeline and proposes immediate adjustments to how we manage candidate records.

## The foundations bottleneck

The Cheese Foundations Certificate is designed as the baseline credential for new entrants, requiring no prerequisites. However, members transitioning between retail monger work and production roles are frequently confused about whether Foundations acts as a prerequisite for specialized paths like the Certified Cheesemonger or Food Safety & HACCP tracks. Because Foundations covers basic milk handling, pasteurization rationale, hygiene practices, and style vocabulary, it serves as the necessary foundation without locking members into a single career track.

Administrative tracking has revealed discrepancies where candidates attempt to register for advanced credentials without verifying their underlying records. This creates manual overhead for staff who must cross-reference completion dates against active member accounts. With 475 course completions already logged this year, our current manual verification workflow is no longer sustainable during peak joining windows.

## Alignment with public communications

Public messaging published this week correctly emphasizes that Foundations is a check on basic competency rather than an advanced badge, noting its five-year validity period and its role in preventing early trade friction caused by terminology gaps. However, internal records show that members calling in with prerequisite questions are often unaware of these details. Communications must ensure that automated welcome sequences direct new members straight to the Education Committee contact points rather than leaving them to search the directory independently.

Furthermore, while our year-to-date figures show robust interest, we must ensure that our internal credentialing database reflects accurate candidate statuses before the upcoming winter cohort cycles begin. We cannot afford administrative delays when members are actively preparing for exam sittings.

## Capacity and next steps

The Education & Credentialing team is currently managing 24 course enrollments taken this week alone, alongside preparation for upcoming cohorts such as the Goat & Sheep Milk Cheesemaking session starting on 9 November and the Fresh & Lactic Cheeses cohort launched this week. Credential record corrections and prerequisite verifications are competing directly with course logistics. We need a streamlined verification protocol that prevents candidates from submitting incomplete credential histories.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: provide a weekly export of new member sign-ups by tier so credentialing intake can pre-verify eligibility without manual lookups.
- Communications: update the welcome email sequence to link directly to the centralized FAQ on Foundations prerequisites by Friday.
- Education & Credentialing: draft a standardized response template for prerequisite inquiries to reduce individual email turnaround times.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that certification administrative capacity is strained and defer any new credential expansion projects until Q1.
