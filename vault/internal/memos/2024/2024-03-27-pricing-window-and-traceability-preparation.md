At the same time, the regulatory clock on federal traceability rules is running toward its 2026 compliance date, creating distinct pressures for producers of short-aged, washed-curd, and soft-ripened styles. This memo outlines what our current operational numbers show for the week of 25 March 2024 and how our internal teams should coordinate responses to member inquiries across these two parallel concerns.

## Connecting feed cost relief to pricing models

The public guidance published this week on margin relief correctly notes that lower corn, alfalfa hay, and soybean meal costs provide an opportunity to revisit make-cost models rather than passing savings directly to buyers. However, members calling the helpline are struggling to separate fixed cave and labor costs from volatile inputs like milk and cultures. Education & Credentialing should ensure that upcoming cohorts of the *Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese* course emphasize practical make-cost worksheet updates. Members need to understand that this pricing window is temporary and should be used to build margin cushions rather than defensive discounts.

## Traceability exemption data collection

On the advocacy front, the advocacy actions table records 1 coalition meeting held this week concerning FSMA small-producer exemptions. While hard cheeses made from pasteurized milk remain carved out of the traceability rule, soft-ripened, washed-curd, and short-aged styles remain covered. We need to collect concrete make-floor numbers from members regarding the cost of full lot-level recordkeeping before exemption boundaries harden. Advocacy & Standards should synthesize these member accounts into a clear cost impact summary.

- Education & Credentialing: verify that course materials for margin classes incorporate current feed-cost cycle data without assuming permanent relief.
