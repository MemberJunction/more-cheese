**Subject:** Wholesale terms and January contract renewal readiness

National milk production has held up better than forecasters expected heading into winter, and dairy trade coverage this fall keeps pointing to flavor-forward, higher-margin dairy categories growing faster than the commodity middle of the case. While that is a genuinely healthy combination, it also creates specific negotiating risks for our producer members as they approach January wholesale contract renewals. Distributors coming into the table after a year of steady production and rising demand tend to assume supply is unconstrained and default the conversation entirely to price and volume. This memo reviews our current operational posture, the 11 membership purchases booked so far for the week totaling $2,029 gross (comprising 8 Individual, 2 Enthusiast, and 1 Small Business purchase, of which 2 are first-time members), and the 4 advocacy actions on dairy pricing reform recorded this week, and outlines what we need to communicate before January paperwork locks.

## What steady numbers hide in renewal discussions

Category-wide data smooths over the operational realities our members face in their caves. A national production figure does not reflect individual cost pressures, feed increases, lost labor, or hot caves in August that compromised a batch. If a producer's output or quality has been variable, letting a distributor assume they are riding a smooth national curve puts the producer at an immediate disadvantage in a renewal discussion. Furthermore, our advocacy team has logged 4 letter campaign actions on dairy pricing reform this week, bringing the annual total in that topic to 28, which underscores how acutely producers feel the gap between commodity indices and their actual cost structures. We need to ensure members pull their own year-end figures before walking into January negotiations rather than letting buyers set the terms.

## Practical checks before contract finalization

Before annual wholesale terms are finalized, producers should verify three practical items. First, volume commitments must match actual production capabilities based on the past year's realities rather than overly optimistic projections. Second, pricing terms tied to moving indices need a careful review to see how those benchmarks have shifted since the last signature. Third, members enrolled in federal margin support programs must verify their enrollments carried over rather than assuming continuity; lapsed enrollments remain one of the most avoidable headaches we observe every January. The Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding these exact questions and can assist members in finding the right comparison points.
