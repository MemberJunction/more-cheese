**Subject:** Membership lapse rates and grace-window handling

This week's operations figures show 909 current members at week end, alongside 15 membership purchases totaling 2,664 USD in gross sales. Among those 15 purchases, 8 were first-time members, distributed as 12 Individual, 2 Enthusiast, and 1 SmallBusiness tier. While new acquisitions remain steady, our ongoing review of members approaching the end of their rolling 12-month period reveals an uptick in failed payments and delayed renewals that require a consistent grace-window policy rather than ad-hoc extensions.

## The renewal friction point

The federation's rolling 12-month membership model means renewals land across every week of the year rather than clustering in a single annual billing cycle. When a credit card fails or an invoice sits unpaid past the expiration date, members frequently request informal grace windows while they sort out cash flow. This is particularly acute for SmallBusiness and Corporate accounts where billing approvals require multiple steps. However, our current tracking does not reliably distinguish between an intentional lapse and a delayed payment, leading to inconsistent treatment across tiers.

Our data shows 62 confirmed orders grossing 11,520 USD and cash captured of 13,294 USD this week, reflecting healthy overall transaction flow. Despite this, membership accounts that slip past their expiration date without an active renewal order risk losing access to member-only benefits, including member pricing on events and education courses. We have received inquiries from members attempting to register for upcoming sessions—such as the upcoming workshop in Querétaro—whose memberships lapsed during the renewal window.

## Alignment with advocacy and education messaging

Our public communication channels this week have emphasized administrative compliance, notably our advocacy guidance on filing federal food-safety attestations and our education guidance on cave airflow systems. The underlying theme across all departments is that administrative friction—whether missing an attestation form or letting a membership lapse—disproportionately harms smaller producers who lack dedicated administrative staff. If our renewal grace window remains opaque, we risk alienating the exact small businesses our outreach efforts are designed to support.

We must ensure that operational procedures match the supportive tone of our public messaging. A member who is actively engaged in federation activities should not find their portal locked simply because an automated renewal notice arrived during a busy production week, such as the seasonal spring milk flush.

## What we need

- Membership & Outreach: review the proposed 30-day grace window framework for lapsed accounts and provide feedback by 29 March.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a standardized notification schedule for failed payments before the next monthly financial close.
- Membership Operations: implement automated flagging for failed transactions to separate genuine lapses from simple payment card expirations.
