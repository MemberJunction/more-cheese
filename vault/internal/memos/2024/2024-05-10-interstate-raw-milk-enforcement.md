**Subject:** Interstate raw-milk enforcement and member guidance

Questions regarding the shipment of raw-milk cheese across state lines arrived in our advocacy inbox twice this week, matching a steady cadence we have observed for months. While the federal framework prohibiting unpasteurized milk in interstate commerce has remained unchanged for decades, and its narrow sixty-day aging exception for raw-milk cheese is equally long-standing, members are increasingly confronting enforcement questions that go beyond the written rules. This memo outlines our current engagement posture, the risks associated with quiet non-enforcement assumptions, and the specific guidance we are providing to creameries.

## The reality of enforcement discretion

Several members have correctly noted that interstate raw-milk shipments occur constantly with minimal visible federal or state interference. However, our advocacy work cannot responsibly build guidance on the premise that informal tolerance today is equivalent to an official rule change. Enforcement discretion is fluid. A member who structures wholesale accounts around an assumption of low enforcement risk becomes exposed the moment agency priorities shift, a competitor files a complaint, or an unrelated public health investigation triggers broader scrutiny. Public health data regarding raw-milk illness outbreaks remains central to regulator conversations, a reality our policy positions must address directly rather than dismiss.

Our current advocacy objective under the interstate cheese shipment banner is not to challenge mandatory pasteurization requirements, but rather to pursue a documented, predictable administrative path for raw-milk cheeses that already satisfy the sixty-day aging and labeling criteria. Members should not be forced to guess at an unwritten enforcement posture to operate a compliant wholesale business.

## Immediate member recommendations

Pending any formal clarification from regulators, our guidance to members shipping across state lines is straightforward and conservative:

- Ensure that state-level permits and microbial testing records are entirely airtight before dispatching any product.
- Verify independently that aging protocols strictly meet or exceed the sixty-day federal threshold and that labels unequivocally state the product is unpasteurized.
- Utilize our Food Safety Committee resources to audit record-keeping practices.
- Provide specific, documented accounts of shipping barriers to our advocacy volunteers, as general expressions of frustration do not carry persuasive weight in legislative or regulatory discussions.

## Capacity and coordination

Managing these inquiries requires close alignment between advocacy, communications, and membership operations. The public-facing blog post published on May 6 addressed these exact friction points, directing members to our committee resources. Membership operations should anticipate an increase in membership inquiries regarding wholesale compliance and direct them to our standardized checklist rather than offering informal legal interpretations.
