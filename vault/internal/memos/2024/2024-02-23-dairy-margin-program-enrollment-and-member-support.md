The federal dairy margin safety-net program enrollment window opens at the end of this month. For farmstead members who milk their own herds, this is the most consequential risk-management decision of their operating year. Last week's federal price forecast data nudged the projected all-milk price for the year closer to twenty-one dollars per hundredweight, even as production forecasts were trimmed. That combination of firmer projected prices and tighter supply growth alters the math for producers choosing their coverage levels. ## What changed for this year's signup

This cycle introduces a specific adjustment that longtime enrollees cannot afford to miss. Producers carrying supplemental production history from earlier iterations of the margin program can now combine it with their regular coverage history in a one-time adjustment. Members who have enrolled in past years without revisiting their baseline coverage are likely underinsuring their herd's actual output. Our advocacy volunteers have been comparing notes on this provision since the rule landed, and it represents a tangible benefit for farmstead operations that have expanded their herds or yields over the last several cycles.

However, a margin program is a balancing act. It acts as affordable insurance during severe margin squeezes, but it becomes a steady out-of-pocket expense in high-price years. With milk prices firming, members must weigh whether maximum coverage makes sense or if a lower tier is a more prudent safeguard against unexpected feed cost spikes. We cannot make that call for them, but we can make sure they know the option to combine historical production exists.
