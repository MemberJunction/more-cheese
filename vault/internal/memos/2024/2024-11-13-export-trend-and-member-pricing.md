**Subject:** Export trends, herd counts, and what we tell members about pricing

This week's public communications emphasize that U.S. cheese exports are running on pace for a record year while the federal dairy herd continues to grow. That headline has brought a wave of phone calls from small and mid-sized creameries asking whether they should expand wholesale capacity or raise prices for the upcoming spring release. We need an aligned internal position so that our committee volunteers and staff office-hours advisors do not give conflicting advice. A macroeconomic export trend is a federation-level tailwind, not an individual business plan.

## Reading the export headline against member reality

Our public messaging correctly notes that strong export demand tightens domestic supplies of hard grating and long-aged wheels, holding up prices through an autumn that might otherwise have felt oversupplied. However, a growing national herd means more milk in the system, which typically softens milk prices before it softens finished cheese prices. Farmstead producers who buy milk on contract or produce their own milk feel those input pressures differently than affineurs who purchase finished blocks.

Members calling our office hours want a simple yes-or-no answer on whether to chase export-adjacent accounts or add wholesale capacity. Our answer must remain grounded in individual margins rather than national export volumes. We know from membership data that 14 new membership purchases were booked this week alone, bringing total current members to 1,065 by the end of the week, with 6 first-time members among them. Those members operate at very different scales, and a blanket recommendation to expand capacity would be reckless for a creamery whose local distribution is already at its margin limit.

## Connecting pricing to the advocacy docket

This pricing anxiety ties directly into our administrative burden. As noted in our advocacy tracking, we recorded 1 testimony action this week concerning raw-milk aging rules, and members are simultaneously wrestling with interstate shipment comment periods. When compliance paperwork triples, a producer's operating margin shrinks just as surely as it does from a shift in milk prices. We must ensure that members evaluating their spring pricing also evaluate the administrative cost of compliance before committing to new distribution channels.

## Capacity and staff coordination

Membership Operations and the Membership & Outreach Committee are currently fielding these gut-check calls during office hours. We do not need additional headcount, but we do need Communications to ensure that future blog posts continue to point members back to individual margin analysis rather than encouraging them to build budgets around macroeconomic export records alone.

## What we need

- Communications: reinforce in upcoming advisories that export records are contextual background, not a substitute for individual margin reviews.
- Advocacy & Standards: keep alignment between our shipment compliance talking points and our pricing guidance so members hear a single consistent message.
- Membership Operations: continue directing office-hours inquiries to review their specific operational margins before taking on new wholesale accounts.
