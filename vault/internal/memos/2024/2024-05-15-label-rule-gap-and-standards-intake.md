**Subject:** Label rule gap for cheese and member intake handling

Following this week's public post on the federal origin-claim rule that was written exclusively for meat and poultry, our advocacy volunteers have received several direct inquiries from members asking why dairy was left entirely unaddressed by the standard. This memo outlines our immediate intake handling for these questions and sets the boundaries for what our committee can promise while the Standards Committee reviews real examples from members.

## The scope of the inquiry

The federal rule finalized in March set a strict definition for voluntary origin claims like "Product of USA" on meat and poultry products, but it left cheese completely outside its jurisdiction. Several of our members who compete directly against imported cheeses marketed with loose origin language have pointed out the unfairness of this discrepancy. A consumer standing at a retail case has no easy way to know that origin terminology means something legally enforceable on ground beef while remaining completely undefined on specialty cheese packages.

We are logging these specific shelf-conversation examples under our "Labeling standards" advocacy topic. As of this week, our total advocacy tracking sits at 134 recorded actions across categories, with labeling standards accounting for 22 of those total actions. However, we cannot treat this as an active regulatory docket change; we are simply gathering real-world instances to present to the relevant dairy oversight authorities when an opening appears.

## Membership intake and spring growth

Concurrently, Membership Operations reports steady intake. Membership purchases for the week of Monday 2024-05-13 total 7 new individual membership purchases, bringing 4 first-time members into the association. Total confirmed orders for the week reached 25, generating a gross order volume of 5669 USD, with cash captured across all payment streams totaling 6518 USD. New members are frequently pairing their joining process with the Cheese Foundations Certificate. As noted in public communications this week, Foundations serves as the baseline infrastructure for all subsequent credentials. We must ensure that staff answering intake questions do not oversell Foundations as an advanced credential, but rather frame it correctly as the prerequisite vocabulary course required before tackling advanced credentials or certification exams.

## Committee alignment and next steps

We must ensure that communications regarding the labeling gap do not imply that ICF is currently lobbying for an immediate text-for-text copy of the meat-and-poultry rule onto dairy. Our members' supply chains and import realities are structurally different, and our policy stance must reflect that nuance.
