**Subject:** Membership tier dispute and month-end invoice reconciliation

This memo outlines an ongoing billing dispute with a Corporate tier member over a renewal invoice, alongside our preliminary month-end reconciliation of orders against cash captured for the week of Monday, January 22 to Sunday, January 28. As our membership base grows toward our year-end target, discrepancies between booked order gross and actual cash collected are surfacing faster than our current reconciliation workflow can categorize them. The specific dispute requires a clear policy decision from the Office of the Chief Executive before we close out January billing.

## The invoice dispute and tier mismatch

Earlier this week, a long-standing Corporate tier member contested their recent renewal invoice, claiming they were billed at the full Corporate rate despite reducing their operating footprint and requesting a switch to the SmallBusiness tier prior to their renewal date. Our database currently shows 45 Corporate members and 162 SmallBusiness members as of year-end, but this specific account was locked under Corporate because the written tier-change request arrived three days after the automated renewal order was generated in our system.

The member is threatening to let their membership lapse entirely unless we credit the difference between the Corporate rate and the SmallBusiness rate. Under our current rules, orders are final once confirmed, and refunds are strictly tied to the product line they reverse. However, forcing this account to pay the higher rate risks a high-profile lapse that could hurt our corporate retention metrics, which currently stand at a solid baseline for the era.

## Month-end order and cash reconciliation

For the current week ending January 28, our preliminary figures show 12 total membership purchases resulting in a membership gross of $3176, driven by 8 Individual, 1 Enthusiast, 2 SmallBusiness, and 1 Corporate membership. Across all product lines, we recorded 44 confirmed orders with a gross of $7882 and zero cancellations. Total cash captured for the association this week reached $28598.

The gap between our confirmed order gross ($7882) and cash captured ($28598) is entirely normal for this point in the month, representing multi-item invoice settlements, prior-period payments clearing, and event registrations. Specifically, we logged 29 event registrations taken for Workshop: Birch Milk Handling and 3 for Workshop: Dairy Blue Styles. None of these free webinars carry registrations or affect order gross, but they do increase administrative traffic across our shared mailboxes. We must ensure our reconciliation ledger accounts for these entries before we publish our monthly financial summary.

## Capacity and administrative friction

Membership Operations is currently managing these reconciliations alongside routine membership renewals and data-quality audits without additional staffing. When invoice disputes require manual intervention by the Chief Executive or legal review, our processing queue backs up. We need a standardized protocol for handling late-notice tier changes so that automated renewals do not generate contentious debt collection conversations with otherwise loyal members.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: make a binding ruling on the Corporate tier dispute by January 30 so we can either issue the credit or close the invoice.
- Advocacy & Standards: review our terms of service regarding automated membership renewal notices and grace windows.
- Membership Operations: complete the full month-end reconciliation of orders against cash captured as soon as the final January payment files clear.
