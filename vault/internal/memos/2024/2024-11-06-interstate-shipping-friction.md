**Subject:** Interstate shipping friction and small-producer compliance

The new testing requirements for lactating dairy cattle moving across state lines have generated significant friction among farmstead members this autumn. While the regulation addresses important herd health concerns, its operational implementation has created severe scheduling bottlenecks. Small-producer members without dedicated compliance staff report losing days of buffer time while awaiting test results, directly threatening their holiday delivery schedules.

## What the current data shows

Our advocacy actions tracker currently records 134 total actions for the year, distributed across letter campaigns, petition signatures, coalition meetings, and formal testimony. Raw-milk aging rules, FSMA small-producer exemptions, and dairy pricing reform represent a major portion of member concern across our active files. However, the immediate complaints arriving this week center exclusively on the turnaround times for pre-movement testing rather than the substantive rules themselves. Members are not objecting to the underlying safety goal; they are objecting to turnaround windows that assume a two-day holding period is harmless during the busiest shipping month of the year.

Furthermore, qualitative feedback gathered through our regional outreach indicates that smaller farmstead operations lack the laboratory access and administrative bandwidth required to process these new documents without disrupting their core creamery work. A test that looks straightforward on paper becomes an insurmountable hurdle when combined with daily milk vats, aging cave maintenance, and packing schedules.

## The holiday shipping crunch and distribution bottlenecks

Demand is climbing as retailers build out gift and holiday cases for the fourth quarter. Members selling direct or through wholesale distribution cannot afford truck delays caused by paperwork pending at state agriculture offices. The friction is concentrated precisely among operations that ship small batches across state borders on tight weekly schedules. If turnaround times do not stabilize immediately, farmstead members risk missing their delivery windows entirely, which would severely impact their fourth-quarter revenue and damage key retail relationships built over years.

Our discussions with distributors suggest that transport logistics are tightening across multiple regions. When a refrigerated truck is forced to wait forty-eight hours for test verification to clear across a state line, the entire local delivery route suffers cascading delays. Distributors are beginning to pass these scheduling costs back to small producers in the form of reduced load priority or surcharge fees, compounding the financial strain on makers who are already operating on narrow margins after a soft first half of the year.

## Engagement strategy and next steps

We are compiling specific timing and cost data to present to state agriculture regulators. General complaints carry little weight; we need exact dates, specific state lines crossed, and actual hours lost in transit. The Membership & Outreach Committee has been asked to funnel member feedback directly to our desk so we can petition for standardized turnaround windows and clearer advance notice when testing rules change.
