**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive

**Date:** 18 September 2024
**Subject:** Staff capacity during the autumn milk price surge and shipment rules review

Following this week's public blog posts on Class III and Class IV milk prices hitting their strongest levels in years alongside member questions regarding interstate shipment paperwork and federal livestock rules, inbound inquiries from small producers have risen noticeably. We are seeing a steady volume of membership orders and payments, but our current staffing model is struggling to handle the concurrent demands of processing fall membership renewals, fielding technical margin queries, and logging member comments for the Standards Committee.

## Current volume and workload pressures

As of the close of the week so far, we have processed 13 confirmed orders generating 2708 gross dollars, alongside 13 new membership purchases totaling 2275 gross dollars, consisting of 8 Individual, 1 Small Business, and 4 Enthusiast tier sign-ups. Total cash captured stands at 3751 dollars, and total membership currently sits at 1034 active members. At the same time, Advocacy & Standards logged a coalition meeting addressing FSMA small-producer exemptions, and the Education team recorded 9 course completions alongside fresh enrollments for upcoming autumn cohorts such as Fresh & Lactic Cheeses starting October 28 and Goat & Sheep Milk Cheesemaking starting November 9.

This administrative volume is running concurrently with a spike in member emails asking whether federal cattle movement rules affect finished cheese shipments or if state-to-state labeling inconsistencies are being addressed. Our analysts are spending significant hours clarifying that live animal testing orders do not apply to cheese vats, while separately trying to catalog shipment friction stories for the Standards Committee.

## The capacity bottleneck

We cannot sustainably maintain this pace across multiple fronts without dropping routine member support tasks. The pricing and margin inquiries triggered by the milk price blog post require personalized responses rather than templated answers, because small creameries are attempting to recalculate make sheets against rising wholesale values. When staff are pulled into answering shipment rule confusion, membership renewal processing delays follow.

## What we need

- Communications: direct inbound email queries regarding shipment paperwork to the published blog guidance before they reach operations staff.
- Advocacy & Standards: consolidate member shipment friction feedback into weekly batches rather than forwarding individual complaints as they arrive.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a temporary re-allocation of administrative support to handle membership processing through the end of the fourth quarter.
