This memo reviews the operational signals from our membership growth and advocacy logs for the week of 11 March 2024. With current total membership standing at 902 members at week end and 12 new membership purchases recorded (grossing 2394 USD), we are seeing two distinct pressures from the field that require cross-departmental coordination: a rising number of smaller producers inquiring about export paperwork, and a growing counter-level interest in formal credentialing that does not match our current exam pipeline.

## Export momentum and small-producer reality

The public commentary on export growth has caught the attention of regional producers who have traditionally shipped only a single pallet annually to domestic distributors. Federal trade circulars point to a low single-digit share trending toward one wheel in twelve leaving the country, particularly into Mexico, Central America, and South America. However, our advocacy and standards tracking shows that members are hitting friction on the administrative side long before their product reaches a port.

During this week alone, we logged an advocacy action concerning testimony on raw-milk aging rules, which intersects directly with the health certificates and certificates of analysis required for cross-border transit. Producers who have never filled out an export form are discovering that state agriculture office signatures and country-of-origin documentation are mandatory gatekeepers. Advocacy & Standards must ensure our guidance reflects the practical hurdles small creameries face when a buyer suggests a container next quarter.

## Counter staff and certification gaps

On the retail side, certification activity remains sparse despite high counter engagement. This week recorded 1 certification enrollment and 1 certification awarded (Food Safety & HACCP Certificate awarded to Milan Holloway), but zero course enrollments taken. Counter managers continue to rely on tenure and informal tasting notes rather than formal study until late in their careers.

As noted in recent member spotlight coverage, mongers often spend years logging cases and handling customer complaints before sitting for exams, discovering too late that the test focuses heavily on shelf life, allergen labeling, and retail judgment rather than blind tasting taxonomy. Education & Credentialing should evaluate whether our introductory offerings, such as the Sensory Evaluation Certificate, are doing enough to bridge the gap for staff who keep detailed notebooks of bad batches and customer feedback but shy away from comprehensive tracks.
