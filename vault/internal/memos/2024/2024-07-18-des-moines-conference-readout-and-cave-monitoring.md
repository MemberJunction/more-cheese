## Heat wave and maturation room risks

Central Iowa temperatures running in the upper 80s and low 90s created immediate shuttle bottlenecks outside the host hotel, validating our badge-pickup contingency of opening at 7 a.m. However, the more acute operational risk is what is happening back home in members' maturation rooms. As noted in association communications this week, extended heat events strain refrigeration compressors that are undersized or running continuously without cycling. Several attendees reported receiving remote alerts about rising humidity and drying rinds. While our Cave Management course addresses thermal load matching, staff need to consider whether our technical advisory channels should issue a brief operational checklist for members returning from Des Moines to struggling cave units.

## CCP testing and competition wing feedback

Running the Certified Cheese Professional track as a full parallel day on Wednesday and Thursday successfully eliminated the bottleneck of separate scheduling, though proctoring resources were stretched thin. Moving the competition entries and judging into a separate hall also prevented the keynote overlap issues seen in prior years, though judges reported that the walk between halls in the July heat was punishing. We will need to factor venue layout and walking distances into site selection for future conferences if summer temperatures remain elevated across the Midwest.
