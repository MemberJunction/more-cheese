As we close out June heading into the summer conference rush in Des Moines, our current membership count stands at 983 at week end. While weekly order volumes show confirmed orders at 86 with gross revenue at 21157 and cash captured at 24523, our membership purchases for the week include 6 total purchases, of which 3 are first-time buyers, all concentrated entirely within the Individual tier. The broader structural challenge requiring executive attention, however, is not new acquisition but the handling of corporate and small business tier renewals that are currently slipping past their expiry dates without an automated safety net.

## The renewal grace-window problem

The root of the friction lies in how we manage membership grace windows when a corporate or small business payment fails or stalls during integration restructuring. As discussed in recent member spotlights regarding corporate acquisitions, parent company onboarding often delays routine dues processing. When a long-standing member experiences a failed payment or delayed renewal authorization, our current system moves them directly into a lapse state without providing an operational grace window that accounts for internal corporate restructuring. Consequently, staff spend manual hours sorting out whether an account is actively negotiating a tier change or has permanently churned.

For instance, corporate accounts operating under new ownership structures frequently require custom invoicing approval chains that exceed our standard 30-day payment window. Applying rigid cutoff rules without coordinating with the account representatives risks alienating members who are actively trying to retain their institutional affiliation. The current year's trajectory shows a total base of 1,051 members by year-end, with 727 retained from prior years against 132 lapsed, and 305 first-time members. If we fail to catch grace-window failures before they hit the hard lapse count, we artificially inflate our attrition numbers during periods of broader industry consolidation.

## Operational bottlenecks and coordination

Membership Operations is currently under capacity constraints as staff split focus between routine member inquiries and supporting the upcoming Annual Conference in Des Moines on 16 July, alongside workshop logistics such as the 17 July Food Safety workshop in Brooklyn and the 24 July webinar on Regenerative Dairy. Because we lack a dedicated billing liaison for transitioned corporate accounts, exception requests land in our general queue, creating delays that frustrate members who are accustomed to immediate service.

We also face discrepancies between membership tiers and the categories of engagement. While Individual memberships represent the entirety of this week's 6 purchases (grossing 996), our larger corporate and small business tiers require personal outreach that our automated renewal notices cannot supply. When a member writes in asking for a grace-window extension while their parent company completes an acquisition audit, we need a standardized protocol rather than ad-hoc approvals that vary by staff member.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a formal 45-day grace window policy for corporate and small business accounts undergoing documented ownership transitions.
- Communications: coordinate with Membership Operations to ensure upcoming newsletters and member advisories direct renewing accounts to our continuity support channel rather than letting lapsed accounts fall through the cracks during integration.
- Membership Operations: establish a dedicated tracking spreadsheet for stalled renewals by Friday to separate administrative payment delays from true churn.
