**To:** Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive; Communications
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 4 October 2024
**Subject:** Membership grace-window review and handling of storm-affected members

This memo addresses an operational conflict that has sharpened over the last four days. Following the severe storm in the southern Appalachians described in our recent communications, several member creameries in western North Carolina and eastern Tennessee have reached out regarding lapsed renewals and failed automated dues payments. Our standard policy enforces a strict 30-day grace window before a membership is moved from current to lapsed. Applying that window literally right now to creameries whose banks are underwater, power is still out, and communication is intermittent risks compounding an operational crisis with an administrative penalty. We need an agreed internal standard for handling accounts in declared disaster areas without breaking our baseline membership tracking.

## What the data shows so far

For the week ending 6 October 2024, our total membership stands at 1043 current members. Membership purchases for the week totaled 13, comprising 8 first-time members and 5 renewals across Corporate (2), Enthusiast (2), and Individual (9) tiers. Gross orders confirmed this week match the 13 purchases at $3674, with 0 cancellations and cash captured at $2262. These figures reflect ordinary processing outside the affected footprint. However, they do not account for the accounts currently held in suspense because automated credit card drafts failed during the power outages last week.

Under normal operating conditions, a failed payment triggers an automated notice sequence, and if unresolved within 30 days, the membership lapses. For members whose physical facilities are currently focused on emergency repairs, feed sourcing, and rebuilding caves, responding to an automated dues notice is not feasible. If we let these accounts lapse automatically, we will see an artificial spike in churn numbers that reflects a natural disaster rather than a loss of engagement with the federation.

## The policy collision

Advocacy & Standards is currently engaging federal liaisons regarding FSMA small-producer exemptions for storm-affected creameries. Meanwhile, Membership Operations is answering calls from the exact same members asking why their portal access is restricted or why their renewal notices are escalating. Telling a member whose cave sat above 70 degrees for four days that their annual membership is suspended over a delayed $175 or $400 dues payment is indefensible.

We are not proposing a permanent waiver of dues, nor are we suggesting we rewrite our core membership status rules. We are proposing an administrative hold: a temporary extension of the grace window from 30 days to 90 days for any member located within the declared disaster footprint, backed by manual verification from our team rather than automated cutoffs.

## Capacity and coordination

Implementing a manual hold requires coordination between our team and Communications. We want to ensure that members who call the office or reach out to the Membership & Outreach Committee receive a consistent answer: their membership remains active while they deal with immediate recovery, and no automated lapse notices will be sent until further notice.

Communications has been handling inbound workload regarding milk prices and storm inquiries, and our team has been fielding direct member calls. We need to align our messaging so that operational flexibility is communicated clearly without creating confusion about broader federation policies.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: confirm whether our proposed 90-day administrative hold aligns with the disaster documentation you are submitting to the regulatory liaison desk.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the temporary suspension of automated lapse notices for accounts in the declared disaster zone.
- Communications: ensure staff answering general inquiries direct storm-affected members with billing questions straight to our team rather than advising them to re-apply from scratch.
