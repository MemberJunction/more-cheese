## What the traceability guidance actually means for small creameries

The federal watchdog report published this month criticizes the food-safety agency for an unfinished enforcement plan, leaving the compliance clock running while regulatory guidance remains ambiguous. Members who expanded wholesale accounts or added new retail channels over the past year may have crossed exemption thresholds without realizing it. However, telling members whether they are exempt is outside our remit; our role is ensuring they understand that clean make sheets and rigorous lot tracking serve as cheap insurance regardless of whether the specific high-risk food rule applies to them.

Advocacy actions this week include one testimony on labeling standards and one coalition meeting on raw-milk aging rules. Both discussions reinforce the same underlying principle: regulators and inspectors evaluate recordkeeping by looking at the batch record. If a creamery's make sheet cannot connect raw milk intake to a finished wheel on short notice, the specific legal exemption under the traceability rule becomes irrelevant once an inspector asks questions.

## Connecting compliance to the cave restock cycle
