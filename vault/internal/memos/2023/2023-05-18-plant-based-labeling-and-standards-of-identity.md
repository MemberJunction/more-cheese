**Subject:** Plant-based labeling guidance and standards of identity

The federal comment period extension on plant-based milk alternatives has prompted several member inquiries regarding our own labeling practices. While the draft guidance from regulators focuses exclusively on drink alternatives such as oat, soy, and almond rather than cheese, it has inadvertently brought standards of identity back into focus for our membership. This memorandum reviews what the current guidance actually touches, where member confusion lies, and how our committee structure should respond without overstating our legal reach.

## What the draft guidance actually covers

Regulators have extended the comment window on plant-based drink alternatives through the summer following requests from both dairy groups and plant-based manufacturers. The draft text addresses naming conventions for liquid plant-based beverages and voluntary nutrient statements. It does not regulate cheese, and nothing in the current text requires our members to alter their packaging or ingredient lists this year.

However, the public discussion has generated a useful secondary effect: members are asking fundamental questions about what terms like "cheese," "milk," and "aged" require under federal definitions. For producers who maintain rigorous internal records—tracking milk sources, exact aging days, and production methods—this is an opportune moment to review wrapper copy against actual production logs. The rules governing standards of identity apply equally to dairy producers, ensuring that claims regarding aging time or milk type must match reality.

## Committee alignment and member inquiries

The Food Safety Committee has been our primary referral point for members working through these questions. While the committee's formal agenda focuses heavily on small-producer exemptions and raw-milk aging rules rather than commercial labeling disputes, the overlap in member concern is significant. Members who call about plant-based labeling often pivot immediately to questions about their own raw-milk aging schedules or compositional standards.

We need to ensure that staff answering these inquiries give consistent guidance: recommend that members audit their labels for factual accuracy regarding aging and milk source, but clarify that no federal mandate requires cheese label redesigns resulting from the plant-based beverage guidance.

## Capacity and next steps

Advocacy and standards inquiries remain manageable, but our bandwidth is constrained by ongoing committee coordination and preparation for upcoming summer workshops, including the Birch competition preparation session scheduled for next month. Communications has published a helpful overview on the blog, but we should make certain that inbound calls are triaged correctly before they reach committee chairs who are already managing heavy spring workloads.
