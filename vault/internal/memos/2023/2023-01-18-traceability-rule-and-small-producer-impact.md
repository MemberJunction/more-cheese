**Subject:** Traceability rule rollout and small-producer impact

The federal traceability rule for certain high-risk foods, published back in November, became effective this week. Nothing changes overnight for our creamery members because the compliance date remains years away, but the effective date starting this week opens the comment and advocacy window. As noted in our public coverage, the Membership & Outreach Committee has opened a letter campaign asking for a clearer small-producer exemption path, specifically aimed at farmstead operations that sell within a single state or a short radius from their vat rooms.

## Why the uniform standard misses small operations

The rule as written applies a uniform recordkeeping standard regardless of production scale. A two-person farmstead tracking a few hundred wheels a month faces the exact same recordkeeping architecture as an industrial plant running thousands of cases a day. This mirrors the structural gap members highlighted during last spring's raw-milk aging threshold discussions. A regulation can be well-intentioned and still land unevenly if it fails to distinguish between a short regional distribution footprint and a national supply chain with multiple hand-offs between packer, distributor, and retailer.

We recorded one coalition meeting this week specifically addressing FSMA small-producer exemptions. Qualitative feedback from members selling through single farmers' markets indicates that building a formal traceability system creates a disproportionate financial and administrative burden for producers who already know every buyer by name. Our advocacy push relies entirely on these specific, personal accounts rather than abstract form letters.

## What we need from member operations

The advocacy page on the member portal now hosts the current draft and a short guide on submitting written testimony. However, staff cannot manufacture individual creamery examples. We need Membership Operations to help surface members operating within tight regional footprints who can describe their exact distribution radius and the estimated cost of maintaining compliance logs.

Without these detailed figures, regulators will default to assuming compliance costs scale linearly with revenue, which is incorrect for small-batch producers. Communications is already pointing members to the portal, but direct outreach from operations will yield the operational anecdotes required for effective testimony before the comment window narrows further.

- Membership Operations: identify three to five regional farmstead members currently selling through direct markets to invite into the testimony drafting pool.
- Communications: maintain the advocacy page links and ensure the portal guide remains accessible.
