This week we published our analysis of the recent wholesale exchange board movement, specifically noting that cheddar blocks pushed past two dollars a pound in August, while federal margin support payments continue to help farm operations facing stubborn feed costs. Because member calls frequently conflate the block price with producer profitability, this memo outlines what our public post can and cannot say, which figures are permitted, and how we handle inquiries regarding forward contracts and global dairy trends.

## What the post may and may not say

The public post relies on the wholesale exchange board numbers and federal margin data. It is permitted to state that cheddar blocks crossed two dollars a pound in August, and that August's income-over-feed margin came in a shade over six dollars a hundredweight. These figures reflect the current market reality without inventing projections.

However, the post must not promise that this price climb signals a guaranteed recovery in wholesale pricing. Global dairy prices continue to slide due to international milk powder surpluses, and domestic demand remains the only reliable floor. We cannot advise members on whether to lock in forward contracts, nor can we use unverified local prices or estimated milk checks not backed by official data sources.

The Advocacy & Standards team has confirmed that interstate cheese shipment questions remain the top inquiry topic, followed closely by raw-milk aging rules. When members inquire about pricing alongside shipping regulations, staff should direct them to the Membership & Outreach Committee to connect with peers who have navigated similar contract terms.
