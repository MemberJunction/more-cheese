This week's helpline volume points directly to a recurring spring friction point: members fielding buyer questions about switching from animal rennet to microbial or plant-based coagulants. With Webinar: Rennet Alternatives 2023 scheduled for April 20, we need to ensure our helpline guidance, our advocacy stance on labeling standards, and the public blog post landing this week are aligned. A coagulant switch is rarely just a recipe tweak; it triggers questions about vegetarian claims, kosher or halal certification, and potential bitterness in aged wheels if make sheets are not adjusted.

## What the helpline is hearing

Small-creamery members are reporting that retail buyers and cooperative partners are pushing for vegetarian-friendly product declarations ahead of the summer season. When our members ask what changes in the vat, the answers are technical and easy to misinterpret. Microbial coagulants operate at higher temperatures and can leave residual enzymatic activity that causes bitterness during affinage if cooking temperatures and wash steps are not modified. Plant-based coagulants—such as cardoon or thistle extracts—behave differently again across styles.

Our advocacy position on labeling standards is straightforward: terms like vegetarian, microbial, and animal-derived must match what is actually in the make sheet and what the supplier certifies. When a member changes coagulants to satisfy one retail buyer's dietary claim, they frequently create an unintentional non-compliance with another certifier or misalign with standard identity-of-interest labeling if the change is not documented in the facility's HACCP prerequisite logs.

The blog post slated for April 14 frames the webinar around practical make-sheet adjustments and chemistry. That is correct, but it must be backed up by consistent advice from the helpline so that members who call in do not receive conflicting counsel from education staff and standards staff.
