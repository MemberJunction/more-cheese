> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT** — International Cheese Federation (ICF), a fictitious trade association created for the MemberJunction More Cheese demonstration environment. All names, figures, events, and decisions are invented.

**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 28 December 2023
**Subject:** Year-end price readout and January margin risks

The federal price reports for December landed softer than November's, and members are already calling to ask what it means for their settlement sheets going into January. Forty-pound cheese blocks slipped, butter slipped further, and Class III and Class IV milk both came in below where they sat a year ago. That softer close caps off a year in which the federal dairy margin safety net paid out in most months, including a summer stretch when margins triggered the catastrophic tier for the first time. This memo reviews our current year-end numbers, the membership inquiries we are fielding, and what we need to coordinate with the public side before the new year.

## What the year-end numbers show

As of the close of this week ending 31 December 2023, our total membership stands at 859 current members. For the week itself, we recorded 10 membership purchases generating $1,620 in gross revenue, comprising 9 Individual tier purchases and 1 Enthusiast tier purchase, with 3 of those being first-time members. Total confirmed orders across all association lines for the week reached 12, with $1,974 in gross orders and zero cancellations. Cash captured for the week reached $9,443. These figures are preliminary until the formal monthly close, but they confirm a quiet holiday week on the transaction side while members focus on year-end financial reviews.

On the broader commercial side, total cheese production for the year finished modestly ahead of 2022, and plants had plenty of milk to keep running through the holidays. For members who sell direct to consumers or through a specialty counter, that abundant supply is background noise. But for members who sell into a co-op, a wholesale contract, or any arrangement where the commodity block price sets the floor, it is the number that dictates their settlement sheets. Early outlook work for 2024 projects feed costs to ease and the milk-to-feed margin to sit meaningfully above parts of 2023, but margin coverage exists precisely because outlooks miss.

## Member inquiries and margin coverage

Members calling this week fall into two camps. Producers whose milk price is set by a formula they do not control are asking whether their feed contracts and coverage elections match what the margin will do in January. We have been directing them to our Membership & Outreach Committee, which can connect them with peers who have walked through the coverage-level math. On the finished-cheese side, creameries selling wheels and shops selling wedges are looking at input costs versus their retail pricing. Several members noted a slow board update let margin erode a little at a time over the past twelve months. We need to ensure our advisory responses remain measured and do not cross into financial or tax advice.

## Capacity and coordination

Staff capacity this week is stretched thin by holiday rotations, but we need to ensure our internal findings align with what the communications team publishes. The blog post on December 26 touched on these exact federal price reports and margin patterns. We must make sure that any member inquiries handled by membership operations or advocacy match the cautious, non-predictive tone of that post.

## What we need

- Communications: maintain the current tone on market posts and avoid taking a predictive stance on January milk prices.
- Advocacy & Standards: prepare guidance notes on the upcoming January federal margin enrollment window for member creameries.
- Membership Operations: complete the preliminary year-end financial reconciliation by Tuesday morning.
