**Subject:** Alpine workshop readout and the case for lineup consolidation

Our Willow Alpine Styles workshop ran on Monday in Greensboro, Vermont, pulling in 134 registrations with 3 refunds, resulting in 129 attendees and 5 no-shows. That room finished just as public interest in specialty case efficiency and farmgate milk costs began surfacing in our member channels. This memo connects what the instructors heard on the ground in Vermont with the wider trend of product-line narrowing reported by specialty producers this month.

## What the Vermont workshop showed

The Greensboro session on tomme and gruyère-style wheels confirmed that members are less interested in adding new recipes and far more concerned with controlling cave drift in existing ones. Instructors reported detailed questions on staged aging and humidity bands. Producers running domestic caves are spending significant labor hours managing rinds when input costs are tight. The discussion returned repeatedly to a central tension: why two cellars running identical processes yield different rinds, and how to stabilize production without expanding the SKU count.

## The specialty case trend

Industry research published this week highlights a quiet narrowing of product lines across specialty cheesemakers. Makers are offering fewer distinct cheeses than they did at the last peak. Farmgate milk pricing from the first weeks of the year points to softer milk prices compared to previous highs, forcing creameries to evaluate the carrying cost of slow-moving inventory. A wheel that ties up cave space and labor for months before it earns its keep is a liability when input cushions thin. Several members calling the office this month asked which existing cheese to retire rather than what new style to introduce.

## Capacity and staff coverage

Running an in-person workshop in Vermont during the first week of February, alongside ongoing membership renewals and advocacy preparation, stretches our small team. The events schedule for the next eight weeks lists only a free webinar on small-room affinage on 23 February, which carries no paid registrations. That lighter event calendar is necessary; without it, post-workshop follow-up and committee support would suffer from staff overlap.
