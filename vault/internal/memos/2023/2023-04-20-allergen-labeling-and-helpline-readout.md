**Subject:** Allergen labeling helpline readout and small-producer support

This week's allergen webinar on sesame labeling has generated more helpline traffic than any other virtual session this quarter. Three and a half months after the federal rule took effect on the first of January, small creameries are still discovering that shared packing lines, seed-dusted rinds, or nearby bakery tenants bring them inside the scope of the regulation. This memo reviews what the helpline is hearing, where members are misinterpreting the guidance, and what educational materials need tightening before the next committee cycle.

## What the helpline is hearing

The questions falling into our queue cluster around two specific misconceptions. First, many producers who sell exclusively through wholesale or small farm stands assumed that retail allergen labeling rules applied only to multi-state distributors or supermarket brands. They are learning that local health inspectors and retail buyers are asking for documented allergen assessments as a standard condition of shelf space, regardless of the creamery's scale. Second, producers are confusing mandatory contains-statements with advisory statements, frequently adding "may contain sesame" as a catch-all disclaimer rather than fixing cross-contact controls on shared packing tables. Regulators have been explicit that advisory language cannot replace proper sanitation, and our helpline advisors are spending hours explaining that distinction.

Another friction point is the paper trail. Auditors are no longer accepting verbal assurances that a supplier's spice blend or coating oil is sesame-free. They want to see the supplier sign-off and the creamery's internal receiving log. For a cheesemaker running a five-day production schedule, maintaining that documentation feels like an administrative burden until the first time an auditor asks for it.

## Committee alignment and public messaging

Our public communication on this topic, including this week's blog post, has emphasized practical compliance over regulatory citations. That approach is correct for our members, but it leaves our committee members exposed when technical questions land on the Standards Committee or Food Safety Committee agendas. The Standards Committee met on 17 April and discussed how draft standards for semi-soft and washed-rind categories must account for ingredient traceability without imposing impossible administrative overhead on small vats. Elena Rodriguez, participating as a committee member, noted that drafting a moisture or handling standard without considering how it interacts with allergen and labeling requirements creates immediate friction in actual production rooms. We need our educational offerings to bridge that gap.

Furthermore, the advocacy side is tracking related discussions around raw-milk aging rules and dairy pricing reform. We recorded an advocacy action this week for our participation in a coalition meeting on dairy pricing and another for testimony on raw-milk aging rules. While pricing reform and labeling are technically separate files, they share a common root: small producers are absorbing higher input costs and tighter regulatory expectations simultaneously, which leaves very little margin for labeling errors or relabeling runs.

## Capacity and next steps

Education & Credentialing reports 28 course enrollments taken this week, along with 3 course completions, and two upcoming cohorts starting on 23 April: the Cut & Wrap Fundamentals (Meadow cohort) and Cave Management (Rind cohort). The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate curriculum covers allergen control, so attendees of this week's webinar should be directed there if they need formal documentation of their training hours. However, our staff capacity to field individual labeling questions is stretched.

We need to ensure that the webinar recordings, which land in the course library within a week, are actively cross-linked from the membership portal so members who missed the live sessions can review the Q&A transcripts without calling the helpline directly.
