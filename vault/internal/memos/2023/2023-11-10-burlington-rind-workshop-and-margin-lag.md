The bloomy-rind workshop ran in Burlington on 8 November with 156 registered, 144 attending, 12 no-shows, and zero refunds. That room was packed, and the conversation stayed on ammonia timing and cave humidity long past the scheduled close. Because our blog this week also touched on how members are experiencing the federal milk-margin recovery, we need to connect what happened on the workshop floor with the financial lag our smaller creameries are reporting. A maker whose margins were squeezed all summer is looking at a bloomy-rind batch with very little tolerance for a spoiled wheel.

## What came out of the Burlington room

The core frustration in Burlington was not recipe failure; it was timing. Makers reported that a bloomy rind transitioning from a mushroomy note to an ammoniated ammonia-taint stage does so on a tighter window than the calendar suggests. The working answer from experienced makers in the room was to shift away from calendar-based aging toward daily sensory checks. That directly supports the material we want to fold into the next Affinage Fundamentals cohort and gives members a concrete reason to look at the Sensory Evaluation Certificate.

Humidity control also dominated the breakout conversations. Smaller caves converted from walk-in coolers are suffering from localized hot spots and draft variance, causing uneven rind development. We need to ensure our technical documentation and upcoming courses address humidity management in non-purpose-built spaces, as the smaller operations in our membership base cannot simply buy new climate-control infrastructure during a tight-margin year.

## The margin lag and what it means for education

Our communications team published a piece this week on the federal milk-margin recovery. While aggregate data shows the margin climbing back above the support trigger since August, the operational reality on the ground is uneven. Members buying milk on monthly contracts absorbed the June and July squeeze immediately and are only feeling the recovery a few cents at a time. Larger operations on long-term contracts are still working through the squeeze.

This lag matters for our education pipeline. When margins are tight, creameries defer discretionary spending on professional development. However, our course completions this week stand at 3, with zero new course enrollments and zero certification enrollments taken during the week itself. While Workshop: Rind Bloomy Rinds drew strong physical attendance in Burlington, and Workshop: Curd Competition Prep has 1 registered for its upcoming December date in Green Bay, the broader conversion from event attendance to paid coursework remains sluggish.

## Capacity and next steps

We cannot treat course marketing as a passive exercise when members are guarding their cash flow. The Burlington session proved that members want practical troubleshooting guidance, but we have to make the bridge from a live workshop to our structured curriculum explicit. We also need to coordinate with Membership Operations to ensure our outreach reflects the contract lag rather than assuming everyone feels the headline margin recovery at the same speed.
