This week we have seen three separate signals from members that touch the cost side of cheesemaking: an unexpected oil market move affecting refrigeration and delivery propane, a first-time export inquiry tangled in quota rules, and the early stages of a federal milk pricing formula review. This memo pulls together what our numbers show so far for the week of 3 April 2023, and where our educational and advocacy work intersects with member questions.

## Membership and orders snapshot for the week

For the week ending 9 April 2023, membership operations recorded 10 membership purchases generating a gross of $1,548 across three Enthusiast tiers and seven Individual tiers, bringing total members at week's end to 704. Among those purchases, seven were first-time members. Confirmed orders across all product lines totaled 17 with a gross of $4,701 and zero cancellations, while cash captured reached $7,605. On the education side, course enrollments taken reached 42, with three completions recorded and zero certification enrollments. Advocacy recorded one coalition meeting regarding FSMA small-producer exemptions, and governance business included the completed Q2 2023 meeting of the Education Committee on 3 April.

## Input costs and margin pressures

The public posts this week correctly highlight that demand for specialty and artisan cheese remains strong, but members are reporting that operating margins are being squeezed from the back of the house. Refrigeration compressors, cave thermostats, and delivery trucks are running on propane and diesel markets that just shifted unexpectedly. A cheesemaker running a single cave cannot simply pass those energy spikes through on a monthly milk check schedule. Our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course remains the correct pointer for members asking how to build energy costs directly into a cost sheet.

## Export hurdles and quota realities

First export orders continue to bring friction. Members stepping outside domestic markets for the first time are discovering that tariff-rate quotas can block a shipment even when a buyer is ready. Mismatched establishment numbers on health certificates or certificates of origin will hold perishable wheels at a border. Our Export Documentation Essentials course covers these exact requirements, and we need to make sure members are aware of it before signing international supply contracts.
