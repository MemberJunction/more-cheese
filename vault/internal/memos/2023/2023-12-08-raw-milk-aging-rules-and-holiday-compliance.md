**Subject:** Raw-milk aging rules and holiday compliance

As the holiday production push reaches its peak and inventory moves off the shelves, our advocacy and standards team has received a steady volume of questions regarding the sixty-day aging requirement for raw-milk cheese sold across state lines. This is the time of year when newer members discover that a batch planned for interstate shipment has to sit in the cave longer than expected, or when recordkeeping gaps collide with state inspector visits. This memo reviews our current advocacy posture, the engagement numbers for the week ending 10 December 2023, and what the public and internal records can and cannot say about food safety.

## What the week's data and advocacy show

For the week of 4 December through 10 December 2023, member engagement on regulatory topics remained active. We recorded 2 petition signatures and 11 letter campaign actions specifically addressing raw-milk aging rules. These actions run parallel to the broader annual totals of 20 petition signatures and 49 letter campaigns across all advocacy topics, which include dairy pricing reform, FSMA small-producer exemptions, interstate cheese shipment, and labeling standards. Members are feeling the pinch of state permit reviews reported in the trade press this fall, making documentation and calibration logs a top priority before inspectors arrive.

We must ensure our public communications do not cross the line into legal or food safety advice. As noted in recent educational outreach, neither a course nor an association memo can determine whether a specific batch is safe to sell. Our role is strictly to point members to the standards required by regulators and the courses—such as *Listeria Control in Small Creameries*—that teach how to build a defensible make sheet and cave log.

## Intersections with the public record and course pipeline

Our public blog post on 6 December walked through the sixty-day aging rule, emphasizing that time, salt, and dropping pH are proxies for controlled conditions rather than magical switches. That post correctly directed members toward our food safety curriculum. Internally, we must ensure that membership operations and communications are aligned on what members are telling us about cave temperature logs and supply-chain documentation.

Furthermore, the education pipeline shows 4 course enrollments taken and 9 course completions this week, alongside 2 certification enrollments and 1 Sensory Evaluation Certificate awarded to Thu Mazur. With the Advanced Sensory Evaluation course starting its Valley cohort on 5 December and Milk Quality & Herd Health starting its Lark cohort on 31 December, members have active avenues to sharpen their technical literacy.

## Capacity and constraints

Advocacy & Standards is currently stretched thin handling year-end inquiries while monitoring state-level regulatory enforcement. We cannot provide individualized audit reviews for member creameries, nor can we verify third-party milk intake records. We must direct members to their state inspectors or private food-safety consultants for specific compliance determinations.
