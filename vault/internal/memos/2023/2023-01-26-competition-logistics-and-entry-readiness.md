**Subject:** Competition logistics, entry deadlines, and scoresheet readiness

As we move through January, preparations for the upcoming competition cycle require immediate alignment across teams. While public messaging correctly focuses on broader trade and food safety issues, our internal calendar is dominated by competition entry deadlines, shipping logistics, and scoresheet standardization. Based on our year-end totals from 2023, where we managed 47 entries across six distinct categories—ranging from Alpine Styles and Soft-Ripened to Washed Rind, Aged Cheddar & Territorials, Blue Veined, and Fresh & Pasta Filata—the administrative and physical intake load is heavier than last season. We cannot afford ambiguity on how cheese samples are received, how scoresheets are formatted, or when the entry window officially closes.

## Entry deadlines and category capacity

The entry deadline is the single hardest constraint in our schedule. Creameries shipping samples from across the regions depend on clear intake windows so their affinage timelines align with judging dates. Last year's data shows strong participation in categories like Blue Veined with 12 entries and Alpine Styles with 10 entries, while smaller categories like Washed Rind and Aged Cheddar & Territorials held steady at 5 entries each. We must ensure our registration portal closes cleanly on the published date. Any exception granted to a late entrant cascades into the receiving room and disrupts the stewarding schedule. Communications must verify that all web copy reflects the exact closing date without soft margins, and Membership Operations must ensure that entering creameries have active memberships so we do not waste days chasing dues status during sample intake.

## Shipping logistics and temperature control

Receiving perishable cheese samples remains our most vulnerable operational point. Last year, several shipments arrived without sufficient refrigerant gel packs or missed the designated courier drop windows, forcing our staging team to scramble. We need a standardized receiving protocol that requires all entrants to use pre-approved insulated shippers and overnight transit tags. The Events Team will issue a mandatory shipping guide by mid-February that details exact receiving dock hours, temperature log requirements, and quarantine procedures for compromised packaging. If a sample arrives out of temperature range, it must be disqualified immediately rather than held for committee review; subjective exceptions compromise the integrity of the judging table.

## Scoresheets and judge calibration

Scoresheet design and judge calibration are tightly linked to our credentialing pipeline, specifically the Competition Judge Accreditation and the Sensory Evaluation Certificate. Judges must evaluate entries across standardized axes: rind development, texture, paste uniformity, and aromatic profile. Last year's medalists—including Fromagerie Saint-Rémille, Pippinvale Dairy Institute, and Damsonwick Creamery Cooperative—benefited from clear, legible scoresheet feedback. However, informal feedback from judges indicated that scoring variance on complex blues and washed rinds was too wide. Education & Credentialing must review the rubric to ensure numerical scores map consistently to descriptive feedback, particularly for nuanced categories like Fresh & Pasta Filata and Soft-Ripened.

## What we need

- Communications: lock the competition entry deadline language across all public and member-facing announcements by 3 February.
- Membership Operations: cross-check all pending competition registrants against active membership rolls to eliminate billing holds.
- Education & Credentialing: provide the finalized judge calibration rubric and scoresheet template to the Events Team by 10 February.
- Everyone: adhere strictly to intake cutoffs; no sample will be accepted off-manifest without prior written sign-off from the Events Team.
