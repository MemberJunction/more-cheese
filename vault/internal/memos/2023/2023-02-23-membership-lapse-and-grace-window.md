**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Communications
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 23 February 2023
**Subject:** Membership lapse rates and the grace-window request policy

This memo addresses the rising volume of grace-window extension requests arriving at the membership desk this month. We closed 2022 with 638 current members and experienced 71 lapsed members from the prior year base, alongside 567 successfully retained members. As we process the winter cycle of 2023, with current membership standing at 682 at the end of this week, several small creameries and individual members have requested extended grace periods for their annual dues payment past the standard window. We need an explicit operational decision on whether to grant these exceptions or enforce strict payment cutoffs.

## The current renewal and lapse pattern

The historical data shows that lapses are not distributed evenly across tiers. Small farmstead producers and individual professional members are the most frequent inquirers when payment processing hits a snag or cash flow tightens in late winter. This week alone, membership purchases totaled 7, comprising 3 first-time buyers and 4 renewals across Corporate, Individual, and Enthusiast tiers, generating a gross of $1,847. Total confirmed orders for the week reached 8 with a gross of $2,409, and total cash captured stood at $5,409. Against these figures, accommodating custom grace windows for accounts past their 12-month renewal deadline introduces tracking overhead that our current team capacity absorbs with difficulty.

## Why informal grace windows fail

In previous quarters, Membership Operations handled late-renewal inquiries on an ad-hoc basis, extending access informally while members scrambled to clear invoices. That informality breaks down when accounts retain member pricing for workshops or competition entries while technically lapsed. A grace window must have a fixed boundary—thirty days past expiration is our operating recommendation—after which member benefits, including discounted entry rates and voting access, must pause until payment clears. Treating every request as a special case creates inconsistent treatment across tiers and leaves us vulnerable to audit discrepancies during our annual close.

## Communications alignment

Communications has been drafting pieces on technical rigor, small-room affinage, and tasting vocabulary this month, but our administrative reality remains anchored to strict billing cycles. If we publish guidance or reminders about member standing, the messaging must align with our actual enforcement threshold. Letting accounts drift for months without payment while still recording them as active distorts our monthly snapshot and misleads the Office of the Chief Executive regarding true retention velocity.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve or reject the standardized 30-day grace window limit for lapsed accounts.
- Membership Operations: implement a hard automated cutoff in the member database when the 30-day window expires.
- Communications: ensure any upcoming renewal reminders reflect the strict cutoff rule without softening our enforcement stance.
