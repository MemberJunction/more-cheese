This week brings two distinct operational pressures that require cross-team coordination. On the advocacy side, federal margin support moved up from its summer low, and members are asking whether their coverage tiers still make sense before the next signup window. On the education side, holiday production ramps are placing heavy demands on facility drains, slicers, and cave staff, making environmental monitoring the primary risk point for the week. This memo records our preliminary figures for the week ending 19 November and sets the workflow between outreach, advocacy, and education.

## What the weekly numbers show so far

Pulling from the week's provisional figures through 19 November, membership purchases total 11, comprising 6 first-time members and 5 renewals. The tier breakdown shows 8 Individual memberships, 2 Enthusiast memberships, and 1 SmallBusiness membership, generating 1,941 USD in gross sales. Advocacy recorded one Coalition Meeting addressing FSMA small-producer exemptions.

## Margin coverage and member inquiries

The federal milk-to-feed margin calculation has climbed for four straight months from its summer trough, moving into single digits per hundredweight by November. While this provides real relief for members enrolled in the catastrophic margin program, the one-year farm bill extension keeps existing programs running without structural changes to feed-cost formulas or coverage tiers. Members calling our office want to know if they should adjust their tier before the next signup. Membership Operations is directing them to review their own books against the current tiers, and where appropriate, we are routing specific, dated examples to Advocacy & Standards for use in state and federal comment dockets.

## Listeria monitoring and holiday volume

Holiday gift-box orders and counter traffic are climbing simultaneously, creating the classic small-creamery risk: environmental monitoring gets skipped just as moisture and foot traffic in the caves reach their annual peak. Trade reporting flagged a raw-milk cheese warning in the mid-Atlantic this November tied to a positive Listeria test, which has prompted several members to ask about our Listeria Control in Small Creameries course. Education & Credentialing confirms that enrollment remains open for the current cohort, and that a quick review of swab maps takes less than an hour for members who took the course earlier in the year.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure any upcoming outreach reminds members to verify their margin coverage tiers and check their environmental swab maps before Thanksgiving week.
- Advocacy & Standards: continue compiling member feedback on feed-cost assumptions and coverage tiers for the upcoming legislative calendar.
- Education & Credentialing: maintain open access to the Listeria Control course portal for creameries ramping up holiday production.
