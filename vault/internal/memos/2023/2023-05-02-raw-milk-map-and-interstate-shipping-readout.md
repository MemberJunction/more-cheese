Following this week's blog post on shifting state raw-milk rules, the Standards Committee has received a fresh wave of member inquiries regarding cross-border shipments and fluid versus cheese exemptions. This memo sets out what our guidance covers, where members continue to trip up, and what we need the communications team to reinforce before the next round of legislative sessions produces more divergent state text.

## The core confusion

The most frequent error we hear on the helpline is a producer assuming that because federal rules permit interstate sales of raw-milk cheese aged at least 60 days at temperatures no warmer than 35°F with unpasteurized labeling, the same carve-out applies to fluid raw milk sold on the farm or at farmers markets. It does not. The federal baseline requires pasteurization for any milk crossing state lines, and the exemption is strictly for cheese. Two Midwestern states passed new direct on-farm raw-milk sale laws this spring, creating two genuinely different regulatory pictures: one requiring monthly pathogen testing and capping herd sizes, the other exempting sales from testing, labeling, and permitting entirely. Members operating near state lines frequently conflate these state-level experiments with federal cheese standards.

## What members are asking the helpline

We are seeing three distinct questions this spring. First, whether a state law exempting fluid sales from local testing also waives the federal 60-day aging requirement for interstate cheese distribution. (It does not.) Second, whether post-sale labeling requirements can be omitted if the milk never leaves the county. (That depends entirely on the state statute, not our federal guidance.) Third, whether the historical 60-day rule itself is open to reinterpretation given modern pathogen studies. Our answer remains firm: until the federal baseline changes, "the science is debated" is not a valid defense before an inspector.

## Capacity and committee alignment

The Standards Committee maintains guidance notes on aging and labeling, but answering these inquiries individually is consuming staff time that should be directed toward our broader advocacy priorities, including the 19 raw-milk aging rules advocacy actions and 22 interstate cheese shipment actions logged so far this year. We need members to read the guidance notes in the portal before calling the helpline.
