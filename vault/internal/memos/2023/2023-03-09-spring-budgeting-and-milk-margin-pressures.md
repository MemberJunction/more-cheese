**Subject:** Spring budgeting and milk margin pressures

The public blog post published on 6 March addressed the thin spread between all-milk prices and feed costs, and members have begun contacting the Membership & Outreach Committee to ask whether this squeeze will impact renewal rates. This memo outlines what our internal figures show so far for the week ending 12 March, and how we should handle inquiries regarding membership renewals and grace periods during this spring budgeting cycle.

## What the membership numbers show

For the week of 6 March to 12 March, we recorded 13 total membership purchases generating $2,703 in gross sales out of $3,383 in total order gross, with 13 confirmed orders and zero cancellations. Cash captured for the week reached $2,650. Among the 13 new membership purchases, 4 were first-time members, while the remainder were renewals. Broken down by tier, the week saw 9 Individual memberships, 3 SmallBusiness memberships, and 1 Enthusiast membership.

While these figures are steady, our year-end baseline shows 690 current members at week end, and we know from historical patterns that a thin-margin quarter tends to show up in our renewal data two quarters later. Smaller producer members without substantial cash reserves are the most vulnerable to letting renewals slip when feed costs compress their margins. We need to be prepared for an increase in requests for grace windows or delayed payment arrangements as these pressures reach their peak.

## Observations on supplier and producer costs

Members operating farmstead creameries face rising feed bills regardless of fluid milk price indices, and those costs must be factored into their wheel pricing to prevent silent margin erosion. Meanwhile, creameries that purchase milk rather than producing it report that suppliers under margin pressure are increasingly seeking mid-contract price adjustments. Advocacy & Standards should monitor whether these supply-side discussions generate formal complaints or requests for intervention before the summer committees convene.

Communications has done well to highlight margin coverage options on the public blog, but we should ensure that staff answering the membership help desk are equipped to walk members through coverage triggers without offering formal financial or legal advice.

## Capacity and risk

Membership Operations is currently managing both the weekly order close and these inbound margin inquiries with existing staffing levels. If renewal lapses rise in Q3 as predicted by our margin-lag model, we will need administrative support to handle grace-window requests before they convert into permanent churn. The Office of the Chief Executive should review whether temporary assistance will be required for the autumn renewal cycle.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure that staff responding to membership inquiries have the updated FAQ on milk margin coverage tiers ready by 15 March.
- Advocacy & Standards: share any incoming feedback on raw-milk aging rules or producer exemptions that might intersect with these spring cost pressures.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note the risk of a soft Q3 renewal season driven by Q1 feed costs, and authorize a review of our grace-window thresholds if lapse rates exceed historical averages.
