**Subject:** Feed costs, regional supply shocks, and small-creamery purchasing

Following our public blog post on regional winter storms and feed availability, we have received inquiries from several creamery members outside the affected areas asking how to handle impending feed-price inflation. This memo summarizes our operational view, connects the feed discussion to our membership data for the week of 13 March 2023, and outlines what we can and cannot offer as guidance.

## What the membership numbers show for the week

For the week of 13 March 2023, we recorded 5 total membership purchases, all in the Individual tier, generating 810 gross dollars in orders. Total cash captured across all operations for the week reached 2,085 dollars. Current total membership stood at 693 members at the close of the week. These figures reflect routine spring acquisition rather than any panic-buying or unusual churn prompted by supply-chain news. Members are calling with questions, but they are not dropping their memberships.

## The lag in feed-price transmission

As noted in our member outreach, feed markets operate on national curves, and regional shortfalls typically take six to eight weeks to manifest in lifted prices for delivered hay, silage, and purchased feed. Creameries that buy rather than grow their feed are entering the exact window where suppliers begin adjusting quotes. Our Membership & Outreach Committee has veteran members available to talk through how prior drought and flood years impacted purchasing agreements, and we should direct inquiring members to those peer connections rather than attempting to forecast commodity pricing ourselves.

## Supply-chain substitution risks

For farmstead producers who grow their own feed, the risk is concentrated in specialty inputs and replacement dairy stock sourced through dealers operating in storm-damaged counties. A quiet substitution or a delayed delivery is more disruptive than an advance phone call. We should advise members to check supplier relationships now rather than waiting for an empty delivery truck.

## What we need

- Communications: maintain alignment between public blog posts and our member-service talking points without overstating the federation's role in commodity pricing.
- Membership & Outreach Committee: keep the veteran contact list updated for members requesting peer-to-peer advisory calls.
- Membership Operations: monitor renewal pacing and first-time purchases through the six-to-eight-week feed-price lag window.
