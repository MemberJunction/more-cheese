**Date:** 3 July 2023
**Subject:** Margin squeeze and milk forecast response

Federal forecasters trimmed the all-milk price outlook for the year in mid-June, and the federal margin-support program has been sitting near the bottom of its coverage tier through the spring months. These figures, published at the start of this week alongside a fresh artisan-industry survey, point directly to the financial pressure our members are carrying as summer operations hit peak volume. This memo reviews what the numbers mean for our membership base, how our course catalog aligns with the squeeze, and what we should and should not publish in response.

## What the data tells us about the membership base

Our current member count stands at 782 as of this week. Membership purchases for the week include 14 total transactions—10 of which are first-time members—contributing $3,000 in gross revenue. The tier breakdown of these purchases shows 4 Enthusiast, 6 Individual, and 4 SmallBusiness memberships. Confirmed orders for the same window total 34 with $11,579 in gross revenue, with no cancellations recorded, and cash captured sits at $10,441.

The drop in projected all-milk prices hits farmstead makers and raw-milk sellers differently than it hits affineurs who buy milk on contract. For members purchasing milk rather than milking their own herd, wholesale forecasts do not translate automatically to loading-dock price cuts. We are seeing a steady stream of questions from newer dairy members regarding the mechanics of the margin-support program and how coverage elections protect against a declining milk check. The Membership & Outreach Committee has fielded these inquiries consistently, confirming that many producers are unsure whether their current elections match their actual exposure.

## The lineup and the margin course

The artisan-industry survey cited this week indicates that specialty and farmstead makers are producing fewer distinct varieties than they did a few years ago, leaning instead into shorter, tightly controlled lineups and direct-to-consumer sales on their own websites. This triage strategy—reducing SKUs to protect cave space and labor—reinforces what we teach in our professional development programs. The federation's Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course covers the exact break-even math required to evaluate core wheels before holiday order sheets arrive in the fall.

Education & Credentialing should ensure that upcoming course promotions emphasize this module. July is the right operational window to run break-even calculations, rather than waiting until October when order volume leaves no room for administrative analysis. Furthermore, we must ensure that any public messaging or blog commentary remains descriptive rather than advisory. We do not set prices or dictate production schedules; our role is to provide the analytical tools and facilitate committee discussions.

## Committee alignment and advocacy focus

Our governance record for the year shows 88 total advocacy actions, led by 49 letter campaigns and 20 petition signatures, with dairy pricing reform and raw-milk aging rules remaining top priorities. The Standards Committee and Membership & Outreach Committee have both addressed member concerns regarding pricing transparency and cost pressures. We should coordinate our committee talking points so that inquiries regarding the federal forecast are answered with consistent references to our existing educational offerings and margin tools rather than speculative market commentary.

Communications has published an initial review of the forecast and the survey findings. We must ensure that subsequent internal updates track how membership renewals respond to these tighter margins as we move through July toward the annual conference in Louisville.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: review promotion schedules for margin and pricing modules to ensure alignment with July planning cycles.
- Advocacy & Standards: compile common questions regarding milk-price forecasts into a brief FAQ for committee volunteers.
- Communications: verify that all public references to milk prices and margins align with federal estimates without offering direct pricing advice.
- Membership Operations: track weekly renewal rates closely to identify any early drop-offs tied to margin pressures.
