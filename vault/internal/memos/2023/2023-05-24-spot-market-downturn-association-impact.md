**Subject:** Spot-market downturn and wholesale membership impact

Wholesale block cheddar has now slid for seven consecutive weeks, touching its lowest spot-market level since June 2021. Barrel cheddar is trading nearly a dollar under its price of a year ago, sitting alongside multi-year lows for nonfat dry milk and dry whey. This memo addresses what these figures mean for our membership base, how our communications should frame the trend without causing panic, and why certain retail comparisons need careful handling across our publishing schedule.

## What the numbers show and where they hit

Our current member count stands at 740 at the end of this week, with 10 membership purchases booked this week totaling 1,827 dollars, divided across 9 Individual tier purchases and 1 SmallBusiness tier purchase. Across the broader portfolio, confirmed orders for the week reached 67 with a gross value of 12,809 dollars and zero cancellations, while cash captured hit 13,076 dollars.

While gross cash flow remains stable, wholesale-focused members selling into commodity-adjacent channels are feeling margin compression. A seven-week slide accumulates faster than daily checks suggest, and makers whose cash reserves are thin may grow nervous. Advocacy & Standards has already noted inquiries regarding small-producer exemptions and pricing reform, and we must ensure our public guidance distinguishes clearly between commodity wholesale pressures and direct-to-consumer farmstead pricing.

## Retail comparisons and case sign guidance

Our public blog posts this week correctly point out that direct sellers face a different competitive set: grocery cases price off wholesale benchmarks, making a nine-dollar supermarket wedge look deceptively attractive next to a fourteen-dollar artisan wheel. However, we must ensure our staff and committee advisors do not accidentally endorse pricing cuts. Members should be steered toward provenance sign-offs and case cards rather than margin-destroying discounting.

Furthermore, our Membership & Outreach Committee should actively direct wholesale-side members toward peer networks if their settlement statements show sustained erosion. A second set of eyes on their numbers can prevent premature inventory dumping.

## Capacity and coordination

As the first real summer heat begins stressing small caves and cooling systems, operational questions are compounding financial ones. Communications is managing the public blog updates on dairy pricing and cave management, while Events Team is tracking upcoming sessions such as the June 8 Export Paperwork webinar and the June 21 Birch Competition Prep workshop in Guelph, which already accounts for 32 registrations taken this week alongside 14 conference registrations for the Louisville event.

We need to ensure our internal data handling reflects that wholesale members are experiencing real stress even while overall federation cash capture remains healthy.

## What we need

- Communications: verify that all public blog posts on wholesale cheddar prices remain descriptive rather than predictive.
- Advocacy & Standards: compile any incoming inquiries on dairy pricing reform for review before next month's committee meetings.
- Membership Operations: flag any SmallBusiness tier accounts showing consecutive renewal hesitation for direct outreach.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that margin compression among wholesale makers may increase demand for hardship-dues flexibility later in the fiscal year.
