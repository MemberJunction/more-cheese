**Subject:** Food Safety and HACCP certification administration and small-producer review

As our public outreach on the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate goes live this week, we need to address several administrative friction points in how these credential files are reviewed. We currently show 1 certification enrollment taken during the week, bringing our running total to 12 certification enrollments for the year, with zero awards so far. While interest in food safety credentials remains steady among small-creamery operators, our review pipeline is frequently bogged down by generic templates that do not reflect farmstead realities.

## The review bottleneck

The HACCP credential is built specifically for small and raw-milk operations rather than scaled-down national plants, but applicants still submit standard industrial templates that assume a multi-shift workforce and dedicated QA personnel. When the Food Safety Committee reviews these submissions, they are forced to spend excessive time marking up plans for twelve-cow farmstead operations where monitoring frequency and environmental controls differ fundamentally from industrial cheese plants. We need a clearer intake filter so applicants understand the expectations before committee review begins.

Raw-milk producers face the steepest climb here. Because raw milk lacks a thermal kill step, the hazard analysis must defend aging times, moisture targets, and strict pH monitoring as primary hurdles. Committee members have had to return multiple plans because the documentation of those hurdles was too thin to survive a real inspection. We cannot treat the review step as a rubber stamp, but we also cannot let administrative delays discourage small makers who are trying to get their HACCP plans approved before summer markets begin.

## Coordination with public messaging

Our communications team published a piece this week highlighting what the credential actually covers, which has driven a slight bump in inquiries. However, incoming questions show that applicants are confused about whether the self-paced modules run concurrently with the written plan. We are currently answering these inquiries individually, which is unsustainable given our staffing levels.

We also need to align our recordkeeping for continuing education units and renewal cycles. Several members who completed earlier credentials have asked whether their annual updates apply to the updated HACCP standards. Advocacy & Standards needs to weigh in on whether we require a supplemental review for legacy holders whose facilities have added raw-milk lines since their initial certification.

## Capacity and next steps

The education team is balancing these certification reviews alongside upcoming course cohorts, including the Rind cohort for Cave Management starting on 23 April and the Meadow cohort for Cut & Wrap Fundamentals on the same day. Credentialing reviews cannot be rushed, but leaving plans in committee queue for weeks damages our relationship with applicants who need their documentation cleared for state regulators.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: review and confirm our guidance on legacy raw-milk facility updates by 7 April.
- Education & Credentialing: draft a pre-submission checklist for applicants that clarifies monitoring frequency requirements before committee review.
- Office of the Chief Executive: acknowledge the committee review backlog in the upcoming QBR prep.
