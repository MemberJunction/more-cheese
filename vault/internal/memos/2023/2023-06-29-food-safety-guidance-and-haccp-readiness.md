**Subject:** Food-safety guidance release and HACCP recertification pressure

Federal food-safety guidance on controlling Listeria monocytogenes in retail delicatessens was published earlier this month, and it has generated a steady volume of member questions through our committee channel. Because many of our artisan creameries operate retail counters, age soft or semi-soft cheeses, or ship cut-to-order wedges, the environmental monitoring practices laid out in the guidance are close enough to their daily operations to require a direct response rather than a casual acknowledgement. This memo reviews what the guidance means for our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate holders and where our educational offerings need to intersect with the questions members are asking.

## Where the guidance meets our credentials

The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate is our shortest-validity credential for a reason. Food-safety practice moves faster than most of what we teach, and a certificate that never expired would stop meaning much within a few years. Holders recertify every two years, and guidance like this month's is exactly the kind of update that recertification is built to catch. We are currently sitting at 1 certification enrollment and 0 awards for the week, which is typical for late June, but we expect an increase in renewal inquiries as members realize their existing HACCP plans need a review against the new federal recommendations.

The free webinar we ran yesterday, Listeria Control 2023, walked through the guidance in plain terms: self-assessment of cutting and slicing surfaces, swabbing frequency, and inspector expectations during tracebacks. It was designed as a gut check rather than a complete training. For members who need to build an environmental monitoring program from scratch rather than perform a one-time review, our longer course, Listeria Control in Small Creameries, goes deeper, walking a cohort through building a sampling map with actual feedback rather than a generic template. The upcoming Birch cohort starting on 19 August is already filling its preparatory window, and we should ensure our communications team aligns any further safety messaging with that start date.

## What members are actually asking

The Food Safety Committee has been fielding questions about whether the new guidance requires immediate capital investment in plant layouts or new equipment. The core message we need to keep reinforcing is that the guidance emphasizes four consistent habits rather than expensive hardware: cleaning and sanitizing food-contact surfaces on a fixed schedule, keeping raw and finished products on separate paths, swabbing hard-to-reach spots rather than easy ones, and writing down results even when they pass. A monitoring log with passing results reads very differently from one with an occasional failure and a documented correction.

However, a cave built into a converted barn has different problem spots than a purpose-built aging room, and generic advice does not replace specific judgment. That is why members are leaning on our committee volunteers for one-on-one sounding boards.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: review the upcoming recertification window for HACCP holders whose credentials expire in Q3, and prepare a targeted reminder highlighting the new guidance.
- Communications: ensure that upcoming blog posts or member briefs direct safety-related inquiries to the Food Safety Committee before members face their next state or federal inspection.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that regulatory inquiries around small-producer exemptions and listeria control are consuming committee bandwidth, and we may need to formalize support for the committee chairs.
