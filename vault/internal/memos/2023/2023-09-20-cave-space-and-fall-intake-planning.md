**Subject:** Cave space and fall intake planning

Every September brings the same wave of member inquiries about full aging caves and limited shelf space before the fall production peak. With milk production numbers coming in higher nationally while farmstead volumes shift toward long-aging wheels, small creameries are facing the usual autumn bottleneck. Our Cave Management course covers the planning needed to prevent a September scramble, but staff have been fielding direct questions this week about whether our course rotation and educational offerings are adequately timed for members hitting capacity issues right now. This memo reviews our current enrollment metrics, the public blog context on affinage and forecasting, and what we need to adjust before winter intake begins.

## What the public posts and member calls reveal

This week's public communications emphasize two intersecting pressures: federal forecasts showing rising cheese price expectations alongside tightening national milk production, and members running out of cave space right as fall production peaks. Members are asking whether their aging logs and rotation schedules are sufficient or if they need to invest in additional temperature and humidity controls. Our Cave Management course walks through the distinction between square footage and proper shelf rotation, noting that traditional aging caves holding steady at 50 to 55 degrees and 85 to 90 percent relative humidity behave differently when crowded with extra inventory. Members using simple spreadsheets to track entry date, target date, and rind condition are finding usable space without spending money on construction.

However, our internal data for the week shows zero new course enrollments and only one course completion so far, alongside 7 membership purchases and 32 confirmed orders totaling 4,560 dollars in gross sales, with cash captured at 6,184 dollars and total current members at 827 by the end of the window. The disconnect between heavy forum traffic on cave management and flat course enrollment numbers suggests that members are looking for immediate troubleshooting advice rather than structured multi-week cohorts during their busiest make days of the year.

## Operational strain and course scheduling

We currently have eight course instances starting across upcoming cohorts, including the Blue Cheese Production course scheduled for the Curd cohort starting on 5 November 2023, and workshops such as the upcoming Granite Sensory workshop in Chicago on 11 October 2023 with 23 registrations taken this week, and the Rind Bloomy Rinds workshop in Burlington on 8 November 2023 with 2 registrations taken this week. These figures are preliminary as the week's totals remain open until Sunday close. The administrative burden of managing workshop registrations while answering technical affinage questions is falling on a very small staff team.

We must ensure that our educational communications do not promise instant consulting or legal advice on food safety compliance thresholds, particularly given the separate federal compliance deadlines approaching in early 2024 that have members asking whether sales thresholds apply to them. Staff must maintain the boundary between federation education and individual business consulting.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: publish a short reference guide on aging-log spreadsheet templates by 29 September to address immediate cave management questions without requiring full course enrollment.
- Membership Operations: cross-reference current member inquiries about cave space with recent membership tier renewals to identify creameries needing technical assistance before winter.
- Events Team: confirm venue logistics for the upcoming Chicago and Burlington workshops and ensure registration capacities align with room humidity tolerances.
- Office of the Chief Executive: acknowledge that shared staff capacity is strained between workshop preparations and fall advisory support.
