The public blog published on 27 September highlighted Listeria Control in Small Creameries as one of our most enrolled courses this year. While the published piece focuses on habits, drainage boundaries, and environmental monitoring programs rather than capital equipment budgets, our internal intake shows a bottleneck in cohort preparation that we need to resolve before the holiday production rush makes finding time difficult for members.

## What the enrollment pipeline shows

Although the course remains a core offering for our educational calendar, the week ending 1 October shows 0 new course enrollments taken and 3 course completions, alongside 0 certification enrollments. Total active membership sits at 830 at week end, with 10 membership purchases grossing $2346 during the week. The disconnect between public interest sparked by the blog and actual registrations indicates that smaller creameries are hesitant to commit staff time to cohort sessions while navigating ongoing regulatory discussions.

Our records confirm that Listeria Control in Small Creameries (Birch cohort) started earlier on 19 August, and the next scheduled course instance across our catalog is Blue Cheese Production (Curd cohort) starting on 5 November. We have no other course cohorts launching immediately this week, which gives us a narrow window to adjust our intake documentation before the Curd cohort opens.

## The intersection with advocacy and compliance

As noted in our advocacy files and the recent 25 September blog post regarding the slipped farm bill deadline, federal rulemaking on FSMA small-producer exemptions and raw-milk aging rules remains parked. Smaller creameries operating under existing thresholds are asking whether their environmental monitoring templates will satisfy state inspectors if federal rules stay stalled. The Listeria course provides the exact written framework members need for their food safety plans, but our intake forms do not currently ask incoming students whether they are responding to an inspector inquiry or building a plan from scratch.

This lack of intake data makes it difficult for the Food Safety Committee to prioritize technical support when positive environmental swabs are reported during course work. We are seeing established creameries enroll quality managers who inherited someone else's plan, alongside newer makers building from zero. Both groups leave with a written environmental monitoring plan scaled to their facility, but their support requirements diverge sharply.

## Capacity and staff constraints

Education and Credentialing is currently managing course completions and supporting the upcoming Granite Sensory workshop scheduled for 11 Chicago with 29 registrations taken this week. With confirmed orders at 41 grossing $6223 and cash captured at $7478, our administrative bandwidth is fully absorbed by workshop logistics and membership processing. We cannot expand cohort sizes without risking the individualized feedback on swab schedules that makes the course valuable.
