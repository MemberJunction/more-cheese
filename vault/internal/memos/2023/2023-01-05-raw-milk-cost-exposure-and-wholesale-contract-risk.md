The drop in January mailbox prices is now moving past trade chatter and into member ledgers. As noted in association communications, early federal order reporting shows January prices stepping down from December levels. While not a collapse, this pullback breaks a run of records and leaves members who locked in fall wholesale contracts exposed to a mismatched cost basis. This memo reviews the structural risks currently facing our creamery members, the committee meetings held this week, and the specific actions we need to take before spring wholesale negotiations close.

## The cost-margin squeeze

Feed costs in the upper Midwest and Northeast have not retreated nearly as fast as raw milk prices have dropped. This creates a severe double squeeze for farmstead operations that must purchase supplemental feed or convert their own harvest. Members report that milk checks and feed invoices are moving in opposite directions relative to historical averages. Creameries that priced spring wholesale deliveries in November based on fourth-quarter milk costs are now finding that their underlying raw milk cost is volatile. If an operation's printed price list does not reflect its true input costs, the margin evaporates before the product even hits the retail case.

Our Food Safety Committee met on 6 January for its Q1 meeting, and while the agenda focused primarily on HACCP cold-chain contingencies, the informal discussion before the call was dominated by margin compression and cash flow planning. Committee members pointed out that small creameries often rely on mental math rather than a strict twelve-month raw-milk-to-wholesale comparison. Without a reconciled ledger, a producer cannot prove whether a contract is profitable until the quarterly tax review, which is too late to adjust pricing.

## Committee activity and regulatory alignment

Beyond Food Safety, our governance calendar is fully engaged for Q1. The Events Committee met on 3 January, followed by the Awards & Competition Committee on 4 January. On the advocacy front, our coalition met to discuss raw-milk aging rules, recording one formal advocacy action this week. These meetings confirm that members are worried about regulatory compliance and market viability simultaneously. When milk prices soften while packaging, energy, and labor costs remain elevated, producers look to the federation for guidance on contract reopening and federal margin coverage enrollment.

It is worth noting that federal margin coverage enrollment closes at the end of January. Several members who produce their own milk have not yet filed, risking the loss of crucial risk-management support for the year. Our role is to ensure our communications and account contacts remind producers of this deadline before the window shuts.
