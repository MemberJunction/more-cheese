**Subject:** Market stand costs, course alignment, and pricing feedback

Farmers market season opened across our footprint this week, bringing a wave of member inquiries about direct-to-consumer pricing. The communications team published a post yesterday addressing folding-table costs, sample loss, and the gap between wholesale sheets and market prices. This memo reviews what the membership data shows for the week so far, how our Direct-to-Consumer course is positioned to handle these inquiries, and where our pricing guidance continues to receive pushback from small producers who feel caught between retail reality and wholesale margins.

## What the week's data shows

As of the close of 30 April 2023, the membership total stands at 719 current members. Membership purchases for the week of 24 April total 5 transactions, all within the Individual tier, generating 810 USD in gross revenue. Confirmed orders across all association lines total 12, with 3,352 USD in gross revenue and zero cancellations. Cash captured for the week reaches 4,890 USD. Event registrations taken this week include 5 for the ICF Annual Conference 2023 in Louisville and 2 for the Birch Competition Prep workshop. Course enrollments and certification enrollments for the week stand at zero.

While conference and workshop registrations continue to trickle in, the dominant operational friction point this week is not event sign-ups but member questions regarding farm-stand pricing and sample-loss accounting. Members are discovering that the traditional wholesale markup formula fails when applied directly to a farmers market tent where labor and tasting wedges must be absorbed entirely by the vendor.

## Course alignment and the Direct-to-Consumer gap

The public post correctly points members toward our Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course, which addresses case layout, sample-loss calculation, and brief counter interactions. However, Education & Credentialing should note that our active course cohort list shows no new enrollments for this specific title during this window. Members are reading the guidance on the blog but are hesitating to enroll, often because they view the course as an optional luxury during a busy spring setup period rather than an immediate operational tool.

We need to determine whether our promotional messaging for direct-to-consumer education is connecting effectively with the small producers who need it most. If members continue to price their market wedges off outdated wholesale sheets, they will face margin compression by June regardless of how strong regional market foot traffic appears.

## Advocacy and standards intersections

Beyond direct sales, our advocacy work on FSMA small-producer exemptions continues to see steady engagement, with 9 letter campaigns and 3 petition signatures recorded this week alongside 1 coalition meeting on interstate cheese shipment. Small producers navigating direct-to-consumer sales channels are frequently the same operations struggling with regulatory paperwork and aging rule compliance. Aligning our educational offerings with our advocacy output remains an ongoing challenge for our team.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: review course promotion for the Direct-to-Consumer curriculum to ensure it links directly from member inquiries regarding market pricing.
- Communications: coordinate with Membership Operations before publishing further pricing commentary to ensure our helpline guidance matches public blog messaging.
- Advocacy & Standards: ensure small-producer exemption messaging accounts for the actual labor costs reported by farm-stand vendors.
