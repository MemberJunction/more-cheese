**Subject:** Milk costs, margin review pacing, and the pricing course pipeline

Federal order milk prices and butter markets are operating at levels not seen in years, and that pressure is reaching our members through wholesale cost increases and unpredictable feed bills. As noted in public association communications this week, specialty cheese makers are facing a tight summer case where everyday commodity sales hold roughly flat while charcuterie and specialty formats absorb higher price tags. This memo reviews how our pricing and education pipeline is responding to those pressures, focusing specifically on cohort pacing and where member record cleanliness intersects with margin recalculations.

## Cohort Pacing and Member Response

The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course launched its Summit cohort on 10 June 2022. Members are enrolling because their historical price sheets are going stale within weeks instead of quarters. When a member contacts membership ops about order discrepancies or disputed invoices tied to sudden wholesale cost adjustments, the underlying issue is rarely a billing error; it is an outdated pricing model that fails to reflect current milk checks. We need to ensure our support team can direct these inquiries straight to the pricing curriculum rather than treating them purely as accounting disputes.

Furthermore, small creamery quality managers are reporting that supplier substitutions made under market pressure require immediate checklist reviews. The parallel start of the Maturation Planning for Small Rooms (Hollow cohort) on 13 June addresses part of this operational risk, but members need integrated guidance on how to adjust margins without losing their retail base.

## The Recordkeeping Seam

Recent internal audits show that membership purchases for the week of 13 June stand at four Individual tier memberships totaling $628, alongside 27 confirmed orders bringing $10060 in gross revenue and $11462 in cash captured. As we process these transactions, our records must clearly distinguish between routine renewals and members who are upgrading or adjusting their tier to access technical courses. If an invoice dispute arises over membership dues or course fees during a period of cash-flow tightness, our response must be swift and reconciled against the generated order lines.

## Capacity and Next Steps

Our education and operations staff are currently managing multiple overlapping cohorts and conference preparations for the Asheville annual conference next month (EVT-2022-CONF, which currently shows 187 registrations). Adding unscheduled billing disputes to this workload risks slowing down response times for active course participants.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: flag any invoice or order disputes where the root cause is margin pressure rather than a transaction error, and route those members toward the Pricing & Margin course resources.
- Communications: ensure future blog posts and member briefs maintain consistency with our preliminary enrollment figures and avoid quoting unverified attendance numbers for upcoming free webinars such as the allergen labeling session on 20 July (EVT-2022-WEB2).
- Education & Credentialing: track attendance and completion milestones closely for the active Summit and Hollow cohorts as they navigate summer milk pricing.
