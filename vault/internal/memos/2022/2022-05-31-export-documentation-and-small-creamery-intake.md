**Subject:** Export documentation uptake and the small creamery intake queue

Dairy export figures released this spring show the highest export value clearing ninety-hundred million for the first time, yet our small and mid-size members report a different reality at the creamery door. While large producers chase container shipments, smaller producers facing tighter domestic case conditions are asking how to evaluate an export-adjacent buyer without getting lost in paperwork. The federation's Export Documentation Essentials course exists for this exact friction, but intake numbers show that smaller operations are hesitating at the threshold.

## What the enrollment numbers tell us

Course enrollment records for the week ending Sunday 2022-06-05 stand at 34 course enrollments taken across the catalog, with 17 course completions and zero certification enrollments. The active course pipeline includes upcoming cohorts such as Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese starting on 2022-06-10 and Maturation Planning for Small Rooms starting on 2022-06-13, both in high demand. However, enrollments in export-focused modules remain soft among farmstead members who assume the documentation burden applies only to industrial-scale brokers.

When we trace inquiries from regional creameries, the hesitation is structural. Members are not refusing the course because they lack interest; they hesitate because a first international shipment requires navigating cross-border quota administration changes and phytosanitary certificates that change underneath them. Without guidance, the paperwork barrier is enough to keep them selling entirely domestically, even when local cheese cases feel squeezed by surplus movement overseas.

## Connecting the public blog to our course messaging

Our public messaging this week emphasizes that record exports tighten domestic cheese cases because surplus cheese heads to containers rather than local wholesale accounts. Members reading that post frequently ask us why their local cases feel tight while national export numbers surge. The answer requires pointing them directly to the paperwork realities covered in our Export Documentation Essentials course.

We need to ensure that our course descriptions explicitly address the broker questions small makers need to ask before committing inventory. If a member signs a first export order without understanding quota rules or customs broker fees, the margin disappears on the first pallet. Staff handling member inquiries should direct small producers to the curriculum rather than treating export readiness as an advanced topic reserved for corporate tiers.

## Capacity and cross-team alignment

Course completions for the week sit at 17, while total confirmed orders reached 40 with a gross of 10319 dollars and cash captured at 9125 dollars. Membership purchases totaled 8, including 3 first-time members, bringing current members at week end to 548. With the annual conference in Asheville approaching on 2022-07-19 and 19 conference registrations taken so far, shared association staff are splitting attention between summer event logistics and course monitoring.

We cannot afford to let course reminder notices slip during the conference buildup. If we want small producers to take advantage of export-adjacent markets instead of feeling squeezed by them, our communication cadence must link domestic market pressures directly to educational offerings.

## What we need

- Communications: include a direct reference to the Export Documentation Essentials curriculum in member advisory replies regarding domestic case tightness.
- Membership Operations: flag any inquiries from small businesses asking about export paperwork and route them to our intake coordinator.
- Education & Credentialing: verify instructor availability for the upcoming Lark cohort of Sensory Foundations starting on 2022-07-14.
