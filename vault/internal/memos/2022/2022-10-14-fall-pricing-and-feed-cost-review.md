**Subject:** Fall pricing, feed cost pressure, and member margin exposure

All-milk prices are running at record highs this fall, and commercial demand for specialty cheese has remained firm through August and September. On the surface, the association's membership numbers reflect a healthy baseline: we closed the week of 10 October with 605 current members, 11 new membership purchases grossing $2,045, and 31 confirmed orders grossing $4,897 against $5,022 in cash captured. Yet our conversations with small producers this month reveal a persistent friction between record milk checks and climbing input costs, specifically alfalfa prices that are eroding margins before wholesale sheets go out for the winter.

## What the ledger and the milk check miss

While individual producers see higher returns on raw milk, the cost of feeding dairy herds through the winter has escalated in tandem. Members who have not locked in their hay contracts early are discovering that the headline milk price is partially offset by a much larger feed bill. When we examine membership purchases this week—five Individual, four Enthusiast, and two SmallBusiness sign-ups—we see steady baseline retention, but members are calling in to ask whether our pricing guidance can account for structural input inflation. Because we do not publish blanket pricing formulas, our role must be to steer them toward internal margin-management resources and peer discussion rather than prescribing retail terms.

## Downstream margin compression

The tension does not stop at the farm gate. Retail buyers and distributors are caught between rising wholesale costs and grocery shoppers who are tightening spending elsewhere. A producer who raises wholesale prices to cover winter feed bills may find that retail partners cannot pass those increases along without losing volume. This disconnect was highlighted in our recent committee meetings, including the Membership & Outreach Committee Q4 meeting on 11 October and the Standards Committee Q4 meeting on 12 October. Committee members report that smaller creameries are revisiting their fall pricing sheets for the first time in several years, realizing that last year's cost base no longer reflects actual production realities.

## Educational and committee support

For members seeking structured help with margin math, the education catalog provides a direct outlet. The *Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese* course, which runs through our cohort model, is specifically designed to build cost bases from feed, labor, and cave time rather than working backward from competitor pricing. Additionally, our upcoming webinar on starter culture basics—scheduled for 30 November—addresses a parallel operational risk: preventing wasted batches of milk at a time when milk ingredients carry higher financial stakes than usual.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure our public blog posts and member touchpoints continue to direct inquirers to the Membership & Outreach Committee for peer-to-peer pricing discussions.
- Education & Credentialing: monitor enrollment numbers for the upcoming winter course cohorts to ensure small producers receive timely margin guidance.
- Advocacy & Standards: maintain awareness of how input costs intersect with federal pricing rules and small-producer exemptions as we prepare Q4 committee follow-ups.
