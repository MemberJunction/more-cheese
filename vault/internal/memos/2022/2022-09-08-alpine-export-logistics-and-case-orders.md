**Subject:** Alpine export logistics, board orders, and case-level inventory

This week's Birch Alpine Styles workshop in Querétaro drew 123 registrations with 118 attending and 5 no-shows, running clean with zero refunds. More importantly, the room included a larger contingent of export-side members than past Alpine sessions. That shift highlights a real operational friction point: members are trying to ship tomme-style and washed-rind wheels across borders while dealing with transit delays that compromise rind quality in unrefrigerated bays. At the same time, retail members are handling a surge in shareable cheese board orders that pull unevenly from case inventory. This memo connects those two conversations and sets what we need to track before the fall rush.

## The export logistics gap

The workshop Q&A spent significant time on cave-to-truck temperature handoffs and border clearance paperwork. When wheels sit in an unconditioned warehouse bay for six hours, the affinage work done in the cave is lost before the shipment reaches the buyer. Members shipping south are finding that federal import quota rules and border licensing updates require much tighter cold-chain documentation than regional runs do. We have heard from distributors that simple temperature log templates are missing from many outbound loads, leading to returned shipments and administrative friction that has nothing to do with the recipe.

## The board order merchandising problem

On the retail side, our recent guidance on shareable cheese boards matches what counter staff are reporting: customers are ordering multi-item boards for six people rather than single wedges. This style of order pulls unevenly from the case, draining firm aged styles and spreadable items simultaneously. Members who haven't built standing board kits are finding themselves improvising during Saturday rushes. Furthermore, online ordering for pre-built boards remains spotty; shops without a simple website ordering option are losing transactions to competitors who make pickup seamless.

## What the weekly numbers show

Our preliminary totals for the week ending 11 September show 9 membership purchases grossing $1,797, with 6 Individual, 1 Enthusiast, and 2 SmallBusiness tiers. Confirmed orders reached 27 grossing $4,379 with zero cancellations, and cash captured stood at $3,862. Course enrollments recorded 18 new sign-ups, and 5 course completions were logged. These figures are preliminary until the weekly close settles.

## What we need

- Events Team: incorporate the export logistics and temperature log template discussion points into next year's Alpine workshop planning.
- Communications: ensure future retail guidance emphasizes simple website pickup options for board kits without recommending full e-commerce overhauls.
- Membership Operations: verify that regional distributor directory contacts are up to date for members asking about border-timing solutions.
