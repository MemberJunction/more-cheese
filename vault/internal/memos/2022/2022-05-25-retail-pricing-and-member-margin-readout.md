**Subject:** Retail pricing, milk cost, and the margin conversation

Retail members have spent the spring dealing with a persistent mismatch between rising wholesale invoices and the prices they can realistically print on counter tags. While retail cheese sales overall have held well above pre-pandemic baselines, the input side has tracked an elevated milk market that shows no sign of returning to historic averages. Members calling the office report that suppliers are passing through freight surcharges and raw-material adjustments on a monthly basis, leaving shop owners to choose between absorbing the compression or risking friction with regulars by adjusting prices more frequently. This memo reviews what our counter members are telling us, how that feeds into our advocacy posture on dairy pricing, and where our communication lines intersect with their daily math.

## What the counter accounts are telling us

The primary friction point for specialty shops is not that demand has dropped—counter traffic is strong, and customers are continuing to ask questions and trade up—but that the cadence of supplier price adjustments has outpaced standard repricing cycles. Members report that waiting for a quarter-end review to adjust wedge prices results in sudden, sharp increases that shock regular shoppers. The shops managing this most effectively are taking an unglamorous approach: smaller, more frequent price bumps paired with direct conversations at the register. Keeping a running log of supplier changes gives counter staff an actual reason to share when a customer asks why a familiar wheel has climbed by a few dollars.

This mirrors the broader industry picture. Dairy input costs are tracking mid-single-digit percentage increases over an already elevated 2021, driven by milk prices that remain high across federal pricing classes. For small creameries making their own product, the squeeze is even more direct. When every class of milk is up substantially year over year, a strong headline price does not automatically translate into a healthy margin if the classification formula itself does not reflect actual operating costs.

## The advocacy and formula connection

Our advocacy work on dairy pricing reform and labeling standards has spent years in the background, but this spring's numbers have brought it back to member inquiries. We recorded two advocacy actions this week alone—one on raw-milk aging rules and one on labeling standards—alongside ongoing engagement on dairy pricing reform. Members asking about margin compression often jump straight from supplier invoices to federal milk marketing orders, wondering why the formulas calculating class prices feel disconnected from their actual ledger reality.

We need to ensure our guidance to members separates what we can fix through policy from what they must manage on their own books. Advocacy can push for formal hearings and review how processors calculate allowances, but it cannot reset a distributor's delivery surcharge tomorrow. The Standards Committee has been compiling member examples to bring to upcoming coalition meetings, and concrete figures from retail and creamery ledgers remain our most useful currency in those discussions.

## Communications and public touchpoints

The public blog and member communications have started touching on retail realities and pricing without getting bogged down in ledger details. We should make sure our published items do not promise simple solutions to a complex structural problem. When members read about counter strategies or pricing formulas, they want validation that the office understands why their invoices look the way they do.

Membership growth this week remains steady, with 5 individual membership purchases totaling 785 dollars booked so far, and total confirmed orders reaching 23 with 4,738 dollars in gross sales and 5,267 dollars in cash captured. Total current members stand at 545. Those figures are preliminary as we approach the end of the week, but they confirm that membership acquisition has not stalled even as operating costs climb.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: continue collecting specific ledger examples from members experiencing margin gaps between market prices and actual payouts.
- Communications: keep referencing retail counter strategies in member briefings without offering financial advice or quoting unverified price thresholds.
- Membership Operations: compile a brief summary of member inquiries regarding pricing and grace windows for the next committee packet.
