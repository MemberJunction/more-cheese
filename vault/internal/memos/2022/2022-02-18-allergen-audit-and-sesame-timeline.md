Public communications has published guidance this week regarding the ninth major food allergen, sesame, which takes effect at the start of next year. While member inquiries are beginning to trickle into the member portal, our internal focus must be on ensuring that our education catalog and documentation templates are ready for members who request a structured review process. We are seeing tighter milk supply conditions this winter, and as the public blog post rightly notes, documentation is frequently the first thing that slides when creamery staff are under operating pressure. Our task is to ensure our course offerings and food safety templates give members a practical baseline before the compliance deadline arrives.

## Reviewing the education pipeline against the new labeling rule

The Allergen Management course includes modules specifically designed to catch unexamined spice blends, co-packer ingredient risks, and supplier spec-sheet drift. However, course enrollments for this week stand at zero, and total course completions for the year so far rest at 1. That is not an immediate crisis, but it means members are largely handling their allergen audits informally rather than through our structured modules. We need Education & Credentialing to verify that our upcoming course cohorts have sufficient marketing support so that members do not leave their label audits to the final weeks of the year.

Furthermore, the Food Safety Committee maintains a set of documentation templates that members frequently request when rebuilding their quality programs. We should verify that these templates explicitly incorporate sesame and trace-allergen verification logs so that members downloading them are fully prepared for the federal rule change.

## The intersection of labeling and interstate shipment

As our advocacy desk frequently observes, labeling questions rarely exist in isolation. Members inquiring about allergen compliance are often the same producers navigating the federal sixty-day aging requirement for interstate raw-milk cheese shipments. If a member discovers an unlisted ingredient or an outdated supplier specification sheet during an allergen audit, it often triggers a wider review of their make records and aging logs. We must ensure our staff are equipped to direct members to the correct committee resources without offering informal legal interpretations of state versus federal jurisdiction.
