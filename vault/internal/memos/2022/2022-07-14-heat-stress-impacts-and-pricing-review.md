**Subject:** Heat stress impacts and margin review pacing

Federal pricing data shows all-milk prices running roughly 30 percent above where they sat a year ago, but feed and cooling costs are eroding farmstead margins faster than the headline check suggests. With a wide swath of the country under an extended heat event this month, members are writing in about feed invoices and milk composition drift. This memo reviews how our pricing guidance and education pipeline align with what members are experiencing right now.

## What the feed and milk numbers are doing

While the all-milk price is up about 30 percent year over year, the cost inputs have moved aggressively in the opposite direction. Corn is running roughly 15 percent higher, soybeans about 10 percent higher, and alfalfa hay as much as 25 percent higher in some markets. For members who milk their own herds, the math is starker than the raw hundredweight price implies. Cows under heat stress eat less, produce less, and require costlier rations, which means the margin on a hundredweight of milk is thinner than last summer's more modest price.

For members who purchase milk rather than milk their own herds, composition drift is showing up in the vats. Sustained heat causes butterfat and protein percentages to drop, leading to slower sets and lower final yields that throw off make schedules. Members tracking intake numbers alongside their make notes are catching the pattern faster, but newer makers are second-guessing their technique when the actual culprit is pasture conditions.

## Education and outreach alignment

Our public blog posts this week cover both the milk check squeeze and the mechanics of heat stress on raw material composition. Education & Credentialing reports that the Milk Quality & Herd Health course covers these exact ration adjustments and composition tracking methods. However, we need to ensure that staff answering member inquiries are pointing them directly to the course and to the feed invoice audit steps we published, rather than treating heat stress purely as an animal welfare topic.

Communications should verify that links from this week's posts land correctly on the course catalog pages. Membership Operations will continue tracking whether member inquiries regarding wholesale pricing adjustments correlate with feed cost jumps.
