**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Communications; Membership Operations
**From:** Events Team
**Date:** 28 July 2022
**Subject:** Staff capacity during conference follow-up and the late-summer event schedule

The Asheville conference wrapped up in Asheville with 187 registered and 166 marked attended, leaving 21 no-shows. That event is behind us, but the administrative load it generated is sitting squarely on the same small group of people who must also run the next workshop. This memo outlines our current capacity constraints and proposes moving a workflow deadline so we do not drop routine operational tasks.

## Where the time is going right now

Conference wrap-up requires reconciling badge scans, sorting notes, processing follow-up inquiries, and assisting Communications with the post-conference recap and competition result tables. At the same time, registrations for upcoming offerings have begun to move. Specifically, the Workshop: Birch Alpine Styles, scheduled for 7 September in Querétaro, picked up 4 registrations this week. While that is a manageable number on its own, managing international workshop logistics alongside post-conference administration is stretching our team thin.

Our historical patterns show that post-event follow-up always takes longer than estimated. When conference follow-up overlaps with early marketing for fall workshops, internal deadlines routinely slip. Last week's events left little margin for error, and if we maintain our current timeline for the September workshop notices, something essential will be rushed or missed.

## The bottleneck in our schedule

The primary friction point is the dual demand of event data reconciliation and member communication. Communications is publishing recap pieces while Membership Operations handles the weekly close figures — showing 10 membership purchases grossing 3253 USD and 14 confirmed orders grossing 4021 USD for the week so far. Because these financial and registration figures require verification across systems, our team is spending hours cross-referencing records instead of preparing the Querétaro workshop materials.

We cannot hire temporary support for a specialized trade association workflow, and reassigning staff from Education & Credentialing is impossible because their own cohorts are active. Therefore, the adjustment must be structural rather than personnel-based.

## Proposed adjustments

To protect association standards and ensure accurate record-keeping, we must adjust our internal delivery dates. We propose shifting the final briefing deadline for the Birch Alpine Styles workshop from early August to mid-August. This buys ten working days of breathing room for the logistics team without disrupting the actual event date or member registration windows.

Furthermore, we need a firm agreement on who owns the attendee follow-up list for no-shows. Leaving it ambiguous means no one acts, or multiple teams duplicate outreach.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the proposed ten-day deadline shift for the Querétaro workshop logistics brief.
- Communications: confirm that moving the briefing window does not conflict with scheduled member newsletter distributions.
- Membership Operations: establish a single-owner workflow for conference attendee reconciliation so the Events Team can focus on workshop venue contracts.
- Everyone: acknowledge that routine inquiries this week will experience a 48-hour response lag while conference reconciliation finishes.
