**To:** Communications; Events Team; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 22 July 2022
**Subject:** Asheville conference readout and the summer milk squeeze

The ICF Annual Conference opened in Asheville on Tuesday, and the week has brought two distinct operational pressures together: a packed event hall that has strained our on-site logistics, and a widening gap between headline milk prices and the actual net margins our members are reporting on advocacy calls. This memo reviews what the numbers and floor reports tell us as the conference closes out, and flags what we need to track before the fall workshop season.

## Asheville conference numbers and capacity

The registration record for the annual conference shows 187 registered, with 166 marked as attended and 21 marked as no-show, and zero refunds. Badge lines at nine in the morning ran longer than anything we have seen since 2019, and the general session room required extra chairs pulled in from the hallway before opening remarks. That turnout confirms the return of in-person attendance for larger operations, but it also exposed our spacing limits.

We should note that the return is uneven. Smaller, owner-operated creameries have been slower to return to in-person events due to staffing and travel constraints. Our Wednesday webinar on allergen labeling had zero paid registrations, which aligns with free webinar patterns but underscores that our virtual and in-person audiences are behaving like two separate constituencies.

## The milk squeeze and member margins

While the conference floor has been upbeat, member conversations this week have centered on the simultaneous pressures of record Class III milk prices and severe summer heat waves. Members on our advocacy and regional calls report that feed, transport, and energy costs are rising right alongside milk prices, leaving net margins flatter than headline figures suggest. Furthermore, sustained heat across major dairy regions is depressing herd yields and dropping butterfat and protein content before volume drops show up in vats.

We need to ensure our communications and education teams do not treat record milk checks as pure good news. Members sourcing locally or importing aged product from European aging facilities facing energy rationing are seeing lead times stretch. We recommend publishing guidance reminding members to check their component yields and build buffer time into fall orders.

## What we need

- Communications: reflect the margin squeeze accurately in upcoming member briefs rather than relying solely on topline price charts.
- Events Team: factor the Asheville spacing constraints and the uneven return of smaller creameries into next year's venue criteria.
- Membership Operations: continue tracking renewal patterns among small business tiers against cash flow reports.
