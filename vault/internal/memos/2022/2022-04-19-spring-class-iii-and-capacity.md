**To:** Communications; Education & Credentialing; Events Team
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 19 April 2022
**Subject:** Spring Class III prices, margin review, and staff capacity

Class III milk prices are running several dollars above last year, and the public blog published yesterday correctly points out that cheese demand is carrying the milk check. For members wholesale-buying milk or selling bulk curd, that top-line strength is real, but it is creating an internal bottleneck on our side. Members are calling the office to ask whether their wholesale pricing contracts and retail margins make sense against feed, packaging, and freight inflation, and those questions are landing on the same small group of staff who are also trying to manage the upcoming course schedule and event preparation.

## What the weekly data shows

For the week of Monday 18 April 2022 so far, we have booked 4 membership purchases totaling $1,034 in gross revenue, split across 2 Individual tier and 2 SmallBusiness tier purchases, with 1 first-time member included. Confirmed orders for the week stand at 24 with zero cancellations, representing $4,274 in gross orders and $4,250 in cash captured. On the education side, course enrollments have jumped to 42 for the week, while course completions sit at zero. Event registrations taken include 18 for the upcoming workshop in Seattle on food safety and 2 for the annual conference in Asheville, while the membership total at week's end stands at 531 members.

The surge in course enrollments—42 in a single week—is directly tied to members asking harder questions about their margins and operational costs. They are not just buying memberships; they are enrolling in courses like *Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese* and the technical make-sheet series because a higher Class III price makes sloppy cost accounting immediately visible on the bottom line.

## Where the operational strain is landing

We cannot service this volume of inquiries and course management without dropping something else. The Education & Credentialing team is managing upcoming cohort starts, including the Brook cohort for Listeria control starting on 28 April and the Alpine cohort for affinage fundamentals starting on 4 May. At the same time, the Events Team is tracking 18 registrations for the Seattle food safety workshop on 13 May and preparing for the annual conference in Asheville.

Communications published a strong piece on milk checks and margins yesterday, but the influx of follow-up questions from members is landing directly on Membership Operations and Education rather than being absorbed by general resources. Members want personalized help running cost-per-wheel models or figuring out if their supply agreements pass through Class III increases cleanly. Our current staffing model does not allow us to offer individual consulting on wholesale contracts without delaying course onboarding notices and workshop logistics.

## What we need

- Communications: direct members asking about wholesale pricing formulas to the *Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese* course rather than forwarding them to Membership Operations for individual answers.
- Education & Credentialing: confirm that the Brook cohort for Listeria control on 28 April is fully staffed for inbound questions so we do not have to pull support from membership operations.
- Events Team: hold off on releasing additional promotional materials for the Asheville conference until we clear the current backlog of course enrollments.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that spring capacity is strained by the combination of high milk prices, active course enrollments, and workshop preparation.
