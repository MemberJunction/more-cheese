Late last month, the federal food-safety regulator published a final rule requiring formal tracking records for fresh, unripened soft cheeses from farm to retail counter. This memo reviews what the rule covers, how our advocacy shaped the final text, and what internal preparations are needed as members begin asking questions on Monday morning. Aged and cave-ripened wheels are exempt from this list, but creameries with mixed product lines must not assume they are sheltered if their operations touch fresh format.

## What the rule covers and what members are asking

The rule establishes key data elements tied to critical tracking events: receiving milk, cheesemaking, packing, and shipping. For members producing soft cheeses like chèvre, ricotta, queso fresco, and cream cheese, this means being able to trace any batch from its milk source to the final delivery. We have already heard from a dozen members this week asking whether this changes their Monday morning routine. It does not immediately alter daily operations, but it requires structured recordkeeping that many small-scale producers currently scatter across informal make sheets and spreadsheets.

The advocacy record ran for more than two years before publication. Our participation emphasized the realities of small-batch operations where a production day equals a lot. That engagement secured flexibility in how lot definitions are applied for smaller producers, even though the broader reporting requirements were finalized largely as drafted.

Members operating mixed farm stands or selling direct-to-consumer alongside cheese production face dual requirements, as shell eggs and certain fresh-cut produce also fall under the rule.
