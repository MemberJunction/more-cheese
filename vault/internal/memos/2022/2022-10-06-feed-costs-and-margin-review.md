**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 6 October 2022
**Subject:** Feed cost pressure, export exposure, and retail pricing review

This week's public blog posts correctly note that record cheese exports and record alfalfa hay prices are landing on producers at the same time, creating a split margin environment. While large wholesale producers with export exposure are seeing their prices propped up by overseas demand, small farmstead and direct-to-consumer operations are absorbing the full weight of high feed bills without an offsetting export-driven revenue bump. This memo examines how our weekly membership figures and order trends reflect this pressure and outlines what we should communicate to members before fall pricing locks.

## What the weekly numbers show

For the week ending 9 October 2022, membership purchases totaled 9 transactions, generating 2,897 USD in gross sales. This brought our total current membership count at week's end to 598 members. Of the 9 purchases, 4 were first-time members, and the tier breakdown ran 7 Individual tier and 2 Corporate tier sign-ups. Confirmed orders reached 11 with a gross value of 3,243 USD, and total cash captured was 3,844 USD with zero cancellations.

While these numbers show steady baseline engagement, they mask the underlying margin stress. The Individual and Corporate sign-ups reflect larger or more established entities rather than small farmstead producers who are currently feeling the tightest margin pinch from feed costs. We need to ensure our outreach recognizes that an industry-wide headline about record exports does not mean every member's balance sheet is healthy.

## The pricing and margin disconnect

The gap between wholesale export strength and direct-to-consumer cost pressures is forcing members to re-evaluate their pricing sheets. Producers who have not reviewed their per-wheel cost since hay prices escalated this summer are operating on squeezed margins. As noted in public commentary, our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course is built to help members reconcile these input costs, but course enrollments this week stood at zero completions and zero enrollments taken, indicating that members are reacting to immediate cash-flow demands rather than long-term planning sessions.

## Committee alignment and food safety exemptions

Compounding the pricing pressure, our Food Safety Committee met on 3 October alongside the Awards & Competition Committee and Events Committee to review Q4 priorities. A major topic remains the small-producer food-safety exemption threshold math, which is currently subject to a federal comment window closing at the end of the month. Members selling through mixed channels face ambiguous rules on whether distributor-assisted farmers market sales count as direct revenue. Advocacy & Standards is circulating draft language to help members submit specific accounts of their sales splits rather than generic form letters.

## What we need

- Communications: Ensure future blog and newsletter framing distinguishes between large wholesale export exposure and small farmstead retail cost pressures.
- Advocacy & Standards: Continue supporting members with draft comment letters on the FSMA small-producer exemption threshold before the end-of-month deadline.
- Membership Operations: Monitor upcoming membership renewals against feed-cost attrition risks, particularly among small farmstead accounts.
