**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Events Team

**Date:** 18 March 2022
**Subject:** Fuel surcharges, freight impact, and distributor route adjustments

This week brought a noticeable spike in member inquiries regarding transport costs, following federal transportation reports putting diesel at $4.849 a gallon for the week ending March 7, with state-level figures climbing even faster in key distribution corridors. While milk checks and dry whey pricing remain firm—with whey values pushing levels not seen since 2007—the freight line on member cost sheets is absorbing those gains. This memo reviews what our data shows so far for the week of March 14 and outlines what we need to communicate before the next round of distributor contracts renews.

## What the week's orders and inquiries show

During the week ending March 20, we recorded 7 membership purchases (gross $1,099), 10 confirmed orders (gross $1,040), 2 cancellations, and total cash captured of $2,036. The week also included 1 event registration taken for the upcoming Brook Food Safety workshop (EVT-2022-W1) and our free webinar on Thursday, March 17, covering whey management (EVT-2022-WEB3), which carried no paid registrations.

While our membership and event numbers remain steady for mid-March, member calls are overwhelmingly focused on logistics. Small creameries that rely on refrigerated transport or run their own delivery vans are finding that a route which made sense in January now operates at a thin margin or a loss. Distributors are beginning to consolidate routes—shifting from twice-weekly to weekly drops or tightening delivery windows—prompting friction with smaller retail accounts that order in lower volume.

## The tension between flat surcharges and route density

Members are split on how to respond. The simplest mechanism is a flat fuel surcharge across all wholesale accounts, but our spot checks indicate this penalizes small, loyal retail customers who are least equipped to absorb it. A better model, demonstrated by some of our distributor members this month, is a complete mapping of delivery routes around customer density rather than historical habit. However, doing so requires time and administrative effort that creamery staff currently lack.

Our course offerings, specifically Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese, directly address this calculation, but members need immediate guidance on contract language regarding fuel surcharge clauses rather than long-term curriculum.

## Capacity and coordination

Membership Operations and Advocacy & Standards are receiving more questions than usual on supply chain costs. We need to ensure our public communications align with the practical realities members face without generating panic about energy costs.

## What we need

- Communications: coordinate with Membership Operations before publishing further logistics advice to ensure we reflect current distributor feedback accurately.
- Advocacy & Standards: review existing committee inquiries on transport regulations to see if guidance on distributor fuel clauses is feasible.
- Events Team: ensure upcoming webinar Q&A sessions, such as the upcoming Water Activity & Shelf Life session in April (EVT-2022-WEB4), allow adequate time for members to discuss operational cost pressures.
