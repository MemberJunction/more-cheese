As spring production ramps up, our member helpline continues to field inquiries regarding the federal sixty-day aging requirement for raw-milk cheese moving in interstate commerce. This memo reviews the scope of our current advocacy engagement, the realities of the aging clock for farmstead producers, and how our standards messaging intersects with member education. With milk supply tight across several regions this quarter, every vat counts, and producers are closely scrutinizing how regulatory constraints impact their shipping calendars and cave utilization.

## The sixty-day rule and advocacy scope

The federal requirement mandating a minimum sixty-day aging period at temperatures not lower than 35 degrees Fahrenheit remains a frequent point of friction for producers with rigorous biosecurity and testing regimes. While the rule serves as a baseline food-safety proxy, it provides zero flexibility for creameries that test raw milk above and beyond statutory minimums. A wheel that passes every microbiological check at day forty still cannot cross a state line. Our Standards Committee has submitted comment text asking regulators to study whether verified testing protocols could eventually support case-by-case exceptions for aged wheels under sixty days. However, food-safety rulemaking moves slowly, and we must be explicit with members that no immediate policy shift is forthcoming.

## Affinage rigor and caving capacity

Concurrently, interest in our Advanced Affinage Certificate has surged as members rebuild full cave programs after period-of-short-staffed operations. Because Alpine-style wheels performed strongly in recent major contests, judging notes on rind development and paste texture are driving creameries to adopt more rigorous caving protocols. This credential tests cave environment management, humidity targets, and evaluated rind assessment. It is designed specifically for programs scaling beyond a single affineur. Linking our advocacy stance on aging to our educational offerings ensures that producers understand that regulatory compliance and cave craftsmanship must advance together.

## Operational coordination

Producers must build the sixty-day aging clock into their production calendar directly from the make date rather than attempting to accelerate shipments.
