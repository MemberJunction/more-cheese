**Subject:** Membership lapse rates, grace windows, and the upcoming renewal cycle

As we approach the end of the year, our active membership count sits at 617 at week end, with 7 membership purchases recorded this week, including 2 first-time joiners and a mix across Individual, Corporate, and Enthusiast tiers yielding 1819 in gross membership sales. However, behind these weekly figures lies a recurring friction point that requires immediate operational policy clarification: member renewals, failed payments, and the rigid enforcement of our grace-window rules. We are currently seeing an uptick in inquiries from commercial and small business members requesting exceptions when automated payment failures push them past their renewal deadline.

## The renewal friction and grace-window exposure

Under our rolling 12-month membership structure, accounts that experience a credit card update failure or cash flow pinch frequently miss their renewal window. While Individual members generally re-enroll without incident—as reflected by our 5 Individual purchases this week—Corporate and SmallBusiness accounts require considerably more lead time. When these larger accounts lapse, their access to federation benefits and member-only pricing abruptly terminates, triggering urgent calls to Membership Operations asking for an informal grace extension.

Our current practice has been reactive rather than systematic. Staff are forced to decide on an ad-hoc basis whether to extend a grace window or insist on a full re-application. This inconsistency risks alienating long-standing members during a period when retention is vital. Furthermore, as noted in recent public commentary regarding winter logistics and sensory training, our members are already navigating seasonal cost pressures and distribution hurdles. Adding rigid administrative cutoffs for payment delays during November and December creates unnecessary friction.

## Data anomalies and historical baselines

It is worth noting for internal record-keeping that historical comparisons remain constrained by our billing history baseline, which begins in 2019. Consequently, first-time member counts and year-over-year retention metrics must be treated with appropriate caveats. For the full year 2022, our system recorded a closing base of 638 members, with 174 first-time members and 454 retained from a prior-year base of 483, against 29 lapses. While the macro trend is positive, individual monthly closeouts frequently reveal that failed automated billing is our single largest controllable source of preventable lapse.

## Capacity and cross-team impact

Membership Operations cannot absorb the manual tracking of grace-window exceptions without dropping our response times on new joiner processing and course enrollment coordination. With course instances such as Maturation Planning for Small Rooms starting on 20 November, and winter webinars scheduled for late November, our administrative bandwidth is fully allocated. We need a clear, codified policy on grace periods before the heavy holiday lapse window opens.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a standardized 30-day grace window for Corporate and SmallBusiness accounts experiencing automated billing failures.
- Advocacy & Standards: review our proposed member-communications template for failed payment notices to ensure tone alignment.
- Membership Operations: implement automated warning notifications 15 days prior to membership expiration starting next month.
