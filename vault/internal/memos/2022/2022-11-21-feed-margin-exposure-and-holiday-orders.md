**To:** Communications; Membership & Outreach Committee; Office of the Chief Executive

**Date:** 21 November 2022
**Subject:** Feed margin exposure and holiday order risks

Retail milk prices have remained near record highs for months, and members buying fluid milk as an input rather than selling it retail are feeling the squeeze from the other direction. The federal Dairy Margin Coverage program has now triggered payments for ten straight months this year. This memo examines how that cost pressure is impacting current membership purchases, holiday wholesale commitments, and what we should communicate before the year turns over.

## The membership and order baseline

As of the close of this week, total current members stand at 619. This week saw 5 membership purchases totaling 1,527 USD in gross orders, with 1,446 USD in cash captured and 0 cancellations. Of those 5 purchases, 2 are first-time members, 4 are Individual tier, and 1 is Corporate. Total confirmed orders for the week match the membership purchases at 5, confirming that non-membership order lines are quiet while holiday production takes precedence.

While gross order volume remains steady, members tell us that the sticker price on fluid milk is only half the story. The underlying feed bill has climbed faster and longer than most producers can pass along in wholesale or retail pricing. For a farmstead creamery buying milk or supplementing its own herd's output, that input cost determines whether a holiday order actually pencils out.

## Wholesale hesitation and holiday contracts

We are hearing from several wholesale producers that retail accounts have been slower than usual to commit to holiday volume this month. Retail buyers are watching their own margins compress from the other direction and are hedging on volume. A member who waits until December to discover a buyer is cutting holiday volume is left with surplus inventory and no room to adjust pricing.

Some members are locking in feed contracts early at a premium to avoid spot market volatility, while others are waiting for spring relief. Neither strategy is guaranteed, but members need to examine their own numbers now rather than defaulting to last year's habits. The Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding these margin inquiries and can facilitate peer comparisons for producers facing similar contract decisions.

## Capacity and committee alignment

With two upcoming webinars scheduled in the next eight weeks—Starter Culture Basics on 2022-11-30 and Cut & Wrap Efficiency on 2023-01-05—staff resources are stretched between routine event logistics and fielding margin concerns. Free webinars carry no paid registrations in our data, so we must be careful not to conflate attendance projections with revenue.

## What we need

- Communications: reflect member feed cost concerns in upcoming outreach without offering financial advice or guaranteeing feed price trends.
- Membership & Outreach Committee: prioritize peer introductions for members rethinking supplier contracts before year-end.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that margin compression is driving member inquiries to our operational teams.
