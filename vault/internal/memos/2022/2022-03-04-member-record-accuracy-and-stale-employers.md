**To:** Communications; Office of the Chief Executive; Advocacy & Standards
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 4 March 2022
**Subject:** Member record accuracy and stale employers

Our current member count sits at 509 at the end of this week, supported by six membership purchases totaling 1123 USD in gross receipts. While renewal processing is holding steady, an internal review of member profiles reveals a persistent data-quality problem that requires immediate correction before the spring mailing cycle. Specifically, a significant block of accounts retain stale employer names or mismatched contact addresses that no longer reflect the members' active operations. This drift between billing records and actual farmstead or creamery affiliations creates friction during renewals and risks misdirecting advocacy communications.

## The scope of the problem

The issue falls into two categories. First, several long-standing members who changed creameries or merged operations over the past two years are still billed under their historical corporate names. Second, a smaller subset of individual and small-business accounts carry bounced mailing addresses or unverified email conduits that fail to deliver governance notices. While these anomalies do not affect total cash captured—which stands at 5598 USD across 30 confirmed orders for the week—they compromise the integrity of our regional committee rosters and make it difficult for members to find accurate peer contacts when seeking advice on seasonal cost pressures, such as the feed contract issues highlighted this quarter.

## Why this matters for spring outreach

As we prepare for the upcoming workshop in Lancaster and upcoming webinars on whey management, accurate organizational data is essential. When the Membership & Outreach Committee attempts to connect a member with a regional peer who has navigated feed cost spikes or cave humidity drift, an outdated employer record dead-ends the referral. Furthermore, duplicate profile entries for members who registered under slightly different business variations cause split history records across our order logs. We cannot effectively advocate for raw-milk aging rules or small-producer exemptions if our own database cannot verify active operational footprints.

## Capacity and next steps

Fixing this requires manual verification rather than a bulk script, because automated matching cannot distinguish between a legitimate DBA change and a lapsed account. Membership Operations will run a targeted audit over the next fourteen days to flag stale employers and reconcile bounced contact points. We ask other functional teams to report any mismatched member affiliations as soon as they encounter them during event registrations or committee correspondence.

## What we need

- Communications: avoid pulling regional member directories directly from unverified profile fields without checking with our team first.
- Advocacy & Standards: share any returned comment-letter mailings immediately so we can update the underlying database records.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that routine data cleanup will occupy staff hours normally allocated to general outreach through the end of March.
