**Subject:** Packaging shortages, wrap substitutions, and margin review

Packaging availability has tightened across the board through the first quarter of 2022. Members calling in this week report partial shipments, extended lead times on cardboard and shrink film, and case vendors unable to commit to firm delivery dates. This memo reviews what we are hearing from the field, how retail buyers are responding to material substitutions, and why this week is an appropriate moment for members to run a quick margin check on their wrap and box costs.

## The current packaging squeeze

The shortage is not confined to a single material; glass, cardboard, and aluminum are all running tight, creating operational friction for small and mid-sized creameries alike. Several members note that delivery timelines for standard shrink bags and case cartons have stretched out by weeks rather than days. For a farmstead operation running close to its cash cycle and storage capacity, a prolonged delay on packaging materials is a production bottleneck, not a minor inconvenience.

This friction is compounding an already strained input picture. High feed costs, tightening cow numbers in major regions, and broader cost inflation are squeezing producer margins. When box and wrap suppliers begin rationing order sizes or pushing out lead times, creameries are forced into a scramble for alternative vendors.

## Retail flexibility and the second-column fix

On a more encouraging note, retailer tolerance for packaging and label substitutions has been higher this spring than in previous seasons. Buyers are accepting plain kraft wraps and alternative label stock without penalizing sell-through, provided the cheese inside remains consistent. Members who have tested these substitutions report that the change has largely gone unremarked by retail customers.

To mitigate supply whiplash, several distributors and producers are adopting a two-supplier approach — maintaining a secondary, smaller vendor on standby even if unit costs are slightly higher. Sharing packaging truckloads or pooling minimum orders with neighboring creameries is also helping smaller operations secure cardboard without absorbing punitive freight rates.

## Margin review and committee action

A ten-cent increase in wrap or box cost per unit is easy to overlook during a busy sales week, but it accumulates across a production run in the exact same manner as a milk-price adjustment. With input costs shifting across packaging and ingredients, members should use this week to re-run their margin calculations. Our Membership & Outreach Committee and Food Safety Committee have been compiling vendor lists and shelf-life documentation notes to assist members navigating these substitutions.
