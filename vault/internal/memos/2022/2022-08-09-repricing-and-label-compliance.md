**Subject:** Repricing and net-weight label compliance under the late-summer cost squeeze

This week's public blog posts correctly observe that while Class III milk has slid off its spring peak and cheddar blocks have leveled out, packaging and energy costs have not followed. Our members are caught between a softening headline market and raw-material invoices that remain up between 15 and 57 percent over last year. In response, several producers are quietly trimming retail wedge sizes or shrinking wheel targets rather than raising prices outright. That business decision is triggering a second operational problem that our committee inbox has fielded repeatedly this month: members shipping resized product with outdated net-weight labels printed on the wrapper.

## The label compliance exposure

Net-weight labeling standards are not optional house styling. The declared weight on a package must reflect the actual weight in it, full stop, regardless of whether the recipe, the mold, or the price changed since the last print run. When a member trims a retail wedge by an ounce or two to absorb rising packaging costs, continuing to ship with the old weight printed on the wrapper is a labeling violation, not a pricing adjustment. Inspectors flag these discrepancies before customers notice, and wholesale buyers are beginning to ask pointed questions when deliveries do not match specifications.

The Membership & Outreach Committee has noted that members selling into regional grocery are particularly vulnerable because their packaging runs on quarterly cycles. A mid-cycle portion change that fails to update the label proof results in non-compliant shipments before anyone checks the math.

## Aligning pricing with our education and standards work

This squeeze connects directly to two existing federation offerings that are seeing heavy engagement this cycle. First, Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese is currently running a larger-than-usual cohort because members are realizing that pricing off the milk market alone is eroding their margins on packaging and cave energy. Second, the Standards Committee is compiling a reference document on current net-weight labeling requirements for hand-cut and scaled wedges, which we expect to publish before the next quarterly newsletter.

We need to ensure these two efforts do not operate in silos. A member who takes the pricing course learns how to calculate a true cost-per-wheel including packaging, but if they resize the wheel accordingly and neglect the label, they create a regulatory liability.
