**Subject:** Price sheet audit and input cost drift under the current cost squeeze

This memo reviews the operational and member-facing implications of input cost drift as outlined in recent public commentary on specialty cheese margins. While Class III milk prices and wholesale cheddar blocks have eased off their spring highs, members across the artisan and farmstead tiers report that packaging, energy, and freight inputs have not followed the same downward trajectory. Flat overhead assumptions built into multi-year price formulas are currently absorbing margin that neither milk cost indices nor headline market summaries capture. This creates a risk of silent margin erosion for small producers who price off a single commodity benchmark.

## The gap between milk cost and total inputs

Our education and outreach programs have tracked a recurring pattern among producers in cohorts such as Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese. When members separate milk cost from packaging, propane, cave climate control electricity, and freight surcharges, they routinely find a margin gap in the range of 15 to 30 percent year over year. A two-person farmstead operation recently analyzed two years of invoices and discovered that milk was their most stable input, whereas packaging and distributor fuel surcharges had quietly become fixed liabilities without an itemized adjustment on their wholesale price sheets.

This discrepancy matters because public commentary often treats commodity milk prices as a proxy for the entire cost of production. When the milk number falls, retail buyers and distributors expect wholesale prices to soften in tandem, even when creamery utility and film costs continue to rise. Producers who lack a dedicated pricing staff are left absorbing the difference.

## Advocacy and member support alignment

Our advocacy work on interstate cheese shipment rules and labeling standards relies on producers having rigorous batch and cost records. The same discipline required to document pH curves and moisture data for raw-milk aging compliance is required to defend a specialty price sheet against flat overhead assumptions. Members who cannot itemize their packaging and utility drift cannot justify a differentiated wholesale price to regional distributors.

We should ensure that our member services team does not direct producers toward generic pricing templates that rely solely on milk indices. Instead, guidance should point toward the Association's structured margin review exercises, which prompt members to sort invoices into three distinct columns: milk, packaging and energy, and everything else.

## Capacity and messaging constraints

Communications has published helpful analysis on margin reviews this month, but our internal operations must ensure that support requests from members do not overwhelm our part-time committee advisors. The Membership & Outreach Committee is currently handling heavy member inquiry volume regarding federal raw-milk aging and interstate shipment comment periods. Adding a surge in pricing inquiries without additional operational support will create bottlenecks.
