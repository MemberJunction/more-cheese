**Subject:** Interstate direct-to-consumer shipping compliance and labeling review

Direct-to-consumer cheese sales have continued to climb through the middle of 2022, and our advocacy file reflects a corresponding rise in member inquiries regarding multi-state shipping compliance. While members have largely mastered the core federal regulations such as the sixty-day aging rule for raw milk, the state-by-state patchwork governing refrigerated food disclosures, minimum font sizes, hold-temperature guidance, and out-of-state facility registrations has created unexpected legal and operational exposure. This memo summarizes our current advocacy posture following our recent testimony and coalition conversations on labeling standards.

## The current state of state-level rules

The primary friction point for small and medium producers is that a product label approved by a creamery's home state inspector frequently fails to satisfy the specific disclosure requirements of a destination state. Several jurisdictions now mandate explicit hold-temperature instructions, unique cooling guidance, and specific out-of-state direct shipper license registrations before a package may be delivered to a residential mailbox. Members shipping more than a couple of regional mail orders per month are running unmapped exposure if their container disclosures and facility filing statuses do not match the receiving state's regulatory expectations.

Packaging integrity compounds these regulatory risks. Insulated cartons, foam liners, and gel packs engineered for a one-day regional delivery often degrade during a three-day cross-country transit, exposing producers to simultaneous labeling and spoilage liabilities. Our advocacy engagement with allied agricultural groups focuses on pushing for a uniform national disclosure standard for perishable direct-to-consumer shipments, though rulemaking of this nature operates on a multi-year horizon rather than a seasonal one.

## Committee findings and advocacy pipeline

The Membership & Outreach Committee has begun collecting specific case studies from creameries that encountered conflicting state labeling rules during the first half of the year. These anonymized examples form the core of our ongoing coalition discussions with other specialty food trade associations. Regulators and legislative committees require documented operational friction rather than generalized complaints to justify rule modernization. Members who have already navigated out-of-state registration hurdles or revised their cold-pack specifications provide the precise empirical backing our advocacy team needs to make a compelling case.

## Staffing and resource constraints

Handling these inbound compliance inquiries is consuming an increasing share of our advocacy bandwidth just as conference preparation ramps up for Asheville in July. We are seeing more members contact the office for guidance after receiving a warning letter from an out-of-state agricultural department rather than before launching their direct-to-consumer programs. Our current advisory capacity is stretched across dairy pricing reform, raw-milk aging rules, and small-producer exemptions, leaving little room for individual label audits.
