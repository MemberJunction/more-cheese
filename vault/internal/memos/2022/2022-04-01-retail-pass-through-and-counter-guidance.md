**To:** Communications; Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 1 April 2022
**Subject:** Retail pass-through, counter guidance, and the inflation response

Grocery inflation has dominated our member inquiries this spring. As wholesale invoices climb for the second and third time since January, retail members are facing difficult choices at the counter. While our public blog highlights members who are successfully explaining price adjustments with short shelf cards, our internal data paints a more mixed picture. This memo reviews what our sales and membership numbers tell us so far, how our retail members are absorbing cost pressures, and what guidance we should offer across our channels without overstepping our remit.

## What the numbers show so far

For the week ending 3 April 2022, membership purchases totaled 8, bringing our current member count to 523 at week's end. Gross orders for the week reached 4,044 USD across 16 confirmed orders, with zero cancellations. Cash captured for the same period stood at 4,104 USD. Looking across the broader year, our 2022 totals include 748 membership purchases and 1,561 confirmed orders against 337,785 USD in gross sales.

These figures demonstrate a stable core of supporters, but they mask the localized margin squeeze happening at independent cheese counters. Retailers report that while unit volume in specialty wedges has held up—largely because households view a good wedge as an affordable indulgence rather than a routine staple—their margins are eroding rapidly when they absorb wholesale cost increases in silence.

## The counter card strategy and its limits

Recent reports from our Membership & Outreach Committee indicate that when retailers raise prices by the exact amount of their wholesale increase and place a simple, plain-language card next to the cheese explaining the supplier-side drivers (such as feed and freight), customer pushback is far milder than expected. Regulars appreciate the transparency. However, this approach relies heavily on existing trust between the monger and the shopper. For shops with lower foot traffic or a highly price-sensitive customer base, passing through increases openly can still trigger a drop in movement for mid-tier cheeses, even if everyday basics and high-end showpieces hold steady.

We must ensure our guidance remains practical. A price increase tied to a real input cost does not need an apology, but it does require enough context that customers do not fill in the worst assumptions by default. We should continue collecting notes from retailers who have navigated this transition successfully and make those templates available through our member briefings.

## Capacity and coordination

Managing these inquiries is placing a heavier burden on our outreach teams just as we ramp up spring workshop registrations. Specifically, we recorded 6 registrations for the ICF Annual Conference 2022 and 2 registrations for the Brook Food Safety workshop this week. Coordinating support for retailers while preparing for upcoming educational sessions requires tight alignment between Communications and Membership Operations.

## What we need

- Communications: Ensure our public messaging highlights practical framing, such as seasonal pairings and monger storytelling, rather than flat price discussions.
- Advocacy & Standards: Continue gathering data on small-producer input costs to inform our ongoing committee notes.
- Membership Operations: Maintain a shared log of retailer pass-through strategies to assist members calling in with pricing questions.
