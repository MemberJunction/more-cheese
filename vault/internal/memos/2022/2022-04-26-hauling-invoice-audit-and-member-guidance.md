**Subject:** Hauling invoice audit and member guidance

Members have forwarded several hauling invoices this month bearing itemized fuel surcharges for the first time in their operating memory. While milk checks look robust on paper due to strong spring prices, these new surcharge lines erode the operating spread faster than producers can reprice finished wheels. This memorandum reviews what our data shows about current member inquiries, evaluates the broader operational impact across small and mid-sized farmstead dairies, and outlines what guidance we can safely provide without straying into business-advice territory.

## What the member inquiries show

The pinch is concentrated among small and mid-sized farmstead operations rather than large-scale corporate members who command flat hauling rates through volume. Our committee calls this week confirmed that fuel costs are compounding existing feed pressures—hay and grain markets remain under global strain. When a member recalculates cost-per-wheel after factoring in the new hauling line, the margin vanishes entirely. Retail buyers are already asking unprompted why prices are shifting, making this a critical window for transparent communication between producers and their customers.

Furthermore, regional milk marketing orders complicate the picture further. Operations drawing milk in regions set up primarily for fluid bottling face different baseline dynamics than those in manufacturing-focused regions. When a hauling surcharge hits on top of existing regional disparities, the cumulative effect on a small creamery's cost sheet is severe. We have been logging these accounts carefully to support our ongoing advocacy discussions regarding federal milk pricing formulas.

## Limits of federation guidance

We cannot dictate what members charge for their cheese. Pricing is an individual business decision that every maker must evaluate using their own production numbers. However, we can and should encourage members to audit their freight and hauling lines immediately rather than relying on last quarter's cost sheet. The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course covers exactly this line-by-line accounting, and we should direct inquiries there. We must also coordinate closely with the blog team to ensure our public communications match the practical realities our analysts are seeing on the ground without creating unrealistic expectations.
