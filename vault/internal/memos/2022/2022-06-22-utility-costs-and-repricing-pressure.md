**Subject:** Utility cost pressures, cold cave accounting, and repricing support

Recent member outreach indicates that rising electricity costs for aging rooms have displaced feed prices as the most urgent operational concern for members holding inventory in cold storage. Holding aging rooms between 52 and 55 degrees with high humidity is driving monthly utility bills upward in older buildings where compressor efficiency was never optimized. This memo outlines the operational impact, our response regarding pricing guidance, and the data limits we must respect in our public communications.

## The aging room utility burden

Unlike milk checks or published feed contracts, electric rate adjustments arrive without advance notice on commercial utility bills. Members running walk-in caves for hard cheeses alongside wet caves for washed-rind wheels are essentially operating two heavy climate systems on top of standard creamery loads. Because these wheels are committed to the cave months before sale, an unplanned jump in holding costs acts as a direct margin squeeze that cannot be recovered until the inventory clears.

We do not currently possess aggregate numbers on the exact percentage increase across our 553 members as of this week. We have deliberately avoided guessing at a macro figure in our public messaging, choosing instead to focus on practical cost-accounting recommendations through our committee channels.

## Accounting and repricing guidance

Our Membership & Outreach Committee has been advising members to treat utility costs for aging rooms as a distinct line item in cost accounting rather than burying them in general overhead. Creameries that adopted this practice in the spring found it easier to justify price increases to wholesale buyers before shipping nine-month wheels. We are actively connecting members who need guidance with peers who have successfully navigated wholesale buyer conversations regarding long-aged inventory.

This ties directly into our broader educational pipeline. With seven new course enrollments and two completions recorded this week, and the upcoming Summit cohort for Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese underway, members are actively looking for concrete margin modeling tools rather than general commentary.

## Capacity and coordination

As we approach the Annual Conference in Asheville next month—which has already logged 15 new registrations this week, bringing our total registered standing to 187—staff bandwidth is split between conference logistics and member inquiries. We need to ensure our communications team and our committee volunteers are aligned so that we do not promise aggregate cost surveys we cannot substantiate with current data.
