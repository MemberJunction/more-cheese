**Subject:** Annual conference early registration readout and venue considerations

Early registration for the ICF Annual Conference opened this week, and we need to align on what figures we are tracking and how we discuss lodging costs in our communications. The conference returns to an entirely in-person program in Asheville this July, marking our first full-scale gathering without a virtual tier since before the pandemic. As registration activity picks up, early indicators suggest strong interest, but we are also monitoring cost pressures that could affect group attendance.

## Registration pace and early figures

As of this week, the order database shows 4 event registrations taken for the ICF Annual Conference 2022. Total lifetime registrations for the event currently stand at 187, with 0 refunds and 21 no-shows recorded in historical projections. These figures are preliminary for the current week and reflect only the initial wave of bookings following the public announcement. We expect the usual surge closer to the early bird deadline, but creamery managers are telling us they are coordinating travel much earlier than last year.

## Lodging costs and industry trends

Hotel rates near the Asheville venue have climbed faster than in previous years. Industry forecasts indicate that lodging prices will continue to rise through the back half of 2022, a trend that is already impacting multi-person creamery delegations. Members who previously booked double rooms or split larger suites are having to reconfigure their arrangements from scratch. Communications should ensure that our public messaging encourages early hotel block bookings so members are not caught off guard by escalating room rates.

## Session capacity and venue constraints

Returning to an in-person venue means we lose the infinite capacity of a virtual chat window. Session rooms in Asheville have strict occupancy limits. In past years, popular sessions such as the Standards Committee's labeling update filled up quickly. We need to advise first-time attendees to arrive early for high-demand sessions rather than wandering in at the last minute. The hallway conversations between sessions remain our most valued feature, but structured sessions will require clear capacity guidance.

## Capacity

The Events Team is currently managing conference logistics alongside routine inquiries without additional headcount. As registration climbs, the burden on badge processing and room coordination will increase. We ask the Office of the Chief Executive to note that if registration volume outpaces our historical baseline by more than fifteen percent next month, we will need temporary administrative support for the final two weeks prior to travel.

## What we need

- Communications: integrate hotel block urgency into the upcoming conference promotion without alarming members about lodging inflation.
- Membership Operations: cross-reference the 4 new conference registrants with current membership tier statuses to ensure group discounts are applied correctly.
- Office of the Chief Executive: review the potential support request if weekly registration volume doubles before June.
