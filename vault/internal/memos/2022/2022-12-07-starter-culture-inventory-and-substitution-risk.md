**Subject:** Starter culture inventory and substitution risk heading into winter

Following our recent outreach on starter culture basics and the ongoing questions from small-batch members regarding supplier backorders, this memo outlines the operational risks of strain substitution in winter make rooms. As members navigate colder ambient temperatures, unexpected culture substitutions from suppliers are introducing variables that many creameries are not logging until flavor drift or sluggish sets appear in finished wheels.

## The winter make-room variable

Our recent educational sessions highlighted a simple rule: a culture percentage dialed in for August ambient temperatures will lead to a sluggish set in a chilly December cheese room. Members who adjust culture percentages upward must do so in small increments rather than altering entire recipes overnight. However, the greater vulnerability this season is not just room temperature—it is unannounced supplier substitution. Several creameries report receiving substitute strains when their primary mesophilic or thermophilic cultures are backordered.

Research indicates that lactic acid bacteria do far more than drive simple acidification; they shape distinct flavor and texture profiles that vary meaningfully between strains. A substitute culture is rarely an equivalent one. When a member accepts a backordered substitution without adjusting their make sheet or logging the strain change, tracing a February flavor defect back to a December culture swap becomes nearly impossible.

## Utility costs and thermal profile

Compounding the culture challenge is the energy reality facing our members this month. Federal energy forecasters have warned of tighter natural gas supply and colder winter forecasts, pushing heating costs higher for facilities running pasteurizers, walk-ins, and aging caves. Members operating electric humidification or heating retail floors with aging-room systems are seeing utility bills climb sharply alongside easing milk costs. When an entire building runs colder, the vat temperature is only one part of the problem; the entire thermal profile of the make day shifts downward.

Members who track room temperature alongside culture percentage are finding that logging these variables together clarifies why a batch acts differently from an autumn make. Without that discipline, members risk blaming milk prices or culture viability for what is actually a room-temperature drop.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: review our current guidance on supplier notification standards and ingredient transparency for artisan cheese makers.
- Membership Operations: ensure our member advisory channels highlight the importance of logging culture substitutions and room temperatures in December make sheets.
- Education & Credentialing: integrate cold-room thermal tracking into upcoming winter course materials, specifically for the Rind Development & Washing cohort starting in January 2023.
