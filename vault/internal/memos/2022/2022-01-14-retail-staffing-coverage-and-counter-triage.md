**Subject:** Retail staffing strain, counter triage, and education support

This week's public dispatches and member calls make it clear that January's winter illness wave is hitting specialty cheese counters harder than our usual delivery delays. When a store loses two or three staff, small retail operations where a single monger runs the entire cheese program feel the absence instantly. Members report closing an hour early or trimming the number of active cheeses on display rather than running the counter thin. Our role here is not to pretend we can solve a national retail labor shortage with association paperwork, but to ensure that when members pull in temporary or cross-trained stockroom employees to fill the gap, those fill-ins get the basic safety and handling refreshers they need before they touch the slicer.

## What the education team is seeing

Our course calendar shows zero course enrollments taken during this week of 10 January, and only one course completion recorded for the entire period. That is entirely normal for mid-January when members are heads-down in holiday closeout numbers and triaging daily schedules. However, the Education Committee has received multiple portal inquiries asking for short, fast modules rather than full multi-week courses. Members want something five minutes long that can be handed to a cross-trained stock clerk before they stand behind the glass.

We cannot improvise custom modules on two days' notice, but we can direct members to specific existing course catalogs—such as Counter Culture: Retailing or Listeria Control in Small Creameries—and extract the standalone compliance checkpoints on allergen labeling and cross-contact rules. The upcoming Willow cohort for Building a Cheese Counter starts on 9 February 2022, but that is a deep planning course, not a morning-of onboarding fix for a sick cheesemonger.

## Margin realities and the January close

Another pattern emerging from member conversations this week links staffing strain directly to holiday margin leakage. Members closing their books found that after freight, packaging, and frantic overtime labor were subtracted, holiday margins on top-selling items were far thinner than expected. This connects directly to our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course (slated for the Summit cohort in June 2022). Producers and mongers are discovering that 'it sold well' is not the same as 'it made money,' especially with agricultural and production input costs climbing.

We need to align our messaging across teams so that when Communications writes about empty cases or staffing strain, we do not treat margin review and staffing as two separate problems. They are the same operational squeeze.

## What we need

- Communications: include a direct link to the short-form Education Committee portal contacts in all retail-focused blog follow-ups this month.
- Membership Operations: flag any renewing small-business members who mention staffing closures so we can route them directly to our food safety and counter-retailing module indexes.
- Advocacy & Standards: ensure our guidance on small-producer exemptions and labeling standards does not assume full pre-pandemic staffing levels at the retail counter.
