**Subject:** Export readiness workshop capacity and the February intake

Registration for Workshop: Valley Export Readiness (EVT-2022-W2) opened with the new year, and early demand is running faster than our previous Seattle sessions. The workshop is scheduled for 1 February 2022 in Seattle and is capped at a small group size to allow participants to bring their actual export paperwork through the morning customs session. This memo reviews where registrations stand at the start of the week, the curriculum updates required by recent regulatory changes, and the capacity strain we face while managing both workshop enrollment and winter staffing absences.

## Registration pace and preliminary figures

As of our early pull this week, registrations taken for the Valley Export Readiness workshop stand at 12. Across the full 2022 calendar, the event has 97 registered with 0 refunded, based on historical order tracking. While the official attendance and no-show numbers will only be known after the event runs on 1 February (past events like Brook Food Safety show 101 attended and 10 no-shows, while Meadow Cut & Wrap shows 94 attended and 11 no-shows), the early registration velocity suggests we will hit our venue ceiling sooner than anticipated.

Members are contacting us because the post-holiday inquiries from overseas buyers are colliding with new border requirements. We also noted 2 registrations taken for Workshop: Meadow Cut & Wrap (EVT-2022-W3) in Lancaster, which sits further out on the calendar. That early movement in March workshops indicates members are planning ahead for spring shipments despite winter disruptions.

## Curriculum adjustments for European Union health certificates

The morning session on customs documentation has undergone a complete rewrite for this session. In mid-January, health-certificate requirements for dairy shipments into the European Union changed significantly, adding animal-health monitoring and veterinarian sign-off steps that were not required previously. If a member's buyer is in the EU, or if their freight route transits through European ports, this paperwork cannot be treated as a routine clerical task. We will walk through an anonymized set of member export paperwork from start to finish, highlighting the exact choke points where first-time exporters get held up at the border.

The afternoon remains focused on cold-chain logistics, selecting experienced dairy freight partners, building appropriate transit-time buffers for specialty wheels, and pricing shipments so freight costs do not consume the operating margin. We must ensure attendees bring their actual drafts so they leave with vetted paperwork.

## Winter illness and staffing capacity

As noted in our public communications regarding the January market start, a wave of respiratory illness is moving through processing floors, co-packers, and distribution centers. Our own team is feeling the friction. Managing workshop inquiries while processing 10 membership purchases (6 first-time, 8 Individual, 2 SmallBusiness) for $1,976 gross requires careful coordination between Events and Membership Operations. We cannot afford dropped communications while members are trying to secure their seats.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: cross-check incoming workshop registrants against membership status so we can flag any lapsed accounts before confirmation notices go out.
- Education & Credentialing: confirm whether any participants in the upcoming Willow cohort for Building a Cheese Counter are also registered for the export workshop, so we can coordinate material distribution.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that Seattle venue seating is nearing our threshold, and we may need a decision on whether to open a virtual auditing tier if demand exceeds room capacity.
