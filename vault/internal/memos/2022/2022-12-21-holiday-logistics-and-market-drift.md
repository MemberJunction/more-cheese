This week brings the single heaviest shipping period of the year right on top of a significant weather front crossing the country and a shifting dairy market. Retail and wholesale members are closing cases, running year-end inventory counts, and watching transit lanes freeze up. At the same time, the broader market context that governed 2022 is beginning to soften. This memo outlines what our data shows for the week so far, how it connects to the public blog posts going out this week, and where our operational risks lie before the holiday break.

## Market softening and farmstead margin exposure

Throughout 2022, milk prices set records driven by strong butter demand, which helped improve producer margins. However, recent weeks have seen butter, powder, and whey prices soften while finished cheese demand has held up. This divergence creates real stress for farmstead members who milk their own herds and make cheese on-site. The federal dairy margin coverage enrollment window was recently extended to January 31, 2023, giving farmstead producers an extra six weeks to lock in protection against feed-cost volatility. Our advocacy team should ensure that communications direct farmstead members to their local federal farm service offices before that deadline closes, without offering financial or tax advice.

## Logistics, weather, and year-end inventory

With a severe winter storm crossing major transit lanes this week, wholesale shipments are facing delays and rerouting. Retailers conducting year-end case counts are finding tighter inventory margins than in past years because consumer demand for specialty cheese remained robust through December despite general inflation. Members who rely on timely inbound holiday stock are currently wrestling with delayed carriers and temperature control risks. If shipments are compromised by transit delays, the food-safety implications require clear guidance from our Food Safety Committee, particularly regarding cold-chain integrity and carrier documentation.
