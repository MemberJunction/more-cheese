**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Advocacy & Standards

**Date:** 28 January 2022
**Subject:** Member renewal lapse, payment failure, and grace-window policy triage

Membership Operations is currently managing an unusual volume of renewal friction as accounts cross the January close. Over the course of the week ending 30 January 2022, we recorded 4 total membership purchases totaling $628 in gross sales, all of which landed in the Individual tier. Concurrently, total confirmed orders reached 34 generating $4678 in gross revenue, with cash captured across all product lines hitting $27971. Against these figures, however, several small business and corporate members are reporting failed credit card transactions and asking for extended grace periods beyond our standard 30-day window, citing recent cash flow volatility tied to wholesale dairy pricing swings.

## The renewal friction and payment failures

The root of the current friction is twofold. First, wholesale cheese and butter prices climbed steadily through the first three weeks of January before dropping hard in the final week, a whipsaw that has left several small-tier accounts hesitant to commit to annual renewals without adjusting their tier mix or requesting structured payment terms. Second, our automated payment gateway retries failed on a cluster of accounts whose credit cards expired at year-end, resulting in automatic lapse flags that triggered standard cancellation warnings before members could update their billing details.

When a member experiences a failed payment or misses a renewal date, our current system moves them immediately into a restricted access state after a brief notification cycle. Members caught in this window are contacting Membership Operations asking for discretionary grace extensions, pointing out that their multi-year loyalty should afford them more flexibility than an automated shut-off. We have also received inquiries regarding tier changes—specifically, businesses wanting to step down from SmallBusiness to Individual tiers temporarily to conserve cash while wholesale markets stabilize.

## Why a rigid grace window risks long-term retention

Our position is that a rigid 30-day grace window, while administratively clean, fails to accommodate members who are actively weathering macro market volatility. A creamery that has been with the federation for years should not lose access to compliance guides, event discounts, and technical resources simply because an automated clearing house charge bounced during a volatile dairy pricing week.

However, we cannot grant indefinite grace periods without distorting our active membership count, which closed the week at 491 members. Allowing accounts to linger in an indeterminate state distorts our renewal tracking and creates an unfair disparity for members who paid on time. We need a clear operational boundary that gives breathing room to members experiencing legitimate payment processing snags without compromising our billing integrity.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a temporary 60-day grace window for accounts experiencing payment gateway failures during January and February 2022.
- Advocacy & Standards: confirm whether temporary tier-change requests (such as SmallBusiness moving to Individual) require a formal board amendment or can be handled administratively by Membership Operations.
- Finance / Payments: provide updated guidance on handling manual invoice overrides for members whose credit card updates are delayed by banking holidays.
- Communications: ensure no public messaging assumes uninterrupted member payment flows while these grace extensions are being evaluated.
