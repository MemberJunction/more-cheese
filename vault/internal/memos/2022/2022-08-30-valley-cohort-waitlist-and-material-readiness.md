Registration opened this week for the Valley cohort of Starter Cultures & Coagulants, starting September 26. As the blog post published on August 31 points out, this course addresses a frequent pain point: members fighting their own culture and coagulant selections when a batch runs soft or behaves unexpectedly. However, opening enrollment without confirming the final instructor schedule and material packet delivery has created an internal bottleneck that requires immediate coordination between Membership Operations and Education.

## Enrollment pacing and waitlist pressure

Because milk supply loosened this summer and members are shifting focus from raw-material shortages to margin and yield management, interest in technical make-room courses has ticked up faster than our standard seat allocations anticipated. We saw seven course completions across the wider education program during the week of August 29, but zero new enrollments recorded in the live data for this specific cohort yet, even though informal inquiries to the Education Committee have already filled half our target roster before the public announcement circulated widely.

The friction point is our capacity limit on hands-on vat participation. The Valley cohort is capped to maintain a workable student-to-vat ratio. If applications continue at the current preliminary inquiry rate, we will hit our ceiling within ten days of the blog post, long before the September 26 start date. We need an agreed protocol for handling the waitlist so we are not improvising seat allocation while instructors are trying to finalize travel arrangements.

## Instructor scheduling and material specifications

The bigger issue is that the instructor assignment for the Valley cohort is still tentative. We are waiting on final confirmation regarding travel availability from our regional instructors, and the supplier specifications for the culture and coagulant sample packets have not been locked against our current inventory. If we promise specific mesophilic and thermophilic strain kits in the classroom materials without verifying distributor lead times, we risk running into the exact supply lag that has plagued wholesale orders all summer.

Furthermore, the course relies on accurate make-sheet documentation that reflects current input costs. Members are already sensitive to the cost squeeze, and a classroom session that ignores real-world pricing realities will feel disconnected from the shop floor.

## Capacity and cross-functional friction

Both programs draw on the same administrative bandwidth. We need Membership Operations to hold off on aggressive promotional pushes until we confirm whether we can expand the Valley cohort cap or whether we must strictly enforce a capped waitlist.
