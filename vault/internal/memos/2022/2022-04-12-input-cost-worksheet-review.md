**To:** Advocacy & Standards; Communications; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 12 April 2022
**Subject:** Input-cost worksheet review and member outreach

Every member call we have taken this spring opens with the same observation: Class III milk prices are high, cheese blocks and barrels have climbed, and members are seeing a milk check that looks encouraging on paper. That part of the trade press narrative is accurate. The part that does not make the press release is the other side of the ledger. Diesel, fertilizer, feed, and hauling costs are moving faster than our standard annual review cycle can capture, and farmstead members milking and making on the same footprint are finding that their cost-per-wheel worksheets from last year no longer pencil out correctly.

This memo reviews what we are hearing from members, what the Membership & Outreach Committee has been asked to advise on, and how we should align our member support with the public blog post published yesterday on the same theme.

## What the calls are telling us

Members who spread fertilizer this spring are paying multiples of what they paid two seasons ago, and that cost is only beginning to show up in feed bills. More immediately, diesel increases are inflating the hauling line on every wheel sent to a regional distributor. For a small creamery, hauling used to be a minor entry that could be rounded off; this spring it is large enough to force separate price conversations with wholesale accounts.

We have assisted several farmstead members who redone their cost-per-wheel worksheets twice since January—once for fertilizer and feed, and a second time when diesel crept up again. Those who shared their numbers found that a plain explanation of fuel and fertilizer costs landed better with their regular buyers than a sudden price adjustment without context. None of the accounts they spoke with pushed back aggressively, though one buyer asked for a price lock through the summer in exchange for accepting the spring increase.

## Alignment with federation communications

Yesterday's communications post correctly advises members to pull actual fuel receipts and feed invoices from the last quarter rather than relying on stale annual averages. Our role on the operations side is to back that up with practical help. We can point members toward peers who have already successfully updated their worksheets and revised their wholesale pricing lists. We should ensure our committee members are equipped to share those templates without stepping into legal or financial advice.

We also need to keep in mind the regulatory backdrop. As the Food Safety Committee monitors traceability rules and recordkeeping proposals, members are already juggling multiple administrative pressures. Adding a complex pricing recalculation on top of spring make schedules is stretching some creamery staffs thin.

## Capacity and next steps

Our team is managing normal spring membership renewals alongside these outreach calls. Membership purchases for the week so far stand at six, with four in the Individual tier, one SmallBusiness, and one Corporate, bringing gross membership sales to $1,887. Total confirmed orders across the federation are 24, generating $5,948 in gross orders with $5,904 in cash captured and zero cancellations. Event registrations taken this week include five for the ICF Annual Conference 2022 and 13 for the upcoming Brook Food Safety workshop in Seattle. Meanwhile, the free webinar on Water Activity & Shelf Life held today carried zero paid registrations, matching our standard data pattern for webinars.

We do not need additional headcount, but we do need our advisory messaging to remain uniform across all committees.

## What we need

- Communications: continue directing members with pricing questions to our committee contact list.
- Advocacy & Standards: ensure any guidance on federal rules acknowledges the operating cost pressures documented in our member calls.
