**Subject:** Membership lapse rates, grace-window requests, and payment failure handling

As we enter May, member renewals and payment friction are surfacing as operational concerns that require a clear, consistent association-wide protocol rather than ad hoc extensions. We currently have 537 current members at week end, but our intake and renewal pipelines are seeing an uptick in failed automatic payments and requests for extended grace windows. Small business and individual members caught between rising operational costs—such as Class III milk prices hitting record highs—and annual renewal notices are asking for flexibility that our automated billing cycle does not currently accommodate.

This memo outlines the current state of membership grace-window requests, examines the friction points between Membership Operations and the billing gateway, and proposes a formalized handling protocol for accounts facing payment failures.

## The current renewal environment and friction points

Our year-to-date data shows steady first-time member acquisition, with four new Individual memberships purchased this week alone, bringing gross membership sales to 628 dollars for the week. However, retention of existing members depends heavily on smooth annual billing. When a renewal payment fails due to expired cards or insufficient funds, our standard automated sequence issues three notices before moving the account to lapsed status. Several members have contacted us directly asking for a grace window beyond the standard thirty-day threshold, citing cash-flow tightness caused by soaring input costs.

The friction arises because Membership Operations lacks a standardized policy for granting grace windows to members whose accounts have lapsed. Some accounts have been granted informal extensions by staff members, while others have been hard-terminated automatically, leading to frustrated communications and unnecessary administrative overhead. Furthermore, when members call or email asking for flexibility, they are often bounced between Communications and Membership Operations without a clear answer on whether their member benefits—such as access to discounted event registrations and member-only courses like the upcoming Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese cohort—remain active during the grace period.

## Operational considerations and capacity

Handling these requests manually is placing an unsustainable burden on our small team. Membership Operations currently shares inbound inquiry management with other association priorities, including preparations for upcoming workshops such as the May 13 Brook Food Safety session in Seattle, which has 24 new registrations taken this week. We cannot afford to spend hours negotiating individual payment plans for routine renewals.

At the same time, treating every lapsed member as lost ignores the broader macroeconomic pressures outlined in our recent public communications. When input costs are squeezing small creameries, a rigid automated cutoff risks alienating long-standing supporters who simply need an extra two weeks to settle their accounts. A predictable, transparent grace-window policy protects association revenue while maintaining goodwill.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a standardized 45-day grace window for annual renewals upon formal request, superseding the current 30-day automated cutoff.
- Communications: update the member portal FAQs to clearly state what happens to benefits during a payment grace period so inbound support tickets decrease.
- Membership Operations: implement a weekly automated report flagging accounts at risk of lapsing due to payment failures so proactive outreach can occur before benefits terminate.
- Finance and Accounting: confirm how extended grace windows affect our monthly revenue recognition and cash-captured reporting.
