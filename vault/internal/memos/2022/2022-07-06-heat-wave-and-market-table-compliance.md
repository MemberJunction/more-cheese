The Standards Committee held its Q3 meeting today, and summer operating conditions occupied the first hour. Following our recent member spotlight on a vendor managing a Fourth of July market stall through extreme heat, we have received several inquiries from small producers asking whether our guidance on probe calibration and ice rotation should be formalised into a standard advisory or folded into upcoming education courses. This memo reviews what the committee decided, what our data currently shows, and what we need other functional teams to align on before the Asheville conference.

## What the committee discussed

The Standards Committee met this morning and reviewed informal reports from members running outdoor market stands during this week's heat wave. While no formal food safety infractions have been reported to our office, members are noting severe strain on refrigeration and cooling capacity. The discussion focused on whether our existing guidance—checking probe thermometers every half hour, rotating ice, and cutting table display size by a third—is sufficient for small farmstead operations that lack climate-controlled retail spaces.

The committee agreed that no new standard or rule change is warranted at this time. However, members asked us to ensure that our upcoming education and event programming reflects the operational realities of high-ambient-temperature processing and retail. Specifically, the Food Safety Committee and the Standards Committee want to ensure that summer event planning does not treat refrigeration as an afterthought.
