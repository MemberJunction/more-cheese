**Subject:** Component price reconciliation and feed-cost margin drift

Recent member queries and our own review of the agricultural data confirm that butterfat premiums have run well above normal for most of 2022. While this props up headline milk checks, it creates a distinct margin squeeze for members making higher-fat styles or purchasing milk as an input. Retail prices are reflecting these broader pressures too, moving through the entire chain rather than stopping at the farm gate. This memo outlines where the structural friction sits and what concrete steps we should take internally to assist members before fourth-quarter wholesale pricing locks in.

## Where the squeeze actually sits

For producer-members running herds, the arithmetic is straightforward and difficult: two consecutive years of tighter feed markets, driven by uncooperative weather across key growing regions, have pushed input costs up faster than producers can pass along. A milk check that appears favorable on paper still leaves margins flat after paying for hay, grain, and transport.

For creameries buying milk, the calculus differs but the pressure matches. When the fat-heavy component of the price index runs hot, styles relying on butterfat — such as triple crèmes and richer washed rinds — cost more to produce than a standard yield calculation indicates. If a member's pricing model uses last year's component ratios, they are likely underpricing their higher-fat lines specifically rather than their catalog evenly.

## Education and pricing outreach

We cannot set prices for members, but we can direct them to the tools we already maintain. The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course addresses exactly this reconciliation, and its next cohort is coming up on the calendar. We should ensure Membership Operations highlights this course in upcoming communications, particularly for members inquiring about fall wholesale adjustments.

Simultaneously, we are tracking 8 LetterCampaign submissions and 5 PetitionSignature submissions this week related to FSMA small-producer exemptions. Regulatory compliance and input costs are the two issues dominating member inquiries this month.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: cross-reference member renewal inquiries with participation in pricing education courses.
- Education & Credentialing: confirm outreach materials for upcoming fall cohorts emphasize component-cost reconciliation.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a brief guidance note for the advisory committees regarding feed-cost trends.
