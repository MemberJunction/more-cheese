This week's operational review examines our current membership standing as we navigate a difficult year for small farmstead creameries. Total active membership sits at 1,214 at week end. With regional feed and energy cost inflation driving financial distress across the sector, membership retention requires careful monitoring. We are currently managing a cluster of accounts approaching or within their grace windows, alongside several payment failures and tier-change requests that require cross-team coordination between Membership Operations and Advocacy & Standards. This memo reviews our current exposure, the mechanics of our grace-window handling, and the specific friction points we are encountering on failed payments.

While these weekly figures reflect steady transactional activity, they mask underlying retention risks among small-scale producers whose cash flows are constrained by summer utility bills and rising feed costs.

## Grace-window protocols and payment failures

We are seeing an uptick in failed payments and expired cards that push accounts into our standard grace window. In a normal operating environment, an expired credit card on an automatic renewal triggers an automated retry followed by a thirty-day grace window before lapse protocols engage. However, several small business and individual members have reached out requesting an extended grace window or a temporary tier drop rather than an outright lapse. These producers are attempting to cut association expenses while weathering peak refrigeration costs, yet they still rely heavily on our technical guidance and safety resources. Extending grace periods indefinitely creates accounting distortions and complicates our active member count, which currently stands at 1,214.

Furthermore, we have encountered friction regarding how we communicate these grace windows without inadvertently encouraging systematic payment delays. Communications wants clear, reassuring messaging for members facing hardship, while Membership Operations must protect Association revenue and maintain clean billing records. When a member requests a tier change downward from SmallBusiness to Individual, or seeks a grace extension, our team must verify whether the change reflects a genuine downsizing of their creamery operations or merely a short-term cash-flow squeeze.

## Alignment with association advocacy and public messaging

Our internal membership discussions intersect directly with the association's broader advocacy work on dairy pricing reform and the recent adjustments to the federal dairy margin safety-net program, as covered in our public blog posts this month. Members who struggle to pay annual dues are often the exact small-producer operations most vulnerable to milk price volatility and high energy bills. Offering rigid payment cutoff dates without acknowledging the broader economic pressures on farmstead creameries risks alienating our core constituency. Conversely, offering informal, undocumented payment extensions creates administrative chaos for our reconciliation process.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a standardized thirty-day extension policy for members formally documenting seasonal weather or utility hardship, distinct from our standard automated grace window.
- Communications: review our template reminder emails for lapsed accounts to ensure the tone acknowledges current operating pressures without compromising payment urgency.
- Membership Operations: complete a full audit of all accounts currently sitting in grace windows by Friday afternoon to establish an accurate baseline before month-end close.
