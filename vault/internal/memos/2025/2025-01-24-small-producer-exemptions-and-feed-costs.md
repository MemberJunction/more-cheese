**Subject:** Small-producer exemptions and feed-cost realities in the 2025 comment window

The federal comment period on small-producer food-safety exemptions closes next month, and the Food Safety Committee has been reviewing the draft language we plan to submit. This rulemaking window arrives at a difficult moment for the regional farmstead creameries that make up our smallest tiers. Nonfeed operating costs — utilities, packaging, and labor — have continued to climb, and margin compression is now the primary driver of distress for independent makers who crossed the exemption sales threshold on paper without growing their actual risk profile or staff size.

## The structural problem with the threshold

The current exemption framework relies on a flat sales dollar limit that was established before the recent wave of utility and packaging inflation. While feed costs have eased somewhat from their multi-year peaks, the ongoing rise in nonfeed overhead leaves farmstead operations with very little room to absorb compliance expenditures. Independent studies examining preventive-control requirements put first-year compliance costs for small and mid-sized processors at roughly $22,000, plus recurring annual costs thereafter. A large plant absorbs that expense without noticing; a ten-person creamery must find those funds within an already tight operating budget.

Our advocacy actions this week reflect this tension. The committee recorded 1 coalition meeting regarding interstate cheese shipment and continues to field inquiries from members asking how to format their actual financial figures into comments that federal regulators will take seriously. General expressions of support for the federation's position are helpful, but regulators weight submissions that include verified production data and specific cost breakdowns far more heavily when reviewing threshold adjustments.

## Committee position and joint filings

The Food Safety Committee's draft comment asks the regulator to index the exemption threshold to a cost-of-production measure instead of a flat sales figure, and to institute a formal grace period rather than an immediate compliance cutoff when a farmstead crosses the line during a volatile operating year. We have also reiterated our call for a fixed schedule of reviews so that inflation does not quietly erode small-producer protections between legislative cycles.

For members who cannot draft a standalone filing, the committee is offering to incorporate their specific numbers into a joint filing through the member portal. That option is essential for farmsteads whose kitchen-table management teams are already stretched thin by calving season and wholesale routing.
