**Subject:** Labeling standards under review and what they mean for member comment rates

The public comment window closing on September 15 regarding the proposed revocation of older labeling standards is rapidly approaching. While our public channels have urged members to review the docket, our internal tracking of advocacy actions shows virtually zero engagement on this specific rule so far. Members are heavily focused on immediate cash-flow survival amid regional feed and energy cost inflation, treating regulatory housekeeping as a distant distraction. This memo outlines why that shrug is dangerous and what we need to do in the final two weeks of the comment period.

## The risk of ignoring obsolete standards

When a standard of identity is struck from the books, the name it formally protected becomes fair game for a broader category of commercial products. Several members have privately remarked that the eighteen obsolete standards under review do not touch their active inventories, leading them to assume the rulemaking is entirely harmless. We disagree. Removing the formal definitions lowers the barrier for industrial substitutes to borrow traditional styling terms without matching the underlying craft criteria. If an artisan wheel sits beside an industrial imposter on a retail shelf, the consumer relies entirely on the clarity of the remaining labeling rules. If those rules erode or fragment, provenance becomes harder to defend against mass-produced volume.

Furthermore, this rulemaking runs parallel to broader food-traceability recordkeeping discussions. While the compliance date for expanded traceability under Section 204 has effectively been pushed back toward 2028, giving farm shops a much-needed longer clock, the underlying pressure on documentation is not going away. Members who split volume between direct farm shops and wholesale accounts are already struggling to keep lot-code tracking consistent across channels. Conflating a longer traceability timeline with regulatory dormancy is a mistake that will cost members dearly during their next distributor audit.

## Engagement realities and staff capacity

Our advocacy records show a cumulative total of 243 actions across the year so far, with labeling standards accounting for 50 actions and interstate cheese shipments leading with 57. However, the intake velocity for comment filings on this specific September 15 docket is essentially flat. Small farmstead creameries are simply too stretched by operational overhead to draft individualized agency comments. They do not need general encouragement; they need concrete templates that translate their actual package labeling into acceptable regulatory feedback.

We also face a bandwidth constraint internally. The upcoming Workshop: Cave Farm Shop Retail scheduled for September 11 in Ludlow is absorbing the operational bandwidth of the events team, and our certification candidates—such as the counter manager two months out from her Certified Cheesemonger exam—are finding it nearly impossible to squeeze study hours out of increasingly busy retail shifts. Expecting these same operators to independently master agency docket submission formats without direct staff assistance is unrealistic.

## What we need

- Communications: reinforce the September 15 comment deadline in upcoming member touchpoints without sounding alarmist, focusing strictly on concrete label protection rather than abstract policy.
- Membership Operations: flag members operating hybrid farm-shop and wholesale models so our advocacy team can offer targeted direct outreach regarding traceability and labeling consistency.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the allocation of two technical support hours from our regulatory desk to assist members with draft comment submissions before the window closes.
