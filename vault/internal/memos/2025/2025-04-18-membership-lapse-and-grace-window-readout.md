**Subject:** Membership lapse rates and grace window request readout

This memo provides a preliminary readout on membership activity and lapse-risk inquiries for the week ending 20 April 2025. With regional feed and energy cost inflation continuing to squeeze small farmstead creameries, our support queue has received a notable increase in requests for extended grace windows on expiring memberships. While membership purchases for the week totaled 18 with a gross value of 4206, and confirmed orders reached 101 with a gross of 18995 against a total cash capture of 19558, several accounts in the SmallBusiness and Corporate tiers have missed their standard renewal dates without initiating payment.

## What the numbers show so far

At the close of the week, total active membership stands at 1152. The week's 18 purchases include 9 first-time members. Broken down by tier, the week's purchases comprise 11 Individual memberships, 4 Enthusiast memberships, 2 SmallBusiness memberships, and 1 Corporate membership. Gross sales for these purchases equal 4206.

These figures demonstrate steady acquisition, but they mask an underlying retention friction. Members operating small farmstead creameries under severe margin pressure are contacting Membership Operations to ask whether the standard 30-day grace window can be lengthened to 90 days. Their argument is straightforward: cash flow is tied up in slow-moving inventory, and paying annual dues during an acute liquidity squeeze forces them to choose between association membership and operational expenses.

## The policy collision

Our current bylaws enforce a strict cutoff after the grace window expires. However, applying a rigid cutoff during an industry-wide cost crisis risks driving permanent lapses among long-tenured small producers who would otherwise renew once their seasonal cash flow stabilizes. We have handled four separate appeals this week from small-business members asking for discretionary extensions.

Granting ad-hoc extensions creates tracking complications and opens us up to equity complaints from members who paid on time. Conversely, enforcing the cutoff without exception risks repeating the prior year's lapses. Our prior year base ended with 1447 members on December 31, and we cannot afford to push struggling producers out of the federation simply because their billing cycle landed in a tight quarter.

## Capacity and coordination

Membership Operations cannot absorb an indefinite manual review process for every late renewal. We need a standardized approach coordinated with Advocacy & Standards and the Office of the Chief Executive to determine whether a temporary hardship extension tier is viable without rewriting our entire dues schedule.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: rule on whether Membership Operations has authority to grant a 60-day extension upon written hardship request for SmallBusiness tier members.
- Advocacy & Standards: review whether temporary payment deferrals conflict with our organizational governance rules.
- Communications: ensure no public statements or blog posts overpromise on fee waivers before executive leadership finalizes the grace-window policy.
