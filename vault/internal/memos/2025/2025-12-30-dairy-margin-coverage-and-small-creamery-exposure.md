The federal margin support program has been rewritten for 2026, opening a six-year lock-in option with lower premiums. As our regional advocacy work on dairy pricing reform continues—recording 1 testimony action this week—the broader economic picture for our membership remains strained. Feed and energy cost inflation is driving small farmstead creamery distress across our regions, and the softening Class I base milk price projected for January 2026 means producers will face tighter margins right as annual paperwork comes due. This memo reviews what the new margin coverage rules mean for our smallest members and where our advisory queue is likely to see friction during the January enrollment window.

## What changed in the 2026 margin rules

The fundamental mechanism pays out when the gap between the national all-milk price and average feed costs drops below an elected coverage tier. The shift for 2026 is the six-year multi-year lock-in option, which discounts premiums for operations willing to commit without annual re-election. For farmstead producers accustomed to treating the signup form as a rushed January administrative chore, this trade-off requires careful calculation. An operation undergoing transition, ownership changes, or herd size adjustments cannot easily bind itself to a six-year formula without risking severe cash-flow friction if feed costs spike or milk prices fall further.

Federal agricultural outlook work suggests all-milk prices could decline by roughly a tenth in the coming year, even as herd sizes and output creep upward. When combined with the regional arctic cold snaps impacting northern Plains members this week—where cave operators are burning extra energy on space heaters just to keep milk houses and ripening rooms stable—the margin between revenue and basic operating costs is razor thin. We know from last year's totals that our membership base closed 2025 with 1,447 current members and 1,741 annual membership purchases generating $422,407 in gross sales, but those broad figures mask the acute vulnerability of single-vessel farmstead makers whose cash reserves are nearly exhausted.

## Operational friction and advisory support

Our advocacy team has noted that regional offices still run the enrollment window through the exact weeks when small creameries are buried under holiday order fulfillment and year-end inventory closing. We have pressed regional offices in past comments to shift the timeline, but that calendar is fixed for 2026. Consequently, our member portal and advisory queues will absorb the burden of helping members interpret the estimator tools.

Members attempting to use the revised estimation tool often discover that their internal bookkeeping lags behind actual production. If a creamery does not have clean figures for milk sold and feed purchased, the estimator yields misleading outputs. We must urge members through our advisory channels to close out their 2025 financial logs before attempting to file their 2026 coverage elections, rather than guessing on the fly.
