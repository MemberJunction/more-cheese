Federal milk-supply surveillance expanded further this month, bringing additional states from voluntary to required bulk-tank sampling. Phone lines to advocacy have been active since Monday's blog post went live. Our members are asking whether the testing protocol alters their make schedule or creates public reporting risks. This memo outlines what the testing order actually requires, how our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate holders are positioned, and what we are tracking in member advocacy.

## What the surveillance program requires

The protocol itself is straightforward: a sample drawn from the bulk tank or silo before pasteurization, tested, and reported back to the herd owner and state. It is not an inspection score or a judgment on creamery operations, and it does not slow down make schedules. What it changes is administrative friction. Farms holding records under an established food-safety plan need only add a single line to their monitoring log recording when a sample was drawn and by whom. Inspectors expect a paper trail.

The rollout remains uneven by design. Neighboring states are on different schedules because larger dairy states were brought in first. Cooperatives spanning state lines are creating confusion for transporters who must manage two sets of compliance rules. We have advised members to verify their state's enrollment status directly with their plant compliance contact rather than relying on last month's guidance.
