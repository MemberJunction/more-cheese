**Subject:** Wholesale pricing softness, feed costs, and small creamery exposure

This week's public blog post on falling wholesale cheese prices touches on a tension that is already showing up in our renewal and contact data. As federal milk production data records a fourth consecutive month of record output—running close to two percent over a year ago—wholesale cheese prices have drifted lower. At the same time, regional feed and energy cost inflation continues to exert pressure on farmstead creameries. While large processors hedge against this downward movement, small operations selling at or near spot prices are feeling the margin squeeze. We need to align our outreach across departments so that member inquiries about hardship and pricing contracts are handled consistently.

## What the membership numbers show so far

For the week of 29 September 2025, our current membership count stands at 1347. Membership purchases for the week total 16 transactions, comprising 12 first-time members across Individual (11), Enthusiast (4), and SmallBusiness (1) tiers, generating a gross of $2843. Confirmed orders for the week stand at 16, with a gross of $3286 and zero cancellations, while cash captured sits at $5763.

While these purchase numbers indicate steady baseline activity, they mask the vulnerability of small farmstead operations. Members on spot-purchase milk contracts are reporting that their cost-to-revenue spread has narrowed noticeably. A softening cheese price combined with higher milk volume creates a quiet squeeze that does not immediately register as a formal billing dispute or lapse, but it does surface when members call to ask whether hardship-dues framing or grace windows apply to their upcoming renewal.

## Intersection with advocacy and standards

Our Advocacy & Standards team has been active on related fronts, logging a recent advocacy action around FSMA small-producer exemptions. However, members calling in this week are less concerned with federal rulemaking than with immediate cash flow. When a small creamery asks whether they should renegotiate a standing price with a milk supplier, pointing them to federal price forecasts is cold comfort. We need our committee networks and outreach volunteers to provide practical peer connections rather than macro-economic summaries.

Furthermore, the Standards Committee's review of obsolete standards of identity connects directly to this financial stress. When margins are thin, the regulatory cost of compliance or the inability to label a traditional style accurately hits a creamery's bottom line immediately. Communications must ensure that blog messaging and member advisories do not treat pricing, labeling, and advocacy as isolated topics when members experience them as a single operating environment.

## Capacity and next steps

Membership Operations is currently absorbing an uptick in informal pricing inquiries. We do not need additional headcount, but we do need Advocacy & Standards and Communications to coordinate on the messaging deployed through our committee channels. If members are told by one team that prices are softening and by another that standards are shifting, they conclude the federation lacks a cohesive read on current farmstead distress.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a brief coordination huddle between Membership Operations, Advocacy & Standards, and Communications before next week's blog cycle.
- Advocacy & Standards: provide a two-paragraph summary of active labeling reviews that Membership Operations can share directly with callers asking about standard of identity changes.
- Communications: ensure upcoming member advisories reference the practical margin-check guidance outlined in this week's blog post without minimizing the severity of regional feed and energy pressures.
- Membership Operations: track all spot-contract pricing inquiries this month and report any cluster of lapses directly to the executive office by 20 October.
