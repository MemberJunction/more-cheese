**Subject:** Holiday case planning, category mix, and small creamery distress

Specialty cheese deli sales are running roughly 8 percent ahead of last year on both dollars and volume through most of 2025 according to national retail tracking, but this growth is concentrated in goat cheese, Hispanic styles, and Italian styles rather than distributed evenly across the case. Traditional staples like brie, blue, and havarti have slowed. For small farmstead creameries facing regional feed and energy cost inflation, misjudging this category divergence in their holiday stocking plans poses an immediate cash-flow risk. This memo outlines how our current membership purchasing patterns and advocacy contacts connect to the retail realities our members face this autumn.

## What the membership and order data shows

So far during the week of October 20, we have recorded 24 total membership purchases totaling $5,493 in gross sales, bringing current membership to 1,382 at week end. Of those 24 purchases, 15 are first-time members joining across individual (18), small business (3), corporate (1), and enthusiast (2) tiers. Total confirmed orders for the week stand at 32 with a gross value of $7,567, and cash captured is $3,422. We also recorded 5 event registrations taken for the upcoming Curd Pasta Filata workshop and 33 course enrollments alongside 4 completions. Advocacy actions continue at a steady pace, including 1 coalition meeting focused on labeling standards.

While these numbers indicate ongoing baseline engagement, they mask the acute inventory and margin pressures reported by our farmstead members. Small creameries relying solely on traditional cheddar or blue wedges without storytelling signage or distinct regional provenance are seeing slower holiday pre-orders from distributors.

## Connecting retail trends to creamery survival

The national shift toward goat's milk cheeses, which are projected to reach $6.2 billion globally by 2034 at a 4.3 percent CAGR, and the broader global cheese renaissance mean that retail buyers are looking for category differentiation. Members who update their wholesale pitches to emphasize specific milk sources and regional stories are securing better case placement. However, creameries with limited staff lack the bandwidth to redesign their product sheets while managing autumn milk yields and rising utility bills.

Our advocacy work on labeling standards and small-producer exemptions under FSMA touches directly on these operational constraints. Yet, regulatory relief takes months, whereas holiday case allocations are being locked in right now. We must ensure our communications and member support offer practical, immediate adjustments rather than broad macroeconomic reassurances.

## Capacity and coordination

Our team is currently managing the autumn webinar stretch, including this week's digital storefront session and upcoming courses on humidity control. Membership operations and communications must align closely so that advisory resources reach distressed creameries before their distributor orders close for November and December.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure any public retail guidance emphasizes practical case-balancing steps without overstating the breadth of consumer growth across every style.
- Advocacy & Standards: prioritize feedback from the recent labeling standards coalition meeting into our small-producer outreach.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a targeted member check-in sequence for small business tier members flagged for renewal risk this quarter.
