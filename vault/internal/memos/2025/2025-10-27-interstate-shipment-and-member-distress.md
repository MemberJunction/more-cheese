**Subject:** Interstate shipment rules, federal shutdown impacts, and member distress

The federal government entered its fourth week of a funding lapse this week, and the number of members contacting us about delayed rulemaking is rising. The comment period on interstate cheese shipment closed in September without a follow-up notice, and members who planned their autumn inventory around a potential regulatory update are discovering that agency staff work has frozen. At the same time, regional feed and energy cost inflation continues to press on small farmstead creameries, creating an overlap between margin pressure and stalled federal action that our current advocacy file does not fully capture. This memo outlines what we can and cannot influence under these conditions and proposes how we handle incoming accounts through the portal.

## What the shutdown has actually frozen

A funding lapse does not cancel a rulemaking, but it halts the economic analysis and interagency review that must happen before a comment period record turns into a proposed rule. Members waiting on both a rule change and margin-coverage backstop calculations are caught in a dual stall. The production data feeding those risk calculations has gone quiet, leaving producers with neither a price estimate nor a shipment roadmap. Our advocacy data shows a cumulative total of 243 advocacy actions this year across topics like raw-milk aging rules, dairy pricing reform, interstate shipment, labeling standards, and FSMA small-producer exemptions, but the volume of inquiries this week specifically targets interstate movement. We cannot accelerate the federal calendar, and we must be careful not to imply that we can.

## Where our records and member accounts intersect

We have heard from regional sales reps and distributors who report that small farmstead accounts are quietly deciding whether to stay in business for another year. The patchwork of interstate thresholds means that a distributor carrying a load three states over may be blocked on the fourth, and each workaround costs valuable margin. While our public blog posts encourage members to submit specific cases through the portal, Membership Operations needs a clear protocol for routing these messages. When a member reports a lost sale or a shipment barrier, Advocacy & Standards needs the exact product, the states involved, and the administrative hurdle so we can strengthen our running file. Without specifics, our advocacy remains abstract when the regulatory window eventually reopens.

## Capacity and risk

The strain is visible across multiple teams. Membership Operations is handling grace-window requests and payment questions as autumn renewals arrive, while Communications fields questions about why federal notices are not moving. We must ensure that our communications remain strictly factual: we tell members what rules are in effect today and avoid offering legal advice on unapproved shipping practices. Our role is to maintain the evidence base so that the moment the federal register resumes normal operations, our record is complete and unassailable.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: route any specific interstate shipping obstacle reported through the portal directly to Advocacy & Standards with full account details.
- Communications: maintain the stance in public updates that while we cannot move the federal calendar, our running file is actively being updated with member evidence.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that regional feed and energy cost inflation is driving small creamery distress faster than our standard quarterly reporting captures, and factor this into upcoming board packet summaries.
