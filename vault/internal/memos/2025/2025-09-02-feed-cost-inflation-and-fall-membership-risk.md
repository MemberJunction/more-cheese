**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Advocacy & Standards; Communications

**Date:** 2 September 2025
**Subject:** Feed cost inflation and fall membership risk

The margin squeeze hitting small farmstead creameries in late August is beginning to register in our membership renewal pipeline. While federal Class III milk pricing moved up over a dollar from July into August, a rapid reassessment of corn yields across the central growing region drove feed and forage prices up sharply at the same time. For members who buy supplemental grain or hay, the feed bill is arriving higher than anticipated, offsetting the milk-price gain. This memo examines how this shift affects our current 1,290 members at week's end and what we should adjust in our outreach before the fall renewal cycle accelerates.

## What the weekly numbers show so far

For the week of 1 September 2025, membership operations recorded 17 total membership purchases generating $3,304 in gross revenue. Of those purchases, 10 were first-time members, while the tier breakdown shows 14 Individual memberships, 1 Enthusiast membership, and 2 SmallBusiness memberships. Total confirmed orders across all association products reached 54, with $9,237 in gross order value and zero cancellations. Cash captured for the week stood at $10,064. Meanwhile, event registrations for the upcoming workshop (EVT-2025-W2 Workshop: Cave Farm Shop Retail in Ludlow on 11 September) stand at 36 registrations taken.

While these numbers indicate steady baseline activity, our member support logs from the past two weeks reveal a different underlying anxiety. Farmstead producers calling our office describe feed bills that exceed their budgeted quotes and milk suppliers tightening delivery terms. Members operating under tight margins are scrutinizing every line item, and while membership dues are modest compared to feed bills, they represent discretionary overhead that operators under severe cash pressure tend to evaluate closely.

## Implications for renewal and risk management

In an era driven by regional feed and energy cost inflation, our traditional assumption that a slight bump in milk checks automatically secures membership retention no longer holds. Members are reading their ledgers sideways: they see the rising grain bill immediately and view the milk check recovery with caution. If we continue to market fall renewals using spring assumptions, we risk surprising members who are currently making month-to-month cash flow decisions.

We need to ensure our communications align with the reality described in recent staff guidance and blog messaging: encouraging members to verify current quotes rather than relying on stale pricing, and highlighting practical educational offerings like the Cheese Foundations Certificate that offer clear value for counter staff and apprentices without requiring heavy capital outlay.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: review whether current feed and dairy pricing inquiries from members warrant an updated resource sheet or targeted outreach before October.
- Communications: ensure upcoming member messaging maintains a realistic tone regarding margin pressures and avoids portraying the recent milk price tick as an immediate solvency fix.
- Membership Operations: monitor SmallBusiness and Individual tier renewals over the next three weeks to detect any localized spike in non-renewals originating from high feed-cost regions.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a brief check-in with regional member representatives next week to gather ground-level ledger reports.
