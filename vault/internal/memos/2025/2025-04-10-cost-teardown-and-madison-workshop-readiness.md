**Subject:** Cost teardown and Madison workshop readiness

Registration for our upcoming Prairie Costing workshop in Madison has reached 36 registrations taken so far this week, bringing total registrations to 206 with 2 refunds recorded in the year data. This timing matches the broader regional distress we are seeing across small creameries. Federal margin-coverage reports and member ledgers alike confirm that feed and energy inflation are squeezing farmstead budgets faster than price sheets can absorb. A costing workshop is drawing interest because members are looking for precise make-by-make numbers rather than last year's estimates.

## What the Madison workshop covers

Unlike our pricing floor course, this session operates upstream on actual production costs. The curriculum breaks down a single make into its component lines: milk cost at current contract or spot rates, cultures and rennet, labor hours categorized by task rather than lumped together, packaging materials, and the electrical cost of maintaining cave and cooler temperatures through a full aging cycle. Members who have attended past sessions report that the energy allocation is consistently the largest surprise, as few small operations have previously split out utility costs by specific batches resting in specific aging rooms.

Because the session relies on a working-lunch format to keep discussions active, attendance must be capped. The physical capacity of the room in Madison dictates our ceiling. If registration continues at the current pace through the remaining weeks before 29 April, we will hit our maximum capacity and have to turn late applicants away or maintain a strict waiting list.

## Overlap with other spring offerings

We also need to coordinate our messaging with Education & Credentialing. Members taking the Humidity & Airflow Control course or reviewing their cave utility bills are touching the exact same cost center that the Madison workshop addresses from a macro costing perspective. If a creamery is burning excess power because their cave humidity band is tighter than the cheese style requires, that inefficiency shows up directly on the cost teardown sheet we teach in Madison. Staff should ensure our communications cross-reference these programs without duplicating content.

Furthermore, the federal labeling proposal regarding ultrafiltered milk, which our Standards Committee is currently reviewing for member comment, touches packaging and ingredient transparency. While it is a slower-moving file, members are asking how labeling changes might require relisting components or reprinting packaging stock. We must ensure our administrative answers remain consistent whether a member asks about labeling, cave energy, or batch costing.

- Events Team: finalize the room setup and catering contracts for Madison before the end of next week.
- Membership Operations: cross-check the 36 new workshop registrations against current membership tiers to verify discount eligibility.
- Education & Credentialing: align our course reminder schedule so that members receive guidance on cave efficiency alongside notices for the costing workshop.
