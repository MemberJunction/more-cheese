**Subject:** Feed and hay cost inflation and farmstead creamery distress

Small farmstead creameries are encountering severe pressure from regional feed, fuel, and hay cost inflation. Recent reports from members operating mixed herds in the upper Midwest highlight that the gap between herd maintenance costs and farm-gate milk checks has narrowed significantly. This memo examines how current input costs affect small producers, reviews our active membership numbers for the week, and outlines what support the Membership & Outreach Committee can realistically provide without overextending internal resources.

## The reality of the hay math

Producers milking small herds are reporting that they must calculate hay costs before running their make sheets. Industry cost-of-production estimates confirm that feed and energy price inflation has pushed the true cost of producing milk well above historical averages, outstripping the milk price increases realized by most small-scale operations. When cheese sales are factored in, margins remain uncomfortably thin. Several members have noted on the forum that they are being forced to choose between scaling back herds or locking in winter feed costs early by tying up operating cash.

This squeeze is driving some farmstead operations toward difficult restructuring decisions. While the federation cannot set feed prices or intervene directly in regional agricultural commodity markets, members are utilizing the membership directory and forum threads to share unglamorous survival strategies—such as moving to slightly larger format tomme wheels to trim labor costs per pound without altering core recipes.

## Committee engagement and resource demands

Producers facing these cost pressures are increasingly turning to committee calls—particularly within Membership & Outreach—seeking practical resources rather than abstract policy debates. Producers want actionable guidance on heating costs for small aging rooms and clearer explanations of margin-coverage programs. The challenge for staff is that these inquiries demand tailored technical resources that our current collateral does not fully address.
