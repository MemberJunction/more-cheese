**Subject:** Cave equipment capacity and August drift

Late-summer heat and outdoor humidity are testing member aging rooms across our growing regions this month. Our public post on August cave management highlights the classic compressor-and-humidifier tug-of-war, but behind the published guidance we are seeing a spike in inquiries from small farmstead creameries whose aging rooms are fundamentally undersized for their heat load. This memo reviews what our course enrollments tell us about member response and what support Advocacy needs to prepare for upcoming regulatory comment periods.

## What the education pipeline shows

Course enrollments for the week ending 10 August stand at 46 new course sign-ups, continuing a steady summer demand for technical instruction. Members are not just reading about humidity; they are registering for structured training. Specifically, the Maturation Planning for Small Rooms (Granite cohort) starts on 22 August, and inquiries into psychrometric chart reading have risen among producers who previously relied entirely on rule-of-thumb adjustments.

However, training on psychrometric charts does not fix a walk-in cooler whose compressor runs constantly and never catches up. When a small creamery has outgrown its aging space, August heat exposes the mismatch between room volume and heat load. We need to be careful that our course advice is not interpreted as telling members they can calibrate their way out of a physical capacity bottleneck.

## Equipment and sensor drift

The most common first-line error reported by members is chasing a humidity drop with an undersized humidifier that injects heat right back into the refrigerated space. Before any equipment replacement is budgeted, our technical notes emphasize verifying the hygrometer against a reference instrument. A five-percent sensor drift is enough to send a cheesemaker into an unnecessary over-correction cycle.

For rooms that are correctly sized but suffering from uneven airflow, simple operational adjustments such as scheduled shelf rotation remain under-utilized. Yet, where walk-ins are pushed beyond their thermal limits by regional temperature spikes, no amount of sensor calibration or salt-tray buffering will prevent rind defects.

- Advocacy & Standards: incorporate small-scale aging room equipment limitations into our ongoing discussions around FSMA small-producer exemptions and compliance recordkeeping.
- Communications: ensure that future technical write-ups clearly distinguish between adjustable environmental drift and underlying refrigeration capacity constraints.
