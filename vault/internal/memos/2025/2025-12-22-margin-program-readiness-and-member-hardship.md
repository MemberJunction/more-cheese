**Subject:** Margin program payout readiness and small-creamery hardship

The federal margin coverage program triggered its first and only payment of the year in December, arriving just as members are closing their books on what has proved to be a punishing stretch for regional creameries. While Communications prepares its public post on the margin payment, this memo reviews our operational posture, current membership metrics as the year closes, and the practical questions members are bringing to our desk about multi-year lock-in contracts and six-million-pound production ceilings.

## What the year-end numbers tell us

Our current member count stands at 1,448 active accounts as of this week. For the week ending 28 December 2025, membership purchases totaled 29, bringing in a gross of $6,384 across all tiers, led by 22 Individual memberships, three SmallBusiness enrollments, three Enthusiast accounts, and one Corporate renewal. Confirmed orders across the federation reached 126 with a gross value of $19,602 and four cancellations, while cash captured hit $9,250.

These figures sit against a backdrop of continuing margin compression. Feed costs and energy inflation have held stubborn all year while milk prices drifted lower. The December margin payment will offer modest relief for farmstead members enrolled at the top tier, but those at lower tiers received nothing. The distinction is driving renewed member questions about whether to commit to multi-year contracts through 2031 or remain on annual renewals.

## Member inquiries and administrative readiness

Calls to our team indicate that creameries are attempting to close their financial records much earlier than usual this year, often between Christmas and New Year, rather than waiting for January. That shift means our staff and committee resources are fielding questions on margin rules and production limits while operating on holiday schedules.

The two key adjustments in the federal program—moving the production ceiling at the top coverage tier up to six million pounds and introducing multi-year contracts through 2031—require members to pull exact production histories rather than rolling forward last year's estimates. Several regional members have noted that the paperwork is daunting for an operation already stretched by labor and feed expenses. We need to ensure our explanatory notes remain strictly factual and do not cross into financial or tax advice.

## Capacity and cross-team coordination

As event registrations for the coming weeks pick up—including 81 registrations taken for EVT-2026-W4 Workshop: Cedar Cut & Wrap in Courtenay and 11 for EVT-2026-W3 Workshop: Stone Sensory in Tandil—our operational bandwidth is heavily committed. We must ensure that members calling about margin coverage or FSA paperwork receive clear guidance without pulling staff away from upcoming course cohorts such as Affinage Fundamentals starting on 26 December.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure public messaging on the December margin payment clearly distinguishes top-tier payouts from general eligibility so members do not expect checks at lower tiers.
- Advocacy & Standards: maintain our running summary of federal support programs and rules changes so member inquiries can be answered with plain-language documentation.
- Membership Operations: continue tracking weekly membership purchases and order confirmations through the year-end close without assuming preliminary figures are final.
