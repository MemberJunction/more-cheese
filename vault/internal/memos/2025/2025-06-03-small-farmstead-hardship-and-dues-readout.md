**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Advocacy & Standards; Communications
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 3 June 2025
**Subject:** Small farmstead hardship and membership dues readout

Regional feed and energy cost inflation continues to drive small farmstead creamery distress across our core regions, and our membership numbers for the week ending June 8 reflect the strain. Current membership stands at 1,192 members. While we recorded 21 membership purchases this week—comprising 16 Individual, 1 Corporate, 2 SmallBusiness, and 2 Enthusiast tiers—generating a gross of $4,764 with 12 first-time members, the intake velocity among small farmstead producers has slowed significantly compared to last quarter. Producers are contacting us to ask about grace periods rather than upgrading tiers, and several longtime members are letting renewals lapse past the customary window.

## What the payment and renewal record shows

Total confirmed orders for the week reached 68, generating a gross of $19,356 against 1 cancellation, with cash captured at $17,197. However, the aggregate figures mask a growing divergence between corporate renewals, which remain stable, and farmstead renewals, which are stalling. SmallBusiness and Individual memberships are seeing delayed payments. When we cross-reference this against inquiries about federal milk marketing orders and interstate shipping rules, the picture is clear: cash flow is tight, and members are questioning whether annual dues fit into an operating budget squeezed by rising input costs.

We also note that event registration intake for the ICF Annual Conference 2025 in Sacramento picked up slightly with 27 registrations taken this week (bringing our week's event registrations total to 29 for EVT-2025-CONF), but members are registering without booking optional workshops, pointing directly to cash conservation.

## Alignment with public advocacy and market notes

Our public commentary on interstate shipment and butter price highs notes that input costs and regulatory gaps are weighing on producers. Internally, we must recognize that these are not separate issues; they are two sides of the same financial squeeze. When butter hits a five-year high at auction and federal order make allowances shift Class III and IV prices down, small producers feel it immediately on their make sheets. Offering rigid payment terms right now risks converting temporary cash flow distress into permanent association churn.

## Capacity

Membership Operations is currently handling double the usual volume of payment plan inquiries and dues extension requests. If we continue managing these case-by-case manually without a standardized hardship protocol, our response times will slip past our service targets before the Sacramento conference arrives in July.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: authorize a formal 60-day dues grace period for SmallBusiness and Individual members in recognized distressed farmstead categories.
- Advocacy & Standards: coordinate with Membership Operations to ensure our member action alerts account for financial distress rather than assuming non-engagement.
- Communications: ensure upcoming newsletters avoid framing retention solely as an engagement issue when our data shows it is an input-cost issue.
