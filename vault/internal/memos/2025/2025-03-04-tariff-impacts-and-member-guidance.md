**Subject:** Tariff impacts on specialty cheese supply chains and member guidance

New tariff lines on goods moving across our primary trade lanes took effect this week, creating immediate uncertainty for small farmstead creameries and importing retailers. The trigger date was announced with unusual precision, giving members with export or import operations almost no runway between confirmation and implementation. While this is a customs and pricing issue rather than a food safety or compliance matter, it directly affects landed cost, which in turn influences wholesale purchasing decisions this quarter. This memo outlines what we are hearing from members, how we are coordinating with our working groups, and what guidance we are issuing to the field.

## What we are hearing from the field

Members moving specialty cheese across affected borders report that customs brokers drafted multiple duty schedules before ink dried on final texts. Meanwhile, retailers importing aged cheeses for tasting counters have opted to hold shipments rather than guess at new rates. Beyond direct product movement, several producers note that imported inputs such as cultures, rennet, and specialized packaging have increased in cost. This lands on top of a year already characterized by regional feed and energy cost inflation, which has driven small farmstead creamery distress.

Our advocacy channels have already recorded 15 petition signatures and 14 letter campaigns on labeling standards, alongside 14 petition signatures and 15 letter campaigns on dairy pricing reform for the week so far. While tariff schedules sit outside our direct control, these advocacy streams reflect the broader economic squeeze facing our membership.

## Action items for staff

Communications has already published an industry notice advising members to verify invoice dates and duty codes with their customs brokers rather than waiting for general headlines to settle. We must ensure that our advice remains practical and specific. We are asking affected members to submit a two-line note to advocacy@ detailing actual impact, including shipment lanes, duty additions per case, and whether shipments were held or repriced.
