**Subject:** Margin pressure and farmstead hardship readout

Enrollment for the federal government's annual dairy margin-protection program closes at the end of this month. Over the past several weeks, our member intake line has logged an unusual volume of calls from small farmstead creameries trying to decide whether to purchase margin coverage or defer routine equipment maintenance, such as cave dehumidifier repairs. This memo reviews what the current intake numbers show, where our membership stands at the end of the week, and how the spring margin outlook is affecting small-producer decisions in the regional supply chain.

## What the membership numbers show

As of this week, total membership stands at 1137. Purchases booked during the week of 24 March 2025 totaled 16 memberships, comprising 6 first-time members and 10 renewals or tier selections. By tier, this week's purchases include 1 Enthusiast membership, 4 SmallBusiness memberships, and 11 Individual memberships. Total gross revenue from these membership purchases is 3572 USD, part of a wider confirmed order volume of 56 orders totaling 10685 USD with zero cancellations and 10397 USD in cash captured.

These figures continue a trend we have monitored all quarter: while total membership remains stable, the mix of inquiries has shifted toward smaller operations asking about hardship provisions, grace periods, and whether the federation offers installment billing for annual dues. The tension between paying fixed operational costs and maintaining safety net protections is acute this year, driven by stubbornly high feed and energy costs against downwardly revised milk price forecasts.

## Feedback from regional calls and member inquiries

Our regional volunteer network reports that members are viewing administrative deadlines with a different lens than in previous years. Producers who have experienced a low-margin year before are treating margin coverage as a fixed operating expense, while newer or smaller members are questioning whether the premium outlay will yield a visible return. As noted in recent member spotlights—such as new affinage apprentices adjusting to daily humidity logs and cave management—the reality of running a small creamery in 2025 involves tight trade-offs between routine upkeep and compliance overhead.

We are also seeing questions about the federation's educational offerings. While course enrollments taken this week stood at 0, course completions reached 11, indicating that members who are already in training cohorts are finishing their requirements despite the operating pressure. However, prospective students are asking whether course fees can be deferred or bundled with advisory services.

## Capacity and staff constraints

Handling the increased volume of margin and dues inquiries is stretching our support queues. The Membership Operations team is currently fielding questions that historically went to regional extension offices or federal farm service bureaus. Because we do not sell insurance or administer government programs, our role is limited to directing members to their local farm service office for paperwork and pointing them to the Education Committee's explainer portal for the underlying calculation formulas.
