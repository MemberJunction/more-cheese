**Subject:** Export discount and member margin review

U.S. cheese exports closed out 2025 at a record volume, but our membership data and feedback from the field show a clear divergence between pounds shipped and bottom-line margins. Members selling through wholesale distributors like Continental Specialty Provisions report that strong volume is failing to translate into healthy milk checks. This memo reviews our current operational metrics for the week ending 21 December 2025, examines the gap between volume and value, and outlines what staff need to communicate as operations close for the year.

## The current operational picture

For the week of 15 December 2025, membership purchases reached a total of 28, generating gross revenue of $5070. Of those purchases, 10 were first-time members, distributed across Individual (19), Enthusiast (7), and SmallBusiness (2) tiers. Total confirmed orders across all federation activities reached 105, with a gross value of $17004 and a single cancellation. Cash captured for the week stood at $13699. Current active membership at week-end closed at 1442. On the education side, course enrollments taken reached 42, with 2 completions, and 1 Food Safety & HACCP Certificate was awarded to Jihun Suzuki. Advocacy logged 1 testimony on FSMA small-producer exemptions. Event registrations taken included 66 for EVT-2026-W4 Workshop: Cedar Cut & Wrap and 8 for EVT-2026-W3 Workshop: Stone Sensory.

## Reconciling volume and margin

The broader market context explains why members feel squeezed. Global dairy auction prices have softened through seven straight downward moves this fall, driven by climbing milk production while demand remains steady. U.S. cheese prices have held at a discount relative to global supply, which is why export volume hit record levels but left farmstead and small-batch producers struggling to cover feed and energy cost inflation. A cheesemaker selling bulk or wholesale faces a low anchor price set by cheap commodity cheese. Retail counters can protect their margins only if staff actively explain provenance, hand craft, and story rather than competing on price against discounted wheels sitting nearby.

## Implications for education and advocacy

Our educational offerings must directly address this cost squeeze as members plan their pricing for the new year. The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course is specifically designed to help producers separate volume from value and establish a price floor. Meanwhile, Advocacy & Standards continues to track regulatory pressures, including the FSMA small-producer exemptions where testimony was submitted this week. We must ensure our messaging to members acknowledges the reality of thin margins rather than simply celebrating top-line export records.

## What we need

- Communications: align blog summaries and member briefs with the margin reality described by producers rather than focusing exclusively on export volume.
- Education & Credentialing: promote the Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course to members struggling with wholesale margin compression.
- Advocacy & Standards: maintain tracking on federal exemptions and small-producer compliance burdens heading into 2026.
