**Subject:** Spring flush and spot milk pricing readout for small creameries

This week brings the peak of the spring flush across our core dairy regions, matching federal forecasters' upward revision of the 2025 milk production estimates. While pasture growth drives regional milk volumes higher, the reality on the ground for our smaller farmstead and artisan members diverges sharply based on purchasing structure. Members buying milk on the spot market are encountering meaningful softness at the farm gate, with spot quotes running well below the federal Class III benchmark in several key producing states. At the same time, specialized cheese demand remains remarkably resilient, keeping plant vats full and absorbing much of the flush where make-room capacity exists.

## The split in our membership experience

The current market conditions highlight a structural divide among our membership base. Creameries tied to fixed multi-year supply contracts are insulated from the spot price drop, neither benefiting from the cheap milk nor suffering from unexpected upward volatility. Conversely, smaller operations relying entirely on spot purchases are seeing immediate relief on their raw material input costs. However, smaller rooms without surplus vat capacity or cold storage are unable to scale production to capture the advantage of lower-priced milk, leaving them exposed to local hauling bottlenecks and processing constraints.

This dynamic feeds directly into the hardship and operational distress we have tracked under this year's economic pressures. Input inflation across feed and energy has squeezed operating margins for eighteen months, and while cheaper spot milk offers temporary relief for some, it is not a structural fix for creameries lacking storage flexibility. We must ensure our pricing and margin resources are framed around these operational realities rather than generalized dairy indices.

## Alignment with current federation resources

The federation's public messaging and educational offerings this month are already touching these themes, notably through the Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course and our ongoing advocacy work around FSMA small-producer exemptions, which recorded a coalition meeting this week. We need to ensure that staff answering member inquiries are aligned on what the spot price softness means: it is a seasonal window driven by the spring flush, not a permanent resetting of baseline milk costs heading into 2026.

Furthermore, membership acquisition data for the week shows 18 total purchases generating $3,231 in gross revenue, bringing our total member count at week-end to 1,175. Among these purchases, 13 are first-time members, predominantly within the Individual tier (15 purchases), alongside 2 Enthusiast and 1 SmallBusiness membership. This steady new-member intake suggests that despite input cost anxieties, independent makers and cheesemongers continue seeking federation resources to navigate supply volatility.
