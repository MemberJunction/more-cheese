We are three weeks from the ICF Annual Conference in Sacramento, which opens on 2025-07-15. This memo outlines the operational status of competition entries, judging panel rotations, and the quiet credential lapses currently sitting in the member portal. With 34 event registrations taken so far this week, the Sacramento conference pipeline is moving, but the administrative load around judging credentials and sample receiving windows requires immediate alignment across teams.

## The competition and receiving pipeline

As noted in our public communications this week, entries for the competition need to arrive and be checked in before judging begins. Because categories have staggered receiving windows this year to protect the cold chain, the Events Team has been fielding calls from small makers trying to align their transit schedules with our receiving docks. We currently show 85 total entries in the year-to-date competition dataset, spanning all six categories (Alpine Styles, Washed Rind, Aged Cheddar & Territorials, Blue Veined, Fresh & Pasta Filata, and Soft-Ripened). Staggering the receiving windows is essential, but it leaves zero margin for carrier delays.

Judging itself runs across two days, split by category, with accredited judges rotating through panels. The Awards & Competition Committee's structure of pairing one technical judge with one aesthetic judge — with both scores averaged — has eliminated the solo-judge outliers we saw in prior years, but it places a hard constraint on panel sizing. We cannot seat a panel without two fully accredited, current judges.

## The credential lapse problem

That constraint brings us to the credential lapse issue highlighted in this week's education blog post. The Judge Accreditation credential runs on a two-year clock and lapses quietly without a warning letter because it tracks active panel participation rather than static knowledge. Education & Credentialing confirms that several members who volunteered for judging panels in Sacramento have let their Sensory Evaluation Certificate prerequisite or their logged panel history lapse without noticing.

Checking the member portal for panel eligibility should be happening this week, not during check-in in Sacramento. If a judge arrives on site with an expired accreditation, we either have to turn them away from the panel or drop them to a supervised apprentice slot, which disrupts the pairing ratios.

## Capacity and next steps

Conference follow-up and registration processing are currently absorbing the bulk of our hours. The events schedule shows three events in the next eight weeks — Webinar: Cheese Photography 2025 on 2025-07-01, the Annual Conference in Sacramento on 2025-07-15, and Webinar: Recall Readiness 2025 on 2025-08-21 — leaving very little slack for manual credential verification.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: run an automated audit of all scheduled Sacramento judges against the credential database by Friday morning and flag anyone whose two-year clock has expired.
- Communications: reinforce the message that judge accreditation status must be verified in the portal before travel, preventing on-site disputes at check-in.
- Office of the Chief Executive: acknowledge that credential verification bottlenecks may require temporary on-site support from education staff during the pre-conference setup days.
