**Subject:** Raw-milk aging research, regulatory tracking, and member compliance readiness

Recent published research questioning the biological efficacy of the 60-day raw-milk aging rule has created immediate anxiety among farmstead producers and affineurs. While our public communications correctly frame this as research to monitor rather than a recall notice, our internal posture must be more operational. Members running small caves are asking whether their inventory timing needs immediate adjustment. This memo outlines what our advocacy team is tracking, how the Food Safety Committee is responding, and where our educational offerings must align.

## What the regulatory tracking shows

The October study indicating that avian influenza virus and other pathogens can survive past 60 days in raw-milk cheese has not yet triggered formal federal rulemaking, but state agricultural laboratories and federal food safety regulators are actively reviewing the data. As noted in recent advocacy coalition meetings on interstate cheese shipment, any future adjustment to the aging standard would disrupt inventory already aging on a clock in small caves across the country. Our role is not to lobby against scientific inquiry but to ensure that any regulatory modernization accounts for the operational realities of farmstead cheesemakers.

Members do not need to pull raw-milk cheese from shelves today, but they do need to tighten their documentation. An inspector examining a cave during an unannounced visit cares less about historical tradition and more about dated pH logs and herd health records. The Food Safety Committee, chaired by Gwen Whitfield, advises that members ensure their acidification logs are rigorous and contemporaneous.

## Education and compliance literacy

We cannot insulate small creameries from shifting regulatory scrutiny through advocacy alone. Producers need practical literacy in reading proposed rules, understanding pathogen control, and building response strategies before comment periods close. Our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate covers exactly these competencies. However, we have observed a disconnect between course enrollment and the urgency expressed by members facing inspection questions.

During this week of 8 December 2025, our education data shows zero certification awards and only a single enrollment, despite heightened anxiety regarding aging rules. We need to direct members toward structured food safety training rather than allowing them to rely on informal peer advice.

## Capacity and member support

Small farmstead creameries are currently navigating severe feed and energy cost inflation alongside these regulatory questions. Adding complex compliance burdens without clear, step-by-step guidance risks accelerating closures among producers who form the backbone of our membership base. Advocacy and education must coordinate closely over the coming weeks to provide unglamorous, highly practical compliance checklists.
