**Subject:** Creamery distress and membership grace window adjustments

First-quarter numbers show a total membership standing at 1139 current members at week end, with five membership purchases booked during this opening week, all at the Individual tier for a gross of 850 USD. Of those five purchases, three are first-time members. While top-line membership counts remain stable, our incoming inquiry queue tells a different story. Small farmstead creameries across the region are experiencing acute distress driven by feed and energy cost inflation. Several members calling in are navigating ownership transitions or quiet consolidations where their corporate affiliation has changed overnight. This memo outlines the operational pressure on our grace windows and proposes adjusting how we handle renewal notices for members whose payroll or operating accounts have been restructured during an acquisition.

## The reality of consolidation transitions

As noted in our recent member communications regarding quality managers adapting to post-consolidation oversight, the daily work in the aging cave continues even when the parent entity changes. However, the administrative side does not transfer cleanly. When a farmstead creamery is folded into a larger regional operation, accounts payable departments are reorganized, and annual dues renewals frequently lapse not because the maker wishes to leave the federation, but because the invoice routing is trapped between a legacy dairy and a new corporate parent.

In previous quarters, our automated system triggered a strict lapse after thirty days past the renewal date. Under the current cost inflation era, enforcing that cutoff without a manual check penalizes members who are already absorbing feed price spikes and acquisition paperwork. We tracked 84 confirmed orders this week totaling 14893 USD against one cancellation, but membership continuity requires a softer operational boundary for smaller producers caught in restructuring.

## Grace window extension for distressed accounts

We propose extending the standard grace period from thirty days to ninety days for any Individual or SmallBusiness tier account located in regions actively reporting supply chain and energy cost distress. This does not waive dues; it pauses automated termination notices while Membership Operations verifies whether the member's employer of record has changed through a recent acquisition.

This approach aligns with our advocacy work on dairy pricing reform and raw-milk aging rules. If members are spending their limited hours managing cash flow and regulatory compliance during a buyout, chasing them with automated billing notices damages retention more effectively than a tariff rumor ever could. The Membership & Outreach Committee can support this by reaching out directly to accounts flagged as transitional rather than relying on automated collection emails.
