This week's public blog post on specialty cheese deli counter sales touches on something our members have been reporting directly: input cost inflation on feed, energy, and hauling has made this stretch the tightest margin period in a decade for small farmstead and small-batch creameries. The public text points toward low-cost tactics—standing sample plates and trial sizes—rather than heavy marketing spend. This memo reviews how that guidance intersects with our current membership intake and what our retail members are actually experiencing on the ground.

## What the membership figures show

Membership intake for the week ending Sunday 6 July stands at 11 purchases totaling $2,503 gross, with 9 first-time members joining across Individual (7), SmallBusiness (3), and Enthusiast (1) tiers. Total membership stands at 1,211 at week's end. While the new intake is steady, field reports from regional members indicate that cash flow remains under pressure. The retail counter approach highlighted in our public messaging—focusing on human interaction and small formats rather than heavy advertising budgets—is a necessary response because members simply do not have the capital for larger promotional outlays right now.

## The gap between advice and reality

We need to be careful about how we frame this guidance. Telling a farmstead maker whose margin is compressed by energy and feed bills to spend extra time cutting sample wedges is sensible from a sales perspective, but it assumes they have available labor. As our retail spotlight member noted for the holiday weekend, staffing the counter with someone who knows the product is the hardest part of the operation. In smaller farmstead units, the person making the cheese is often the person working the market stall or deli counter. Recommending additional customer-facing effort without acknowledging the labor bottleneck risks frustrating members who are already stretched thin.

## Alignment with upcoming events

We should ensure our upcoming programming reinforces practical relief rather than generic marketing. The Annual Conference in Sacramento on 15 July (with 67 registrations taken this week and 402 total registered in the year data) will give us an immediate read on how small producers are managing these pressures. Similarly, the virtual webinar on Cheese Photography held on 1 July offered low-cost digital staging tips for direct-to-consumer sellers, which pairs well with the sampling discussion. However, we should avoid overselling these tactics as a complete fix for structural input cost inflation.
