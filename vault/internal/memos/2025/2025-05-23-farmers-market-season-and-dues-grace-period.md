**Subject:** Farmers market season and dues grace-period adjustments for distressed creameries

As regional feed and energy cost inflation continues to drive farmstead creamery distress this spring, membership operations has been tracking an unusual pattern in this week's renewals and new purchases. With the farmers market season opening across our regions, several producers who let their membership lapse or faced payment delays on wholesale accounts are reaching out to ask about direct-to-consumer payment terms. This memo outlines what our data shows for the week of Monday 19 May 2025 and proposes a structured grace period for small producers pivoting their sales channels away from lost wholesale accounts.

## What the week's numbers show

For the week ending Sunday 25 May 2025, our current membership count stands at 1,177. Membership purchases for the week totaled 18, comprising 7 first-time members and 11 renewals or returning accounts. Across the tiers, the breakdown includes 13 Individual memberships, 2 SmallBusiness memberships, 2 Corporate memberships, and 1 Enthusiast membership. Gross membership purchases for the week brought in $5,082.

Total confirmed orders for the week reached 59, generating a gross order volume of $17,889 against 1 cancellation, with cash captured at $14,576. Notably, event registrations taken this week include 27 registrations for the ICF Annual Conference 2025, which takes place in Sacramento on 15 July. While conference interest remains steady, the composition of our small-business membership tier shows increasing strain: two small-business accounts requested an extension on their annual dues invoice, citing cash-flow gaps tied to rising feed bills and delayed wholesale settlements.

## The direct-to-consumer pivot and payment friction

As noted in our recent public coverage of opening market weekends, many producers are attempting to replace lost wholesale accounts by setting up direct market stands. However, our internal records reveal a data-quality bottleneck that complicates this transition. Specifically, we have identified a persistent mismatch in several member records where the primary contact organization name listed in our CRM does not match the active billing entity or where old wholesale distribution addresses remain attached to farmstead accounts that have shifted entirely to retail and market sales.

When producers attempt to register for market-readiness courses or update their profiles to reflect direct sales, these stale records trigger bounced notifications or payment reconciliation holds. Furthermore, our current grace-period rules are rigid, treating a delayed small-business dues payment the same way regardless of whether the producer is expanding or contracting. Under the current operating pressure, forcing a small farmstead creamery into immediate lapse status because their wholesale check cleared late is counterproductive.

## Capacity and administrative bottlenecks

Membership operations is currently managing these reconciliation requests alongside routine conference registration intake for Sacramento. The volume is manageable, but resolving mismatched organization profiles and verifying farmstead restructuring requires manual outreach that cuts into our processing time for new member onboarding. We cannot sustain ad-hoc extensions without a clear policy standard.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a temporary 45-day grace extension for SmallBusiness tier members who submit a written attestation of wholesale account disruption.
- Membership & Outreach: review the flagged organization records to clean up stale wholesale employer listings before we process upcoming market-season inquiries.
- Advocacy & Standards: coordinate with our team to ensure our internal records accurately reflect which farmstead members are actively operating retail market stands versus wholesale silos.
