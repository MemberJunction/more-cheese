**Subject:** Make allowance transition and small-producer impact under the new rule

With the federal milk-pricing overhaul taking effect on June 1, we are fielding an uptick in calls from farmstead and small-scale members trying to work out what the shift means for their next check. The public post on May 27 outlined the mechanics—specifically the twenty-five-year hold on manufacturing cost allowances and the removal of the barrel cheddar quote from the Class III formula—but our phone queue and portal messages show that members want help translating those formulas into their own contract realities. This memo sets out what we can and cannot say, how the numbers landed this week, and where our advocacy outreach should focus next.

## What the preliminary week figures show

As we close the week ending June 1, our overall activity reflects a steady operating rhythm against a difficult economic backdrop. Total confirmed orders stand at 62 with a gross value of 19688 USD, against cash captured of 18251 USD. Membership purchases total 15 for the week, bringing in a gross of 3525 USD, with 9 of those being first-time members. The breakdown by tier shows 1 Corporate, 11 Individual, 1 SmallBusiness, and 2 Enthusiast purchases. On the events side, we recorded 33 new registrations for the ICF Annual Conference 2025 in Sacramento, while course completions stand at 3. The current total membership count at week end is 1185. None of these figures directly isolate the new pricing rule, but they confirm that members are maintaining routine interactions while privately worrying about farm-gate margins.

## The advisory boundary

We must remain disciplined about our advisory boundary. We do not have visibility into individual co-op pooling decisions, proprietary milk buyer contracts, or specific plant utilization mixes. When a member calls asking for their farm's exact number under the June 1 rule, we cannot give it to them. What we can do—and what we should direct our team to emphasize—is pointing them to their marketing area's advance and final price bulletins once they post, and helping them structure a conversation with their milk buyer or co-op field representative.

Specifically, members should be bringing their last twelve months of utilization by class to their field reps right now. That is the only document that reveals whether the make-allowance increase will register as a minor rounding error or a severe squeeze on their net check.

## Committee alignment and sensory backstops

In conversations with committee chairs this week, it is clear that financial stress and technical compliance are intersecting. As noted in recent member spotlights, chairs are spending less time on abstract protocol drafting and more time on triage—helping members find the minimum viable version of testing and food safety plans when cash is short. Furthermore, with federal proficiency testing programs for Grade A milk labs experiencing pauses elsewhere in the sector, our training credentials—such as the Sensory Evaluation Certificate—take on renewed operational value. A trained palate and a shared vocabulary around defects provide a vital backstop when laboratory trails get thin.

## What we need

- Communications: Ensure our helpline guidance continues to direct members to marketing area bulletins rather than estimating individual farm checks.
- Membership Operations: Flag any member accounts reporting acute cash-flow distress from co-op settlement adjustments so we can route them appropriately.
- Advocacy & Standards: Prepare follow-up guidance once June final pricing posts on July 2, comparing projected impacts against actual milk check movements.
