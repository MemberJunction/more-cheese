**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Communications; Advocacy & Standards
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 31 July 2025
**Subject:** Milk supply trends and fall wholesale pricing readout

Federal trade and dairy price data released this month confirm that national milk production is running well ahead of last year with the national herd near a three-decade high, while milk-fat-equivalent export volumes set a new monthly record. For our farmstead and small creamery members, these aggregate figures mask a difficult reality. While large-scale processors benefit from export volumes and softening commodity floors, small producers face persistent feed and input costs that have not dropped alongside the milk check. This memo reviews our membership purchases for the week of July 28 and outlines how these market pressures affect our fall wholesale outreach.

## Membership and order activity for the week

For the week ending August 3, membership operations recorded 17 total membership purchases totaling $4,840 in gross sales, comprising 12 first-time members and 5 renewals. Purchases by tier include 2 Corporate, 9 Individual, 4 Enthusiast, and 2 SmallBusiness memberships. Across all association products, we logged 25 confirmed orders for a gross of $6,934 with zero cancellations, and captured $5,019 in cash. Event registrations taken this week stand at 6 for the upcoming Workshop: Cave Farm Shop Retail, and course enrollments reached 18.

These figures show steady baseline engagement despite the seasonal slowdown, but membership inquiries this week reflect genuine margin anxiety among small farmstead members. A herd of forty cows does not capture economies of scale from record export volumes of butter and dry whey, leaving farmstead producers vulnerable to flat feed bills and softer commodity pricing.

## The specialty split at the case

Trade reporting indicates that while plain commodity-style American cheese slumps at retail, specialty and artisan varieties continue to show relative resilience. Our members operating on the specialty side of the market are largely insulated from direct block-market fluctuations if their distributors maintain strong export relationships. However, distributors whose books are heavy in shrinking export categories are pushing back on wholesale pricing for the fall case.

We must ensure our communications and advisory resources do not give blanket pricing advice. As noted in our public guidance, members should pull actual trade reports rather than reacting to broad headlines. Our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course remains our primary operational tool for members rebuilding cost models against shifting input costs.

## Farmstead support and closure risk

With regional feed and energy cost inflation driving small farmstead creamery distress across our regions, our farmstead and closure resources remain heavily utilized. Small producers contacting the office are not asking for macroeconomic forecasts; they are asking how to restructure wholesale pricing when fixed overhead exceeds the farm-gate milk check. We need closer coordination between Membership Operations and Advocacy & Standards to ensure regional reps can connect distressed members to peer networks before arrears turn into permanent lapses.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure upcoming blog posts and newsletters continue directing members to the Pricing & Margin course and trade report resources rather than oversimplified market summaries.
- Advocacy & Standards: compile a short briefing on current federal milk price reporting to assist regional reps answering member inquiries.
- Membership Operations: flag any SmallBusiness or Individual tier renewals from farmstead members showing consecutive grace-window requests.
