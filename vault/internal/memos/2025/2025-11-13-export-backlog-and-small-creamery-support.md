**Subject:** Export backlog, federal rulemaking, and small creamery support

The federal funding lapse that stalled comment periods and export paperwork through October is moving toward resolution under new appropriations legislation. As noted in this week's blog coverage, agencies are reopening dockets on staggered schedules and export certification processing is returning in a gradual trickle rather than a single flood. This memo reviews how that backlog affects our small-producer members, what data we currently hold on advocacy actions, and where our departments must coordinate support over the next six weeks.

The comment period reality and member engagement requires careful attention. The interstate cheese shipment proposal and raw-milk aging dockets are reopening individually, and members cannot assume their previous drafts or timelines hold. We recorded 14 petition signatures and 15 letter campaign actions directed at FSMA small-producer exemptions during this week, reflecting a modest rebound in engagement as agency notices reappear. However, members who sat out the six-week pause have a significant gap in their public record. Our advocacy digest is tracking revised deadlines, but regional reps must remind creameries that waiting until a deadline's final week carries severe risk when agency turnaround is still constrained.

Export documentation and the shipping calendar represent another operational hurdle. For creameries that ship internationally, the trickle of export certificate processing is creating severe planning friction. Documentation turnaround has approximately doubled for several members, matching the operational distress described in our member spotlights. We do not yet have a federation-wide aggregate number for delayed shipments, but export documentation inquiries to our team are up significantly. Creameries must build slack into their shipping windows rather than quoting hard dates to overseas buyers. Telling a buyer that a shipment window is flexible is difficult up front, but it prevents the cascading penalties of missed vessel departures.

Departmental coordination across our organization is vital. Our advocacy work cannot be separated from education and membership operations. Small farmstead creameries facing energy and feed cost inflation are making stark choices about whether to invest time in regulatory filings or day-to-day survival. When a member contacts membership operations about dues hardship or delayed export paperwork, that is a signal that our advocacy resources and education offerings — such as our export documentation guidance — need to be pushed directly into their hands without waiting for them to browse the dashboard.

What we need to execute immediately includes several actionable steps. Membership Operations must flag any member reporting doubled export documentation turnaround so we can track the operational pattern across regions. Education & Credentialing should ensure discussion threads on export documentation remain actively monitored by staff for emerging slowdown reports. Events Team needs to incorporate practical regulatory check reminders into upcoming workshop messaging for December events. Finally, everyone must reinforce to members that open rulemaking dockets require immediate rechecking of posted agency deadlines.
