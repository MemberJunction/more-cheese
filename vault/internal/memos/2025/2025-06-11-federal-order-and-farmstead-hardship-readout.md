This memo outlines the operational impact of the federal milk pricing formula update that took effect on June 1, 2025, alongside our ongoing tracking of farmstead creamery distress driven by regional feed and energy cost inflation. ## The pricing formula shift and butterfat pressure

The revised Class I skim-milk pricing formula and updated manufacturer make allowances are now active. While not arriving with a public press conference, the recalculated baseline is showing up on June settlement statements across our membership. For farmstead producers purchasing supplementary milk or cream, the high butterfat baseline—sitting above 7,900 USD per ton for four consecutive months—continues to compress margins on rich-paste styles and triple-crèmes. Supplying creameries report firmer supplier quotes and shorter negotiation windows this spring. Producers relying entirely on their own herds are less exposed to the direct formula change, but feed, hauling, and energy cost inflation remain at multi-year highs.

## Raw-milk sampling and regulatory contact

The ongoing federal sampling assignment on 60-day aged raw-milk cheese, running since January 2025, has generated considerable inquiry from our raw-milk producers. We must reiterate internally and in member guidance that this sampling exercise is a data-gathering operation, not a rule change or emergency order. Members contacting us regarding inspector visits are advised to document collections carefully and route correspondence through their co-op field reps or state agriculture contacts rather than federation staff. Our Food Safety Committee continues to monitor the process for any formal rulemaking petitions.

## Capacity and cross-team alignment

Conference preparation for the upcoming ICF Annual Conference 2025 in Sacramento (scheduled for July 15, 2025) is active, with 24 registrations taken this week. However, staff bandwidth remains constrained as we balance conference logistics with member hardship inquiries and inquiries regarding federal order changes. Communications published a blog post on June 9 addressing the pricing formula shift, and a second post on June 11 detailing off-calendar seasonal dairying constraints from an island sheep dairy member experiencing extreme drought.
