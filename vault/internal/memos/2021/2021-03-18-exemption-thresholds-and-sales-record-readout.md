The Food Safety Committee has received a rising volume of inquiries this month regarding federal food-safety rules and small-producer exemptions. Following recent public guidance and our ongoing coalition work on FSMA small-producer exemptions—which recorded a coalition meeting action this week—this memo sets out what our records require and where members are misinterpreting their rolling three-year sales calculations. Exemption status is not an administrative box checked once; it requires continuous documentation that matches what inspectors examine on site.

## Exemption mechanics and rolling thresholds

Members frequently assume that dropping below a sales ceiling in a single year secures an exemption permanently. Regulatory review evaluates average annual sales over a multi-year window, meaning a strong recovery year can push a small creamery across the statutory threshold without the operator noticing until an inspector arrives.

Our advocacy team has noted that members maintaining clean, running sales logs alongside their HACCP records experience significantly shorter and less contentious inspections. Conversely, creameries that attempt to reconstruct sales figures during an audit routinely trigger secondary document requests. The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate program now integrates a dedicated module covering these exact calculation errors, but we need Membership Operations to help flag accounts approaching the boundary before renewal time.

## Insurance and liability exposure

Another recurring gap identified by the committee is the assumption that exemption from routine inspection equates to protection from liability. An exempt producer facing a product recall or illness outbreak carries the exact same legal and financial exposure as a fully inspected plant.

Several farmstead members carrying only basic farm-policy liability limits discovered upon review that their coverage excludes specialized cheese distribution risks. Recommending a specialized insurance broker conversation alongside the exemption paperwork is a necessary preventive step, though the federation must frame this as operational guidance rather than formal financial advice.

## Capacity and committee workflow

Coalition meetings and direct member inquiries on raw-milk aging rules and labeling standards continue to consume staff hours. The Food Safety Committee cannot field every individual exemption recalculation without straining our other advocacy commitments. We must rely on clear self-service materials and targeted committee referrals.
