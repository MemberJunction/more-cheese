The Standards Committee held its Q1 2021 meeting on 11 January 2021, and the agenda was dominated by the federal comment period on interstate cheese shipment closing at the end of this month. Alongside that rulemaking, the committee formally noted a mid-term vacancy following a member stepping down from their seat. This memo outlines what the committee decided, how we are handling the comment guidance published in this week's blog, and what we need from Membership Operations to fill the committee seat before the Q2 cycle.

## Standards Committee meeting readout

The Q1 meeting concluded that our advocacy messaging must center on specific working numbers rather than general objections. As noted in our public guidance this week, the rulemaking addresses lightened compliance paths for small-batch interstate shipments and temperature-logging requirements at pickup and delivery. The committee reviewed the advocacy totals for the preceding year—which stand at 100 total actions across petition signatures, coalition meetings, letter campaigns, and testimony—and agreed that small-batch producers need practical assistance translating their shipment counts into comments before the end-of-month deadline.

We are not taking an institutional position telling members what to write. Instead, the committee reaffirmed our role: providing a plain-language explainer and a letter template so that working creameries can submit three-sentence letters detailing what they ship, how they ship it, and what specific temperature-logging requirements would cost or save them. Generic complaints are easily dismissed by regulators; a hundred specific letters from working creameries are not.

## The committee vacancy

The committee also addressed a vacant seat resulting from an unexpired term. Under our governance rules, filling this seat requires an open call to the membership via Membership Operations, targeting producers with active experience in dairy pricing reform, labeling standards, or raw-milk aging rules—topics that accounted for 21, 22, 18, and 21 advocacy actions respectively across our historical counts. We cannot leave the committee operating below quorum for the Q2 initiatives.

## Capacity and coordination

Communications is managing the public explainer and the open advocacy calls where members can talk through their shipment figures before writing their letters. Advocacy & Standards is supporting those calls directly. However, the overlap between running the comment-period outreach and managing the committee appointment process is putting strain on our shared workflow. We need Membership Operations to pull the eligible candidate list for the committee seat while we focus on the end-of-month comment deadline.
