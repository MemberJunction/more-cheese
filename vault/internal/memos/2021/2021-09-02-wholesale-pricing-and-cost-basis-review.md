**Subject:** Wholesale pricing review and cost-basis alignment

National milk output has started to slow after running well ahead of last year for most of the year. That shift is already showing up in firmer cheese and butter prices across several regions, and members selling into wholesale channels report that buyers are slightly less willing to wait for a better number. While this firmer wholesale tone is a welcome relief after a spring where many members were pricing defensively, a slowdown in production growth is not the same thing as a shortage. This memo reviews how members should evaluate their price sheets in light of these market movements without chasing headline numbers.

## What the market shift means for members

When milk production growth cools on a slower-growing base, cheese moves a bit easier. Members who have had wholesale accounts go quiet on negotiating and simply place their orders instead are experiencing the net effect of this plateau. However, prices that move up on thinner supply can move back just as fast if large operations decide to push output in response.

For members setting a fall price sheet, the useful move is the boring one: check your own cost basis against where your prices actually sit, rather than chasing the market number itself. A firmer wholesale tone is good news, but your margin lives in the gap between your input costs and your price, rather than in the headline direction of Class III. If a member has not revisited their price sheet since spring, this is a reasonable week to do it.

## Feeding into the member guidance

We need to ensure that communications going out this week remain aligned with what producers are telling the Membership & Outreach Committee on the ground. Feed costs have stayed elevated enough to matter for anyone deciding on herd size, and margin-support coverage for August has firmed up slightly as milk prices ticked up and feed costs eased off their highs. These are small signals, but they point in the same direction.

Communications should emphasize that checking cost bases is a matter of hygiene rather than an attempt to time the market. We must avoid making any forward-looking forecasts about winter supply that could be misread as official market advice.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure weekly blog framing directs members to review their price sheets against local input costs rather than headline market rates.
- Membership Operations: flag any accounts reporting severe pricing disputes during this transition so Advocacy & Standards can review them.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that regional feed cost variances are creating uneven margin pressures across small versus large operations.
