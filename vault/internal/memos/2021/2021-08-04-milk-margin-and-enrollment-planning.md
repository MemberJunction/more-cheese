**Subject:** Milk margin pressures and the fall enrollment window

The federal dairy margin data for July landed this week at $5.68 per hundredweight, marking the lowest reading since May 2020 and a drop of 56 cents from June. That decline is driven by an all-milk price falling to $17.90 per hundredweight alongside flat-to-rising feed costs, with regional feed contracts in drought-affected areas running tighter than national averages imply. Because this squeeze coincides with our upcoming fall program window, we need to decide whether our current course and membership renewal communications address member cash-flow anxiety directly, or whether we are talking past the realities of small creamery balance sheets.

## What the numbers and feedback show

Our current week-over-week operational tracking shows 3 membership purchases, all in the Individual tier, bringing confirmed orders for the week to 3 with a gross of $459 and $306 captured in cash. We recorded zero new course enrollments and 3 course completions, alongside 1 advocacy action focused on small-producer exemptions under FSMA. While membership stands at 464 current members at week's end, renewal discussions on our pricing listserv indicate that members are increasingly concerned about feed costs, particularly for alfalfa sourced from western drought zones. National averages smooth over these regional spikes, but our members are paying the higher figures on their bills today.

For makers selling finished cheese rather than raw milk, the read is slightly different. A softer milk price can ease input costs on the way in, but retail demand has held up well enough that most members have not chased volume with price cuts. However, two consecutive soft months after a firmer spring warrant caution. We should encourage members to examine their coverage and pricing assumptions now rather than waiting for the fall enrollment crunch.

## What the public posts have opened

Our public communications published a piece on July's margin slide on August 2, followed by an announcement of two free webinars: Herd Health & Milk Quality on August 18 and Raw Milk Rules on August 24. Both webinars are virtual and open to all members, requiring no course enrollment or credential in progress. Members are looking for practical guidance, and these sessions will field live questions on somatic cell counts, flavor defects, and interstate shipment rules. We must ensure our internal staff are prepared for the inbound questions these topics will generate.

## Capacity and alignment

Education and membership teams are currently balancing routine inquiries with preparations for the August webinars. If members use the webinar Q&A sessions to ask detailed questions about pricing and margin coverage, we need our support materials ready. We should coordinate our outreach so that members inquiring about milk quality or raw milk rules are also directed to appropriate educational resources without creating an administrative backlog.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: review our current renewal messaging to ensure we acknowledge margin pressures without offering unauthorized financial advice.
- Education & Credentialing: confirm that registration links and Q&A handling for the August 18 and August 24 webinars are fully tested.
- Advocacy & Standards: prepare a brief reference guide on FSMA small-producer exemptions for staff fielding member inquiries after the raw milk session.
