**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Advocacy & Standards
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 21 October 2021
**Subject:** Member lapse handling and grace-window requests

Membership Operations is currently handling an unusual volume of inquiries regarding renewal grace windows and failed payment notices. As we approach the end of October, several small creameries that joined or renewed during the autumn push are encountering cash flow friction. This memo reviews our current operational constraints around grace periods, outlines the tension between rigid automated billing rules and our retention goals in the virtual pivot era, and proposes a structured waiver process for accounts requesting extended grace windows before the holiday ordering season locks in.

## Current volume and renewal friction

Federal market reporting this week shows Class I milk prices climbing roughly fifty cents per hundredweight while cheese prices soften, creating a split margin squeeze for small cheesemakers. Against this macroeconomic backdrop, our weekly tracking shows five new membership purchases booked this week, bringing our current members at week end to 484. However, renewal notices sent earlier this month have triggered requests for grace-window extensions from accounts facing delayed payments from wholesale buyers. Our standard automated cutoff terminates access after a thirty-day grace window, a rule originally designed for stability but one that risks turning temporary cash flow delays into permanent lapses.

In previous years, rigid enforcement worked without significant backlash, but the 2021 retention surge among small creameries relies heavily on flexibility. When an account calls in a panic because a distributor delayed a pallet payment, telling them their membership has lapsed does not encourage them to renew; it encourages them to drop out as a hedge against perceived administrative hostility. We need a consistent internal policy rather than ad-hoc exceptions.

## Operational constraints and the data limits

Our billing data shows that orders confirmed this week total five with gross sales of 1,430 USD, while cash captured stands at 437 USD. The gap between gross orders and cash captured illustrates why members are asking for grace: accounts are billing wholesale clients for holiday stock, but invoice collection cycles have stretched from thirty to sixty days across the distribution tier. Membership Operations cannot indefinitely float unpaid accounts without distorting our financial reporting, but neither can we afford to lose long-standing small business members over a two-week payment lag.

We also face internal capacity limits. The same staff managing membership renewals are assisting with preparation for the upcoming Webinar: Packaging Sustainability 2021 on November 2 and handling inquiries for the Maturation Planning for Small Rooms (Meadow cohort) course that started on October 20. Staff time spent arguing over automated expiration notices is time pulled away from onboarding new first-time members.

## Proposed grace-window protocol

To balance financial rigor with our retention goals, Membership Operations recommends implementing a standardized forty-five-day grace window for SmallBusiness and Individual tiers upon written request, provided the member submits a signed payment schedule. This avoids permanent lapses while giving accounts breathing room through the holiday shipping crunch. Accounts requesting extensions beyond forty-five days would require explicit review by Membership Operations rather than automatic termination.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the forty-five-day extended grace window protocol for accounts affected by wholesale distribution delays.
- Advocacy & Standards: confirm whether extending grace windows conflicts with any voting eligibility rules for upcoming committee ballots.
- Membership Operations: draft the member-facing communication template for grace-window requests by Monday.
