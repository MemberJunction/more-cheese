**Subject:** Small-producer pricing, feed costs, and the Class III signal

The federal price announcement pointing to climbing Class III and Class IV prices this spring has triggered a wave of member inquiries. Small producers selling direct or wholesale want to know if they should immediately raise their invoice prices. This memo reviews what the federal number means for our membership base, where the actual cost pressures lie, and how our communications and education teams should frame the discussion so we do not mislead makers into assuming commodity benchmarks apply directly to artisan wheels.

## What the Class III signal does and does not do

A rising Class III does not hand our members a higher price automatically. It changes what buyers expect to see and what fluid milk is worth if a creamery is buying it in rather than milking its own herd. Our members mostly sell hand-turned wheels, fresh chèvre, and small-batch clothbound cheddar, not 40-pound commodity blocks. Conflating the two at a distributor meeting is a quick way to lose margin.

Furthermore, margin coverage is tighter than the headline price suggests once feed and fluid-milk costs are factored in. Feed costs have been stubborn this year, erasing gains faster than a Class III move can cover them. If a maker is setting summer pricing, their cost sheet is a safer guide than the federal announcement.

## What the public post and courses should reinforce

The public blog post going up this week correctly advises members to bring their own cost numbers to distributor negotiations rather than copying federal numbers onto a price sheet. We need to back that up internally and ensure our educational offerings point makers in the right direction.

The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course walks through how to read a Class III move against a real cost sheet rather than a headline. Meanwhile, the Export Documentation Essentials course remains critical for members dipping a toe into overseas shipments following recent strong export numbers. We should make sure those pointers are visible in member comms without overstating what a national commodity price series can do for a regional creamery.
