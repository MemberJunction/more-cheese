**Subject:** Feed cost pressures and member pricing decisions

This week's public blog post on milk and feed prices has triggered a high volume of informal outreach from farmstead members, particularly west of the Mississippi where drought conditions have pushed hay and feed invoices up sharply. While the headline Class III and Class IV futures prices appear relatively calm, our members report operating costs that diverge significantly from those national benchmarks. This memo outlines the feedback gathered by the Membership & Outreach Committee and recommends how staff should support producers through upcoming price reviews.

## What members are experiencing

Producers in drought-affected regions report feed expenses running close to double last year's figures, with transportation costs compounding the issue as local hay supplies dwindle. The core frustration among farmstead members is the widening gap between their actual monthly cost-per-hundredweight and the pool milk price. Although specialty cheese demand remains resilient, producers who have not adjusted their wholesale price sheets since January are quietly absorbing margin compression that threatens their summer cash flow.

Several members have begun reworking their pricing models from the make sheet up, incorporating current feed and labor expenses rather than relying on historical multipliers. However, many are hesitant to bring those revised numbers to wholesale buyers, particularly given the shift toward virtual negotiations where competitors' catalogs are only a browser tab away.

## Alignment with association programming

Today's free webinar on wholesale negotiation arrives at an opportune moment, addressing the exact communication and pacing challenges members face on video calls. Furthermore, the upcoming coursework on specialty pricing provides the structured cost-calculation framework producers need to defend their price sheets. Staff should ensure that these educational offerings are actively cross-referenced when members reach out with margin concerns, rather than treating them as isolated calendar entries.

We must also acknowledge that membership purchases this week remain modest—totaling three confirmed orders (one Corporate and two Individual) for a gross of 1,181 USD, with 547 USD in cash captured—reflecting a cautious operating environment. Retention among small creameries is holding, but support must be practical and direct.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: compile a list of members in drought-designated regions who have active support tickets regarding cost-sheet revisions so the committee can conduct peer outreach.
- Advocacy & Standards: review whether any federal dairy outlook data or feed assistance program summaries can be condensed into a brief reference sheet for farmstead members before the next board cycle.
- Communications: ensure future blog posts continue to link practical cost-accounting resources directly to upcoming educational sessions.
