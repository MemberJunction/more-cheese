**From:** Education & Credentialing

**Subject:** Certification record audit and federal rule impacts

This week brings two distinct operational friction points that overlap in our database and deserve a coordinated response before the fall renewal and exam cycle accelerates. First, we are seeing recurring inquiries regarding certification prerequisites and credit histories, particularly for members who completed coursework across different cohorts. Second, the new federal labeling rule taking effect this week has prompted several credentialed mongers to ask whether our credential records reflect their current professional standing accurately enough for third-party compliance verification.

## The certification record backlog

For the year to date, our records show zero certification enrollments and zero certifications awarded during this specific week, sitting against a year-end total of five certification enrollments and four awards across our various certificate programs. While the volume is low during the summer months, the administrative overhead per record is climbing. Several members attempting to register for advanced modules or credential renewals are hitting roadblocks where their prior course completions—such as those from the 2020 and early 2021 cohorts—are not cleanly linked to their active member profiles.

This is not a system failure, but rather a data hygiene issue. When members update their business affiliations or email addresses, their historical credential records occasionally detach from their active membership ID. Membership Operations and Education need to establish a weekly reconciliation protocol so that credential checks do not stall when a candidate applies for an exam or requests a replacement certificate under the stricter regulatory environment.

## Intersection with origin and labeling standards

The Advocacy & Standards committee has rightly pointed out that our members are examining every label and website claim this month due to the updated federal origin rules. Credentialed cheesemongers and farmstead producers frequently list their ICF certifications on product inserts, counter cards, and websites to substantiate their expertise. If an auditor or a commercial buyer cross-references an ICF credential and finds a discrepancy between the member's current status and our database, it creates unnecessary exposure for the creamery.

We need to ensure that every active certification record is bulletproof. A member holding a Food Safety & HACCP Certificate or a Certified Cheesemonger credential must be able to pull a verifiable record instantly. At present, our manual verification turnaround is too slow for members facing sudden compliance audits or retailer vetting.

## Capacity and next steps

Neither team has dedicated staff for a full data migration, but we cannot leave credential verification half-automated. We propose a joint review of all pending credential accounts before the September course cohorts begin. This will prevent small administrative oversights from turning into member attrition during a period when farmstead margins are already constrained by feed and operational costs.

- Membership Operations: provide a list of all active membership accounts with pending credential inquiries by Friday.
- Education & Credentialing: audit the 2021 certification award log to ensure every completed exam matches our generated records.
- Advocacy & Standards: confirm whether our standard certification disclaimer language requires any updates in light of the new trade regulations.
