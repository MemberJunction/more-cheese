## Small-producer exemption paperwork and inspection reality

Federal preventive-controls rules include a size-based exemption, but members continue to report that regional inspectors expect comprehensive documentation regardless of the rule's letter. Producers running single-vat creameries find themselves assembling full food-safety plans simply because inspectors cannot provide clear guidance on what lighter records require. Our recent advocacy work shows 100 total actions recorded in the system, with 18 tied specifically to FSMA small-producer exemptions and another 21 covering raw-milk aging rules. Members want a short, plain-language checklist rather than vague assurances. When creameries reach out to Membership Operations or our committee chairs, they are looking for practical templates, not legal theory.

## Spot milk premiums and plant staffing

Simultaneously, the spot market reflects a widening spread. As noted in industry reports and member feedback, bottling demand has eased with schools reopening, but cheese plants remain short-staffed. This labor bottleneck means that even when raw milk is available, plants cannot always take delivery or run second shifts. Spot prices range from roughly a dollar and a quarter under Class price to a dollar over it depending on region. For members setting fourth-quarter contracts, we must continue advising them to price off their own cost bases rather than treating spot premiums as a baseline. The practical constraint is delivery lead time; distributors should build slack into their fall schedules.
