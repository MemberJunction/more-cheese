**Subject:** Border pricing and import case review following the tariff truce

The five-year truce in the aircraft subsidy trade dispute, announced last week, has suspended the 25 percent tariff on European cheese imports. This memo reviews what that suspension means for our import case, how members are handling landed costs, and what domestic specialty producers are telling us about shelf competition. Communications published a public summary on 21 June; this document records the internal operational guidance we are giving to customs brokers and member creameries.

## What changed at the border and what did not

The suspension lifts the 25 percent tariff line that has applied to European cheeses since late 2019. However, our inquiries with custom brokers confirm that goods already in transit or cleared under the prior rate receive no retroactive relief. More importantly, landed costs depend entirely on how individual European suppliers absorbed or passed on the tariff over the last eighteen years. Some suppliers absorbed part of the 25 percent to maintain US market share; others passed the full increase to buyers. Consequently, the price relief at member loading docks will vary widely by supplier contract.

We must caution members against assuming a uniform 25 percent reduction on all European wheels. Furthermore, because this is a five-year truce rather than a permanent repeal, multi-year import agreements must include provisions for tariff reinstatement. A contract assuming permanent removal exposes wholesale buyers to severe margin risk if political conditions shift before the five-year window closes.

## Impact on domestic specialty producers

The removal of the tariff shifts the competitive dynamics for domestic makers who compete directly with imported washed rind and alpine styles. European wheels will anchor lower price points on retail counters, increasing margin pressure on domestic artisan producers. While domestic cheese retains clear advantages in freshness and regional provenance, producers must re-examine their counter positioning. We are advising members not to engage in defensive discounting, but rather to emphasize provenance and make-style integrity.
