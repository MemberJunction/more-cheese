**Subject:** Raw milk aging rules, state-level inspection variance, and our next steps

The federal aging requirement for unpasteurized cheese is currently back in the conversation following a bill introduced this session. While the legislative text does not alter the 60-day aging window itself, it has prompted a high volume of member inquiries to our Food Safety Committee. Small producers are not asking us to lobby for radical changes to federal law; their questions are much narrower. They want clearer guidance on what documentation actually satisfies an inspector during the aging period, because enforcement practice varies noticeably from one state to the next. This memo outlines what we learned from yesterday's webinar on raw milk rules and what our advocacy priorities should be for the remainder of the quarter.

## What the 60-day compliance mechanism requires

The 60-day rule functions as the compliance mechanism for shipping raw milk cheese across state lines. Held under proper cave conditions, the aging window allows pathogens such as *Listeria monocytogenes* and pathogenic *E. coli* to decline to levels regulators accept in standard cheese matrices. However, as our committee notes, the calendar alone is insufficient. A wheel reaching day 60 without documented humidity and temperature logs has demonstrated nothing to an inspector. Environmental monitoring plans and defensible logs remain paramount.

Our webinar on Tuesday, August 24, covered these operational realities. Because it was a free webinar, no paid registrations were recorded in our data, but attendance was robust and member participation highlighted the friction points in state-level oversight. Producers reported that inspectors in different jurisdictions apply conflicting standards to the exact same moisture and temperature records. That operational ambiguity is the core frustration.

## Member feedback and advocacy focus

The Food Safety Committee is tracking this variance as our primary live advocacy topic. General frustration does not move a rule, but specific, documented inspection experiences do. We are actively asking members to share how their state-level inspections intersected with their aging logs. If we can aggregate three or four clean case studies showing how conflicting state interpretations create compliance traps, we can bring a constructive proposal to regulators for harmonized guidance.

Communications has been fielding related inquiries following the blog post published on August 23. We need to ensure our public messaging stays aligned with our internal goal: pushing for clarity on inspection documentation rather than taking a premature position on broader federal legislation that is unlikely to move quickly.

## Capacity and coordination

Advocacy and committee support are currently running alongside conference follow-up and fall course preparation. The Membership & Outreach Committee is keeping an informal list of members navigating import sourcing and inspection hurdles. We should coordinate our outreach so that members contacting us about state-level rules are routed directly to the Food Safety Committee's documentation collection effort.
