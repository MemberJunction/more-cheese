**Subject:** Food Safety & HACCP certification recertification and grace-window audit

This audit reviews the current standing of our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate holders and examines the operational bottlenecks driving certificate lapses ahead of the year-end transition. Of the seven credentials we offer, the HACCP certificate carries the shortest validity window at two years. That short cycle is intentional, reflecting the rapid pace of regulatory guidance updates, environmental monitoring protocols, and the ongoing industry debate surrounding the sixty-day aging requirement for raw-milk cheeses. However, it also means that small producers deep in holiday-volume production are frequently letting their credentials slip into grace windows without realizing it until a wholesale buyer or auditor asks for proof of currency.

## What the certification numbers show

For the year to date, our total association education metrics record 369 enrollments and 351 completions across all active course instances. Within the certification program specifically, we have processed 5 overall certification enrollments and awarded a total of 4 credentials across the catalog, breaking down as 2 Food Safety & HACCP Certificates, 1 Cheese Foundations Certificate, and 1 Certified Cheesemonger. While course completions remain steady, the recertification pipeline for HACCP holders approaching their two-year mark requires immediate administrative intervention. Several small creameries currently operating in grace windows are unaware that an expired HACCP credential compromises their compliance paperwork during third-party audits.

## The recertification review module and producer friction

Prolonged grace windows do not stem from a rejection of credential value; rather, they align with the broader retention patterns documented across our virtual programming this year. Members do not fail to recertify because they disagree with the material; they drift because the recertification module requires completing a hazard-analysis exercise specific to their own physical processing layout, rather than answering generic worksheet prompts. When holiday-volume milk processing peaks in November and December, cheesemakers lack the bandwidth to audit their own facility logs. If a certificate lapses, the producer is left scrambling to schedule a review module at the exact moment their floor staff is working double shifts to meet retail orders.

## Capacity and coordination

Membership Operations and Education & Credentialing have historically handled lapsed credentials reactively, reaching out only after a grace window closes. Given that our current membership count sits at 485 with 6 new membership purchases booked this week alone—including 5 Individual tiers and 1 Enthusiast tier, generating 896 USD in gross membership revenue—we cannot afford to handle credential lapses manually one by one. The total confirmed orders for the week stand at 10, bringing in 1604 USD in gross order value against a total cash captured figure of 2347 USD. Administrative time is currently diverted toward reconciling these transactions and preparing for upcoming events, including 3 registrations taken this week for the upcoming workshop on Cedar Competition Prep (EVT-2021-W2) and the upcoming free webinar on Judging Standards (EVT-2021-WEB4) scheduled for November 23.

- Advocacy & Standards: review the updated hazard-analysis exercise prompts to ensure they reflect current environmental monitoring expectations without imposing undue administrative burdens on small creameries.
- Membership Operations: draft a targeted, pre-expiration notification message linking directly to the recertification review module rather than sending a generic expiration warning.
