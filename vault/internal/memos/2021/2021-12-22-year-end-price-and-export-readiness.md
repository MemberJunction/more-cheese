We need our internal tracking to align with what we are telling members on the site. ## The Class III and Class IV crossover

For years, cheesemakers have budgeted around Class III milk sitting comfortably below Class IV. The shift moving into the new year — with Class IV pricing above Class III — removes the traditional input cost cushion precisely when holiday-week volume is at its peak and suppliers are least willing to renegotiate terms. Members buying on spot prices or handshake agreements are the most vulnerable. Our advocacy desk has received inquiries from members whose bookkeepers are confused by component statement variances on protein and butterfat. We need to ensure that the Cheese Chemistry pricing modules are prominently pointed to so members can review their federal order calculations before signing new contracts.

## Quota disputes and secondary market pressure

Simultaneously, the trade dispute panel ruling on neighboring dairy tariff-rate quotas means allocation allowances will remain restricted to a narrow set of processors rather than opening up broadly. While smaller producers do not hold export licenses, the secondary effect is tangible: exporters who cannot move anticipated volume abroad will hold or redirect inventory into the domestic market. This puts downward pressure on wholesale prices right here in the U.S. We must advise members not to assume near-term relief at the border and to maintain strong domestic fallbacks in their sales agreements.
