**Subject:** Class III softness, wholesale contracts, and counter traffic

Members are calling the office with contradictory signals from the same spring market. Cheese-based Class III pricing has softened as milk production climbs, which makes wholesale buyers nervous about their index contracts, while dairy export demand stays high enough to absorb extra domestic volume. At the same time, farm stands and specialty counters that were thrown together in a hurry are buckling under higher foot traffic and failing basic temperature or signage checks. This memo reviews what our data shows for the week ending June 13, 2021, and coordinates how our communications and member services should respond before inquiries overwhelm the support inbox.

## Market signals and wholesale contracts

The softening in Class III pricing is real, but it affects members differently depending on their contract structure. Producers tied strictly to index pricing feel the pressure immediately, whereas those selling direct or through distributor relationships without a formal index have more room to hold their price if they can explain why to buyers. Our recent advisory noted this divide, but members still want guidance on contract language. Membership Operations reports steady order activity overall—confirmed orders for the week stand at 23 with a gross of 6975 USD and cash captured at 7018 USD, reflecting a stable baseline despite market anxiety. Only one individual membership purchase was recorded this week at 153 USD, bringing total current members to 459 by week end.

## Counter traffic and operational strain

The second pressure point is retail. Specialty perishables sold by weight are seeing accelerated growth, turning temporary curbside tables and farm stand fridges into permanent bottlenecks. We have seen 29 course enrollments taken this week and 4 course completions, alongside 14 event registrations for the ICF Annual Conference 2021 and 7 registrations for the Workshop: Lark Defect Diagnosis. The demand for our counter-building coursework reflects an urgent need among small-scale operators to fix layout, portioning, shrink, temperature logging, and signage before health inspectors or customer complaints catch up with them. A counter built hastily last year cannot handle sustained foot traffic without proper logging routines.

## Advocacy and coalition engagement

On the legislative and regulatory front, Advocacy & Standards participated in one coalition meeting this week concerning interstate cheese shipment rules. Small producers navigating interstate logistics continue to face friction, and our advocacy actions remain aligned with coalition priorities. We must ensure that members separating export paperwork from domestic wholesale contracts understand the documentation requirements, particularly for those looking to expand abroad while domestic pricing fluctuates.

## What we need

- Communications: reinforce the distinction between index pricing and fixed-sheet contracts in upcoming digests without offering house price forecasts.
- Membership Operations: flag any member accounts reporting sudden contract cancellations so we can track regional trends.
- Education & Credentialing: prioritize inquiries regarding retail counter layout and temperature-logging templates for immediate member support.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve the outreach schedule for small creamery members navigating FSMA exemptions and interstate shipping rules.
