As outdoor temperatures swing and small rooms struggle with humidity, our focus this week splits between advising members on spring affinage and coordinating comments on the federal labeling proposal. The Maturation Planning for Small Rooms course is seeing seasonal interest from members whose converted basements or walk-ins are drifting off their winter stability. ## The maturation room pinch and course demand

Spring humidity swings are the hardest season for a small maturation room to hold steady. Winter humidity is low and steady, summer is high and steady, but the weeks in between swing hard as outdoor temperatures climb and fall unevenly. A room without a mechanical buffering system feels every bit of that swing. Our course on maturation planning walks members through low-cost fixes like door habits, draft placement, and daily temperature and humidity logs rather than jumping straight to expensive equipment retrofits.

While course completions for the week stand at three, overall enrollments are constrained because staff are currently splitting their time between educational support and advocacy inquiries. Members calling about cracked rinds are often the same ones asking how to adapt their packaging to the proposed labeling standard.

## Labeling rule friction points

Our Standards Committee has been reviewing the proposed federal labeling rule alongside small producers. While standardized aging claims and moisture disclosures are reasonable goals, the draft language assumes commercial-scale operations with dedicated laboratory relationships and large label spaces. A fixed-format disclosure box eats label space on compact artisan wheels, and frequent moisture testing is a disproportionate cost for producers making a few hundred wheels a season.

We are drafting comments asking for a scaled documentation standard tied to annual production volume and a smaller allowable disclosure format for small packages. Members who make and label cheese themselves are submitting individual comments using our template, which carries more weight than association form letters.
