**Subject:** January pricing, component spreads, and small creamery margins

Class III and Class IV milk price futures ended December further apart than they started it, widening a spread that members relying on cheese-block formulas need to understand before they finalize January pricing sheets. The public blog post published yesterday lays out the baseline, but our inbox is already accumulating questions from members whose component pricing does not match the headline futures. This memo reviews what the numbers show as we close out 2021, and what our team needs to tell members who reach out for a second set of eyes on their margin sheets.

## What the year-end numbers tell us

National cheese production for 2021 came in essentially flat versus 2020. Italian-style output ticked down slightly year over year, while American-style cheese was up heading into the holidays. More importantly, the divergence between Class III and Class IV prices created a margin squeeze for creameries whose whey stream feeds into dry powder formulas while their cheese-side numbers looked steady on paper. We saw 557 total membership purchases in 2021 generating $128,592 in gross sales, closing the year with 483 current members on December 31, of whom 353 were retained from the prior year and 89 were first-time members. That retention figure is a strong indicator of small-creamery stability during a volatile year, but it hides the individual margin pressure that pricing spreads introduce.

## Staffing and holiday strain

Beyond milk pricing, several members reported staffing gaps around the holidays this month, tied to the wave of respiratory illness moving through communities in December. A tighter crew changes how much cushion a plant or retail counter has if a pricing surprise lands in January. We should ensure our upcoming workshops and webinars — such as the Cedar Competition Prep workshop scheduled for December 31 in Chicago, which has 34 registered attendees — account for these operational realities when staff assist attendees on site.
