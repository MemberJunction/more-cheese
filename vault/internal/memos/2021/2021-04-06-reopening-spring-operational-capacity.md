**Subject:** Reopening spring and operational capacity across membership tiers

This week's public blog posts correctly observe that retail and foodservice demand is picking up as vaccination coverage climbs and indoor dining capacity limits ease, but our inbound inquiry logs show that members are experiencing this recovery unevenly. While mail-order subscription lists built during the shutdown continue to provide a stable floor for farmstead producers, institutional accounts and wholesale distributors remain constrained by patchy school district reopening schedules and regional dining rules. This memo examines our current membership numbers for the week ending 11 April 2021 and outlines the operational adjustments needed to support small creameries navigating the transition back to farmers' markets and retail counters.

## Membership intake and current totals

At the close of the week on 11 April 2021, our current membership stands at 450 members. Purchases processed during the week totaled 5 transactions, comprising 3 first-time members and 2 renewing accounts. By tier, the week's purchases include 1 Enthusiast, 3 Individual, and 1 SmallBusiness membership, generating a gross sales total of $940. Total confirmed orders across all association products reached 11 with $2,885 in gross receipts, while cash captured through 11 April 2021 totaled $2,989.

Although the absolute number of weekly membership transactions remains modest, the shift toward first-time sign-ups at the Individual and SmallBusiness tiers reflects the retail staffing push described in our recent outreach. Creameries are bringing counter staff back and investing in credentialing, specifically the Certified Cheesemonger track, to handle increased customer questions as specialty shops reopen.

## Farmers' markets and subscription lists

Members who pivoted to mail-order distribution last year are finding that maintaining a direct-to-consumer subscription list alongside a revived farmers' market table provides the most resilient business model. The primary operational challenge reported to the Membership & Outreach Committee is not a lack of consumer demand, but rather the friction of managing dual distribution streams with leaner staffing. Producers who downsized their part-time help during the pandemic are hesitant to rehire until outdoor market traffic proves consistent across the entire season.

We must ensure our advisory resources match this cautious posture. Recommending an aggressive production ramp-up based on national foodservice headlines would be irresponsible when local institutional contracts remain uncertain.

## Capacity constraints

Membership Operations and the Events Team are currently managing workshop logistics, including the Reggio Emilia workshop held on 5 April 2021, alongside member renewal inquiries and grace-window requests. With 4 total event registrations taken this week—specifically 2 for the Annual Conference and 2 for the Stone Alpine Styles workshop—staff workload is approaching the threshold where direct member support calls risk experiencing delays.

## What we need

- Membership & Outreach Committee: prioritize outreach calls to small-producer members inquiring about farmers' market stall rules and subscription list retention.
- Events Team: confirm that upcoming webinar scheduling does not conflict with committee meeting calendars for Q2 2021.
