This memo reviews our upcoming course enrollment pacing and instructor scheduling for the spring and summer cohorts, specifically examining the Judging & Competition Preparation (Curd cohort) starting on 20 May 2021, alongside the Rind Development & Washing (Prairie cohort) and Advanced Sensory Evaluation (Prairie cohort) both starting on 24 June 2021 and 19 June 2021 respectively. With 10 course enrollments taken this week and 3 course completions recorded so far, our online credentialing pivot continues to sustain strong engagement among small producers, but lead times on instructor availability are tightening faster than make sheets anticipated.

## Enrollment pacing and cohort capacity

The 10 enrollments secured this week bring our spring running total upward, following a year where our overall education metrics reached 369 enrollments and 351 completions across the catalog. The upcoming Curd cohort for Judging & Competition Preparation is filling in line with our seasonal projections, but the lead times required for sample kit distribution and instructor coordination mean we must lock the participant roster earlier than we did during the winter sessions. Small creameries participating in these online sessions frequently report that juggling daily make schedules leaves little margin for delayed material delivery.

## Instructor scheduling and materials

Unlike standard webinars—such as our free sessions which carry no paid registrations and therefore track no attendance—our structured cohort courses require rigorous synchronization between the instructor, the physical sample kits shipped to participants, and the digital learning modules. Instructors for the Prairie cohort courses starting in June have flagged potential scheduling conflicts with regional judging events scheduled later in the summer. We need to confirm that teaching materials and make-sheet reading assignments are dispatched at least three weeks prior to the opening session so that participants operating under tight aging schedules can adjust their cave work accordingly.

## Coherence with public member spotlights

Recent member spotlights have highlighted how specialty buyers and producers rely heavily on remote tasting and structured provenance presentations when evaluating new stock. Our education catalog must directly support this operational reality. When producers learn advanced sensory evaluation or competition preparation, they are not just studying theory; they are preparing for the exact video-call screening processes that specialty buyers have relied on for over a year. Ensuring our course materials reflect these current commercial practices is essential for member retention.
