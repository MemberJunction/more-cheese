**Subject:** Interstate shipment rules and member renewal lapses

This week we ran into a recurring operational friction point: small creamery members calling about interstate raw-milk shipping rules while simultaneously sitting in a lapsed or grace-window membership status. A member trying to untangle a receiving state's handling and disclosure requirements is naturally frustrated when their portal access is restricted due to a failed or delayed payment. Membership Operations handled two separate inquiries this week where the underlying frustration with state-level distribution rules was misdirected at our annual dues notice. We need a clearer handoff between the petition work managed by Advocacy & Standards and our own renewal grace window.

## The current lapse pattern

Our total membership stands at 462 at week end, with only two membership purchases recorded this week, both in the Individual tier totaling 306 USD gross. Against that backdrop, small producers caught in state-level regulatory shifts are asking for grace periods they assume we automatically grant during drought or regulatory stress. Last month's state raw-milk rule change just south of several members has prompted a wave of calls about interstate shipping. When members call us to sign the interstate shipment petition or ask about receiving-state paperwork, our support staff has to check their membership standing first. If a creamery has lapsed, the conversation instantly stalls on whether they can still participate in committee sign-ons or access our running log of state rule changes.

## Why the petition workflow conflicts with billing

The Advocacy & Standards team encourages any member to add their name to our open interstate cheese shipment petition, pointing out that documented lists of working producers carry weight with state agriculture offices. However, Membership Operations cannot verify petition validity or add signatures for accounts that are past their renewal window without triggering audit exceptions. When a producer spends twenty minutes detailing a state labeling snag on the phone, telling them we cannot log their petition signature because their SmallBusiness dues payment failed creates an entirely avoidable adversarial exchange. We need Advocacy & Standards to either route non-members through a separate intake or give us clear parameters for handling grace-window exceptions when a member is actively participating in advocacy campaigns.

## Capacity and data clean-up

We also continue to grapple with underlying data anomalies, including stale organizational affiliations and duplicate records that complicate grace-window notices. When we send automated lapse warnings, some members respond by pointing out that their physical shipping address or designated contact person changed months ago. Membership Operations is currently managing renewals, conference registration support for the upcoming Burlington event, and grace-window exceptions with the same lean team. Without an agreed adjustment to how we handle members with active advocacy files who miss a renewal date, we will continue spending hours reconciling billing disputes instead of supporting the conference rollout.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: provide a clean list of members actively contributing to the interstate shipment petition by Monday so we can cross-reference their standing before sending further notices.
- Communications: ensure any upcoming blog or newsletter text directing members to sign the petition includes a brief note that active membership status is required for committee-linked advocacy actions.
- Membership Operations: draft a standardized grace-window exception protocol for members engaged in active regulatory petitions by 23 July.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that billing friction during regulatory transitions is becoming our primary source of member complaints this quarter.
