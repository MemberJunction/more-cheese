This week's intake reflects four membership purchases totaling 1,531 gross, bringing current membership to 436 at week's end. This includes two first-time joiners distributed across Individual (two), SmallBusiness (one), and Corporate (one) tiers. At the same time, federal dairy data shows Class III and Class IV milk prices easing through January and into February, with cheese stocks running higher than a year ago. We need to examine how softer milk input costs intersect with small-creamery retention before spring ordering cycles lock in.

## Membership intake and the tier mix

The four new purchases booked this week keep our renewal pipeline steady, but the broader picture at week's end is 436 current members. Small creameries buying milk under a formula tied to Class III are experiencing their first input cost relief in months. However, the same reports showing lower milk prices also highlight building cheese stocks, which typically push finished-goods prices down. Lower input costs and lower finished-goods prices can cancel each other out faster than either appears on a spreadsheet. Members watching only one side of that ledger may assume business has eased when only half of it has.

## Regulatory background and labeling watch

Advocacy & Standards continues to track the slow-moving federal labeling rulemaking concerning how milk ingredients are described on cheese labels. While this docket has remained stalled for years without a final rule landing, small producers cannot afford to ignore it. A large operation retains food-law consultants; a two-person creamery usually discovers compliance shifts only when a distributor or state inspector flags an ingredient statement. The federation must keep this watch list active so members do not have to monitor the federal docket independently.

## Operational alignment

Communications published a piece this week examining the winter cheese case and pricing strategy, directing members to our resource library for second-set pricing worksheets. Membership Operations and Advocacy should ensure those worksheets align with current formula realities before spring orders accelerate. We should also remind members that culture and rennet costs do not drop with federal milk orders.
