Members have spent the fourth quarter calling about two realities that are currently dictating their holiday stocking: climbing commodity prices for Class III and Class IV milk, and a freight squeeze that has made delivery windows entirely unreliable. Our public blog post this week on freight covers the surface, but internally we need to examine what these logistics constraints mean for our small producers and how member accounts are reflecting invoice surprises at the counter and on the loading dock. This memo summarizes where we stand as we approach the end of the year and prepare for January renewals.

## The reality on the loading dock

Federal reporting this week confirms that Class III and Class IV prices remain well above their spring levels, continuing the upward trajectory seen in global dairy auctions since late summer. For a creamery selling direct to consumers, passing that increase along via a shelf tag is straightforward. For members locked into wholesale contracts signed back when the board was quiet, absorption is the only option until renewal. Several members report that suppliers they have used for years can no longer confirm delivery windows, forcing them to carry higher inventory than normal and tying up both cash and cave space during peak volume.

This is not a pricing signal that can be hedged against; it is an operational bottleneck. Staff should advise members calling into the office to pad their holiday order math by at least a week and to push for shorter contract terms next year. Carrying extra inventory is a costly workaround, but missed holiday deliveries are worse.
