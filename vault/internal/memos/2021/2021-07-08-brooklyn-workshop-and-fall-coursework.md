> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT** — International Cheese Federation (ICF), a fictitious trade association created for the MemberJunction More Cheese demonstration environment. All names, figures, events, and decisions are invented.

**To:** Events Team; Communications; Office of the Chief Executive
**From:** Education & Credentialing
**Date:** 8 July 2021
**Subject:** Brooklyn workshop findings and implications for fall coursework

Our first in-person workshop in over a year, Workshop: Lark Defect Diagnosis, wrapped on Tuesday in Brooklyn. We capped the roster, registered 29, issued 1 refund, and saw 25 attendees walk through the door with 4 no-shows. What happened in the room revealed a significant calibration gap that our virtual coursework over the last year did not catch. We need to adjust our fall course design before the next cohort starts.

## What the Brooklyn room showed us

Going into the session, the instructor planned to focus heavily on the cause-and-effect reasoning behind crumbly pastes and uneven rinds. That turned out not to be where the attendees struggled. Because members spent a year and a half troubleshooting from memory and photo comparisons, their diagnostic book knowledge remained sharp, but their hands-on sensory calibration had quietly eroded. Attendees spent the first half of the day relearning what a proper rind actually feels like under a thumb and how to evaluate paste texture through touch rather than visual description alone.

Several members noted unprompted that they had grown fluent in describing cheese while getting rusty at physically handling it. The instructor adjusted the schedule on the fly, slowing down the tactile diagnostic steps. That adjustment made the session valuable, but it proved that our virtual defect-diagnosis training cannot be treated as a full substitute for physical handling.

## Implications for the fall catalog

We are taking these findings back to the Education Committee for the upcoming term. The virtual defect-diagnosis session scheduled as an alternative for members who could not travel remains on the calendar, but we must embed explicit sensory-calibration guidance into its materials. Furthermore, the next course starting on our calendar is Counter Culture: Retailing (Hollow cohort) on 14 July, which generated 25 course enrollments this week alone. While that retail course focuses on counter management rather than make-room defects, the surge in enrollments indicates high member demand for structured learning right now.

We must ensure our course advisors are prepared to brief incoming cohorts on the difference between theoretical troubleshooting and physical affinage work. Members returning to in-person sessions later this year should expect a slower, more foundational first hour than they might anticipate based solely on their virtual coursework history.

## Capacity and follow-up

We are collecting the specific case notes from the Brooklyn session to distribute in written form to members who requested them but could not attend. However, our staff bandwidth is stretched thin between conference preparation for the upcoming ICF Annual Conference 2021 in Burlington and managing these course enrollments. We must ensure that writing up defect case notes does not compromise our support for the 17 conference registrations taken this week.

## What we need

- Events Team: share any venue feedback from Brooklyn so we can factor it into future workshop logistics.
- Communications: ensure our blog and member communications regarding upcoming virtual and in-person sessions set proper expectations about tactile calibration time.
- Office of the Chief Executive: acknowledge that fall course planning requires additional review time for sensory components.
- Education & Credentialing: finalize the revised curriculum notes for the Hollow cohort retail course by Monday.
