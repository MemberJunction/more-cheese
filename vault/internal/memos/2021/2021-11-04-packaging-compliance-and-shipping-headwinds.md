This week's public blog posts covering packaging sustainability and interstate shipping rules touched directly on the operational pressures our members are facing this fall. On paper, export volumes are climbing toward record heights and farm-gate milk prices are firming, but container availability, ocean freight expenses, and film shortages are eating the gains before they reach shop shelves. We need to ensure our internal tracking and committee responses align with the realities small creameries are reporting.

## Packaging substitution and compliance risks

Tuesday's Packaging Sustainability webinar ran long on questions about mono-material film and substitute wraps. When suppliers backorder a standard laminate, producers are forced to switch materials mid-run. That substitution is not merely a purchasing adjustment; it triggers compliance checks against existing food-safety plans and state-specific labeling rules. If a creamery changes its wrap to maintain availability, shelf-life trials must precede any shelf-life claims. We have heard from members who slowed their packaging lines down rather than reworking setups mid-season, accepting higher labor costs to avoid regulatory missteps. The Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding these inquiries all month and can direct members to peers who have completed similar shelf-life trials.

## Interstate shipment paperwork and advocacy timing

Our ongoing advocacy campaign addressing interstate cheese shipment highlights the heavy administrative burden placed on small producers. The patchwork of state-by-state licensing requirements costs small creameries more in time and legal fees than the extra sales justify during their first year across a border. We are asking for a lighter administrative lift and a predictable application process for producers under a defined production threshold, while strictly maintaining all food-safety inspection requirements. Several state agriculture committees hold annual rule-review sessions in the first quarter of the year, making November and December critical windows for gathering member stories and letter signatures before the next legislative cycle closes.
