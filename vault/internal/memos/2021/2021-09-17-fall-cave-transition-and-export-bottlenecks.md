This week has brought a convergence of inquiries from small creameries dealing with unseasonably warm September weather, volatile aging room humidity, and port delays that are holding finished wheels in caves longer than planned. At the same time, six membership purchases were processed this week, bringing our current member count to 473 by the close of Sunday, 19 September. This memo outlines how these operational pressures intersect across our programs and where we need coordinated team responses before October.

## Aging rooms and seasonal volatility

External climate summaries note that mid-September has run significantly warmer than normal across key cheese-producing regions, putting sudden thermal stress on small, non-engineered aging rooms and converted basements. Members relying on ambient moisture are finding their traditional summer settings inadequate as outside air dries out. Our Education Committee's Maturation Planning for Small Rooms course, which starts its Meadow cohort on 20 October, directly addresses this transition. However, members cannot wait until October to fix a cracked rind or a shifting humidity source. Education & Credentialing should ensure our current cohort materials and informal advisory notes emphasize active humidity monitoring and daily logging right now.

## Export container shortages and packaging

Exporters continue to report severe bottlenecks, with ocean container shortages and rising freight costs forcing caves to hold inventory. This is compounding the packaging squeeze: paper pulp and lumber for pallet construction are running well above last year's cost, affecting both export crates and domestic wholesale cases. While we have no direct fix for port congestion, our Membership & Outreach Committee has been fielding these inquiries. We need to ensure that staff answering member queries are pointing exporters toward freight variance budgeting and early booking windows rather than unrealistic delivery promises.

## Advocacy and coalition alignment

Advocacy actions this week included coalition meetings addressing FSMA small-producer exemptions and dairy pricing reform. Small producers facing both input cost inflation and delayed shipping schedules are particularly vulnerable to regulatory compliance timing. Our advocacy positions must reflect the reality that time spent holding inventory in a cave is capital tied up without cash flow, a point raised repeatedly in recent member communications.

## Capacity and coordination

As we look toward the 2 November webinar on Packaging Sustainability, our internal workflow is stretched thin between managing routine membership inquiries, supporting course enrollments (five course completions were logged this week, with zero new enrollments taken), and tracking advocacy coalitions. We must align our messaging so that members contacting different teams receive consistent guidance on both packaging forecasts and cave management.
