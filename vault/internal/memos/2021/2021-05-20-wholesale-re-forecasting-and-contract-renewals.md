**Subject:** Wholesale re-forecasting, Class III price shifts, and spring contract renewals

Restaurant dining rooms are reopening faster than most spring forecasts anticipated, pulling Class III milk past $17.50 after sitting near $15.75 in February. While this firmer market offers a welcome tailwind for wholesale cheese producers, it also introduces acute margin pressure for creameries locked into rigid formula-priced contracts written earlier in the year. This memo reviews how members are navigating mid-season contract adjustments, why our advocacy and education channels need to align on pricing guidance, and what the current order data tells us about financial stability so far.

## Market signals and milk costs

The gap between February pricing and current market realities has left several small-to-midsize members exposed. Production is up an estimated 4.6% higher in May compared to a year ago, driven by rising milk cow numbers and increased output. Ordinarily, more milk on the ground coupled with rising demand creates a balanced recovery, but the volatility in the futures market is causing friction. Creameries that rely on formula pricing tied directly to Class III benchmarks are seeing their input costs climb without a matching automatic adjustment in wholesale finished-cheese agreements signed during the winter lull.

Members who took on debt or deferred maintenance during the prolonged 2020 lockdowns are especially vulnerable. Rather than locking in new fixed costs or assuming the price curve will climb indefinitely through the summer, producers should be utilizing this window to check their pricing formulas and catch up on deferred repairs. We must ensure our guidance remains practical and cautions against overproducing into a market that remains susceptible to feed cost swings and shifting regulatory pressures on raw-milk aging rules, which saw an active coalition meeting take place this week.

## Rebuilding wholesale accounts and communication

Our outreach indicates that the hardest operational hurdle this spring is not vat-level production, but rather rebuilding wholesale accounts that went quiet last year. Distributors who dropped artisan lines when restaurant clients closed have not automatically reinstated them now that dining rooms are filling back up. Generic inquiries are falling flat; the members seeing success are those reaching out with specific reorder asks and updated availability sheets.

Communications should coordinate closely with Membership Operations to ensure that members looking at wholesale channels are directed toward appropriate association resources. The Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course, for instance, offers concrete frameworks for mid-stream season re-forecasting, which directly addresses the pricing dilemmas members are currently bringing to our helpdesks.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: cross-reference current renewal inquiries with members operating wholesale channels to identify who might need immediate contract formula reviews.
- Communications: ensure upcoming blog drafts and member newsletters point clearly toward operational forecasting tools without overstating the durability of the current Class III peak.
- Office of the Chief Executive: review our advocacy tracking on raw-milk aging rules to ensure consistency between our legislative posture and member economic guidance.
