**Subject:** August cave management inquiries and dairy safety-net workload

Member calls this month are diverging into two separate operational tracks that are stretching our small team. On one side, farmstead producers are ringing the office about the federal margin-support window closing next month, wanting guidance on dairy margin coverage before the deadline. On the other side, affineurs running small aging rooms are wrestling with late-summer heat and humidity control, generating technical inquiries that our standard winter support tickets never anticipated. We need an aligned response across desks before the volume forces ad-hoc answers.

## The safety-net inquiry spike

Calls regarding the current federal margin-support window have picked up sharply. Members are asking whether it is too late to sign up and how to factor supplier protection into autumn wholesale contracts. Class III milk has climbed from its February low under fourteen dollars into the high sixteens and low seventeens, but independent feed costs are moving independently, making producers nervous. Several members who signed up early reported that pulling together production history took less than an hour, but deciding on the correct coverage level remains a stumbling block. Because coverage elections do not carry forward automatically, missing this deadline leaves producers bare until the next signup cycle. Communications published a blog post today outlining the timeline, and Membership Operations has been routing callers to the correct federal contacts, but we need Advocacy & Standards to review whether our brief FAQ covers the distinction between farmstead producers and wholesale buyers.

## Cave management and humidity drift

Simultaneously, the technical help desk is fielding a high volume of environmental monitoring complaints from small creamery caves. Following the regional heat wave last month, passive and small-compressor aging rooms are pulling moisture out of the air faster than wheels can tolerate. Members are reporting cracked rinds near vents and dry spots on shelves closest to cooling units, mirroring the operational challenges highlighted in our recent cave management coursework. Several callers admitted they only caught the drift because they started walking rooms with handheld meters rather than trusting wall thermostats. This is not a capital equipment failure; it is a seasonal monitoring adjustment. However, answering these detailed airflow and hygiene inquiries is pulling time away from our regular member intake.
