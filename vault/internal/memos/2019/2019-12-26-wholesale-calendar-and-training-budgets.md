The quiet week between the holidays is when our wholesale and retail members typically map their training budgets and buying schedules for the upcoming year. This memo reviews what the year-end sales and membership figures tell us about how those behaviors are shifting, and where our internal communications need to align with retail realities before January arrives. We closed out 2019 with 371 current members and 4 membership purchases recorded during this holiday week, generating $775 in dues and $846 across four confirmed orders. Total cash captured for the week stands at $2707. These figures are preliminary until the final year-end reconciliation is complete.

## Wholesale adjustments and buyer behavior

Recent conversations with longtime regional sales members reinforce that traditional cheddar and specialty wholesale lines are operating on much shorter lead times than they did a decade ago. Retail buyers are no longer locking in their holiday assortments in September and letting them ride through January. Instead, adjustment calls continue right into December as shelf sets shift and promotions alter weekly needs. Furthermore, buyers are asking for specific aging points, rind treatments, and origins rather than ordering generic product.

For our membership, this means creameries and distributors must build more flexibility into their 2020 terms. When members ask how to structure their wholesale book for the coming year, our standard guidance should point them toward closer coordination with their buyers' calendars rather than static annual agreements. The Membership & Outreach Committee maintains an informal list of members willing to compare notes on these operational changes, which we should actively promote to newer members in January.

## Training budgets and credential timing

Retail members are also finalizing training allocations for counter staff. Industry data highlights specialty food and cheese sales leading retail growth, which places greater pressure on counter personnel to act as educators rather than simple clerks. Members inquiring about credentials during this holiday week are focusing heavily on the Certified Cheesemonger credential, which is specifically designed for cut-and-wrap, storage, stock rotation, and customer guidance.

However, we need to ensure our educational messaging distinguishes counter-side programs from production-focused courses like the Advanced Affinage Certificate. If retail members plan to enroll counter teams, they must start those conversations immediately after the new year before cohorts fill up. Education & Credentialing should coordinate with Communications to ensure the January messaging directs retail members toward the right credentials from day one.

## Capacity and upcoming webinars

Looking ahead to January, our events calendar shows two webinars scheduled: Webinar: Counter Culture 2020 on 14 January 2020 and Webinar: Whey Management 2020 on 15 January 2020. As free educational offerings, these carry no paid registrations in our data, and we must refrain from quoting attendance figures for them. Staff resources will be stretched as we transition from holiday closeout to Q1 programming. We need agreement across teams on how we handle member inquiries regarding both export planning and retail credentialing.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure upcoming blog posts and member briefs direct retail inquiries toward the Certified Cheesemonger credential rather than general production training.
- Education & Credentialing: confirm cohort availability for retail members looking to book counter training in January.
- Events Team: verify logistics for the mid-January webinars without assuming attendance metrics.
- Membership Operations: finalize the year-end cash and order reconciliation before publishing any final 2019 summaries.
