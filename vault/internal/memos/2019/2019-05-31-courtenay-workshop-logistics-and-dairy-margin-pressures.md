These figures are now closed for this venue. As we prepare for the next workshop in the series—Workshop: Curd Retailing in Allgäu on 4 June 2019, which currently sits at 59 registered and 6 refunds—we need to align our logistics with the wider market conditions reported in our industry channels this week. ## Courtenay attendance and venue performance

The Courtenay event absorbed five refunds without breaking the room capacity, and the 59 attendees produced clean feedback sheets. However, the 7 no-shows represent a noticeable friction point that we are tracking across all regional workshops this year. Unlike the larger ICF Annual Conference in Madison, which draws on a national pool, regional workshops depend heavily on local creameries whose operating margins are currently tight. When a farmstead or small-scale processor faces unexpected milk check volatility or harvesting pressures, travel to a regional workshop is often the first discretionary expense cut, frequently resulting in a late no-show rather than an advance cancellation.

## The dairy margin backdrop and member sentiment

Our recent communications have emphasized separating market price movements from federal safety-net program changes. For our workshop participants, that distinction is not academic. While Class III futures have firmed since February and national milk output is running close to flat, many small cheesemakers buying on a co-op pool basis report that their local cost structures are lagging behind the headline recovery. The retroactive margin coverage payments closed earlier this month helped some primary producers, but our processing and retail members do not touch those safety nets directly. Consequently, members attending our regional workshops are running leaner inventories and watching their supplier scorecards more closely, as highlighted in this week's member spotlight.

## Operational adjustments for upcoming events

We must ensure our venue contracts and catering guarantees for the Allgäu workshop and the subsequent Clover Pasta Filata session in Courtenay on 1 July account for this margin sensitivity. Venue hosts are pushing for firm headcount guarantees two weeks out, but our registration curves show late activity right up to travel day. We cannot afford to absorb high attrition penalties if regional participants pull back due to localized milk pricing pressures.
