**Subject:** Import tariff modeling and member retail exposure

The proposed federal tariff list tied to the aircraft-subsidy dispute has generated a high volume of member inquiries this week. Members carrying both domestic and imported lines want to know how exposed their cases are if the proposed rates take effect. This memo outlines what our modeling shows, what our three committees discussed during their Q2 meetings on 11 April 2019, and how we should handle inbound questions from retail and distribution members.

## What the exposure looks like for members

Our data confirms that members relying on imported washed-rind, alpine, or blue styles from continental producers face the most immediate planning friction. A tariff on an import line does not merely raise landed cost; it disrupts supply timing. Distributors are already front-loading inventory before any final rate takes effect, which creates a short-term inventory glut followed by an unavoidable supply gap.

For domestic producers, the picture is more nuanced. While margins have been recovering slowly off a tight winter and milk production has stayed close to flat across the broader domestic market, a tariff on an import does not automatically translate to a clean sale for a comparable domestic wheel. Buyers selecting specific French or Italian styles for provenance or culture profiles do not always substitute domestic equivalents. However, it does open real shelf space in cases that have remained static for years.

## Committee feedback from 11 April

All three standing committees that met on Thursday — Food Safety, Awards & Competition, and Events — touched on market stability. The Food Safety Committee noted that small-producer compliance with labeling rules remains vulnerable when supply chains shift rapidly. Members revising labels due to alternative sourcing must ensure their packages continue to satisfy federal standards of identity, particularly regarding moisture content, ingredient order, and allergen disclosures.

We must remain neutral on the underlying aircraft dispute. Our role is strictly tracking how food categories get swept into external trade disputes and helping members model their actual purchasing and production volumes against alternative tariff scenarios rather than reacting to headlines.

- Membership Operations: flag any renewing corporate accounts on the distribution side that may need proactive outreach regarding supply timing.
- Events Team: ensure upcoming workshops (such as Meadow Affinage and Curd Retailing) incorporate discussions on supply chain resilience where relevant.
