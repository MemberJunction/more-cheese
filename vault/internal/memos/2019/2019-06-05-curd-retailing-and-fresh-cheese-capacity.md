**Subject:** Curd retailing workshop follow-up and fresh cheese handling capacity

The Workshop: Curd Retailing ran on 4 June 2019 in Allgäu. Final post-event reconciliation shows 59 registered, 6 refunded, 52 attended, and 7 no-shows. Because the session filled faster than expected and generated more interest in fresh curd handling than our usual aged-wheel curriculum anticipates, this memo reviews what the workshop teaches, where the operational gaps are, and what we need to adjust before scheduling repeat sessions or publishing the slide deck.

## What the session covers and why it matters

Fresh unaged curd behaves entirely differently from the hard or washed-rind cheeses that occupy most of our curriculum. Shelf life is measured in days rather than months, and the merchandising problems center on rotation, cold-chain timing, and rapid stock movement rather than humidity control or rind washing. Several attendees who manage cheese counters inside independent specialty grocers noted that fresh curd has grown from a small side case two years ago into a full third of their weekly cheese orders. Staff training across our membership has not kept pace with that shift.

During the planning for Allgäu, two distinct issues emerged that our current education catalog does not fully address. First, retailers sourcing fresh curd from regional producers struggle with inconsistent information regarding production dates versus pack dates. Second, pricing perishable fresh product against a compressed spoilage clock leads either to premature markdowns that erode margin or holding stock too long and writing it off. Our courses cover make sheets and aging rooms, but nobody on the retail side has a standardized rotation framework for a product that must move in days.

## Operational friction and capacity

Running a specialized fresh-cheese workshop requires closer coordination between the Events Team and Education & Credentialing than we currently maintain. The inquiries we received following the early May opening of registration showed that many members view fresh cheese retailing as an urgent operational problem rather than a theoretical seminar topic. The instructor for Allgäu developed a rotation worksheet to address the four-day limit on moving fresh curd, which several attendees began adopting immediately.

However, we lack a formal mechanism to capture these practical frameworks and integrate them into the broader education catalog. If the Events Committee schedules a repeat session or a recorded version later this year, we need Education & Credentialing to review the rotation materials and determine whether they feed into existing retail courses such as Counter Culture: Retailing or form the basis of a standalone module.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: review the Allgäu rotation worksheet by 14 June and advise whether to incorporate it into the retail catalog.
- Membership Operations: cross-reference the Allgäu attendee list against members who completed fresh-cheese education courses in the past year to identify cross-promotional opportunities.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that regional demand for fresh-cheese retailing content exceeds our current single-day workshop capacity and authorize a discussion on scheduling repeat formats for Q3.
