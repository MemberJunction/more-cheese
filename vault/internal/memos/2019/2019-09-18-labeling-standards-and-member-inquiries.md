**To:** Communications; Membership Operations
**From:** Advocacy & Standards
**Date:** 18 September 2019
**Subject:** Labeling standards and member inquiries after the autumn post

The public post on labeling standards published on Monday has generated an immediate wave of inquiries to the Membership & Outreach Committee. Members are asking whether the federation is taking a formal position on federal descriptive terms like "aged" or "farmstead," or if we are merely collecting complaints. This memo clarifies our current standing, outlines the data gaps in our advocacy file, and sets boundaries on what staff may tell inquirers before the Standards Committee reviews the comments in October.

## What the inquiries are showing

The calls and portal messages arriving this week cluster around two distinct anxieties. Small creameries that invest in long-cave affinage want a federal or regional floor under descriptive terms so that shoppers can distinguish a twelve-month wheel from a sixty-day product sold under the same label. Conversely, smaller artisanal producers worry that any new standard will be drafted around industrial production timelines and will price them out of language they have used honestly for years.

Our public post framed this as a fact-gathering stage rather than a petition. However, members calling in want to know what kind of evidence the Standards Committee considers valid. Right now, our advocacy file on labeling standards contains six actions logged for the year, alongside ten on raw-milk aging rules and seven on small-producer exemptions. That is a thin baseline for a policy shift, and members who expect an immediate legislative fix will be disappointed.

## Coordination with communications

Communications did well to keep the post descriptive rather than prescriptive, avoiding any premature policy stance while the court case involving regional milk sourcing works through the system. However, we need to ensure that staff answering member calls do not overpromise what the comment file can achieve.

If a member asks whether their specific label is compliant, staff must refer them to their own legal counsel or state dairy program, not offer an informal review. Our role is strictly aggregative: we log conflicts, label rejections, and internal definitions of "aged" to build a representative picture before drafting guidance.

## Capacity and next steps

The autumn cycle is already heavy with committee calls, course cohorts starting soon, and webinar preparation, such as today's free webinar on judging standards. Adding a major labeling initiative on top of existing advocacy priorities will stretch our policy analysts thin. We need to manage expectations so that members understand the comment file remains open through the autumn without consuming staff time that belongs to scheduled education and governance work.

## What we need

- Communications: direct any inbound media or legal questions regarding descriptive labeling straight to Advocacy & Standards.
- Membership Operations: log all specific member grievances about state line crossing and label rejections into the central comment file rather than answering them ad hoc.
- Everyone: maintain the line that this is a fact-gathering stage, not a rulemaking proceeding.
