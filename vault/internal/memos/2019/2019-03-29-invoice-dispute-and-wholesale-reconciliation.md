**To:** Office of the Chief Executive; Advocacy & Standards
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 29 March 2019
**Subject:** Wholesale invoice dispute and March order reconciliation

This memo records the ongoing dispute regarding an overdue corporate membership invoice from a regional dairy distributor, alongside our monthly reconciliation of confirmed orders against cash captured for March 2019. As we close out the first quarter, we are seeing a widening gap between orders confirmed on the books and cash hitting the federation bank accounts, complicated further by a member contesting the tier pricing applied to their annual corporate renewal.

## The invoice dispute

A mid-sized regional distributor and corporate member has formally disputed an invoice for one thousand dollars, claiming they were billed at the Corporate tier rate while operating under a restructured distribution arrangement that they argue should qualify for SmallBusiness pricing. Under our schedule, Corporate membership lists at one thousand dollars while SmallBusiness sits at four hundred dollars. The member has refused to clear the balance until the fee is adjusted retroactively.

Our records show their renewal was processed under the Corporate tier because their active distribution volume exceeded the threshold when the order was confirmed. However, the member points to this spring's broader margin squeeze—where feed, fuel, and labor costs have pinched operating margins across the sector—as the reason they cannot absorb the higher fee this year. We cannot unilaterally re-tier an active membership without committee sign-off, but leaving the invoice contested blocks our month-end cash reconciliation.

## Month-end reconciliation of orders and cash

For the week of March 25 through March 31, our figures show four membership purchases totaling 746 dollars in gross sales, comprising two Individual memberships at 175 dollars each, one SmallBusiness membership at 400 dollars, and one Enthusiast membership at 150 dollars. Across the entire month, our running totals indicate confirmed orders standing at 11 orders with a gross value of 2891 dollars, against cash captured of 2714 dollars. Zero cancellations were recorded for the week.

While the weekly numbers balance closely, the cumulative variance between gross orders and cash captured for the quarter stems entirely from disputed or pending wholesale and corporate invoices such as the one described above. A headline recovery in milk prices reported on the public blog does not automatically translate to liquid cash on our ledger when members are fighting over tier boundaries.

## Capacity and next steps

Membership Operations is spending an unsustainable amount of time manually matching wire transfers to open invoices because our current invoicing tools do not flag partial payments or disputed tiers automatically. Until Advocacy & Standards or Executive intervenes on the corporate tier definitions, we will continue to hold disputed renewals in a pending status, which suppresses our retained member count.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: issue a definitive ruling on whether distribution subsidiaries may claim SmallBusiness tier pricing during margin compression.
- Advocacy & Standards: review the corporate tier criteria before the next billing cycle so we avoid similar disputes next quarter.
- Finance / Cash Management: accept a temporary write-down or payment plan for the disputed thousand-dollar invoice if Executive decides to settle.
