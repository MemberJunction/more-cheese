**Subject:** Spring flush supply readout and summer contracting strategy

Federal price data confirming a modest spring flush rather than an oversupply flood has altered how members are approaching their seasonal contracts. Membership Operations has reviewed the week's order intake and cash capture alongside the supply commentary published on Monday and Wednesday. This memo outlines what our current transaction numbers show so far and what we should advise members who call the office for contract guidance.

## What the order intake shows so far

For the week of Monday 22 April 2019, confirmed orders stand at 13 with a gross value of 3151 USD and zero cancellations. Cash captured totals 3939 USD. Membership purchases for the week include 5 first-time joiners distributed across SmallBusiness (2) and Individual (3) tiers, generating 1099 USD in gross dues. Event registrations taken this week include 4 for the ICF Annual Conference 2019 and 4 for Workshop: Curd Retailing. Course enrollments recorded 3 new sign-ups, with 2 course completions and no new certification enrollments.

While these figures reflect normal operating rhythm, the underlying conversation among members involves milk procurement. Because federal data points to a gentler ramp in production than last year, creameries are not facing the frantic spot-milk dumping that disrupted make sheets last spring. Our members report greater stability in fat and protein percentages week to week.

## Implications for member contracting

Members inquiring through the portal are asking whether to lock in summer contracts or rely on spot availability. Based on the steadier supply surge, neither side of the negotiating table is cornered. Suppliers are seeing improved regional mailbox prices compared to March and prior years, making them disinclined to offer deep discounts. Conversely, because the milk is not flooding the market, cheese makers have a reliable runway to adjust culture and coagulant dosages without chasing erratic bulk tank compositions.

We must ensure our public communications do not overstate these trends. While a calm spring is helpful for affineurs and make-sheet consistency, dry weather later in the season can tighten milk supply faster than anticipated. The Membership & Outreach Committee keeps regular office hours to help members handle these exact terms before signing agreements.

## Capacity and upcoming schedule

Staff bandwidth is currently stretched between processing renewals and preparing for upcoming educational events, notably the Webinar: Judging Standards 2019 on 14 May and Workshop: Meadow Affinage in Courtenay on 28 May. Communications should coordinate with Advocacy & Standards before publishing further supply commentary to ensure we do not create unrealistic expectations about summer price stability.

## What we need

- Communications: align upcoming blog excerpts with the caveat that regional flush intensity varies.
- Advocacy & Standards: direct member inquiries regarding milk pricing to the Membership & Outreach Committee office hours.
- Membership Operations: continue tracking weekly order confirmations and report any anomalies in cash capture ahead of the monthly close.
