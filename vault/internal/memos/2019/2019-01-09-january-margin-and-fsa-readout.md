**Subject:** January milk margins, FSA delays, and member exposure

Milk margins are opening 2019 near their tightest point in several seasons, and the combination of high feed costs, a delayed federal safety net, and closed Farm Service Agency offices is beginning to generate inbound calls from our producer members. This memo reviews what we are seeing on the ground, what the data from this week's committee meetings tells us about member inquiries, and how we should handle communications over the remainder of the month.

## The current margin and safety-net gap

The 2018 farm bill replaced the old Margin Protection Program with Dairy Margin Coverage, written to apply retroactively to January 1 of this year. While the retroactive coverage is positive, the sign-up window is not yet open. Consequently, producers facing narrow feed-cost margins this month cannot count on immediate federal relief. Our advocacy contact points have heard from several member creameries and farmstead operations in the upper Midwest that are currently budgeting on January's realized numbers rather than anticipating near-term assistance.

Layered over this margin pressure is the partial federal government shutdown that began in late December, which has shuttered local FSA offices. For producer members relying on these offices for operating loan approvals, guarantee processing, or disaster assistance paperwork, approvals that typically clear in weeks are currently stalled indefinitely. While no news is not automatically bad news, the lack of staffing means that ordinary spring financing preparations are sitting untouched.

## Committee activity and member-to-member stopgaps

The Standards Committee, Events Committee, and Membership & Outreach Committee all held their Q1 2019 meetings this week. Membership & Outreach reported handling several inquiries regarding loan timing and feed-cost exposure. Rather than waiting on unresponsive federal offices, producers in several regions have begun calling one another to compare notes on financing timelines and processor contracts. This peer-to-peer problem-solving is constructive, but it highlights the administrative friction our members are absorbing.

Communications published an initial advisory on dairy markets this week, which correctly frames the situation without creating panic. However, we need to ensure that our guidance remains measured and does not promise regulatory solutions that we cannot deliver.

## Capacity and risk

The risk for January and February is not an immediate association-wide crisis, but rather cumulative cash-flow exposure for small producer members carrying high feed costs without operating loan guarantees. If local FSA offices remain closed into February, loan processing backlogs will compound, affecting spring purchasing commitments across our producer base.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: continue logging member inquiries regarding FSA loan delays so we can quantify the regional impact before our next committee cycle.
- Communications: coordinate any follow-up messaging with Advocacy to ensure our advice on bridge financing and lender talks aligns with what our committees are hearing.
- Office of the Chief Executive: note that regional advocacy requests may increase if the federal shutdown persists past the end of the month.
