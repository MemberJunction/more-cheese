**Subject:** Holiday order timing and wholesale block-barrel spreads

Retailers have been telling us all week that holiday orders are arriving earlier than usual. The public blog post published on 4 November framed this as a split between strong demand and nervous buyers front-loading their cases. This memo examines the operational reality behind those early orders, what the current volume data actually shows for the week ending 10 November, and why our wholesale supplier members need to watch the block-and-barrel spread rather than just headline pricing when planning their make schedules for December.

## What the week's order numbers show

For the week ending 10 November 2019, Membership Operations recorded 7 confirmed orders with a gross value of 1708 USD and zero cancellations. Cash captured for the week stood at 1178 USD. Total current membership at week-end reached 338. Membership purchases for the week totaled 7, all of which were first-time joins comprising 3 Enthusiast-tier purchases, 3 Individual-tier purchases, and 1 Corporate-tier purchase, with a membership gross of 1637 USD.

While these order numbers reflect normal pre-holiday activity, the timing is the anomaly. Producers supplying retail counters report that buyers are placing December orders a full two weeks ahead of historical averages. This is not driven by an actual shortage of product—production and supply remain healthy across our regional networks—but rather by wholesale buyers attempting to lock in capacity before settlement dates and freight bottlenecks compress their shipping windows.

## The block-and-barrel spread and margin exposure

For producer members operating in block-and-barrel markets, headline cheese prices can be misleading. The spread between block and barrel prices shifts continuously through the fall as processing plants adjust their output ratios. A wholesale supplier whose margin depends entirely on the block price without accounting for the barrel spread risks underpricing their inventory as early holiday demand pulls more milk into standard blocks.

Members who contacted the Membership & Outreach Committee this week noted that repricing an entire retail case overnight in response to a single supplier increase creates unnecessary friction with regular customers. A better approach, which aligns with our Pricing & Margin curriculum, is to isolate the specific cost shock to the affected line item while holding the rest of the case steady. Sorting products by how easily a regular customer can find a substitute provides a rational framework for absorbing input cost shifts without alarming the retail base.

## Capacity and advocacy alignment

Early holiday orders also present a scheduling opportunity for farmstead producers. Shifting make schedules earlier in November prevents the severe production crunches that typically occur during the second week of December. However, this operational adjustment coincides with ongoing advocacy work on raw-milk aging rules and FSMA small-producer exemptions. During this week, members engaged in 1 coalition meeting regarding raw-milk aging rules and submitted 4 petition signatures concerning FSMA small-producer exemptions. Coordinating production shifts while managing regulatory compliance paperwork requires careful internal scheduling.

## What we need

- Communications: Ensure future public messaging on holiday retail trends distinguishes clearly between direct-to-retail case strategies and block-and-barrel wholesale margin pressures.
- Advocacy & Standards: Maintain alignment between member inquiries on early wholesale delivery bottlenecks and our ongoing tracking of raw-milk aging rules.
- Membership Operations: Continue monitoring weekly membership purchase velocity and cash capture to ensure no early holiday invoicing anomalies are missed.
