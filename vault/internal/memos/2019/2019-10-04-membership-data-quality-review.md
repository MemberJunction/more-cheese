**Subject:** Membership data quality review — duplicate records and stale employers

This memo reviews ongoing data-quality issues in the member database, specifically focusing on duplicate record pairs, bounced communications, and employer affiliations that do not match current operations. As the federation approaches the end of 2019 with a recorded membership count of 317 current members at week's end, clean member data is critical for both dues collection and our advocacy letter campaigns.

## The scope of duplicate and stale records

Our periodic database audits have identified a recurring problem with duplicate profiles and stale employment data. Certain members, particularly those operating across multiple farmstead sites or transitioning between creamery cooperatives, have generated duplicate membership entries. In several instances, a single practitioner appears under two distinct profile IDs, splitting their participation history between committee work and event registrations.

Compounding the duplicate issue is the prevalence of stale employer affiliations. Members who have changed creameries or whose cooperatives have undergone restructuring often retain outdated organization links in their profile records. This directly impacts our advocacy outreach. When the Advocacy & Standards team circulates petitions or letter campaigns on raw-milk aging rules or FSMA small-producer exemptions, messages routed to stale email addresses bounce or reach former employers.

## Impact on advocacy and committee outreach

The low-visibility advocacy work highlighted in recent member spotlights relies entirely on accurate contact records. When an engaged farmstead owner spends twenty minutes drafting a comment on state agriculture committee notices, their message carries weight because it originates from a verified working producer. If that member's email address is misrouted due to a stale employer record or if their advocacy actions are split across duplicate profile keys, their engagement score is artificially suppressed, and our reporting to committees loses fidelity.

Furthermore, committee rosters require precise alignment between paid membership status and active participation. While our governance structure operates smoothly across six committees, inconsistent member records risk creating administrative friction during autumn meeting cycles and voting procedures.

For the week ending 6 October 2019, membership operations recorded 5 new first-time membership purchases (1 SmallBusiness, 3 Individual, 1 Corporate) totaling 1597 USD in gross sales. ## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: review the flagged duplicate profile pairs to determine which historical activity logs should be merged.
- Communications: verify that email distribution lists for advocacy alerts exclude bounced addresses and inactive profile IDs.
- Membership Operations: execute a systematic deduplication pass on all individual and small business accounts before the Q4 closing cycle.
