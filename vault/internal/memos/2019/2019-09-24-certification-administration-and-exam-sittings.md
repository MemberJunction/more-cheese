This week brings the usual autumn volume of credential inquiries, and our certificate records are due for a rigorous review before the winter course cohort begins. Members on the production side are currently reconciling their records against the upcoming January regulatory deadlines, which has created an immediate surge in questions regarding our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate and the Cheese Foundations Certificate. ## The administrative bottleneck

The gap between course completion and formal certification issuance is wider than it should be. When a member finishes a cohort like the upcoming Labeling Law & Compliance track starting in October, their exam results sit in our queue while Membership Operations verifies their active member status. That verification is necessary, but doing it manually for every candidate introduces avoidable delays during peak compliance seasons. We have heard from several members who completed requirements months ago and are still waiting for their official documentation to clear before an impending state inspection.

Furthermore, the distinction between holding an active credential and maintaining it through continuing education has not been communicated with sufficient clarity. Members assume that passing an exam once secures the title indefinitely, without realizing that recertification fees and continuing education requirements apply. We need a unified administrative protocol between credentialing and membership ops before the winter surge hits.

## Aligning with regulatory deadlines

As noted in our public communications this week regarding the small-producer compliance window closing in January 2020, very small businesses must maintain precise records even when they qualify for exemptions. Certification records serve as primary evidence of due diligence during food safety audits. If our records do not match a candidate's actual training history, the resulting discrepancies fall back on the association during regulatory reviews. We cannot afford ambiguity in our credential database.

Our records show that the 2019 total awards across all three active certificate categories stand at two for the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate, three for the Cheese Foundations Certificate, and one for the Sensory Evaluation Certificate. These figures must be accurate and accessible to members who need proof of training before their inspector arrives.

## Capacity and next steps

Education & Credentialing is currently managing course logistics for eight upcoming cohorts while simultaneously handling credential verification requests. Without additional coordination, administrative lag will continue to frustrate members who rely on our credentials for their compliance filings. We must streamline the verification handoff between our team and membership operations.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: review the pending credential queue by Friday and flag any membership status discrepancies.
- Advocacy & Standards: verify whether the Food Safety Committee's upcoming guidance document will reference our HACCP certificate prerequisites.
- Education & Credentialing: draft a clear, plain-language checklist for candidates awaiting exam results.
- Everyone: treat all preliminary certification counts as unverified until the monthly close reconciles the database.
