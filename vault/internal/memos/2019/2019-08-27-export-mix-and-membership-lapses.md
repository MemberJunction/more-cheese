> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT** — International Cheese Federation (ICF), a fictitious trade association created for the MemberJunction More Cheese demonstration environment. All names, figures, events, and decisions are invented.

**To:** Advocacy & Standards; Office of the Chief Executive; Communications
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 27 August 2019
**Subject:** Export mix and membership lapse review for the September close

As the new tariff line takes effect next week on US cheese bound for China, our membership records show a parallel operational problem that we need to settle. Several export-facing members currently sitting in our grace window are asking whether the federation can offer a temporary dues deferral while they re-calculate their landed costs lane by lane. We currently hold 297 current members at week end, and while four new members joined this week—three Individual and one SmallBusiness tier, generating 767 dollars in purchases—we are seeing small producers push back on renewal invoices by citing margin pressure from cumulative trade duties.

## What the payment and membership data show

This week brought six confirmed orders totaling 1,199 dollars with zero cancellations, and we captured 580 dollars in cash. However, the aggregate membership count masks the friction at the margin. Members who rely on regional consolidators for Pacific ports are reporting that classification questions under the new HS codes are creating unexpected cash-flow delays. When an import-export intermediary flags a misclassified wheel, the invoice stalls, and the membership renewal sits behind it on the desk.

We must decide whether to treat these stalled accounts as standard lapses or extend a structured grace window. A blanket policy is inappropriate here because the tariff impact is highly uneven. A larger cave operator shipping high-value wheels can absorb a single-digit rate change without altering their domestic wholesale commitments, whereas a smaller producer shipping thinner-margin formats is actively shifting fall inventory toward domestic accounts instead of export orders.

## Communications and advocacy overlap

Communications published a plain-language guide on the upcoming schedule change this week, pointing members toward the Membership & Outreach Committee to compare notes. That guidance is accurate, but it has generated inbound inquiries to our team asking whether the federation can intervene directly in customs broker disputes or negotiate fee relief. We cannot and should not do that. What we can do is ensure our grace-window practices do not penalize members who are actively re-checking their export mix.

We also note that zero course enrollments were taken this week, though ten course completions were recorded across existing cohorts, and no certifications were enrolled or awarded. The education pipeline is running quietly while administrative bandwidth is consumed by billing inquiries.

## Capacity and risk

Handling these grace-window requests individually takes time away from our monthly close reconciliation. If we grant informal extensions without a clear cut-off date, we risk distorting our membership retention baselines as we approach the end of the third quarter.

## What we need

- Advocacy & Standards: confirm whether any coalition meetings scheduled for next month will address trade documentation burdens so we can point inquiring members to a specific resource.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a standard thirty-day grace extension for members whose renewal payments are delayed specifically by export customs re-classifications.
- Communications: maintain the current line that tariff adjustments are commercial decisions for individual producers while directing billing questions squarely to our team.
