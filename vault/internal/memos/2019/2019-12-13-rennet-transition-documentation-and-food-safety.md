Following Thursday's webinar on microbial and fermentation-produced coagulants, our team has received several inquiries regarding what paperwork members must update when changing coagulants in the vat. This memo clarifies that a coagulant swap is a make-sheet and food-safety documentation change, not merely a label revision. If a producer alters their rennet source without updating their formal hazard analysis or batch records, an inspector will flag the discrepancy on a routine visit. We need to ensure our advisory responses and course materials align on this requirement before the new year.

## The make-sheet versus label distinction

The central issue is that coagulant source changes alter set times, moisture targets, and whey drainage patterns. Members moving from standard animal rennet to microbial coagulants or fermentation-produced chymosin often experience a softer set during the first few batches. If the make-sheet is not adjusted, the finished cheese moisture can drift outside the parameters specified in the creamery's food safety plan. The public blog post noted that the swap is a technical decision, but internally we must emphasize that inspectors check the HACCP parameters against actual vat records.

## Labeling and dietary claims

Retail buyers frequently check coagulant sources when cheese is marketed under religious or specialized dietary claims. A mismatch between the declared ingredient on the label and the actual coagulant purchased from the ingredient supplier creates an immediate compliance risk. We should remind members through our advisory channels that checking landed costs with distributors at year-end must go hand in hand with verifying supplier specification sheets.

## Capacity and education alignment

The Education & Credentialing team has noted strong interest in coagulation mechanics. While the Cheese Chemistry course covers these topics in depth, we should coordinate our responses so that members asking basic operational questions are directed appropriately without overwhelming our advisory bandwidth. The 12 December webinar drew zero paid registrations, as free webinars carry none in our records, but follow-up questions from attendees are landing in our inbox.
