**Subject:** Export tariff adjustments and second-year herd health risks

This internal memorandum addresses two critical operational friction points that have surfaced across our membership base during the first week of April 2019. First, mid-sized and exporting producers are navigating a split regulatory and tariff environment characterized by the unwinding of retaliatory duties in Mexico alongside persistently high tariff rates in major Asian markets. Second, farmstead cheesemakers in their second year of production are facing acute seasonal pressures during the spring milk flush, forcing abrupt recipe adjustments and exposing vulnerabilities in cost accounting and federal milk pricing formulas. Both issues require coordinated cross-departmental outreach and careful alignment between our advocacy positions, educational course offerings, and public communications.

## Export lane shifts and federal transport compliance

Members shipping across international borders are actively re-routing trade volume to capitalize on friendlier tariff lines in Mexico, while simultaneously working around prohibitive duties in Asian destinations. This strategic re-routing is far from frictionless. Moving product to a new international market requires updated export certificates, rigorous labeling verifications, and entirely new cold-chain logistics partners. Furthermore, heightened federal attention on the sanitary transport of refrigerated foods means that adopting a new shipping lane immediately triggers a thorough audit of carrier documentation and recordkeeping practices.

We have received a substantial volume of inquiries from members asking whether their existing distributor networks can absorb these new export corridors or if secondary distribution partnerships are strictly necessary. Communications must ensure that public-facing advisories accurately reflect the administrative burden and regulatory overhead involved in modifying export channels, avoiding any implication that tariff reductions eliminate compliance obligations.

## Second-year production and spring flush pressures

On the domestic front, farmstead producers operating in their second year are encountering the inevitable end of their initial operational honeymoon phase. The spring flush brings a sudden influx of richer, higher-volume milk from mixed herds, completely disrupting make sheets and cheese parameters that were carefully calibrated over the winter months. Cheesemakers are being forced to relearn culture and rennet timing on the fly as the raw milk composition shifts beneath them.

Compounding these technical hurdles, many second-year operations lack a precise understanding of their actual cost per wheel. Recent amendments to federal milk marketing orders and the Class I skim milk price formula have quietly shifted the baseline pricing floor against which small creameries benchmark their wholesale products. Without robust margin tracking, these growing operations risk severe underpricing.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: review whether the Milk Quality & Herd Health course adequately addresses mixed-herd seasonal swings and recipe adjustments for second-year producers.
- Communications: align upcoming public blog posts and member advisories with the practical realities of export documentation, carrier compliance, and sanitary transport rules.
- Membership Operations: flag any member accounts reporting operational distress, supply chain bottlenecks, or margin compression resulting from recent pricing formula shifts.
