> **FICTIONAL DEMONSTRATION DOCUMENT** — International Cheese Federation (ICF), a fictitious trade association created for the MemberJunction More Cheese demonstration environment. All names, figures, events, and decisions are invented.

**To:** Advocacy & Standards; Food Safety Committee; Communications
**From:** Membership Operations
**Date:** 1 November 2019
**Subject:** Member complaint on utility blackout and cave monitoring oversight

Following this past week's planned utility shutoffs in Northern California, Membership Operations received a formal complaint from a foothills member whose aging room experienced a severe humidity drop during a forty-eight-hour grid interruption. While the member managed to preserve the lot using a rented generator at the eleventh hour, she is disputing the practical utility of our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate program. Specifically, she argues that the credential's theoretical focus on critical control points and corrective actions fails to address real-world utility vulnerabilities, such as backup generator availability and supplier unreliability during regional blackouts.

## The nature of the member's grievance

The core of the complaint is not a request for financial compensation, but a challenge to our advisory positioning. The member notes that she completed the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate believing her written corrective-action plan was fully robust. However, when the shutdown occurred, the rental generator she relied upon in her spring plan had already been rented to another local business, forcing her into an emergency scramble that put two days of consistent rind development at risk. She argues that ICF educational resources should incorporate physical infrastructure audits, backup power validation, and supplier contingency planning rather than focusing exclusively on documentation and recordkeeping frameworks.

This grievance bounces between teams because it touches advocacy, certification design, and communications. Advocacy handles small-producer regulatory burdens, the Food Safety Committee owns the HACCP curriculum, and Communications has been publishing blog coverage celebrating members who rode out the outages. We need a unified internal stance before responding to her directly.

## Data context for the week

Our operational figures for the week ending 3 November 2019 show steady baseline activity that contrasts with this localized friction: total membership purchases stand at four, comprising one Enthusiast and three Individual sign-ups, generating five hundred fifty-nine dollars in gross dues. Confirmed orders reached five for eight hundred ninety-one dollars, with cash captured totaling seven-hundred fifty-four dollars. We recorded zero course enrollments, though Frida Mabry successfully completed requirements for the Cheese Foundations Certificate, and three petition signatures were logged regarding FSMA small-producer exemptions. The upcoming webinar on Rennet Alternatives 2019 is scheduled for 12 December, while two cohort courses—Washed-Curd & Dutch Styles in the Willow cohort and Counter Culture: Retailing in the Granite cohort—begin in early December.

None of these routine metrics address the operational vulnerability highlighted by the California complaint. If our credentialed members assume that holding a certificate guarantees operational resilience against utility failures, we are exposed to justified criticism regarding the scope of our educational offerings.

## Operational recommendations

We must decide whether to treat this as an isolated customer relations issue or a valid curriculum gap. Brushing off the complaint as an external utility failure ignores the reality that our training materials encourage members to write corrective-action plans without verifying their physical supply chains. The Food Safety Committee should review whether future revisions of the HACCP coursework should explicitly require physical verification of backup equipment rather than merely documenting paper procedures.

## What we need

- Food Safety Committee: review the complaint details and determine if supplementary guidance on backup generator verification should be drafted for existing certificate holders.
- Advocacy & Standards: verify whether any ongoing petitions concerning utility reliability or small-producer exemptions intersect with this member's operational concerns.
- Communications: ensure that ongoing blog posts regarding cold-chain management continue to emphasize practical supply chain auditing rather than abstract hazard analysis.
