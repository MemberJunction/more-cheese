**Subject:** Invoice dispute reconciliation and March close audit

Following the recent member billing inquiry from a corporate-tier account regarding a disputed invoice, Membership Operations has completed a line-by-line reconciliation of orders against captured cash for the year to date. While overall numbers remain healthy with confirmed orders reaching 24 and cash captured hitting 7315 for the current week ending July 7, the dispute highlights a recurring friction point: mismatched payment allocations between the initial order line and subsequent adjustments. This memo outlines the findings of our audit, the root cause of the disputed ledger entry, and the corrective workflow required before we close out the next billing cycle.

## The nature of the dispute

The member in question contested a billed charge that did not align with their annual dues schedule. Upon pulling the underlying order history, we found that a partial payment line had been misattributed to an incorrect product category due to an unlinked refund sequence in our legacy ledger mapping. The data shows that first-time membership purchases and tier mixes require stricter validation before invoices leave our desk. Although our total current membership sits at 271 at week end, discrepancies of this nature risk undermining member confidence precisely when export-sensitive channels and milk-over-feed margins are drawing heightened scrutiny from our base.

## Reconciliation findings

Our review of the broader order data indicates that the issue is isolated to this single account rather than a systemic ledger failure, but it exposes a vulnerability in how we handle mid-tier adjustments. The cash captured for the week stands at 7315 against confirmed order values of 7005. The gap between cash received and billed orders reflects timing differences in payment clearing rather than missing funds, yet without explicit audit trails, members attempting to reconcile their own statements will continue to flag these variances.

## Corrective controls

We cannot afford manual guesswork when dealing with corporate and small business accounts, particularly as preparations for the upcoming Annual Conference in Madison draw staff attention away from routine ledger maintenance. We must enforce a strict verification step for any invoice carrying adjusted line items before it is dispatched.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: authorize the proposed credit memo adjustment for the disputed account by close of business Wednesday.
- Communications: ensure no billing figures or preliminary order totals are quoted in external channels until our monthly reconciliation is fully signed off.
- Membership Operations: implement the dual-signoff rule for all corporate membership invoice modifications starting July 8.
