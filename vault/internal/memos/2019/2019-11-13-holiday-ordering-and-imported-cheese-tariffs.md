**Subject:** Holiday ordering, retail margins, and the new import tariff line

As we enter the holiday buying window, members are beginning to see the real-world impact of the federal tariff line on imported specialty cheese that took effect in mid-October. This memo outlines what our committees are hearing from the supply side, how retailers and domestic producers are reacting, and what we need to communicate before holiday orders are locked in. The broader wholesale cheddar market has behaved reasonably well for mid-November, remaining neither remarkably tight nor remarkably loose, but the import tariff is introducing friction into mixed cases that buyers were not planning for.

## What members are reporting on landed cost

Retailers who lean heavily on imported staples are seeing their landed cost per wheel move for the first time in quite a while. Because distributors and importers are absorbing portions of the increase, the change is not always obvious as a standalone headline on an invoice, but it is accumulating across line items. Retailers now face a classic holiday dilemma: absorb the margin squeeze through December or pass the increase along to consumers on the price tag and revisit pricing strategies in January.

On the other side of the counter, domestic producers are reporting a bit more room in cases next to their own products. Because a tariffed import is no longer undercutting domestic wheels on price quite as sharply as it did in September, buyers who are feeling the squeeze on imports are showing unusual receptiveness to domestic substitution conversations.

## Guidance for holiday case planning

We need to make sure our communications and member touchpoints encourage practical preparation. Buyers should check their suppliers' current landed cost rather than relying on pre-October price sheets. If a distributor has not yet issued updated wholesale pricing for affected categories, buyers should ask directly before finalizing holiday volumes that exceed typical monthly averages.

For domestic producers, this is a legitimate window to pitch alternative wheels. However, those conversations must be grounded in flavor profiles and aging characteristics rather than pure price comparisons. Producers who can speak plainly about how their product holds up against an import are finding success.

## Capacity and committee follow-up

The Membership & Outreach Committee has been logging these reports, and patterns we hear from three or four members in a single week are worth flagging before they catch the rest of the membership by surprise in January. We should ensure our upcoming communications do not overstate the disruption while still giving members actionable advice on checking supplier sheets.

## What we need

- Communications: integrate the practical advice about checking landed cost sheets into this week's outreach without creating alarm.
- Membership Operations: track whether any incoming membership inquiries or orders cite tariff pressures on wholesale purchasing.
- Events Team: keep these market dynamics in mind as we prep materials for upcoming webinars and winter meetings.
