## The Sixty-Day Rule and Inter-State Exposure

Under national regulations governing interstate commerce, cheese made from unpasteurized milk can move across state borders only if held at temperatures of not less than 35 degrees Fahrenheit for at least sixty days. While mountain cheeses and long-aged cheddars easily clear this hurdle, short-aged styles remain confined strictly to intrastate trade or require recipe alterations for pasteurized vats. When state public-health debates erupt over fluid milk outbreaks, state inspectors frequently tighten scrutiny on craft make rooms that adhere strictly to curing protocols. Our standards committee is currently compiling technical data on aging kinetics, water activity curves, and environmental pathogen verification to support risk-based regulatory frameworks rather than arbitrary calendar thresholds.
