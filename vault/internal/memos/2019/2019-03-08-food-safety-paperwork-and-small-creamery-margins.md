**Subject:** Food-safety paperwork and small-creamery margins

Following last week's Prairie Food Safety workshop in Lancaster and our ongoing discussions with members about small-producer exemptions, we need to align our advocacy messaging with the economic reality our members are facing. Federal figures released this quarter put the income-over-feed-cost margin under nine dollars per hundredweight for three months running, and that tightness is shaping every conversation we have about compliance paperwork. This memo outlines what we heard from workshop attendees, where the confusion lies regarding exemptions, and what we need from Education and Membership Operations before the end of the month.

## What the Lancaster workshop revealed

The Lancaster session made it clear that while members understand the intent of environmental monitoring, their primary operational hurdle is documentation. Several attendees admitted they have no written corrective-action process for a positive swab result—they handle it informally and move on. That informal approach is precisely what regulators flag. Our advocacy work is currently focused on asking the federal food-safety regulator for a standardized, single-owner corrective-action template that requires minimal administrative overhead.

At the same time, we are seeing widespread misunderstanding of qualified exemptions. Members frequently assume that qualifying for a modified hazard-analysis requirement means skipping recordkeeping entirely. When an inspector arrives and expects a written evaluation, the gap between assumption and reality causes severe friction. We must ensure our communications draw a bright line between a lighter form and no form at all.

## The margin pressure factor

When feed costs squeeze operating margins for three consecutive months, small creameries have zero slack for surprises. A compliance requirement that arrives without adequate runway is not just an administrative annoyance; it is a serious financial threat. We have formally asked the regulator for a longer transition period whenever exemption thresholds are adjusted, ensuring producers who outgrow a threshold mid-year receive sufficient notice to build their compliance plans.

Membership Operations should note that our advocacy actions this week include two letter-campaign submissions on interstate cheese shipments, one coalition meeting on raw-milk aging rules, and one petition signature. These topics directly intersect with the regulatory burden small producers carry.

## Capacity and coordination

Our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate coursework covers these qualification criteria, but course materials alone will not fix the immediate anxiety in the field. We need Education to ensure our course instructors emphasize practical, one-page templates that students can implement on Monday morning rather than theoretical frameworks designed for industrial plants.

## What we need

- Membership Operations: continue collecting specific examples of exemption confusion through the end of the month to strengthen our regulatory comments.
- Education & Credentialing: review the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate curriculum to verify that modified hazard-evaluation templates are featured prominently.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve our upcoming engagement letter to the federal regulator regarding template standardization by 15 March.
