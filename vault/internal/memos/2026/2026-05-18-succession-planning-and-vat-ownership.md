**Subject:** Succession planning, assistant handoffs, and vat ownership

Recent member inquiries and feedback from spring farmstead operations highlight a quiet structural bottleneck: small creameries are reaching succession moments without an overlap period, leaving assistants to step up to the vat with no log, no runway, and no formal handover. The public spotlight this week on assistant handoffs captures what we are hearing privately. Operators are calling in because a head cheesemaker retired or stepped back, leaving an assistant who ran the physical make sheet for years but never carried the final authority on pH, set times, and defect triage. This memo reviews what our education and membership channels can offer before the fall workshop season.

## What the current feedback shows

When an assistant takes over a vat without a structured overlap, the friction points are predictable. The recipe does not change, but the weight of small, unrecorded judgment calls does. Assistants report second-guessing themselves constantly during the first month—not because they lack the technical capability, but because they have never had to articulate the reasoning behind a make sheet line by line to someone else. Industry surveys this spring confirm that a high percentage of small dairy operations are heading toward succession without an obvious successor or a planned handover.

Our current course catalog provides partial support through courses like Milk Quality & Herd Health and the upcoming Building a Tasting Vocabulary cohort starting on 30 May, but neither is designed specifically as a succession framework. We have 63 course enrollments taken so far this week, showing healthy general engagement, but very few of those enrollments are explicitly tied by creameries to transition planning.

## What we should change in our outreach

We need to stop treating course marketing as purely technical training and start framing certain offerings as transition tools. When a member calls about an assistant stepping up to head cheesemaker, our standard response should point them toward structured cohorts that require the outgoing and incoming operators to sit down together and walk through the make sheet out loud. The credential itself matters less than having a forced institutional reason to audit the process before the retirement date arrives.

Furthermore, we should review whether our membership onboarding questions can better capture when an operation is undergoing a management transition. At present, our renewal records and new member counts (17 membership purchases this week, including 5 first-time members across Individual, SmallBusiness, and Corporate tiers) tell us who is paying, but they do not tell us who is actually running the vat.

## Capacity and coordination

Education & Credentialing is currently managing a heavy slate of course instances, including the upcoming Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets cohort on 28 June. Adding a succession advisory packet to their plate without additional administrative support is not feasible. However, a short, shared resource document compiled from existing blog commentary and member spotlights could be assembled by the end of the month without requiring new course development.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: review whether the Milk Quality & Herd Health curriculum can incorporate a short checklist for outgoing cheesemakers.
- Office of the Chief Executive: advise whether succession support should be added as a formal priority in our autumn advocacy and member outreach.
- Events Team: ensure that upcoming event sessions touch on labor and succession rather than focusing solely on technical processing.
- Everyone: treat transition inquiries as operational risk signals rather than routine customer service questions.
