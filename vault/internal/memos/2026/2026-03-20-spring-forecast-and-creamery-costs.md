Mid-March brought a federal forecast update that nudged the 2026 all-milk price outlook up alongside cheese product forecasts, while weekly market reports showed block cheddar at its highest print since last November. This memo reviews how those commodity movements intersect with our current membership numbers, the 21 membership purchases totaling $5,975 booked so far this week, and the ongoing advocacy work on labeling standards and raw-milk aging rules. ## What the forecast update actually changes for members

The federal production and price forecasts are doing distinct jobs. A higher production forecast indicates more milk on the ground, particularly from Central region herds, while a higher price forecast expects that milk and its cheese products to sell for more. For small creameries buying milk rather than milking their own herds, the critical metric is the spread between those figures. A rising production forecast paired with modest price bumps can easily soften if shipping volume increases. Trade outlets describing the underlying picture as still tight on margin match what our team has heard since January: costs have eased from last year's severe squeeze, but nobody is calling this a genuine rebound.

Our current member count stands at 1,516 at week's end, with 21 new membership purchases (8 first-time members across 15 Individual, 3 SmallBusiness, 2 Corporate, and 1 Enthusiast tier) generating $5,975 in gross sales. Confirmed orders reached 25 totaling $7,465, with 1 cancellation and $1,000 in cash captured so far. These numbers show steady baseline retention, but they also highlight why members are sensitive to cost-per-wheel calculations rather than headline commodity prices.

## Alignment between public posts and member realities

Communications published a piece this week examining the federal forecast update and exploring how distribution reps rebuild routes after closures. That public guidance is sound, but we must ensure internal operations support it. When members call to discuss their spring budgets or ask about the Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course (run through the Clover cohort earlier this year), we need our team to point them toward concrete cost models rather than commodity speculation. Similarly, Advocacy & Standards logged activity around labeling standards and Coalition meetings this week, which directly addresses the regulatory pressures our smaller producers face.

We should also note that certification and course completion metrics remain active, with 11 course completions recorded this week and upcoming cohorts scheduled for May, including Listeria Control in Small Creameries (Summit cohort) and Judging & Competition Preparation (Lark cohort). Connecting these educational offerings to the current cost and forecasting reality will help members protect their operating margins.
