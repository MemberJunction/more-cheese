**Subject:** Succession and vendor documentation rollout

Following this week's member spotlight on an accelerated farmstead handoff, this memo reviews how our membership files and documentation packages support smaller operations through ownership transitions. While stabilization-era conditions have slowed creamery closures, accelerated handoffs triggered by unexpected health events remain a primary friction point for family-run dairies. When a founder steps down abruptly, the incoming maker inherits vendor lists, food safety plans, and payroll schedules all at once. Our data shows that current membership records and course history migrate cleanly, but informal operating knowledge—such as late-paying wholesale accounts and uneven aging cave temperatures—frequently does not.

## What transfers and what does not

Our membership database currently tracks 1,534 members, including 22 new purchases recorded this week. Among those, 19 are Individual memberships, 1 is SmallBusiness, 1 is Corporate, and 1 is Enthusiast, yielding a gross of $4,875 for the week. While these accounts maintain their directory listing and course history when ownership shifts, our intake forms do not capture the informal operational notes that keep a small creamery functioning. A second-generation maker taking over mid-season spends weeks untangling distributor relationships that live entirely in the prior owner's head.

## Bridging the documentation gap

We propose developing a standardized succession checklist covering vendor contacts, local health inspector reporting schedules, and key supplier payment terms. This checklist should pair directly with our existing technical offerings, such as the Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese and Listeria Control in Small Creameries courses. Members who take over under duress rarely have time to discover margin gaps by trial and error; having structured templates for make-sheet logging and margin calculation prevents the new operator from simply repeating past pricing mistakes or trimming profitable accounts out of panic.

## Capacity and next steps

Building and distributing this template package will require coordination between Membership Operations and Advocacy & Standards, without adding meeting overhead to committees that already hold full agendas. The Food Safety Committee and Standards Committee met successfully on 13 April, and the Membership & Outreach Committee completed its Q2 meeting on 15 April. We should utilize those existing committee channels rather than scheduling standalone working groups.
