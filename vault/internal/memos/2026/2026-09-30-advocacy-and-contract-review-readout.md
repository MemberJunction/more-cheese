The one-year extension of the federal farm bill expired on Wednesday, September 30. As outlined in our public communications, the lapse pauses the federal dairy indemnity program and the dairy forward-pricing program for any new contracts established on or after October 1. While the bigger structural date remains December 31, the immediate operational impact falls on members attempting to renew or sign fixed-price milk and cheese agreements this week. This memo reviews our advocacy actions so far and the inbound questions hitting our help desk.

## What the advocacy log shows

Our current week totals reflect steady engagement from members navigating regulatory and pricing uncertainty. Specifically, the advocacy log records one coalition meeting held this week focusing on interstate cheese shipment. Across the broader year, members have logged a total of 165 advocacy actions, with 34 directed specifically at interstate cheese shipment and 32 addressing dairy pricing reform. The volume confirms that our key topics match the areas where members feel the most pressure.

Members are asking us whether existing forward contracts signed on or before September 30 are protected. As noted in public guidance, existing contracts running through September 30, 2029 remain exempt, but handlers executing new agreements today lose the exemption from federal minimum order pricing. This is generating friction with milk suppliers and distributors who are shortening contract terms and factoring potential regulatory noise into fourth-quarter invoices.

## Committee scheduling and member outreach

The Events Committee met on October 1 for its Q4 scheduled meeting, where members discussed upcoming workshops and outreach priorities. The Membership & Outreach Committee is organizing member calls to assist producers with drafting concise, two-paragraph notes to their congressional delegations detailing wheel production counts and cost exposure. We are also directing inquiries regarding margin calculations to the Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course, which provides practical models for handling variable milk-price ranges rather than single-point assumptions.

## Capacity and risk

Staff capacity is currently stretched between answering individual contract inquiries and preparing materials for upcoming workshops, notably the Curd Food Safety workshop scheduled in Boise on October 27, which has already taken 59 registrations this week. We cannot resolve individual contract disputes between members and their suppliers, but we must maintain clear guidance on what the law states and what the December 31 expiration date means for permanent law reversion.
