**Subject:** Traceability recordkeeping and creamery handoffs

The federal food-safety regulator released its new training curriculum on additional traceability recordkeeping this week, prompting several member inquiries about whether soft-ripened and semi-soft cheese operations need to alter their current batch and lot logging. The short answer is that the underlying requirement stands, the compliance deadline remains July 2028, and our Food Safety Committee advises members to focus on handoff documentation rather than waiting for potential exemptions. This memo reviews what the training asks for, how it intersects with our education catalog, and what we need to communicate to members.

## What the new curriculum changes

The regulation itself is not new. It requires specific records at each point a cheese on the federal traceability list changes hands: who sent it, who received it, the lot, the quantity, and the date. While a cottage cheese exemption earlier this year led some producers to assume broader relief, the rest of the list, including soft-ripened and semi-soft styles, remains covered. The new curriculum provides structured teaching material on what a compliant critical tracking event record looks like in practice.

Members are struggling primarily with translation rather than intent. A creamery wrapping wheels on a busy Tuesday afternoon finds it difficult to capture handoff data cleanly when a distributor's driver is waiting at the dock. Furthermore, the regulator floated a discussion paper this week exploring potential flexibilities on lot-level tracking for smaller operations. Our committee's stance is clear: members should build systems that satisfy the plain-language rule as written today rather than restructuring their workflows around a regulatory proposal that is not yet final.

## Intersection with education and advocacy

Our advocacy log records one active testimony on raw-milk aging rules this week, but our committee is spending most of its time fielding traceability questions. Members who already maintain strict quality control records are well-positioned, but the documentation gap almost always appears at the shipping dock.

Education & Credentialing has a natural bridge here. The *Listeria Control in Small Creameries* course already touches lot tracking in its environmental monitoring unit. We should ensure staff answering member queries are directing them to that material, particularly for creameries that ship across state lines and need their interstate shipping paperwork and traceability logs to align.

## Capacity and member guidance

Inbound volume on food safety and compliance is manageable so far, but it will rise as the July 2028 compliance date approaches and more operators review the new federal curriculum. We need a consistent, plain-language advisory that does not give legal advice but helps members map their handoff points on paper before an inspector asks to see them.
