Late June is the peak of the farmers' market season, and our member support queue has spent the week dealing with the same three questions: table temperature controls, state disclosure rules for raw-milk cheeses, and federal enforcement posture around interstate shipments. This memo consolidates what we are hearing from members on the ground and compares it against what we published in the member portal this week. We are seeing a steady volume of inquiries from small farmstead producers who set up market tables for the first time this spring, alongside established wholesale producers trying to hedge against soft wholesale pricing by selling direct.

## What the support queue shows

Calls regarding farmers' market compliance have doubled compared to May. The primary friction point is not spoilage or refrigeration failure, though table-top temperature drift during four-hour markets in high summer heat remains a recurring operational headache. The real source of friction is labeling. Members are discovering that a market table is a point of sale subject to the same strict disclosure requirements as a retail shop label: product name, ingredient list, net weight, and business name and address. For raw-milk products, state requirements vary widely, and federal enforcement regarding interstate raw-milk sales has tightened perceptibly this season. Several members reported inspectors asking whether they ship out of state to customers who sample cheese at a market stall.

Simultaneously, education enrollments are responding to the season. The federation's Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course, scheduled for its Curd cohort launch on June 28, has logged solid registration numbers this week. The 24 course enrollments taken across the board reflect strong interest in pricing and margin management for direct sales, rather than cheesemaking fundamentals. Producers who spent years selling exclusively to distributors are finding that retail pricing at a folding table requires an entirely different mental math.

## What the public posts cover

Our communications team published guidance on market stand setup and labeling this week, which aligns well with the questions arriving in our queue. However, the blog post touches on interstate raw-milk shipping risks without spelling out the internal referral pathway for members who receive an inquiry from their state department of agriculture. We need to ensure that when members call the helpline after reading the portal post, Advocacy & Standards and Membership Operations are giving consistent advice: direct them to their state agency first, and do not offer legal interpretations on federal interstate transport boundaries.
