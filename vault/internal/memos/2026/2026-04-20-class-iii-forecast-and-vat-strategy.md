**Subject:** Class III forecast increase and vat-level strategy

The federal forecasters raised the Class III price and the milk production estimate this month, moving both in the same direction. Normally, an expanded production outlook softens prices, but this spring's gain is driven by Mozzarella and Italian-style output running well ahead of last year while Cheddar production drops. Members are asking whether this pricing environment will hold. This memo outlines what the numbers mean for vat-level planning and how our communications should frame the forecast without overcommitting.

## What the production mix tells us

The divergence between Mozzarella and Cheddar is the critical signal for creameries this spring. Because the volume growth is concentrating in styles moving rapidly through food service and export channels rather than accumulating in cold-storage inventory, the market is absorbing the flush. For members operating on Class III-linked contracts, the forecast bump is tangible, but it remains a projection. The economics working group continues to advise members to treat single-month pricing as provisional and to build plans around a range rather than a point estimate.

For cheesemakers producing aged and cheddar-adjacent styles, the concentration of Mozzarella output in fast-moving channels creates relative headroom in caves and case placement. Several makers have indicated they are shifting flush-season output toward slower-turning aged lines precisely because high-volume styles are well supplied elsewhere. However, larger dairy cow inventories mean this herd size is slower to unwind if demand softens later in the year.

## Federation posture and risk management

April is traditionally the month for spring risk-management planning, forward contract locking, and margin coverage reviews. We must ensure our guidance remains measured. The positive report arrives just as members are sitting down to review hedge positions, which risks making a single favorable reading feel more decisive than the underlying auction data supports. Global dairy auctions have been choppy, alternating up and down cycles.

Our advocacy and standards messaging should reinforce caution. We are not issuing policy shifts based on one month of positive milk forecasts. Instead, we encourage members with renewing contracts to engage buyers now while forecasts are favorable, while keeping risk-management cushions intact.
