**Subject:** January milk price forecast and small creamery exposure

The federal milk production forecast moved higher this month, and the accompanying price outlook for cheese and butter has softened. For members who spent all of 2025 absorbing punishing feed and energy costs, a softer base milk price looks like relief on paper, but farmstead producers milking their own herds experience it as a direct cut to their milk check while their case costs remain entirely unchanged. This split is widening the gap between commodity-reliant makers and artisan producers whose pricing is driven by their own cost structure and reputation. This memo reviews our exposure, what the data is telling us so far this week, and how we coordinate our guidance.

## What the market data shows

Base pricing opened January below December's close, continuing a run across three straight order announcements. While national milk production is running well ahead of last year and cold storage holdings of natural cheese are close to flat, regional co-op blend prices and cull-cow numbers are moving independently of the national average. Members buying spot milk face a different ledger than farmstead operators. Our current active membership stands at 1,459 members, with 12 membership purchases recorded so far this week (11 Individual tier and 1 SmallBusiness tier, generating $2,325 gross). Confirmed orders across all federation products stand at 98 for $15,036 gross, with one cancellation and total cash captured reaching $18,428.

## Guidance and education alignment

We must ensure our messaging does not treat a national headline number as every member's reality. We have two courses directly relevant to this exposure: Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese (starting its Clover cohort on 27 January 2026) and our Listeria Control in Small Creameries curriculum. Members need to be reminded that a softer milk check is a prompt to revisit their case pricing now rather than waiting for summer rushes. Education & Credentialing should verify that enrollment pacing for the upcoming Clover cohort captures members reacting to margin pressure.

## Capacity and committee outreach

Committee volunteers are asking for our regional breakdown data from the economics briefing. Advocacy & Standards is fielding inquiries regarding dairy pricing reform, having logged one coalition meeting and one testimony session on that topic this week. We cannot resolve individual supply contract disputes, but we can ensure our pricing and margin guidance is actively routed to state committee contacts before February.
