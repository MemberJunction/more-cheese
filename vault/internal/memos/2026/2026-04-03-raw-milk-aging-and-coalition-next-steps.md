**Subject:** Raw-milk aging coalition next steps and committee coordination

The federal food-safety regulator's January release of its priority list put the raw-milk aging standard back on the docket for 2026. Following our Food Safety Committee coalition meeting this week, the room filled well past expectations. Members who produce hard, long-aged raw-milk cheeses are concerned that a flat standard treats a nine-month wheel the same as one at the sixty-day floor. This memo sets out what we know, what we do not know, and what we need from other departments before any public comment period opens.

## What the meeting surfaced

The current standard requires a minimum sixty-day aging period for raw-milk cheese, unchanged for decades. The agency's stated intention to examine aging practices sits alongside dairy safety training and food traceability work. We must emphasize to our members that an examination is not a proposed rule, does not mean the sixty-day floor is disappearing, and does not automatically signal a stricter regime. Both directions have appeared in past reviews.

Members attended in large numbers because they want an organized voice ahead of any formal draft. Scrambling to write a comment letter in sixty days, as happened during the previous traceability cycle, is exactly what the committee wants to avoid. We are maintaining a direct-notice list for any member who makes or ages raw-milk cheese and wants immediate word when draft text appears.

## Coordination across departments

Advocacy cannot run this campaign alone without tying into existing member services:

- Communications has already published a blog piece on the coalition meeting. We need to ensure that our direct-notice sign-up sheet is linked wherever raw-milk producers look for regulatory updates.
- Education & Credentialing owns the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate. Members who feel lost in the regulatory debate often lack the foundational chemistry knowledge that the certificate provides. We should cross-reference our certificate prerequisites in our committee updates.
- Membership Operations should note that engagement on this issue is concentrated among small farmstead producers who otherwise feel detached from federal policy.

## Capacity and risk

The stabilization era has eased feed and energy costs, but regulatory anxiety remains high. Staff time is currently split between dairy pricing reform, FSMA small-producer exemptions, labeling standards, and interstate shipment rules. Adding a major raw-milk aging campaign will stretch committee bandwidth if the agency moves faster than anticipated. We are not asking for more headcount, but we must be disciplined about what we commit to write.

## What we need

- Communications: verify that all links from this week's aging-coalition post point cleanly to our internal inquiry intake.
- Education & Credentialing: review whether the Food Safety & HACCP Certificate syllabus adequately addresses moisture and pathogen risk profiles for long-aged raw-milk wheels.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve our proposed timeline for stakeholder outreach ahead of any summer regulatory updates.
