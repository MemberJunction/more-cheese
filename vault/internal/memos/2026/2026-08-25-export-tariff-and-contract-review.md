## The scope of the inquiry wave

Export volume for US specialty cheese has held up reasonably well across the year, but exposure is concentrated among a minority of our corporate and small-business members who maintain regular cross-border shipments. When a trading partner alters tariff lines on short notice, the immediate operational friction is contractual. Many export agreements are silent on who absorbs sudden duty increases, leading to hurried negotiations between cheesemakers and overseas buyers.

Our role is not to interpret individual commercial contracts or provide customs brokerage advice. We must keep our guidance anchored to what the federation can legitimately support: pointing exporters toward standard documentation checks, highlighting the need to review shipment dates against the September 8 effective date, and reminding members of our existing educational resources such as the Export Documentation Essentials course. ## Internal coordination and messaging boundaries

We must not tell any member whether their buyer will pay the new duty, nor should we speculate on whether the trade dispute will expand to additional product categories.
