Recent regulatory shifts in several states have altered how milk supplies are monitored for avian influenza, moving oversight from individual farm visits to plant-level surveillance testing on pooled milk in bulk silos. For our farmstead and raw-milk members, this change is not merely bureaucratic. Pooled testing catches potential flags faster across a broader footprint, but it also creates immediate vulnerability for clean producers whose milk is co-mingled with an affected supplier prior to processing. We recorded one instance this month where a member creamery faced two days of production holds despite clean herd tests simply because their milk shared a receiving tank under investigation. ## What the regulatory shift means for members

Our filed public comments emphasize that any pooled-sampling framework must include a published, predictable trace-back schedule rather than open-ended holds.
