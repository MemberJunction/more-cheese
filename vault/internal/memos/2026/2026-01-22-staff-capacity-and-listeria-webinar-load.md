**Subject:** Staff capacity and the upcoming Listeria webinar prep

Next Tuesday's free webinar on Listeria Control is scheduled right at the moment when spring production ramps up and cave environmental monitoring programs across our membership are shifting from winter maintenance to active processing. That timing makes it an essential session for small-format creameries trying to establish a documented swabbing schedule rather than reacting to audits. However, it also creates an acute staffing bottleneck for us. The same functional leads who manage the live event delivery are currently processing 43 course enrollments taken this week, handling the upcoming Clover cohort start for Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese next Tuesday, and preparing logistics for the Stone Sensory workshop in Tandil on 30 January.

## The workload collision

The volume coming across our desks this week is real and immediate. We recorded 43 course enrollments and confirmed 114 orders grossing 17604 USD so far this week, alongside 10 membership purchases generating 2200 USD in gross revenue. While feed and energy costs have eased compared to last year's severe squeeze, our internal administrative load has not. The Education Committee met on Monday, 19 January, to review Q1 milestones, and the action items from that meeting overlap directly with the preparation for EVT-2026-WEB1. We are attempting to run a live technical webinar, onboard a major cohort, and finalize workshop travel for Tandil within a five-day window.

Communications published a preview of the webinar this week highlighting why environmental monitoring is critical as small creameries ramp up spring milk volumes. That public post has driven a surge of inquiries to our help mailbox about swab site selection and corrective action plans. Because free webinars do not carry paid registrations in our tracking data, we have no automated way to gauge the exact headcount before Tuesday, making capacity planning for the live Q&A session entirely speculative. We are guessing at server loads and moderator availability based on past sessions rather than hard registration counts.

## Where the work breaks

When multiple operational priorities collide like this, routine association tasks get dropped. Last week's cave management blog post generated significant member interest in humidity and airflow control, and members are emailing Education & Credentialing for follow-up resources they saw referenced online. But those inquiries are sitting unanswered because the staff assigned to answer them are also building the slide decks for Tuesday's webinar and reconciling orders from the 114 confirmed transactions this week.

We cannot indefinitely absorb overlapping event preparations, course starts, and committee deliverables without dropping member correspondence or cutting corners on event technical checks. The current staffing model assumes that administrative bandwidth expands automatically whenever milk production or course enrollment spikes. It does not. The 1461 current members at week end expect prompt technical responses, but our response window has stretched past our internal targets.

## What we need

- Education & Credentialing: provide one designated staff lead to handle all live Q&A moderation for Tuesday's webinar so Events Team can manage the technical backend.
- Communications: pause any additional promotional blasts for the Listeria webinar so inbound support queries stabilize before Tuesday.
- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a temporary reallocation of administrative support from general committee correspondence to course enrollment processing through the end of the month.
- Everyone: acknowledge that non-critical member inquiries regarding winter cave logs will face a delayed response until after the Tandil workshop departs.
