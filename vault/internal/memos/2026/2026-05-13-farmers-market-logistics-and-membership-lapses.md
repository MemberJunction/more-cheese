## The direct-sales shift and cash flow

The pivot toward farmers markets and direct-to-consumer sales described in our public communications is putting real pressure on working capital for small-tier members. Producers shifting away from exclusive wholesale arrangements are spending money upfront on equipment that does not show up in traditional distribution models. When their annual renewal invoice lands, we are seeing more requests for grace windows rather than immediate payment. These are not refusals to pay; they are timing issues caused by the upfront costs of operating a retail table before the summer market season generates cash.

We need to ensure our grace-window policy accommodates these seasonal cash-flow dips without letting accounts drift into permanent lapses. The 13 membership purchases booked this week—split across nine Individual tiers, two SmallBusiness tiers, and two Enthusiast tiers—show that new intake is holding, but retaining the existing base requires us to be flexible when a member calls to explain that their capital is tied up in a market tent and a reliable card reader.

## Connecting education to operational hurdles

The operational hurdles our spotlight member faced—choosing the right three cheeses, pricing against supermarket blocks, and keeping a cooler at temperature—are exactly what Education & Credentialing covers in the Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets course and the Cave Management curriculum. We have 47 course enrollments taken this week across active cohorts, showing healthy appetite, but we need to make sure members know these courses solve practical booth and cave problems rather than just offering theoretical instruction.

When a member calls Membership Operations asking for a billing extension because market setup costs wiped out their monthly cash, pointing them toward courses that improve margin or streamline retail logistics gives them a practical reason to maintain their standing with the federation.

## Advocacy and small-producer exemptions

On the advocacy front, the Coalition Meeting on labeling standards held this week ties directly into the same group of members. A producer running a farmers market tent has to defend their label and price point dozens of times a day against industrial alternatives. If our labeling guidelines and FSMA small-producer exemption positions do not reflect the realities of direct sales, our members spend their market day arguing with shoppers instead of selling cheese.

- Events Team: share any upcoming market logistics inquiries so we can flag accounts that might need temporary billing flexibility.
