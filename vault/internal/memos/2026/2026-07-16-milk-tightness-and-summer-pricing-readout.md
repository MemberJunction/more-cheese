**Subject:** Milk tightness, component drift, and summer pricing readout

This week's public post on spot milk tightening has brought a noticeable surge of inbound calls to our member support line. Small creameries across several dairy regions are reporting that hot weather is suppressing dry matter intake, which in turn depresses milk volume, butterfat, and protein components. Because many members pay on a component basis, the practical effect is an unexpected upward creep on their cost sheets just as the July retail push and our annual conference lead-up demand full attention. This memo consolidates what our membership records and committee discussions show so far this week, and sets out what we need to coordinate before next Tuesday's conference opening in Madison.

## What our numbers show this week

As of the close of our Food Safety Committee meeting on 15 July and our committee cycle through Thursday, membership and order volumes remain steady despite the summer heat. We recorded 10 membership purchases grossing 3,000 USD, bringing our current member count to 1,577 at week's end. Of those 10 purchases, 5 are first-time members, distributed across Individual (6), SmallBusiness (2), Enthusiast (1), and Corporate (1) tiers. On the commercial side, confirmed orders stand at 155, generating 40,135 USD in gross sales with zero cancellations, and cash captured is 39,960 USD. Course enrollments taken this week reached 76, with 56 completions, alongside two certification awards: a Cheese Foundations Certificate to Ishita Grantham and a Sensory Evaluation Certificate to Matteo Ferrier.

However, the transactional numbers do not capture the operational friction members are facing on the floor. Creameries relying on spot milk are finding that cream multiples are running higher than normal as component levels dip, and triple-crème makers are discovering that cream-enriched recipes are sensitive to these shifts. Several producers have asked whether our Pricing & Margin for Specialty Cheese course covers mid-cycle repricing conversations with retail buyers who locked in spring pricing. It does, but we need to ensure our helpline staff are pointing callers directly to that curriculum rather than offering informal pricing advice.

## Operational strain and committee alignment

Beyond milk checks, the heat is creating logistics headaches that overlap with conference preparations. The Events Committee met on 15 July to review transport risks for members driving competition entries and workshop supplies to Madison. With temperatures pushing past normal thresholds, members hauling soft-ripened or washed-rind categories are running into pre-cooling and cooler-maintenance questions that our usual pre-event briefings did not anticipate. Meanwhile, the Food Safety Committee reviewed Q3 items, and the Awards & Competition Committee completed its Q3 meeting on 17 July, confirming that intake procedures for Madison and the Bega Stone Food Safety workshop (EVT-2026-W1) are fully staffed.

We also note that event registrations taken this week include 51 for the ICF Annual Conference 2026 (EVT-2026-CONF) and 94 for Workshop: Stone Food Safety (EVT-2026-W1). Both events occur next week, and our registration counts are preliminary until the respective event dates arrive.

## What we need

- Communications: ensure any follow-up posts or member newsletters direct milk-check inquiries toward our Pricing & Margin course and highlight component testing cadences rather than treating spot milk volatility as an isolated regional anomaly.
- Events Team: verify that competition check-in volunteers at Madison are briefed on handling heat-stressed rinds at the intake table without causing friction for arriving makers.
- Education & Credentialing: confirm that our upcoming cohorts starting next Monday, specifically Cave Management (Curd cohort) and Direct-to-Consumer & Farmers' Markets (Granite cohort), have all necessary instructor materials dispatched despite travel and heat disruptions.
- All Teams: log any specific creamery complaints regarding milk shortfalls or supplier substitution language so we can feed them into the Standards Committee's next review cycle.
