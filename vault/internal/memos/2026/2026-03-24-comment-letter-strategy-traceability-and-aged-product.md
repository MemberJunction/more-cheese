The federal food-safety regulator's open listening sessions on lot-level tracking have generated a steady trickle of member inquiries. While the public sessions are scheduled for later this quarter, our members are already asking whether lot codes require re-labeling every wheel that leaves the cave. This memo outlines our advocacy response and the comment letter strategy we are preparing before the comment window closes.

## What the comment letter must address

The core anxiety among our farmstead and small-scale members is the burden of software compliance and separate cooler tracking. A federation survey from last fall indicates that most farmstead members under fifteen employees still maintain batch records on paper or via self-built spreadsheets. The traceability rule does not mandate specific software; it requires that records containing the traceability lot code, date, quantity, and transformation location be producible within twenty-four hours upon request. We must make this distinction explicit in our guidance so members stop assuming expensive commercial software is compulsory.

Furthermore, the letter needs to address whether cheese sits appropriately on the high-risk foods list at all. Products undergoing long aging cycles present entirely different biological and moisture profiles than fresh, unaged cheeses. Treating an aged wheel under the same rigid lot transformation rules as a fresh curd product ignores the realities of affinage.

## Committee alignment and member input

The Food Safety Committee is currently compiling member input, drawing on practical examples from producers who have already built functional batch logs. A paragraph from an active cheesemaker carrying real cave experience holds significantly more weight in the administrative record than generalized industry testimony. We are urging the committee to finalize their draft so we can submit before the formal deadline.

Communications has already published initial guidance pointing members toward our Food Safety & HACCP Certificate coursework, which breaks down required traceability fields field by field. The comment letter will reinforce these resources while pushing for specific exemptions on extended-aging product lines.
