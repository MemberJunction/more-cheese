**Subject:** Membership renewal grace-window requests and payment failures

As we move through the first month of stabilization in 2026, Membership Operations is seeing an uptick in manual support requests around lapsed accounts and failed renewal payments. While input costs and feed prices have eased compared to the squeeze of 2025, small creameries and individual members are still navigating tight cash flows. Our current data shows 13 membership purchases booked so far this week, totaling 2675 USD in gross sales, but we are also managing a growing backlog of payment processing failures and requests for grace-window extensions that require a standardized operational response.

## The current intake pattern

Memberships that lapsed at the end of the prior year are attempting to renew, but automated credit card charges are failing due to updated bank security requirements or temporary cash crunches. Under our current operating rules, an account drops out of active standing immediately upon payment failure, triggering automated lapse notices. This is causing friction with members who have been part of the federation for years and view the lapse as an administrative glitch rather than an intentional exit. The public blog has focused heavily this week on succession conversations and herd cold-stress management, but behind the scenes, our front-line queue is dominated by billing inquiries and grace-window requests.

## What the data tells us so far

For the week of Monday 2026-02-02, we recorded 13 total membership purchases, comprising 10 first-time buyers and 3 renewals or tier selections. The breakdown by tier includes 9 Individual purchases, 2 SmallBusiness purchases, and 2 Enthusiast purchases. Total confirmed orders stand at 14 with a gross value of 2821 USD, alongside 1 cancellation and a total cash capture of 17054 USD across all association receivables. The volume of new first-time members is encouraging, but it masks the retention friction on the renewal side where expired credit cards are not being updated promptly.

## Operational risks

If we continue to handle these failed payments on an ad-hoc basis, we risk alienating long-standing members who simply need a 14-day grace window to update their payment credentials. Conversely, granting indefinite extensions without a clear policy creates accounting discrepancies between cash captured and receivable balances. We are not equipped to offer financial or legal advice to creameries struggling with cash flow, but we can provide a predictable, transparent window for payment reconciliation.

## What we need

- Office of the Chief Executive: approve a standardized 14-day grace window for renewal payments before accounts are officially moved to lapsed status.
- Communications: align member-facing FAQ updates with the new grace-window policy so support staff can direct inquiries to a clear reference page.
- Membership Operations: establish a weekly tracking spreadsheet to monitor failed payments versus successfully recovered renewals through the end of Q1.
