Tuesday's Webinar: Listeria Control 2026 filled up its capacity without any paid registrations in the data — as a free webinar, it carries zero registration lines — but the volume of follow-up questions reaching the member portal since yesterday morning makes it clear that the recent trade press recall notices are driving a high level of anxiety among producers. This memo covers what members are asking, why their environmental monitoring logs are coming under sudden internal review, and how we should route the resulting inquiries without overstepping our advisory boundaries.

## What members are asking in the portal

The questions arriving through the member portal are not about the federal regulations in the abstract. They are specific and tactical. Producers are asking whether their swab schedules are adequate, how often condensate lines and floor drains should be sampled, and whether an aging cave with multiple renovations needs a completely rewritten testing map. Several members reported spending their evenings walking their rooms with notepads after the webinar, discovering that their current logs still test spots that haven't physically existed in their facilities for years.

This is a quiet, positive correction happening across our member base, but it is generating a heavy support load. Producers want confirmation that their testing schedules meet the standard required by their certifying bodies. We need to be entirely clear in our replies: an internal webinar and a rewritten swab log are good hygiene, but neither constitutes a formal review or a legal guarantee of compliance.

## Aligning our guidance with coursework and committee review

The Food Safety & HACCP Certificate and the Listeria Control in Small Creameries course are the correct structural destinations for members who realize their testing plans are outdated. However, we should avoid pointing members to the webinar recording as a substitute for professional food safety consultation. The Food Safety Committee is preparing a short written summary for members who requested the recording, but that document will explicitly state that facility-specific questions belong with a qualified consultant or certifying body.

We also need to watch our handling of the milk pricing split that hit the trade press this week. While milk check numbers are moving in opposite directions for cheese-linked versus powder-linked producers — a topic covered in our industry updates — members dealing with margin pressure are occasionally conflating their input costs with their food safety compliance expenses. We must keep those two support queues separate.

## Capacity and routing

The surge in portal messages is hitting Advocacy & Standards and Education & Credentialing simultaneously. We cannot answer individual facility layout questions without taking on liability we are not structured to carry. Membership Operations should ensure that standard template guidance is deployed for all incoming swab-log inquiries, directing producers toward our structured courses rather than custom advisory chats.

## What we need

- Communications: verify that all public blog posts and webinar summaries carry the explicit disclaimer that ICF does not provide individual food safety opinions.
- Education & Credentialing: ensure the next cohort enrollment pages for Listeria Control in Small Creameries are accessible to members inquiring through the portal.
- Membership Operations: deploy the standard routing template for environmental monitoring questions by end of week.
- Advocacy & Standards: finalize the committee's written summary of Tuesday's session for distribution.
