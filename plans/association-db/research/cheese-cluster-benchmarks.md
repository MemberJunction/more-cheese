## 1. Bread Bakers Guild of America — THE size-and-shape twin

**EIN 25-1706251** (Cranston, RI). Artisan-bread education guild, founded 1993. Nonprofit, files full 990. 3–5 employees, ~150 volunteers.

- **Members:** "nearly 2,000 members — including professionals and home bakers" (Wordloaf profile; multiple secondary sources; an older Macrina Bakery reference says "more than 1,300"). Explicitly mixes professionals with a **serious-home-baker enthusiast contingent** (there is a live members' debate thread titled "The Future of Serious Home Bakers In The Guild?" on their Groups.io — the enthusiast-periphery tension is real at the twin, not just at ACS).
- **Dues structure (bbga.org/join, 2026):** Individual **$100**/yr (or $10/mo), Student $100, Culinary school $200, Company tiers **$154–$1,760** banded by company revenue ($250k → $10M+). Cheaper than ACS ($175 individual) but the same shape: individual + revenue-banded company tiers.
- **990 dues trend (Part VIII "Membership Dues" line, ProPublica full filings):**

| Tax yr | Membership dues | Δ YoY | Total revenue | Classes & events rev |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018 | $214,565 | — | $424,925 | $163,145 |
| 2019 | $224,838 | +4.8% | $582,225 | (in contrib-heavy yr) |
| 2020 | $216,125 | −3.9% | $340,024 | — |
| 2021 | $205,698 | −4.8% | $363,087 | $104,552 |
| 2022 | $203,643 | −1.0% | $451,697 | $92,539 |
| 2023 | $196,398 | −3.6% | $433,958 | $167,000 |
| 2024 | $187,034 | −4.8% | $538,363 | $133,264 |

  **Verdict: DECLINING.** Peak 2019, then five consecutive down years (2020–2024, 990-verified); −16.8% cumulative from peak, ~−3.6%/yr average, accelerating to −4.8% in the latest year. With no evident dues-price increase (individual has sat at ~$100), this is a headcount decline. If acquisition runs ~10%/yr, the implied renewal is ~86–88%.
- **Events vs membership:** No large flagship conference — the model is many master classes/seminars (program expense $218k FY2024) plus the occasional Camp Bread (2024 edition was **members-only**, small-scale). Events revenue ≈ $130–170k/yr ≈ 25–30% of total revenue. Revenue mix FY2023: dues ~45% of revenue — dues-heavier than ACS because there's no big conference+competition engine.
- Sources: ProPublica EIN 251706251 full filings (objects 201931359349312848 … 202513189349312641); bbga.org/join; newsletter.wordloaf.org/bread-bakers-guild-of-america-647/.

## 2. Specialty Coffee Association — the certification+competition culture twin (bigger)

**EIN 52-1312827** ("Association for Specialty Coffee", Irvine, CA). 46 employees (FY2024).

- **Members:** **just under 8,000 paid** individuals+companies before the March 2024 model overhaul (SCA spokesperson to Daily Coffee News). In 2024 SCA collapsed four plans into two — a **free "Associate" tier** + paid tiers — explicitly to fix stagnant paid membership; within six months claimed membership "swelled to more than 100,000 companies and coffee professionals" (2024 Annual Report). I.e., the era's craft-org growth lever is a **free enthusiast tier**, not paid-member growth.
- **990 dues trend:** TY2022 dues **$1,141,329**; TY2024 dues **$1,136,743** — **FLAT** across the model change (paid core neither grew nor collapsed). Meanwhile "Membership Training" (education/certification: CSP courses, Q-grader-adjacent programs) went **$6.92M (TY2022) → $9.22M (TY2024)**, +33%, and is the single largest revenue line — **43% of $21.4M total revenue**. Conferences & meetings $2.13M → $3.55M.
- **Events:** 2025 Specialty Coffee Expo (Houston): **~17,000+ registered attendees** (organizers, via Daily Coffee News) — >2× the paid membership; the 2022 Expo broke the 2019 record by +23%. Competitions: US/World Barista Championships etc. run as spectacle within Expo.
- **Reading:** at the certification-rich twin, **education/certification revenue is 2× conference revenue and 8× dues revenue**, and event registrants far exceed paid members. Dues are a minority revenue stream (~5%).
- Sources: ProPublica EIN 521312827 (objects 202323489349301822, 202503219349310485); dailycoffeenews.com 2024-03-07; sca.coffee 2024 annual report announcement; dailycoffeenews.com 2025-04-27.

# CLUSTER VERDICTS

## Verdict 1 — Overall renewal: **0.87 stands (keep the R8 adjustment; do NOT revert to 0.89)**

Dues-trend proxy across the cluster, ordered by similarity to ACS:

| Org | Model | Dues-line trajectory | Health read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bread Bakers Guild (twin) | individual+company | **−3.6%/yr for 5 straight yrs, −4.8% latest** | soft |
| American Cider Assn | producer tiers + $75 individual | **−6.7% latest yr**, post-boom plateau | soft |
| FCIA | company+individual+enthusiast | **−16% latest yr**, dues cut to retain | soft |
| AMMA | producer+hobbyist | **collapsed** (rev −78% from 2019) | very soft |
| CA Artisan Cheese Guild | creamery+supporter | revenue −26% over 5 yrs | soft |
| ADGA | individual registry | flat (−1%, then +16% incl. price action) w/ declining registrations | neutral |
| SCA | paid indiv+company | **flat** paid dues 2022→2024; went free-tier because paid was stagnant | neutral-soft |
| WCMA | company trade org | +44% 2018→2024 | healthy (but trade model, weakest analog) |

The only clearly growing dues line in the cluster belongs to the one org that is NOT an individual-member society. Every individual/craft-producer-member analog is flat-to-declining. A flat-to-−3% membership base with a typical ~10%/yr new-member inflow implies renewal ≈ **85–88%**. The team's 89% would imply modest growth at normal acquisition — which nothing in the closest cluster exhibits.

**Recommendation:** `renewal_rate_overall` = **0.87 ±0.02, confidence med→high** (upgrade confidence: this pass replaces the IACP/AHA-based inference with same-shape orgs; the ±0.02 band's top (0.89) now represents the org on a good year, not the central tendency). Keep the re-anchored first-year (0.68) and tenured (0.92) curve as-is — nothing in the cluster contradicts it, and FCIA/SCA behavior (dues cuts, free tiers) independently corroborates soft first-year economics.

## Verdict 2 — Conference attendance 35% of members: **keep 0.35 ±0.10; the cluster says the risk is UNDER-stating, not over-stating**

- **ACA/CiderCon:** ~1,100 attendees vs ~1,000-ish member entities → registrants ≈ 100%+ of member count (member companies send multiple people; non-members attend). CiderCon = 60% of ACA revenue.
- **WCMA/CheeseExpo:** ~5,200 attendees vs 850 member orgs (≈6×) — trade-show model.
- **SCA Expo:** ~17,000 registrants vs ~8,000 paid members (≈2×) — enthusiast/industry periphery floods in.
- **BBG (the twin):** the counter-case — no flagship conference at all; distributed small classes instead. When a craft society *has* a flagship, it is the revenue center and pulls registrant counts ≥ the membership count; when it doesn't, event participation fragments into workshops (which the separate `course_participation_rate` = 0.50 already models).

The generic-society 25% prior is clearly wrong for this cluster; every flagship-holding analog runs registrants ≥ 50% of members once non-members and multi-attendee companies are included. The v0.6 decomposition — **35% member attendance + 30% non-member registrant share** — reproduces both ACS's observed numbers and sits comfortably below the cluster's (company-inflated) ratios.

**Recommendation:** `conference_attendance_pct_membership` = **0.35 ±0.10 unchanged, confidence med→high** (mark the R7 "PENDING team confirmation" as now cluster-corroborated). Optionally raise `nonmember_share_of_conference_registrants` central value 0.30 → **0.32** (cluster shows 30–50%+ at SCA/WCMA; ACA implies ≥30%) — or leave 0.30 and simply widen tolerance to ±0.10. No-change is defensible.

## Verdict 3 — Other targets the cluster moves

1. **`dues_individual` $175 — ~~CONFIRMED AGAIN, independently~~ RETRACTED 2026-07-07.** ACA's *individual* tier is $75; the $175 was their smallest commercial-cidery tier (raised to $185 July 2024). The $175 target stands on ACS alone — verified directly on cheesesociety.org 2026-07-07. (BBG is $100 — the low end for a guild without a conference/competition engine.) Keep $175 ±25, confidence high.
2. **`dues_corporate_range` [225, 3000] — validated.** ACA producer tiers $185–$3,450; BBG company tiers $154–$1,760; WCMA company tiers larger. ACS's $225–$3,000 sits mid-cluster. No change.
3. **`dues_share_of_revenue` 0.22 — validated for a flagship-holding society.** ACA dues ≈ 19–21% of revenue; WCMA ≈ 19% of organic revenue; SCA ≈ 5%. BBG (no flagship) runs ~40–45% — the exception that proves the mechanism: dues share is low *because* the conference/competition engine exists. Keep 0.22 ±0.04. The `nondues_revenue_mix` events-dominance (~60%) is directly reproduced by ACA (CiderCon 60% of revenue).
4. **`cert_candidates_per_year` 0.045 — keep, but raise the non-member share of candidates.** ACA's CCP runs exam fees of ~$40.8k/yr (order 500+ candidates) against ~1,000 members — because craft-food certifications sell to the hospitality/retail *periphery*, not the member core (same at SCA, where education/cert revenue is 43% of total and 8× dues). The 4.5%-of-members candidate rate is right for the ACS-style single-sitting CCP, but the "~20% of candidates are non-members" note should move to **~30% (tolerance ±10)**, and this is a growth lever worth a demo storyline (education revenue is THE growth line at both certification-rich analogs).
5. **`competition` block — no recalibration (per the $decisions ACS anchor), one regime nuance to add:** WCMA's contest revenue was flat through COVID ($495k→$624k→$619k→$627k, 2018→2024; 3,302 entries 2024, 3,375 for 2026) while its conference went to zero and back. In the `covid_shock` regime, competition entries = 0 is an *ACS policy fact* (they canceled), not an industry inevitability — keep it, but the recovery regime can legitimately show entries rebounding faster than conference attendance.
6. **`staff_fte` 10 ±3 — nudge down to 8 ±3.** Cluster: BBG 3–5, ACA 5, WCMA 8–11, FCIA ~1, guilds 0–1. An ACS-scale society sits at 5–11; PARN's 13-FTE average reads high for craft-food. (Low-stakes; ±3 tolerance already brushes the cluster.)
7. **`enthusiast` modeling — corroborated.** BBGA's home-baker contingent, FCIA's enthusiast tier + installment dues, SCA's free Associate tier: every analog is actively courting (and struggling to monetize) an enthusiast periphery. Keep `renewal_rate_enthusiast_tier` 0.65 and the free/low-tier storyline; the cluster suggests a *free-tier introduction* is a realistic mid-series regime event if the generator ever wants one.
8. **`scenarios.decliningOrg` — strengthened.** AMMA (revenue −78% from 2019, reserves draining) and CACG (six down years) are in-cluster, same-domain declining orgs — the preset no longer rests on the IACP outlier. Cite AMMA/CACG in the scenario note.
9. **`total_revenue_usd` $1.7M — keep.** Cluster context: BBG $0.43–0.58M at ~2,000 members (no flagship), ACA $0.8–1.0M at ~1,000 members (flagship, no big competition), ACS $1.71M at ~2,300 (flagship + competition + certification). ACS's number is the right anchor for an org that has all three engines; no change.
